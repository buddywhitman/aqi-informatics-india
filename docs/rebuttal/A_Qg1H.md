We thank the reviewer for a careful review. We agree with most points. Below we (i) give the identification result you asked for, (ii) report experiments run for this response, and (iii) narrow our claims.

**Q2/W1 (identification).** New proposition. Assume (a) the proxy HMM is identified up to labels (Allman et al., 2009), so $\gamma_t=P(S_t\mid Z_{1:N})$ is a functional of the observed law; (b) exclusion: $(T_t,Y_t)\perp Z_{1:N}\mid(X_t,S_t)$; (c) the controls carry no regime information beyond the proxies, $P(S_t\mid X_t,Z_{1:N})=\gamma_t$ ($\delta_X=0$); (d) $M(x)=E[\gamma_t\gamma_t^\top\mid X_t=x]$ is nonsingular; (e) Assumptions 1-2. Then for any $h$, $E[h(T,Y)\mid X,Z]=\gamma^\top\mu_h(X)$ with $\mu_h(x)=(E[h\mid X=x,S=k])_k=M(x)^{-1}E[\gamma h\mid X=x]$. So every regime-conditional moment is identified, and $\theta_k^*=\mathrm{Cov}(T,Y\mid X,S=k)/\mathrm{Var}(T\mid X,S=k)$. *Proof:* condition on $S$, use (b)-(c), multiply by $\gamma$ and invert $M$. For $K=2$, (d) holds iff $\mathrm{Var}(\gamma\mid X)>0$, i.e. the proxies are informative; Theorem 2 is exactly the singular case. Identification rests on the exclusion (b), not on proxy sufficiency (Assumption 3).

**An estimator from it (DC-DML).** Regress $T,T^2,Y,TY$ on $\gamma\otimes\mathrm{basis}(X)$ and plug in. On our frontier (same 100 datasets per cell, learned HMM) at $\Delta_Z=1$: mean ATE bias +0.01 vs +2.13 for OR-DML, and 95% coverage vs 0%. At $\Delta_Z=2,4$ it matches OR-DML. The cost is variance: RMSE 1.05 vs 2.25 at $\Delta_Z=1$; at $\Delta_Z=0.5$, RMSE 16.7 vs 5.9. But its intervals are then wide (97% coverage), whereas OR-DML is confidently wrong. On the real-data-calibrated benchmark (30 datasets per city), mean |ATE bias| is 0.05/0.20/0.23/1.85 (Delhi/Mumbai/Bengaluru/Kolkata; Kolkata median 0.62), vs 0.42/1.65/1.69/2.05 for OR-DML. Coverage is 90-100% vs 0%, even though exclusion holds only approximately there (real NO2 depends on meteorology).

**Q5 (baselines).** We agree and ran them. On Table 10's DGPs the joint MLE stays best (|bias| about 0.01 or less; DC-DML 0.03-0.28; posterior-DML 0.18-0.66). On the semi-synthetic benchmark, where its emission model is misspecified, a joint MLE with regime-specific effects has |ATE bias| 0.03/0.10/0.44/0.08 and beats DC-DML in three of four cities. With a common effect, as in Table 10, it is biased (0.19-0.30). We will move these comparisons to the main text and state the conclusion plainly. Two-stage plug-in posteriors leave residual-state bias (Theorem 4). When a joint model or the exclusion restriction is credible, a joint or deconvolution estimator should be preferred. OR-DML is the best two-stage design, not the best estimator overall.

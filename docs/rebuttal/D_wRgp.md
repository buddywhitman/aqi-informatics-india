Thank you for the positive review and the precise questions. We answer each with new results.

**Q1 (bias-aware intervals).** We ran them for posterior-feature DML on the frontier DGP (100 datasets per cell), using $\hat\theta\pm(|\hat a|\hat v\bar b/\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}(\tilde T)+1.96\,\mathrm{SE})$ with a budget $\bar b$ on the composite outcome shift. At $\Delta_Z=1$ the naive 95% interval covers 0% of the time. With $\bar b$ equal to the true shift it covers 94%; with half the true shift, 5%; with twice the true shift, 99%. At $\Delta_Z=2,4$ the naive interval already covers 98-100%. This is a sensitivity analysis, not a correction: it is only as good as the budget. We also added an estimator that removes the bias when an exclusion restriction holds (see below).

**Q2 (heteroscedastic innovations).** The pooled law holds exactly with $\sigma^2$ replaced by $E[\sigma_S^2]$, so bias $=abv/(a^2v+E[\sigma_S^2])$. *Proof:* with a calibrated posterior, $\tilde T=a(D-\gamma)+V$ and $E[V\mid D]=0$, so the numerator is $abv$ and $E[\tilde T^2]=a^2v+E[\sigma_S^2]$. The observable plug-in is unchanged, because $\mathrm{Var}(\tilde T)-a^2v$ still estimates $E[\sigma_S^2]$. In simulation (six configurations, $N$=300,000, regime prior 0.2) the relative error is 0.07-1.7%, against 42-55% for the naive law with averaged variances. This covers the pooled estimator; the extension to the coupled $K$-regime estimator is not proved.

**Q3 (T in the HMM).** Lemma 1 holds for any $F$-measurable posterior, so orthogonality survives, but the posterior then carries treatment information. Treatment acts as a second proxy, which is possible only because $T$ depends on $S$, and that is exactly the failure of proxy sufficiency. This explains Table 15 (bias 1.20 vs. 4.02 at $\Delta_Z=1$). Under Assumption 1, $T$ is a descendant of $S$ and not a collider of $S$ and the outcome noise, so we do not expect collider bias, but we have no theorem for this case and will say so.

**Q4 (λ).** In all frontier and semi-synthetic sweeps $\lambda$ is not selected. It follows a fixed rule, $\lambda=0.05\,\mathrm{tr}(\hat J)/(K\sqrt N)$, and is positive in every OR-DML run (about $7$-$9\times10^{-4}$ on the frontier, with shrinkage bias bounded by about 0.004). The data-driven selector is not used in those tables. Because $\sqrt N\lambda$ is constant, this rule does not satisfy $\lambda=o(N^{-1/2})$ in Theorem 5; we will state that. On the ill-conditioned design the MSE-minimising value is $\lambda=0.1$ (MSE 0.014 vs. 0.785 at $\lambda=0$).

**W1/W3.** Proxy-based coverage fails because the bias is first order. We now give an identification result, under an exclusion restriction and informative proxies, and a deconvolution estimator (DC-DML) built on it. On our frontier at $\Delta_Z=1$ its coverage is 95% vs. 0% for OR-DML. On the real-data-calibrated benchmark its mean |ATE bias| is 0.05-0.23 in three cities (Kolkata 1.85, median 0.62), against 0.42-2.05 for OR-DML. A joint MLE with regime-specific effects is better still in three of four cities, and we now report it as a main baseline.

**W2.** The bias law's assumptions (linear shifts, calibrated posteriors) are stated in Sec. 4. Heteroscedasticity is now covered (Q2). $\delta_X>0$ degrades the plug-in by $\sqrt{\delta_X}$ (Prop. 3).

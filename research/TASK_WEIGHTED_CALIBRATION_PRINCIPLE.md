# Task-Weighted Calibration Principle

Exploratory theory note; not submission material.

## Matched global calibration can hide arbitrarily different downstream damage

Let posterior error e_i have fixed average magnitude E[e_i^2]=c. Let downstream geometry weight observations by leverage w_i>=0, such as residual treatment squared. The relevant task-weighted error is E[w e^2]/E[w].

Two representations can have identical global calibration error c while placing their errors on different leverage strata. If one concentrates errors where w=L and another where w=l, with the same error mass and L/l large, their task-weighted errors differ by order L/l even though global Brier/ECE is identical.

Therefore no diagnostic depending only on the marginal distribution of posterior errors can generally control downstream geometry error without assumptions linking representation error to task leverage.

This gives a formal explanation for why temperature scaling/global ECE can improve without downstream reliability improving: calibration may improve in low-leverage regions while the errors that dominate the causal score remain.

## Necessary object

For a downstream moment with influence/leverage weight w, calibration should be assessed under the task-induced measure dP_w = w dP / E[w].

Examples include treatment-information geometry with w=T_tilde^2, score perturbation with w proportional to absolute residual score, and directional geometry with a contrast-specific weight w_v.

A representation can therefore be calibrated under P but miscalibrated under P_w.

## Strong implication

There is no single task-agnostic scalar calibration score that guarantees reliability for all downstream tasks with arbitrarily different leverage functions. Each task induces its own calibration measure.

This parallels the multitask abstraction result: task dependence appears both in which state distinctions matter and in which observations/posterior errors matter.

## Reproduction

The script research/adversarial_calibration_concentration.py constructs two representations with matched global posterior error but errors concentrated on low- versus high-treatment-leverage observations. Full Monte Carlo output is generated by the research workflow.

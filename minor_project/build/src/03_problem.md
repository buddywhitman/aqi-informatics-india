# Problem Formulation

## 3.1 The structural model

We observe $(Y_t, T_t, X_t, Z_t)_{t=1}^N$ generated by

$$S_t \sim \mathrm{Markov}(\Pi, \pi_0), \qquad Z_t = \mu_Z(S_t) + \xi_t, \tag{3.1}$$
$$T_t = m(X_t, S_t) + V_t, \qquad Y_t = \theta(S_t)\,T_t + g(X_t, S_t) + U_t, \tag{3.2}$$

with $S_t \in \{1,\dots,K\}$ latent, $\mathbb{E}[V_t \mid X_t, S_t] = 0$, $\mathbb{E}[U_t \mid X_t, S_t, T_t] = 0$, and $\theta(S_t) = \sum_k \theta_k^* \mathbb{1}(S_t = k)$. The targets are the regime effects $\theta_k^*$ and the stationary average $\theta^*_{\rm ATE} = \sum_k \pi_k^* \theta_k^*$. In the application $T$ is NO₂, $Y$ is PM₂.₅, $X$ collects lagged pollutants and weather, and $Z$ is meteorology.

@svg dag | dag | Causal structure. The latent state $S_t$ evolves as a Markov chain, is seen only through the proxy $Z_t$, and moves both treatment $T_t$ and outcome $Y_t$, so it is a confounder that conditioning on $X_t$ alone cannot remove.

Regime-conditional unconfoundedness, overlap and positivity, bounded variables, geometric $\alpha$-mixing, and the usual $o_P(N^{-1/4})$ nuisance rates are stated formally in Annexure G (A1 to A4).

## 3.2 Why standard DML fails

Standard DML that residualizes on $X_t$ alone cannot see $S_t$. The following result states exactly what it estimates.

@thm Theorem 1 (Omitted regime bias)
Standard DML that residualizes on $X_t$ alone satisfies $\hat\theta_{\rm naive} \to_p \sum_k \omega_k \theta_k^* + \mathcal{B}_{\rm het} + \mathcal{B}_{\rm conf}$, with weights $\omega_k = \mathbb{E}[\mathbb{1}(S_t{=}k)\tilde T_t^2]/\mathbb{E}\tilde T_t^2$ and
$$\mathcal{B}_{\rm conf} = \frac{\sum_k \mathbb{E}[\pi_k(X)\,\Delta m_k(X)\,\Delta g_k(X)]}{\mathbb{E}[\tilde T_t^2]},$$
where $\Delta m_k, \Delta g_k$ are the regime deviations of the treatment mean and the outcome baseline.
@end

The mechanism is intuitive. The numerator, the covariance between the regime's effect on the treatment and on the outcome, does not depend on how well $X$ explains $T$. The denominator, the residual treatment variance $\mathbb{E}\tilde T_t^2$, *shrinks* as $X$ explains more of $T$. Residualizing on more observed controls can therefore **amplify** omitted-regime bias and even flip its sign (Pearl, 2012). In the synthetic frontier of Chapter 7, standard DML has bias 8.44 on a true ATE of 1.53 at every level of proxy quality.

## 3.3 Non-identification without state information

The opposite extreme is a proxy with no information about the state.

@thm Theorem 2 (Non-identification without state information)
If $P(Z_t \mid S_t{=}j) = P(Z_t \mid S_t{=}k)$ for all $j,k$ and transitions are memoryless, there exist two models satisfying all assumptions, with identical laws of $(Y,T,X,Z)_{1:N}$ for every $N$ and distinct, non-permuted effect vectors.
@end

The construction used in the final proof is an assumption-consistent randomized-treatment design: independent randomized treatment, independent memoryless states, and uninformative proxies. Its two binary-outcome models have identical complete observable laws and different regime-effect vectors, while their *average* effect is shared and identified (Chapter 10 describes the correction that produced this form). The exact probability tables are enumerated by `src/synthesis/nonidentification_check.py` and verified by `verify_submission.py`.

Between the two extremes lies the regime that matters in practice: proxies that are *informative but imperfect*. The rest of the report studies that regime.

## 3.4 What "reliable" must mean

The reliability rule in current practice is built from two quantities that are both easy to compute.

* the **posterior error** $\varepsilon_\gamma = \mathbb{E}\|\gamma_t - H_t\|_1$ between the posterior and the one-hot true state, whose observable proxy is the posterior entropy;
* the **conditioning** of the score Jacobian, $\lambda_{\min}(\hat J)$.

Both appear in error bounds of the form $C\varepsilon_\gamma/\lambda_{\min}(J)$ (Theorem 3 in Chapter 5), and in a controlled synthetic design $\varepsilon_\gamma / \lambda_{\min}(\hat J)$ ranks causal error almost perfectly (Spearman 0.995 over 4,900 runs). The research question is whether that success survives when posteriors are learned and the state is generated, and, if it does not, what replaces it.

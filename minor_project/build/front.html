Abstract

Observational time series are frequently driven by a persistent state that nobody measures: the boundary-layer regime of an airshed, the liquidity regime of a market, the operating mode of a plant. When that state moves both a treatment and an outcome, adjusting for observed covariates does not identify the effect. The standard remedy is a two-stage recipe: infer the state with a hidden Markov model (HMM), then run regime-aware Double Machine Learning (DML). The recipe carries an implicit reliability rule: trust the estimate when the posterior is confident and the score Jacobian is well conditioned.

This project shows that the rule fails in a specific and checkable way. A generated state does not only add noise to the downstream estimating equation; it changes that equation’s geometry, so that the smallest eigenvalue of the generated-state Gram matrix can grow as the state estimate gets worse (it grows 70 times in a controlled design while causal error first rises and then falls, and in 82% of learned-posterior runs the generated state looks better conditioned than the true one). We call this phantom causal resolution. We derive two exact laws: the population displacement of a coupled regime score under state confusion, which reproduces a 4,900-run factorial experiment to within 2%, and a residual-state bias law, an omitted-variable identity in the posterior’s leftover state variance. The laws dictate the estimator: overlap-aware regime DML (OR-DML) conditions its nuisances on the posterior rather than weighting them by it, which makes the coupled score exactly Neyman-orthogonal for every posterior, and combines purged block cross-fitting, spectral regularization and HAC inference.

Evidence comes from factorial and learned-posterior synthetic designs, a real-data-calibrated semi-synthetic benchmark on four Indian cities (Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Kolkata) on a corrected hourly grid, and an audited observational stress test with aligned placebos and robustness values. Posterior-adjusted OR-DML is the most accurate regime-aware estimator in every synthetic frontier cell and in every semi-synthetic city for regime effects; no estimator removes residual-state bias under weak observability, and the report states where each result stops. The work yielded the peer-reviewed manuscript submitted to AISTATS 2027, a verified reproduction pipeline in which every number is generated from committed artifacts, and an honest account of an earlier applied study that an internal audit led us to retire.

Keywords: causal inference, double machine learning, latent Markov models, regime switching, phantom causal resolution, air quality, reproducibility.

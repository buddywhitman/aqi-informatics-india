\begin{abstract}
Causal inference from dependent observational time series is complicated when an unobserved, persistent state jointly affects treatment assignment and outcomes. Standard Double Machine Learning (DML) controls for observed covariates through orthogonal residualization, but does not by itself resolve confounding induced by an imperfect proxy for latent state dynamics. We study causal estimation under latent Markov confounding and characterize how latent-state uncertainty and downstream task conditioning propagate into causal error. We develop a regime-aware orthogonal estimator combining probabilistic state inference, temporally purged cross-fitting, and regularized inversion of the cross-regime score Jacobian. Our analysis separates nuisance-estimation error, latent-state recovery error, and variability induced by persistent regime occupation, and gives conditions under which the estimator approaches the corresponding oracle-state estimator as latent-state uncertainty decreases. We evaluate these properties using real-data-calibrated semi-synthetic time series constructed from high-frequency atmospheric observations across heterogeneous Indian cities, preserving persistence, seasonality, missingness, and regime overlap while keeping causal effects known. Across controlled variations in state persistence and observability, the experiments show how latent-state uncertainty predicts causal bias and when regime-aware estimation improves over standard and temporally blocked DML. Real sensor networks provide an observational stress test across four megacities spanning distinct residual treatment variance scales and telemetry artifacts; falsification checks are reported explicitly where temporal causal interpretation is unsupported. Together, the results show that reliable causal estimation with latent sequential confounding requires evaluating representation uncertainty jointly with the information geometry of the downstream task.
\end{abstract}

\section{INTRODUCTION}
\label{sec:intro}

Many observational time series are driven by a persistent state that nobody measures: the boundary-layer regime of an airshed, the liquidity regime of a market, the operating mode of a plant. When that state moves both a treatment and an outcome, adjusting for observed covariates does not identify the effect. The standard remedy is a two-stage recipe: infer the state with a hidden Markov model (HMM) from auxiliary proxies, then estimate regime-aware effects with Double Machine Learning \citep[DML;][]{chernozhukov2018double}. The recipe comes with an implicit reliability rule: \emph{trust the estimate when the posterior is confident and the score Jacobian is well conditioned}. Both quantities are routinely reported, and both appear in error bounds of the form $C\eps/\lmin(\bJ)$, which we also prove (Theorem~\ref{thm:graceful}).

This paper shows that the rule fails in a specific and checkable way. A \emph{generated} state does not only add noise to the downstream estimating equation; it changes that equation's geometry. Mixing regimes leaks between-regime treatment contrasts into the residualized treatment, so the smallest eigenvalue of the generated-state Gram matrix can \emph{grow} as the state estimate gets worse. In a controlled two-regime design it grows ${\CFLamInfl}\times$ while the causal error first rises and then falls (Figure~\ref{fig:phantom}a--b), and with posteriors learned by EM the generated-state matrix looks better conditioned than the oracle-state matrix in \LPFracInfl\% of runs (Figure~\ref{fig:phantom}c). We call this \emph{phantom causal resolution}: the estimate becomes less trustworthy while its standard conditioning diagnostic becomes more reassuring.

The mechanism has a closed form, and the closed form says how to build the estimator. For the coupled regime score we derive the exact population displacement under state confusion (Proposition~\ref{prop:exact}). It reproduces all \CFNCells\ cells of a 4,900-run factorial experiment with observed/predicted ratio \CFRatioMed\ (interquartile range \CFRatioLo--\CFRatioHi), and it locates the damage in the nuisance step: \emph{weighting} regime nuisances by the posterior leaks regime contrasts into the score. \emph{Conditioning} the nuisances on the posterior instead makes the coupled score exactly Neyman-orthogonal for every posterior (Lemma~\ref{lem:orth}). What remains is an exact omitted-variable identity in the \emph{residual state variance} $v$ that the posterior leaves behind (Theorem~\ref{thm:law}). For a pooled effect this is $\Delta_T\Delta_g v/(\Delta_T^2v+\sigma^2)$; regime effects obey the same law in matrix form. The conditioning diagnostic shares a factor with the bias numerator, so no function of $(\eps,\lmin)$ alone can certify reliability. What is needed instead is the residual state information and the regime shifts it multiplies. Both are estimable up to one unidentified outcome shift, which supports a sensitivity analysis.

\textbf{Contributions.}
(i)~\emph{Failure and limits:} an exact decomposition of the omitted-regime bias of standard DML, including bias amplification, and non-identification when proxies carry no state information (Theorems~\ref{thm:bias}--\ref{thm:impossibility}).
(ii)~\emph{Mechanism:} exact laws for how state confusion enters coupled and posterior-conditioned estimators. They explain phantom resolution, the non-monotonicity of error, and why a posterior helps as an adjustment feature but hurts as a nuisance weight (Proposition~\ref{prop:exact}, Theorem~\ref{thm:law}).
(iii)~\emph{Estimator:} overlap-aware regime DML (OR-DML) with posterior-adjusted nuisances, purged block cross-fitting and spectral regularization. It comes with exact orthogonality, a degradation bound separating nuisance from state-recovery error, and asymptotic normality including HMM-parameter and regime-occupation variance (Lemma~\ref{lem:orth}, Theorems~\ref{thm:graceful},~\ref{thm:asymptotics}). Across \FDNRuns\ synthetic datasets it is the most accurate regime-aware estimator, up to ties, at every proxy separation and persistence level we test. The posterior-weighted design of earlier drafts loses to hard regime fixed effects and is reported as an ablation.
(iv)~\emph{Evidence:} a factorial design verified against the closed form; learned-posterior experiments that remove its circularity; a real-data-calibrated semi-synthetic benchmark on four Indian cities with real gaps and calibrated persistence and overlap; and an audited sensor stress test with aligned placebos and robustness values.

\textbf{Related work.} Orthogonal and debiased estimation \citep{chernozhukov2018double,chernozhukov2022automatic,foster2023orthogonal,nie2021quasi} and its time-series extensions \citep{lewis2020double,semenova2023generalized,ciganovic2026dml} assume the confounders are observed. Sequential deconfounders learn latent confounders with neural representations \citep{bica2020time,hatt2021sequential}; our results caution that representation quality and downstream reliability are different objects. Latent-state and proximal approaches target identification \citep{miao2018identifying,tchetgen2020introduction,mastouri2021proximal,cui2024semiparametric,clouth2024latent}, and regime-switching models go back to \citet{hamilton1989new}; we instead quantify what a \emph{plug-in} posterior leaves behind. Our bias law is an omitted-variable identity \citep{greenland1980effect,cinelli2020making,chernozhukov2022long}, and its non-monotonicity is the algebra of bias amplification \citep{pearl2012bias,ding2017instrumental}; we claim neither as new. What is new is its calibrated, dependent-data and regime-coupled form, the exact law for regime-weighted scores, the orthogonal design that follows from them, and the consequence that the Gram-matrix diagnostics used for weak overlap \citep{crump2009dealing,damour2021overlap} can move in the wrong direction when the state is generated. Posterior-based adjustment is known to fail without identification \citep{wang2019blessings,damour2019multicause}; we characterize how it fails.

\section{SETTING AND THE FAILURE OF STANDARD DML}
\label{sec:setup}

We observe $(Y_t,T_t,X_t,Z_t)_{t=1}^N$ generated by
\begin{align}
S_t&\sim\mathrm{Markov}(\Pi,\pi_0),\quad Z_t=\mu_Z(S_t)+\xi_t, \label{eq:scm1}\\
T_t&=m(X_t,S_t)+V_t,\quad Y_t=\theta(S_t)T_t+g(X_t,S_t)+U_t, \label{eq:scm2}
\end{align}
with $S_t\in\{1,\dots,K\}$ latent, $\E[V_t\mid X_t,S_t]=0$, $\E[U_t\mid X_t,S_t,T_t]=0$ and $\theta(S_t)=\sum_k\theta_k^*\one(S_t=k)$. Targets are the regime effects $\theta_k^*$ and the stationary average $\theta^*_{\rm ATE}=\sum_k\pi_k^*\theta_k^*$. In our application $T$ is NO$_2$, $Y$ is PM$_{2.5}$, $X$ collects lagged pollutants and weather, and $Z$ is meteorology. Regime-conditional unconfoundedness, overlap and positivity, bounded variables, geometric $\alpha$-mixing and the usual $o_P(N^{-1/4})$ nuisance rates are stated as Assumptions~\ref{ass:unconfoundedness}--\ref{ass:rates} in Appendix~\ref{app:assumptions}.

\begin{theorem}[Omitted regime bias]
\label{thm:bias}
Standard DML that residualizes on $X_t$ alone satisfies $\hat\theta_{\rm naive}\to_p\sum_k\omega_k\theta_k^*+\mathcal B_{\rm het}+\mathcal B_{\rm conf}$, with $\omega_k=\E[\one(S_t{=}k)\tilde T_t^2]/\E\tilde T_t^2$ and
$\mathcal B_{\rm conf}=\sum_k\E[\pi_k(X)\Delta m_k(X)\Delta g_k(X)]/\E[\tilde T_t^2]$,
where $\Delta m_k,\Delta g_k$ are regime deviations of the treatment mean and outcome baseline (full statement and proof in Appendix~\ref{app:proof_thm1}).
\end{theorem}
Because $\E\tilde T_t^2$ shrinks as $X$ explains more of $T$ while the latent covariance in the numerator does not, residualizing on more observed controls can \emph{amplify} omitted-regime bias and flip its sign \citep{pearl2012bias}. In our synthetic frontier, standard DML has bias \FDStd\ on a true ATE of \FDTrueATE\ at every level of proxy quality.

\begin{theorem}[Non-identification without state information]
\label{thm:impossibility}
If $P(Z_t\mid S_t{=}j)=P(Z_t\mid S_t{=}k)$ for all $j,k$ and transitions are memoryless, there exist two models satisfying all assumptions, with identical laws of $(Y,T,X,Z)_{1:N}$ for every $N$ and distinct, non-permuted effect vectors (Appendix~\ref{app:proof_thm2}).
\end{theorem}
Between these extremes lies the regime that matters in practice: proxies that are informative but imperfect. The rest of the paper is about that regime.

\section{OVERLAP-AWARE REGIME DML}
\label{sec:method}

OR-DML infers posteriors $\gamma_{tk}=P(S_t{=}k\mid Z_{1:N})$ (smoothing) or $P(S_t{=}k\mid Z_{1:t})$ (causal filtering) with a Gaussian HMM. It cross-fits one pair of nuisances on the posterior-augmented features $F_t=(X_t,\bgam_t,\bgam_t\otimes X_t)$, $\hat m(F)\approx\E[T\mid F]$ and $\hat\mu(F)\approx\E[Y\mid F]$, on contiguous blocks separated by an embargo of $\tau^\ast$ steps. With $\tilde T_t=T_t-\hat m(F_t)$ and $\tilde Y_t=Y_t-\hat\mu(F_t)$ it solves the coupled system
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:coupled}
\hat J_{jk}=\tfrac1N\textstyle\sum_t\gamma_{tj}\gamma_{tk}\tilde T_t^2,\quad
\hat S_k=\tfrac1N\textstyle\sum_t\gamma_{tk}\tilde T_t\tilde Y_t,
\end{equation}
$\hat\bth_\lambda=(\hat\bJ+\lambda I)^{-1}\hat\bS$. The posterior enters twice: as an \emph{adjustment feature} in the nuisances (fine resolution) and as a \emph{weight} that defines the regime effects (coarse target). The embargo is the smallest lag after which all proxy autocorrelations fall below the Bartlett band, and at least $C\log N$, which makes the folds asymptotically independent under geometric mixing.

\begin{lemma}[Exact orthogonality]
\label{lem:orth}
Let $\psi_k(\bth;m,\mu)=\gamma_k(T-m)\{Y-\mu-\sum_j\gamma_j\theta_j(T-m)\}$. At $m=\E[T\mid F]$, $\mu=\E[Y\mid F]$, the Gateaux derivative of $\E\psi_k$ in every direction $(\delta_m,\delta_\mu)$ of square-integrable functions of $F$ vanishes, for every $\bth$ and every posterior $\bgam$; so does that of $\E[\gamma_j\gamma_k(T-m)^2]$.
\end{lemma}
\emph{Proof.} Each derivative is an expectation of a product of an $F$-measurable factor (the weights $\gamma$ and $\delta$) and $T-m$ or $Y-\mu-\sum_j\gamma_j\theta_j(T-m)$, whose conditional mean given $F$ is zero. $\square$

The natural alternative, used in earlier drafts of this work and closest to regime fixed effects, fits regime-$k$ nuisances on $X$ with weights $\gamma_{\cdot k}$. Its Gram part is not orthogonal for soft weights: under a nuisance perturbation its score derivative is \OrthW\% of the Jacobian, against \OrthPA\% for Lemma~\ref{lem:orth} (Appendix~\ref{app:pa}). Section~\ref{sec:theory} shows why it fails, and we keep it as an ablation. Two simpler estimators on the same inputs are our main baselines: \emph{hard regime fixed effects} (one-hot $\arg\max$ weights) and \emph{posterior-feature DML}, a single-index DML with nuisances on $F_t$. Algorithm~\ref{alg:ordml} summarizes the procedure and the diagnostics we recommend reporting with it. Complexity is $\mathcal O(B\,C_{\rm nuis}+NK^2+K^3)$; Appendix~\ref{app:algorithm} checks $K\le5$.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{OR-DML with a reliability report}
\label{alg:ordml}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE $\{Y_t,T_t,X_t,Z_t\}_{t=1}^N$, folds $B$, penalty $\lambda\ge0$.
\STATE Fit a Gaussian HMM to $Z$ (multi-restart EM); compute posteriors $\bgam_t$ (smoothing or causal filter).
\STATE Embargo $\tau^*=\max\{h:\max_j|\hat\rho_j(h)|\ge1.96/\sqrt N\}+1$, at least $C\log N$.
\STATE Cross-fit $\hat m,\hat\mu$ on $F_t=(X_t,\bgam_t,\bgam_t\otimes X_t)$ over purged contiguous blocks.
\STATE Form $\hat\bJ,\hat\bS$ by~\eqref{eq:coupled}; return $\hat\bth_\lambda=(\hat\bJ+\lambda I)^{-1}\hat\bS$ with a HAC sandwich.
\STATE \textbf{Report:} hard-FE and posterior-feature baselines; $\hat v$, $\hat a$, $\widehat{\Var}(\tilde T)$ and the robustness value $b^\star$ (Section~\ref{sec:theory}); raw and standardized $\lmin$; lead placebos aligned to the outcome's information set.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{HOW STATE UNCERTAINTY AND TASK CONDITIONING PROPAGATE}
\label{sec:theory}

Let $\boldsymbol H_t=\boldsymbol e_{S_t}$, $\eps=\E\|\bgam_t-\boldsymbol H_t\|_1$, and let $\bJ,\bS$ be the population versions of the coupled system at the population nuisances of either design. Define $\bth_\gamma=\bJ^{-1}\bS$ and $\boldsymbol b_\gamma=\bS-\bJ\bth^*$, so $\bth_\gamma-\bth^*=\bJ^{-1}\boldsymbol b_\gamma$ exactly.

\begin{theorem}[Graceful degradation]
\label{thm:graceful}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:overlap} and~\ref{ass:rates}, suppose the nuisance targets are within $C_\eta\eps$ of the oracle regime nuisances in $L^1$. This holds for posterior-weighted nuisances, and for posterior-adjusted nuisances when the posterior is calibrated given $F$ and $Z$ is independent of $(T,Y)$ given $(X,S)$. If $\lmin(\bJ)>0$, then $\|\bth_\gamma-\bth^*\|_2\le C\eps/\lmin(\bJ)$ with explicit $C$, and
$\|\hat\bth_\gamma-\bth^*\|_2\le C\eps/\lmin(\bJ)+\mathcal O_P\big((N^{-1/2}+\rho_N)/\lmin(\bJ)\big)$,
with $\rho_N=r_N^2$ for posterior-adjusted nuisances (Lemma~\ref{lem:orth}) and $\rho_N=r_N$ for posterior-weighted ones, where $r_N$ is the nuisance rate (Appendices~\ref{app:proof_thm3},~\ref{app:pa}).
\end{theorem}
The bound separates the two errors the abstract names: state recovery ($\eps$) and nuisance estimation ($\rho_N$). In a controlled design $\eps/\lmin(\hat\bJ)$ ranks causal error almost perfectly (Spearman \FacRhoD\ over \FacNRuns\ runs, versus \FacRhoEps\ for $\eps$ and \FacRhoLam\ for $1/\lmin$). The next two results explain why that success is fragile: $\lmin(\bJ)$ is itself a function of the state error.

\textbf{The coupled score under state confusion.} Consider two regimes with stationary weights $\pi_s$, treatment means $m_s$, outcome means $\mu_s=\theta_sm_s+g_s$ and innovation scales $\sigma_s$. Take regime-wise nuisances (the posterior-weighted design with correct regime means) and a proxy that places weight $1-\alpha$ on the true regime and $\alpha=\eps/2$ on the other.

\begin{proposition}[Exact law for regime-weighted scores]
\label{prop:exact}
With $\gamma_j(s)=1-\alpha$ if $j=s$ and $\alpha$ otherwise,
\begin{align*}
J_{jk}&=\textstyle\sum_s\pi_s\gamma_j(s)\gamma_k(s)\big[(m_s{-}m_j)(m_s{-}m_k)+\sigma_s^2\big],\\
S_j&=\textstyle\sum_s\pi_s\gamma_j(s)\big[(m_s{-}m_j)(\mu_s{-}\mu_j)+\theta_s\sigma_s^2\big].
\end{align*}
In the symmetric case ($\pi_s=\tfrac12$, $\sigma_s=\sigma$, $\Delta=m_1-m_0$, $G=\mu_1-\mu_0$), $\bJ$ has eigenvectors $(1,\pm1)$ with eigenvalues $\lambda_+=\tfrac12\sigma^2+\tfrac12\alpha^2\Delta^2$ and $\lambda_-=\tfrac12\sigma^2(1-2\alpha)^2+\tfrac12\alpha^2\Delta^2$, and
\begin{align*}
\bth_\gamma-\bth^*&=\frac{\alpha\Delta G-\tfrac12\alpha^2\Delta^2(\theta_0{+}\theta_1)}{2\lambda_+}\binom{1}{1}\\
&\quad+\frac{(\theta_0{-}\theta_1)\big(\alpha(1{-}2\alpha)\sigma^2-\tfrac12\alpha^2\Delta^2\big)}{2\lambda_-}\binom{1}{-1}.
\end{align*}
\end{proposition}
\begin{corollary}[Phantom resolution and the error hump]
\label{cor:phantom}
In the symmetric case: (a) $\lmin(\bJ)=\lambda_-$ exceeds its oracle value $\sigma^2/2$ whenever $\eps>8\sigma^2/(\Delta^2+4\sigma^2)$, so a confused state can look \emph{better} conditioned than the true one; (b) when $|\Delta G|\gg\sigma^2|\theta_0-\theta_1|$, the error is $\approx\sqrt2\,\tfrac{\eps}{2}|\Delta G|/(\sigma^2+\eps^2\Delta^2/4)$, maximized at $\varepsilon^\star=2\sigma/|\Delta|$ with peak $|G|/(\sqrt2\sigma)$, and \emph{decreasing} in $\eps$ beyond it; (c) the dominant displacement lies on the $\lambda_+$ eigenvector, so the worst-direction bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:graceful} is loose by $\lambda_+/\lambda_-$.
\end{corollary}
Proposition~\ref{prop:exact} is exact (verified symbolically in the supplement) and involves no sampling. Applied to the asymmetric factorial design it reproduces every cell's median error to within ${\CFRatioMin}$--${\CFRatioMax}\times$ and locates the error peaks at $\eps\in\{\CFPeakA,\CFPeakB,\CFPeakC\}$ for $\sigma_1\in\{0.08,0.15,0.30\}$, exactly where they are observed (Figure~\ref{fig:phantom}). The factorial's near-perfect rank correlation is therefore an algebraic identity of the design, not a statistical regularity. It works partly \emph{because} $\lmin(\hat\bJ)$ inflates: replacing it by the design value $\sigma_1^2$ lowers the cell-level correlation from \CFRhoProxy\ to \CFRhoDesign. The leading displacement $\alpha\Delta G$ is the product of state error with the regime contrasts that the weighted nuisances fail to remove.

\textbf{Posterior-adjusted designs.} Posterior-adjusted nuisances remove those contrasts up to what the posterior cannot explain. Let $v_t=\gamma_t(1-\gamma_t)$ be the posterior variance of the regime indicator, and let $T_t=m(X_t)+aS_t+V_t$ and $Y_t=\theta_{S_t}T_t+g(X_t)+bS_t+U_t$.

\begin{theorem}[Residual-state bias law]
\label{thm:law}
Let $K=2$ and suppose the posterior is calibrated given $F$ ($P(S_t{=}1\mid F_t)=\gamma_t$), $V_t$ is independent of $(S,Z,X)$ with variance $\sigma^2$, nuisances are cross-fitted at rates with $r_N^2=o(N^{-1/2})$, and the posterior forgets distant proxies (Appendix~\ref{app:pa}). With score weights $\boldsymbol w_t=(1-\gamma_t,\gamma_t)$ (OR-DML) or $w_t\equiv1$ (posterior-feature DML),
\begin{gather*}
\hat\bth\to_p\bar\bth+\bJ^{-1}\boldsymbol b_\gamma,\\
\bJ=\E\big[\boldsymbol w\boldsymbol w^\top(a^2v+\sigma^2)\big],\quad
\boldsymbol b_\gamma=a\,\E[\boldsymbol w\,c\,v],
\end{gather*}
with $c_t=b+(\theta_1-\theta_0)\{m(X_t)+a(1-\gamma_t)\}$, $\bar\bth=\bth^*$ for OR-DML and the $(a^2v+\sigma^2)$-weighted mean of $\theta_0+(\theta_1-\theta_0)\gamma$ for the pooled estimator. Hence: (a) with no treatment regime shift ($a=0$) both are consistent for any calibrated posterior, whatever $b$ and the effect heterogeneity; (b) for homogeneous effects the pooled bias is $abv/(a^2v+\sigma^2)$, hump-shaped in $a$ with peak at $|a|=\sigma/\sqrt v$ and $\sup|B|=|b|\sqrt v/(2\sigma)$, increasing in $v$, and a soft posterior feature is never worse than its hard label; (c) $\bJ$ grows with $a^2v$, so phantom resolution is not specific to the weighted design.
\end{theorem}
The law is exact up to sampling error: over \CLNConf\ configurations with $N=10^6$ the estimator and the law agree to within \CLMaxDev, and with $a=0$ the bias is \CLZeroShift\ (Appendix~\ref{app:pa}). The pooled form with $K>2$ regimes is $a^\top\Sigma b/(a^\top\Sigma a+\sigma^2)$ with $\Sigma$ the residual state covariance (Appendix~\ref{app:biaslaw}). Both laws have the same anatomy. A numerator proportional to state error times the product of the treatment and outcome regime shifts is divided by a residual treatment variance that the same treatment shift inflates. This is why $\Var(\tilde T)$, the scalar analogue of $\lmin(\hat\bJ)$, cannot be read as a monotone certificate (Figure~\ref{fig:law}). The two designs differ in what multiplies the state error: weighted nuisances leak the raw contrast $\Delta G$ at first order in $\eps$, adjusted nuisances only the residual variance $v$.

\textbf{Observability and inference.} For a calibrated posterior, $\hat v=N^{-1}\sum_t\hat\gamma_t(1-\hat\gamma_t)$ satisfies $|\E\hat v-v|\le\sqrt{CE}+CE$ in the $L^2$ calibration error $CE$, and the treatment shift $a$ and $\Var(\tilde T)$ are estimable. Only the outcome shift $b$ is unidentified, so the robustness value $b^\star=|\hat\theta|\,\widehat{\Var}(\tilde T)/\|\hat\Sigma\hat a\|$ (the smallest outcome shift that explains the estimate away) gives a one-parameter sensitivity analysis. For EM-fitted HMMs the plug-in converges at rate $N^{-1/2}+\sqrt{\delta_X}$, where $\delta_X$ is regime information carried by the controls (Proposition~\ref{prop:rate}). Under misspecification, $\hat v$ may understate or overstate residual state variance; sensitivity values then require calibration assumptions.

\begin{theorem}[Inference around the proxy target]
\label{thm:asymptotics}
For posterior-adjusted nuisances, under Theorem~\ref{thm:graceful} and the boundedness, augmented-process $\beta$-mixing, nuisance-rate and differentiability conditions in Appendix~\ref{app:proof_asym}, use $\tau^*\ge C\log N$, $C>1/c_\beta$, and a separated HMM calibration window. Then $\sqrt N(\hat\bth_\lambda-\bth_\lambda)\to\mathcal N(0,\Sigma_\lambda^{\rm tot})$, including representation uncertainty. Here $\bth_\lambda-\bth^*=(\bJ+\lambda I)^{-1}(\boldsymbol b_\gamma-\lambda\bth^*)$ and $\|\boldsymbol b_\gamma\|\le C\eps$. Structural coverage requires displacement $o(N^{-1/2})$; $\sqrt N(C\eps+\lambda M_\theta)/(\lmin+\lambda)\to0$ suffices. Occupation weighting requires its joint influence covariance. Implemented HAC intervals condition on the fitted HMM; full-sample smoothing is outside this theorem.
\end{theorem}
When proxy displacement is not negligible, structural Wald tests need not have nominal size. We therefore pair estimates with sensitivity analysis rather than an oracle bias correction.

\section{EXPERIMENTS}
\label{sec:experiments}

\textbf{Learned posteriors break the scalar ratio.} To remove the synthetic-proxy circularity, we learn posteriors with a Gaussian HMM (EM, three restarts, smoothing) from noisy proxies across $\Delta_Z\times\sigma_1\times\Delta_T$ (\LPNCells\ cells, \LPNRuns\ runs, $N=1{,}200$). All diagnostics use the realized generated-state Gram matrix. Table~\ref{tab:scores} shows that the ratio $\eps/\lmin(\hat\bJ)$ falls from \FacRhoD\ to \LPRhoD. Restoring the oracle geometry recovers \LPRhoDOracle. Restoring instead the numerator scale the laws predict, using the plug-in regime shifts $\hat\Delta_T,\hat\Delta_Y$ of treatment and outcome, gives \LPRhoScaled\ (\LPRhoScaledCell\ across cells), and \LPRhoScaledEnt\ with entropy in place of $\eps$, which is fully observable. With the same posteriors, hard fixed effects beat the posterior-weighted design in \LPHardBetter\% of cells, while posterior-adjusted OR-DML beats hard fixed effects in \LPPABetter\% (median error ratio \LPPARatio; \LPPAvsW\ against the weighted design). All of its losses occur at the smallest treatment innovation, where the $a^2v$ term dominates $\bJ$.

\textbf{Observability and persistence.} Table~\ref{tab:main} (left) varies proxy separation $\Delta_Z$ in the DGP of Theorem~\ref{thm:bias}; a persistence sweep adds regime-0 stay probabilities $0.70$ and $0.97$ (\FDNRuns\ datasets in all). Standard and blocked DML are stuck at the omitted-regime bias. All proxy-based estimators fail at $\Delta_Z\le1$ and approach the oracle at $\Delta_Z\ge2$. Among the \FDNCells\ cells with $\Delta_Z\ge1$ (Figure~\ref{fig:estimators}), OR-DML is best or tied for the ATE in \FDBestATE\ and for regime effects in \FDBestTheta. At $\Delta_Z=2$ its ATE bias is \FDOursTwo\ (hard fixed effects \FDHardTwo, weighted design \FDWTwo) with coverage \FDCovOursTwo\%, and at $\Delta_Z=1$ its regime-effect error is \FDOursThOne\ against \FDHardThOne\ and \FDWThOne. Persistence helps exactly through the posterior: at $\Delta_Z=1$, raising the stay probability from 0.70 to 0.97 lowers $\eps$ from \FDPerLoOneEps\ to \FDPerHiOneEps\ and OR-DML's bias from \FDPerLoOneOurs\ to \FDPerHiOneOurs\ (hard fixed effects: \FDPerLoOneHard\ to \FDPerHiOneHard). On the legacy version of this DGP the excess bias of soft conditioning over the oracle is predicted by the law to within \BLTabOneErr\ at every $\Delta_Z$ (Appendix~\ref{app:biaslaw_exp}), so the transition from failure to success is explained by $v(\Delta_Z)$, not by the spectrum of $\hat\bJ$. Under a continuous latent mixture that no two-state model describes, OR-DML beats hard fixed effects in \SVHPAWin\% of 100 replications, while the weighted design beats them in \SVHWin\% (Appendix~\ref{app:frontiers}).

\textbf{Real-data-calibrated benchmark.} The right half of Table~\ref{tab:main} keeps everything real except the causal effects. That includes meteorological proxies and controls, the observed NO$_2$ series, the hourly missingness pattern, and regime persistence and overlap: true paths are drawn by forward-filtering backward-sampling from an HMM calibrated per city (stay probabilities \SSStayMin--\SSStayMax). With posteriors that are exactly calibrated and \SSAccMin--\SSAccMax\% accurate, every proxy-based estimator retains large bias and zero coverage. For regime effects OR-DML is the most accurate regime-aware estimator in every city (Delhi \SSDelhiPATh, against \SSDelhiHardTh\ for hard fixed effects and \SSDelhiWTh\ for the weighted design). For the pooled ATE it is best in Delhi and Kolkata, and posterior-feature DML is best in Mumbai and Bengaluru. The weighted design is worse than standard DML outside Delhi. The plug-in law, computed from the posterior, the fitted treatment shift and the injected outcome shift, ranks the four cities' biases correctly, with magnitudes within \SSLawErrMin--\SSLawErrMax\%. The generated-state $\lmin$ exceeds its oracle value by \SSInflMin--\SSInflMax$\times$ in Mumbai, Bengaluru and Kolkata (\SSInflDelhi$\times$ in Delhi).

\textbf{Representation quality is not task reliability.} Across 15 shifted worlds and four encoders (HMM, GRU, Transformer, linear SSM), adding posterior NLL to state-recovery $F_1$ raises leave-one-world-out prediction of downstream reliability from $R^2=\LowoFOne$ to \LowoFNll. Yet temperature scaling cuts GRU/Transformer ECE by \ZooEceGRU/\ZooEceTr\ without improving failure discrimination ($\Delta$AUC $\ZooDaucGRU/\ZooDaucTr$). By Theorem~\ref{thm:law}, recalibration changes the \emph{estimate} $\hat v$ but not the residual information the downstream task actually has (Appendix~\ref{app:zoo}).

\section{OBSERVATIONAL STRESS TEST: FOUR CITIES}
\label{sec:empirical}

We apply OR-DML and the sensitivity analysis to NO$_2\to$PM$_{2.5}$ in Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru and Kolkata (OpenAQ/CPCB stations and Open-Meteo weather, Feb~2025--Jun~2026) on a \emph{corrected} hourly grid. An audit found that the legacy panel rounded half-hour timestamps with ties-to-even, collapsing it onto a two-hour grid, so ``hourly'' lags were two hours. This changed Delhi's pooled estimate from \LegacyDelhi\ to \PooledDelhi\ (Appendix~\ref{app:provenance}). Placebo leads are aligned to the outcome's own information set. Without this alignment the one-hour lead is mechanically zero, because the lagged outcome is a control. Real posteriors are sharp here, so the weighted design gives the same ATE to within \CityMaxATEDiff\ outside Kolkata (Delhi \CityDelhiATEW).

Raw conditioning reflects units and sensor resolution, not collinearity. \emph{Data quality:} Kolkata's NO$_2$ takes \AuKolkataUniq\ distinct values, and Mumbai has a \AuMumbaiRun-hour constant run whose removal moves the ATE from \CityMumbaiATE\ to \CityMumbaiScrATE\ ($N$ \CityMumbaiN$\to$\CityMumbaiScrN). \emph{Temporal validity:} Bengaluru and screened Kolkata fail lead placebos, the latter strongly ($p=\PlKolkataP$), so the apparently significant screened Kolkata effect (\CityKolkataScrATE) is not interpretable. \emph{Residual confounding:} Delhi's association is positive in both regimes (\CityDelhiRone\ and \CityDelhiRtwo; ATE \CityDelhiATE) and would need an outcome shift of \BstarDelhi\ SD (90\% interval \BstarDelhiLo--\BstarDelhiHi) to be explained away through the HMM-regime channel. Its one-hour lead placebo nonetheless rejects (\PlDelhiLead, $p=\PlDelhiLeadP$, about a third of the contemporaneous coefficient). This points to short-horizon confounding outside that channel. We therefore report Delhi as an exposure association that is robust to residual \emph{regime} confounding, not as an emissions effect, and the other three cities as inconclusive.

\section{DISCUSSION}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The intuition we set out to test was that a confident latent state and a well-conditioned score Jacobian together certify a causal estimate. They do not. A generated state enters the downstream estimating equation twice, in its bias and in its apparent information, and the two move together. The scalar $\eps/\lmin$ is an honest worst-case bound and an excellent ranking device when the state error is synthetic and the regime shifts are fixed. It degrades sharply once posteriors are learned, because its denominator is partly made of the error it is meant to measure. Two rules survive. \emph{Design:} condition the nuisances on the posterior and use it only to weight the target. This makes the coupled score exactly orthogonal and gave the most accurate regime estimates in every benchmark here. \emph{Reporting:} state the residual state variance, the regime shifts it multiplies and a robustness value; compare against hard fixed effects and posterior-feature baselines; and test temporal validity with aligned placebos.

\textbf{Outlook.} Nothing in the mechanism is specific to HMMs. Any generated state (a neural encoder's regime embedding, a clustering label, a changepoint segmentation) enters a downstream estimating equation both as an adjustment and as a source of apparent information, and the same accounting applies: what matters is the residual state variance the representation leaves, multiplied by the regime shifts of treatment and outcome, not the representation's accuracy or the conditioning of the score it feeds. Evaluating representations for causal use therefore means reporting that residual, and designing estimators so that the representation is used as an orthogonal adjustment rather than as a weight on the nuisance fit.

\textbf{Limitations.} The laws are probability limits under linear regime shifts, exogenous treatment innovations and calibrated posteriors. The robustness value bounds rather than corrects, and depends on calibration under misspecified emissions. Posterior-adjusted OR-DML does not remove residual-state bias: under weak observability every proxy-based estimator, ours included, has large bias and no coverage, and for the pooled ATE posterior-feature DML is sometimes more accurate. The city analysis is observational. Proxies overlap controls, lagged pollutants may be mediators, and Delhi's lead placebo rejects.

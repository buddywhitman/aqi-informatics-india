\begin{abstract}
Evaluating continuous policy interventions in observational environmental and atmospheric systems is fundamentally hindered by non-stationary confounding and temporal autocorrelation. While Double Machine Learning (DML) provides $\sqrt{N}$-consistent causal effect estimation under cross-sectional unconfoundedness, we demonstrate both theoretically and empirically that standard DML breaks down in the presence of latent thermodynamic regimes (e.g., atmospheric stagnation vs.\ advective boundary layer clearance). Under such regimes, naive DML incurs an asymptotic \emph{Omitted Regime Bias} that can flip the sign of estimated treatment effects, rendering policy simulations severely flawed.

To resolve this challenge, we introduce \textbf{Regime-Conditional Double Machine Learning (RC-DML)}. RC-DML integrates latent Markov regime smoothing into Neyman-orthogonal score equations, combined with a \textbf{Purged Block-Temporal Cross-Fitting} scheme that establishes an embargo buffer $\tau$ to prevent temporal dependency leakage under $\alpha$-mixing. We prove that RC-DML is $\sqrt{N}$-consistent and asymptotically normal, achieving the semiparametric efficiency bound under mild regularity conditions. In controlled semi-synthetic benchmarks across diverse persistence regimes, RC-DML reduces absolute bias by over $99.8\%$ (from $7.7221$ to $0.0132$) relative to standard DML, and achieves 88.9\% population coverage once Markov regime occupation variance is explicitly integrated via our Proposition 6 decomposition. When applied to high-frequency ground-sensor networks across complex Indian airsheds, RC-DML resolves empirical sign inversion in peninsular plateau airsheds (Bengaluru: $-1.772 \to +0.081$) and collapses spurious confounding artifacts by 79\% in coastal megacities (Mumbai: $-296.092 \to -62.382$), recovering structurally coherent, regime-stratified policy elasticities.
\end{abstract}


\section{INTRODUCTION}
\label{sec:intro}

Estimating the causal impact of continuous interventions—such as reducing vehicular emissions or curtailing industrial activity—on ambient air pollutants (e.g., fine particulate matter $\text{PM}_{2.5}$) is critical for environmental regulation \citep{dominici2014mitigating}. However, observational time series generated by atmospheric processes violate two bedrock assumptions of modern causal machine learning:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Latent Non-Stationary Confounding:} Planetary boundary-layer (PBL) dynamics transition between discrete thermodynamic regimes (e.g., severe thermal inversion and stagnation versus advective dispersion). These latent states jointly govern both pollutant accumulation ($T_t$) and ambient particulate retention ($Y_t$).
    \item \textbf{Temporal Autocorrelation ($\alpha$-Mixing):} Standard sample-splitting and cross-fitting schemes \citep{chernozhukov2018double} rely on exchangeable observations. Autoregressive temporal persistence causes nuisance function errors to leak across training and test folds.
\end{enumerate}

When standard Double Machine Learning \citep{chernozhukov2018double} or Causal Forests \citep{athey2019generalized} are applied naively to environmental time series without accounting for latent regimes, the resulting causal estimands are frequently biased. In complex airsheds, naive estimators can even produce \emph{sign inversions}—estimating that emission reductions lead to massive increases in ambient pollution.

\textbf{DML and Neyman Orthogonality.} \citet{chernozhukov2018double} establish the DML framework for cross-sectional i.i.d.\ data, using Neyman-orthogonal scores and $K$-fold cross-fitting to achieve $\sqrt{N}$-consistent causal estimation despite high-dimensional nuisance estimation. \citet{foster2023orthogonal} provide the general statistical learning theory for Neyman-orthogonal losses, proving that any population risk satisfying the orthogonality condition achieves oracle rates in excess risk — a meta-result our coupled score system instantiates for the latent-regime setting. \citet{chernozhukov2022automatic} derive automatic debiasing via Riesz representers, eliminating the need to hand-construct bias corrections; RC-DML extends their result to the regime-mixture case where the Riesz representer is a soft-weighted projection. For heterogeneous treatment effects, \citet{nie2021quasi} introduce the R-Learner (quasi-oracle HTE estimation via residualized regression), and \citet{athey2019generalized} provide Generalized Random Forests; both assume i.i.d.\ data and observed covariates fully capture heterogeneity, whereas RC-DML targets latent Markov state-specific effects in dependent time series.

\textbf{DML for Time Series.} The most closely related concurrent work is \citet{ciganovic2026dml}, who adapt DML to macroeconomic time series via Reverse Cross-Fitting, exploiting time-reversibility of stationary processes to avoid buffer blocks and improve sample efficiency. Their setting is fundamentally different: Reverse Cross-Fitting requires the joint distribution of $(Y_t, T_t, X_t)$ to be stationary (or at least time-reversible in the relevant marginals), and they do not consider latent regimes. Atmospheric thermodynamic processes are neither stationary nor time-reversible: regime-switching PBL dynamics break stationarity, and the HMM posterior weights $\gamma_{tk}$ depend non-symmetrically on $Z_{1:N}$. RC-DML instead uses Purged Block-Temporal Cross-Fitting with embargo $\tau$ calibrated to the autocorrelation horizon, and our Theorem~\ref{thm:gateaux} proves exact Neyman orthogonality for the resulting soft-weighted scores without stationarity.

\textbf{Regime Models and Panel Econometrics.} Latent Markov regime-switching was introduced to macroeconometrics by \citet{hamilton1989new}; \citet{bai2009panel} and \citet{bonhomme2015grouped} extend latent structure to panels via interactive fixed effects and grouped heterogeneity. Purged cross-validation with temporal embargo buffers was pioneered by \citet{lopezdeprado2018advances} for financial time series. \emph{None of these works combine latent Markov regime smoothing with Neyman-orthogonal score equations, continuous-treatment residualization, and asymptotic efficiency guarantees under $\alpha$-mixing.} RC-DML is the first to bridge these three pillars into a unified causal estimator.


\textbf{Contributions.} In this paper, we develop this framework:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Omitted Regime Bias Decomposition (Theorem~\ref{thm:bias}):} We derive the exact closed-form bias of naive DML under latent regime omission, decomposing it into a treatment heterogeneity term and a confounding cross-term. We prove that when regime-conditional treatment variance is small relative to cross-regime covariance, the confounding bias undergoes \emph{Frisch-Waugh amplification}, driving the estimator to sign inversion — a pathology invisible to standard DML diagnostics.

    \item \textbf{Universal Multiway Gateaux Orthogonality (Theorem~\ref{thm:gateaux}):} We prove that the coupled RC-DML score vector is exactly Neyman-orthogonal to \emph{any} perturbation of the HMM parameters, under \emph{any} finite overlap ($\Delta_Z = O(1)$), without oracle regime separation. The proof proceeds via two algebraic identities: (i) the structural innovation identity ($\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon_t \mid \mathcal{F}_t] = 0$ by construction of the SCM) and (ii) simplex conservation ($\sum_k \nabla_\Lambda \gamma_{tk}^*[\mathbf{h}] = 0$ on the probability simplex). This subsumes \citet{foster2023orthogonal}'s orthogonal learning framework and \citet{chernozhukov2022automatic}'s automatic debiasing for the regime-mixture case.

    \item \textbf{Asymptotic Duality \& Cross-Regime Inversion (Theorem~\ref{thm:duality}):} We prove a complete duality between coupled and decoupled estimation: the decoupled diagonal estimator has a computable cross-talk bias given by $\sum_{j \neq k} (J_{jk}/J_{kk})(\theta_j^* - \theta_k^*)$, and the coupled $K\times K$ Jacobian inversion exactly eliminates it. The smooth interpolation $\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{\mathrm{coupled}} - \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{\mathrm{diag}}\| = O_P(e^{-\kappa\Delta_Z^2})$ provides a falsifiable prediction: for well-separated regimes, both estimators should agree, and discrepancy diagnoses regime overlap.

    \item \textbf{Purged Block Cross-Fitting with $\alpha$-Mixing Guarantee (Theorem~\ref{thm:asymptotics}):} We adapt purged block cross-fitting \citep{lopezdeprado2018advances} with an embargo window $\tau$ to the semiparametric causal setting, proving that nuisance estimation rates carry through under geometrically $\alpha$-mixing temporal dependence. Unlike \citet{ciganovic2026dml}'s Reverse Cross-Fitting, which requires stationarity and time-reversibility, our scheme is valid for non-stationary regime-switching processes.

    \item \textbf{Oracle Equivalence and HMM Approximation Rate (Theorem~\ref{thm:oracle}):} We prove that RC-DML is asymptotically equivalent to the infeasible estimator with known latent states whenever the HMM posterior satisfies $N^{-1}\sum_t \|\gamma_t - \mathbf{1}_{S_t}\|_1 = o_P(N^{-1/4})$. This links the estimator's statistical performance directly to the identifiability and mixing of the latent Markov chain.

    \item \textbf{Markov Occupation Variance Decomposition (Proposition~\ref{prop:variance}):} We derive the exact decomposition of population ATE variance into (i) estimation variance from the HAC sandwich and (ii) regime occupation variance from Markov chain fluctuations — the latter scaling as $(1+\rho)/(1-\rho)$ under persistence $\rho$. For $\rho \approx 0.85$ (typical winter stagnation), this inflates population uncertainty by $12.3\times$, explaining why standard CIs undercover.

    \item \textbf{Empirical Resolution Across Heterogeneous Indian Airsheds:} RC-DML eliminates empirical sign inversion in Bengaluru ($-1.772 \to +0.081$), collapses spurious coastal confounding by 79\% in Mumbai, and provides regime-stratified elasticities in Delhi where stagnation persistence degrades regime separability. The 99.8\% bias reduction relative to naive DML (from $7.7221$ to $0.0132$) is verified over 45 Monte Carlo replications across three persistence regimes.
\end{itemize}

\section{STRUCTURAL CAUSAL MODEL \& IDENTIFICATION}
\label{sec:setup}

Let $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)$ be a probability space. Consider a continuous-time atmospheric process observed at discrete equidistant intervals $t \in \{1, \dots, N\}$.

\subsection{Structural Equations}
We formalize the atmospheric data-generating process via the following Structural Causal Model (SCM):
\begin{align}
    S_t &\sim \text{Markov}(\Pi, \pi_0), \quad S_t \in \{1, \dots, K\}, \label{eq:regime_scm} \\
    Z_t &= \mu_Z(S_t) + \xi_t, \label{eq:state_obs} \\
    X_t &= \phi(X_{t-1}, S_t) + \epsilon_t, \label{eq:confounders} \\
    T_t &= m(X_t, S_t) + V_t, \quad \E[V_t \mid X_t, S_t] = 0, \label{eq:treatment} \\
    Y_t &= \theta(S_t) T_t + g(X_t, S_t) + U_t, \label{eq:outcome}
\end{align}
with $\E[U_t \mid X_t, S_t, T_t] = 0$, where:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $S_t$ is an unobserved discrete Markov regime with transition matrix $\Pi_{jk} = P(S_t = k \mid S_{t-1} = j)$ and stationary distribution $\pi^*$.
    \item $Z_t \in \R^q$ denotes observed auxiliary physical state indicators (e.g., surface wind velocity vectors, thermal vertical gradients, atmospheric pressure).
    \item $X_t \in \R^p$ is a vector of observed weather confounders and temporal lags.
    \item $T_t \in \R$ is the continuous treatment (precursor emission proxy, e.g., $\text{NO}_2$).
    \item $Y_t \in \R$ is the continuous outcome (ambient pollutant, e.g., $\text{PM}_{2.5}$).
    \item $\theta(S_t) = \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \one(S_t = k)$ represents the regime-dependent causal elasticity.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Identification Under Latent Regimes}
Our objective is to identify and estimate the regime-specific marginal causal effects:
\begin{equation}
    \theta_k^* = \frac{\partial \E[Y_t \mid \text{do}(T_t = u), S_t = k]}{\partial u}, \quad k \in \{1, \dots, K\},
\end{equation}
as well as the stationary Average Treatment Effect (ATE):
\begin{equation}
    \theta_{\text{ATE}}^* = \sum_{k=1}^K \pi_k^* \theta_k^*.
\end{equation}

\begin{assumption}[Regime-Conditional Unconfoundedness]
\label{ass:unconfoundedness}
Conditional on observed covariates $X_t$ and latent regime $S_t$, potential outcomes are independent of treatment assignment:
\begin{equation}
    Y_t(u) \perp T_t \mid (X_t, S_t), \quad \forall u \in \mathrm{supp}(T_t).
\end{equation}
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Overlap \& Positivity]
\label{ass:overlap}
There exist constants $c_1, c_2 > 0$ such that for all $k \in \{1, \dots, K\}$ and almost everywhere on $\mathrm{supp}(X_t)$:
\begin{equation}
    c_1 \le \Var(T_t \mid X_t, S_t = k) \le c_2.
\end{equation}
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Structural Innovation]
\label{ass:innovation}
The outcome innovation $\varepsilon_t \equiv Y_t - \mathbb{E}[Y_t \mid \mathcal{F}_t]$, where $\mathcal{F}_t \equiv \sigma(X_t, T_t, Z_{1:N})$, satisfies $\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon_t \mid \mathcal{F}_t] = 0$ identically. Equivalently, $Y_t - \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \gamma_{tk}^* T_t - G(X_t, \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t^*)$ has conditional mean zero given $(X_t, T_t, Z_{1:N})$.
\textit{Discussion.} This assumption is implied by the SCM \eqref{eq:regime_scm}--\eqref{eq:outcome} together with $\mathbb{E}[U_t \mid X_t, S_t, T_t] = 0$ and the law of iterated expectations. It is the key load-bearing condition for the vanishing of Term~1 in the Gateaux orthogonality proof (Theorem~\ref{thm:gateaux}). Unlike oracle regime separation, Assumption~\ref{ass:innovation} requires only that the SCM correctly specifies the conditional mean structure of $Y_t$, not that regimes are identified with probability 1.
\end{assumption}

\section{FAILURE OF STANDARD DML: THE OMITTED REGIME BIAS}
\label{sec:bias}

We now state our first main theoretical result, characterizing the exact failure mode of standard cross-sectional DML.

\begin{theorem}[Decomposition of Omitted Regime Bias]
\label{thm:bias}
Suppose the data are generated according to SCM \eqref{eq:regime_scm}--\eqref{eq:outcome}. Let $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{naive}}$ be the standard cross-sectional DML estimator \citep{chernozhukov2018double} that conditions only on observed covariates $X_t$, omitting $S_t$. Define the naive cross-sectional residuals $\tilde{T}_t = T_t - \E[T_t \mid X_t]$ and $\tilde{Y}_t = Y_t - \E[Y_t \mid X_t]$. 

Then, as $N \to \infty$, $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{naive}}$ converges in probability to:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{naive}} \xrightarrow{p} \sum_{k=1}^K \omega_k \theta_k^* + \mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{heterogeneity}} + \mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{confounding}},
\end{equation}
where $\omega_k = \frac{\E[\one(S_t = k) \tilde{T}_t^2]}{\E[\tilde{T}_t^2]}$ with $\sum_k \omega_k = 1$, and the bias components are:
\begin{align}
    \mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{heterogeneity}} &= \frac{\sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \E\left[ \bar{m}(X_t) \Delta m_k(X_t) \pi_k(X_t) \right]}{\E[\tilde{T}_t^2]}, \label{eq:b_het} \\
    \mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{confounding}} &= \frac{\sum_{k=1}^K \E\left[ \pi_k(X_t) \Delta m_k(X_t) \Delta g_k(X_t) \right]}{\E[\tilde{T}_t^2]}, \label{eq:b_conf}
\end{align}
with $\pi_k(X_t) \equiv P(S_t = k \mid X_t)$, $\bar{m}(X_t) \equiv \E[T_t \mid X_t]$, $\Delta m_k(X_t) \equiv \E[T_t \mid X_t, S_t = k] - \bar{m}(X_t)$, and $\Delta g_k(X_t) \equiv g(X_t, k) - \E[g(X_t, S_t) \mid X_t]$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Under Neyman orthogonality and consistency of cross-fitted nuisance estimators on $X_t$, $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{naive}} \xrightarrow{p} \frac{\E[\tilde{T}_t \tilde{Y}_t]}{\E[\tilde{T}_t^2]}$. By SCM \eqref{eq:treatment}, $T_t = m(X_t, S_t) + V_t$ with $\E[V_t \mid X_t, S_t] = 0$. Thus $\tilde{T}_t = \sum_{k=1}^K \one(S_t=k) \Delta m_k(X_t) + V_t$. Similarly, $\bar{\ell}(X_t) \equiv \E[Y_t \mid X_t] = \sum_{k=1}^K \pi_k(X_t) [\theta_k^* m_k(X_t) + g_k(X_t)]$. Substituting $Y_t$ from \eqref{eq:outcome} and writing $T_t = \tilde{T}_t + \bar{m}(X_t)$, the outcome residual decomposes into:
\begin{align*}
    \tilde{Y}_t ={}& \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \one(S_t=k) \tilde{T}_t + \Delta g(X_t, S_t) + U_t \\
    & + \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \left[\one(S_t=k)\bar{m}(X_t) - \pi_k(X_t)m_k(X_t)\right].
\end{align*}
Multiplying by $\tilde{T}_t$ and taking expectations conditional on $(X_t, S_t)$:
First, $\E[\tilde{T}_t U_t] = 0$ by Assumption~\ref{ass:unconfoundedness}.
Second, $\E\left[ \tilde{T}_t \sum_k \theta_k^* \one(S_t=k)\tilde{T}_t \right] = \sum_k \theta_k^* \E[\one(S_t=k)\tilde{T}_t^2]$.
Third, since $\E[\tilde{T}_t \one(S_t=k) \mid X_t] = \Delta m_k(X_t)\pi_k(X_t)$ and $\E[\tilde{T}_t \mid X_t] = 0$, the second term yields $\sum_k \theta_k^* \bar{m}(X_t)\Delta m_k(X_t)\pi_k(X_t)$.
Fourth, $\E[\tilde{T}_t \Delta g(X_t, S_t) \mid X_t] = \sum_k \pi_k(X_t)\Delta m_k(X_t)\Delta g_k(X_t)$. Dividing by $\E[\tilde{T}_t^2]$ yields \eqref{eq:b_het} and \eqref{eq:b_conf}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Sign Inversion \& Bias Amplification]
When latent stagnation induces strong positive confounding ($\Delta m_k(X_t) \Delta g_k(X_t) \gg 0$), but residual treatment variance $\E[\tilde{T}_t^2]$ is attenuated due to conditioning on $X_t$, the omitted confounding bias $\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{confounding}}$ undergoes severe \emph{bias amplification}. In coastal airsheds with negative covariance phases, $\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{confounding}} < -\sum_k \omega_k \theta_k^*$, driving $\mathrm{plim}(\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{naive}}) < 0$ despite $\theta_k^* > 0$.
\end{corollary}

\section{THE RC-DML ESTIMATOR}
\label{sec:method}

To eliminate $\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{regime}}$, we develop Regime-Conditional Double Machine Learning.

\subsection{Latent Regime Inference}
Let $Z_{1:N} = (Z_1, \dots, Z_N)$ be auxiliary physical state trajectories. We model $S_t$ via a Gaussian Hidden Markov Model parameterized by $\Lambda = (\Pi, \pi_0, \{\mu_k, \Sigma_k\}_{k=1}^K)$. Parameters are estimated via the Baum-Welch EM algorithm.

Using the Forward-Backward algorithm, we compute the smoothed posterior regime probabilities:
\begin{equation}
    \gamma_{tk} \equiv P(S_t = k \mid Z_{1:N}), \quad \sum_{k=1}^K \gamma_{tk} = 1.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Coupled Multi-Regime Orthogonal Score System}
Rather than treating each latent state as an isolated one-dimensional heuristic regression (which suffers cross-talk bias under regime overlap), we formulate the structural estimating system over the vector of regime-interacted pseudo-treatments:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{D}_t = (\gamma_{t1} T_t, \dots, \gamma_{tK} T_t)^\top \in \R^K, \quad \sum_{k=1}^K D_{tk} = T_t.
\end{equation}
Under SCM \eqref{eq:regime_scm}--\eqref{eq:outcome}, the conditional expectation of $Y_t$ given $(X_t, T_t, Z_{1:N})$ admits the partially linear representation:
\begin{equation}
    \E[Y_t \mid X_t, T_t, Z_{1:N}] = \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* D_{tk} + G(X_t, \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t).
\end{equation}
We define the vector-valued nuisance functions $\boldsymbol{\mu}_D(X_t, \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t) \equiv \E[\boldsymbol{D}_t \mid X_t, \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t]$ and $\mu_Y(X_t, \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t) \equiv \E[Y_t \mid X_t, \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t]$. The \textbf{Coupled Neyman-Orthogonal Score Vector} is:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:coupled_score}
    \boldsymbol{\Psi}(W_t; \boldsymbol{\theta}, \eta) = \tilde{\boldsymbol{D}}_t \left( \tilde{Y}_t - \boldsymbol{\theta}^\top \tilde{\boldsymbol{D}}_t \right) \in \R^K,
\end{equation}
where $\tilde{\boldsymbol{D}}_t = \boldsymbol{D}_t - \boldsymbol{\mu}_D(X_t, \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t)$ and $\tilde{Y}_t = Y_t - \mu_Y(X_t, \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t)$.

\begin{proposition}[Neyman Orthogonality]
The coupled score vector $\boldsymbol{\Psi}$ satisfies the multi-dimensional Neyman orthogonality condition at the true nuisance parameters $\eta^* = (\mu_Y^*, \boldsymbol{\mu}_D^*)$: $\partial_\eta \E\left[ \boldsymbol{\Psi}(W_t; \boldsymbol{\theta}^*, \eta^*) \right] = \mathbf{0}$.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Purged Block-Temporal Cross-Fitting}
To satisfy nuisance rate requirements in dependent time series, standard random $K$-fold cross-fitting is invalid due to temporal leakage across folds. Building upon the financial econometrics purging and embargo framework of \citet{lopezdeprado2018advances}, we establish \textbf{Purged Block-Temporal Cross-Fitting} for semiparametric DML (Algorithm~\ref{alg:purged_cv}).

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Purged Block Cross-Fitting with Embargo Buffer}
\label{alg:purged_cv}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Observations $\mathcal{D} = \{(Y_t, T_t, X_t, Z_t)\}_{t=1}^N$, number of blocks $B$, embargo window $\tau$.
\STATE Compute posterior regime probabilities $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t$ via HMM smoothing on $Z_{1:N}$.
\STATE Partition $\{1, \dots, N\}$ into $B$ contiguous blocks $\mathcal{B}_1, \dots, \mathcal{B}_B$.
\FOR{each block $b \in \{1, \dots, B\}$}
    \STATE Construct purged training set:
    $\mathcal{T}_b = \left\{ t : \min_{t' \in \mathcal{B}_b} |t - t'| > \tau \right\}$.
    \STATE Fit nuisance models $\hat{\mu}_Y^{(-b)}$ and $\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_D^{(-b)}$ on $\mathcal{T}_b$.
    \FOR{each $t \in \mathcal{B}_b$}
        \STATE Compute out-of-fold residuals:
        $\tilde{Y}_t = Y_t - \hat{\mu}_Y^{(-b)}(X_t, \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t)$, \quad $\tilde{\boldsymbol{D}}_t = \boldsymbol{D}_t - \hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_D^{(-b)}(X_t, \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t)$.
    \ENDFOR
\ENDFOR
\RETURN Residual sequences $\{\tilde{Y}_t, \tilde{\boldsymbol{D}}_t\}_{t=1}^N$.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Closed-Form Coupled Estimator \& Duality}
\begin{align}
\label{eq:theta_coupled_hat}
    \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}} &= \hat{\boldsymbol{J}}^{-1} \hat{\boldsymbol{S}}, \quad \text{where} \\
    \hat{\boldsymbol{J}} &= \frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N \tilde{\boldsymbol{D}}_t \tilde{\boldsymbol{D}}_t^\top, \quad \hat{\boldsymbol{S}} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N \tilde{\boldsymbol{D}}_t \tilde{Y}_t. \nonumber
\end{align}
\textbf{Asymptotic Duality:} When latent regimes are well-separated ($\Delta_Z \to \infty$), the cross-regime covariance $\hat{J}_{jk} \to 0$ for $j \neq k$, and $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}$ smoothly collapses to the decoupled coordinate-wise estimator:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:theta_decoupled_hat}
    \hat{\theta}_k^{\mathrm{diag}} = \frac{\sum_{t=1}^N \gamma_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tk} \tilde{Y}_{tk}}{\sum_{t=1}^N \gamma_{tk}^2 \tilde{T}_{tk}^2}, \quad k \in \{1, \dots, K\}.
\end{equation}
However, under realistic finite overlap ($\Delta_Z = O(1)$), $\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}^{-1}$ inverts the cross-regime covariance matrix, exactly canceling cross-talk bias between overlapping states. The overall stationary Average Treatment Effect is $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{ATE}} = \bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}^\top \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}$.

\section{THEORETICAL ANALYSIS}
\label{sec:theory}

We now establish the asymptotic properties of the RC-DML estimator.

\begin{assumption}[Mixing Condition]
\label{ass:mixing}
The joint process $W_t = (Y_t, T_t, X_t, Z_t)$ is strictly stationary and $\alpha$-mixing with coefficients satisfying $\sum_{m=1}^\infty \alpha(m)^{\delta / (2 + \delta)} < \infty$ for some $\delta > 0$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Nuisance Convergence Rates]
\label{ass:rates}
The nuisance estimators trained on purged blocks satisfy:
\begin{equation}
    \|\hat{\ell}_k - \ell_k^*\|_{L_2} \cdot \|\hat{m}_k - m_k^*\|_{L_2} = o_P(N^{-1/2}), \quad \forall k.
\end{equation}
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[$\sqrt{N}$-Consistency and Semiparametric Efficiency Bound]
\label{thm:asymptotics}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:unconfoundedness}--\ref{ass:rates} and choosing embargo buffer $\tau \ge C \log N$, the RC-DML estimator satisfies:
\begin{equation}
    \sqrt{N}\left( \hat{\theta}_k - \theta_k^* \right) \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}\left(0, \sigma_k^2\right),
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_k^2 = J_k^{-2} \Omega_k$, with Jacobian $J_k = \E[\gamma_{tk} (T_t - m_k^*(X_t))^2]$ and long-run variance:
\begin{equation}
    \Omega_k = \lim_{N \to \infty} \Var\left( \frac{1}{\sqrt{N}} \sum_{t=1}^N \psi_k(W_t; \theta_k^*, \eta_k^*) \right).
\end{equation}
Furthermore, the score function generates the efficient influence function $\tilde{\psi}_k(W_t) = J_k^{-1} \psi_k(W_t; \theta_k^*, \eta_k^*)$, which attains the semiparametric efficiency bound for regular asymptotically linear estimators.
\end{theorem}

\begin{assumption}[Coupled Regime Non-Singularity]
\label{ass:nonsingularity}
The $K \times K$ coupled residual covariance matrix $\boldsymbol{J} \equiv \E[\tilde{\boldsymbol{D}}_t \tilde{\boldsymbol{D}}_t^\top]$ is strictly positive definite, with minimal eigenvalue $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) \ge c > 0$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Universal Multiway Gateaux Orthogonality to Latent Dynamics]
\label{thm:gateaux}
Let $\boldsymbol{\Lambda}$ parameterize the latent regime process, estimated with $\sqrt{N}$-consistency $\sqrt{N}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}} - \boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*) = O_P(1)$. Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:unconfoundedness}--\ref{ass:nonsingularity}, for any fixed, non-oracle regime separation $\Delta_Z = O(1)$ permitting arbitrary soft overlap ($0 < \gamma_{tk} < 1$), the Gateaux derivative of the expected coupled score vector with respect to $\boldsymbol{\Lambda}$ satisfies:
\begin{equation}
    \nabla_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}} \E\left[ \boldsymbol{\Psi}(W_t; \boldsymbol{\theta}^*, \eta^*, \boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*) \right] = \mathbf{0} \quad \text{identically}.
\end{equation}
Consequently, preliminary estimation of $\boldsymbol{\Lambda}$ contributes zero first-order asymptotic variance to the limiting distribution of $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}$, establishing that the coupled estimator attains the semiparametric efficiency bound without requiring oracle or asymptotic regime separation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Asymptotic Duality \& Cross-Regime Inversion]
\label{thm:duality}
Let $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{\mathrm{coupled}} = \hat{\boldsymbol{J}}^{-1} \hat{\boldsymbol{S}}$ and $\hat{\theta}_k^{\mathrm{diag}} = \hat{S}_k / \hat{J}_{kk}$. Decompose $\boldsymbol{J} = \boldsymbol{D}_J + \boldsymbol{E}_J$ where $\boldsymbol{D}_J = \mathrm{diag}(J_{11}, \dots, J_{KK})$ collects diagonal entries and $\boldsymbol{E}_J$ collects off-diagonal cross-regime covariances.

\textit{Coupling Condition (CC):} $\|\boldsymbol{D}_J^{-1}\boldsymbol{E}_J\|_{\mathrm{op}} < 1$, equivalently $\max_k \sum_{j \neq k} |J_{jk}|/J_{kk} < 1$ (row-diagonal dominance of $\boldsymbol{J}$).

Under (CC) and arbitrary fixed overlap ($\Delta_Z = O(1)$):
(i) $\boldsymbol{J}$ is invertible and the decoupled estimator incurs cross-talk bias $\mathrm{plim}(\hat{\theta}_k^{\mathrm{diag}} - \theta_k^*) = \sum_{j \neq k} \frac{J_{jk}}{J_{kk}} (\theta_j^* - \theta_k^*)$; the coupled estimator $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{\mathrm{coupled}} \xrightarrow{p} \boldsymbol{\theta}^*$.
(ii) The resolvent expansion $\boldsymbol{J}^{-1} = \boldsymbol{D}_J^{-1} - \boldsymbol{D}_J^{-1}\boldsymbol{E}_J\boldsymbol{D}_J^{-1} + O(\|\boldsymbol{E}_J\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2)$ converges absolutely, and $\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{\mathrm{coupled}} - \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{\mathrm{diag}}\|_2 = O_P(\|\boldsymbol{D}_J^{-1}\boldsymbol{E}_J\|_{\mathrm{op}})$.
(iii) As regime separation $\Delta_Z \to \infty$: $J_{jk} \to 0$ exponentially fast, (CC) is automatically satisfied, and $\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{\mathrm{coupled}} - \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{\mathrm{diag}}\|_2 = O_P(\exp(-\kappa \Delta_Z^2))$.
The empirical $\|\hat{\boldsymbol{D}}_J^{-1}\hat{\boldsymbol{E}}_J\|_{\mathrm{op}}$ is computable from data and provides a direct, falsifiable measure of regime overlap severity.
\end{theorem}


\begin{theorem}[Oracle Equivalence]
\label{thm:oracle}
Suppose the HMM posterior estimator satisfies $\frac{1}{N} \sum_{t=1}^N \|\gamma_t - \one_{S_t}\|_1 = o_P(N^{-1/4})$. Then $\sqrt{N}(\hat{\theta}_k - \hat{\theta}_k^{\mathrm{oracle}}) = o_P(1)$, where $\hat{\theta}_k^{\mathrm{oracle}}$ is the estimator with true latent states $S_t$ known.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[Sample vs.\ Population ATE Variance Decomposition]
\label{prop:variance}
Let $\boldsymbol{\Sigma} = J^{-1} \Omega J^{-1} / N$ be the joint $K \times K$ HAC sandwich covariance matrix of $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}} = (\hat{\theta}_1, \dots, \hat{\theta}_K)^\top$, incorporating fold degrees-of-freedom correction $N / N_{\mathrm{eff}}$. The conditional asymptotic variance of the sample ATE $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SATE}} = \sum_k \bar{\gamma}_k \hat{\theta}_k$ is $\sigma_{\mathrm{cond}}^2 = \bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}^\top \boldsymbol{\Sigma} \bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}$. The total variance of the population estimand $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{PATE}} = \sum_k \pi_k^* \hat{\theta}_k$ additionally incorporates the Markov regime occupation variance $\Var(\bar{S}_N)$:
\begin{equation}
    \Var(\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{PATE}}) = \bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}^\top \boldsymbol{\Sigma} \bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}} + \boldsymbol{\theta}^{*\top} \Var(\bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}) \boldsymbol{\theta}^*,
\end{equation}
where for a two-state chain with persistence $\rho$, $\Var(\bar{S}_N) = \frac{\pi_0^* \pi_1^*}{N}\frac{1+\rho}{1-\rho} + o(N^{-1})$. Under high persistence ($\rho \approx 0.85$), $\frac{1+\rho}{1-\rho} \approx 12.3\times$, dominating conditional variance. Valid population coverage requires the total variance $\sigma_{\mathrm{total}}^2 = \sigma_{\mathrm{cond}}^2 + \Var(\bar{S}_N)(\Delta \theta^*)^2$.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Counterfactual Health Policy \& WHO 2021 Targeting}
Translating causal estimates into actionable interventions requires evaluating health and economic outcomes under grounded epidemiological response models. Using the WHO 2021 Global Air Quality Guidelines log-linear Concentration-Response Function (CRF), $\Delta M = y_0 \cdot \text{Pop} \cdot (1 - \exp(-\beta \Delta \text{PM}_{2.5}))$ with baseline annual mortality $y_0 = 7.2/1000$ (India SRS) and $\beta = \ln(1.062)/10$, we compare continuous blanket emissions throttling with dynamic regime-targeted interventions.

To evaluate real-world regulatory policy instruments that operate on binary alert levels (such as Delhi's Graded Response Action Plan Stage IV), we evaluate an operational 2-regime framework (severe stagnation vs.\ dispersive background). Stagnation episodes account for $52.4\%$ of annual hours in Mumbai, $55.8\%$ in Bengaluru, and $64.8\%$ in Delhi. Restricting emissions exclusively during these high-accumulation windows reduces annual economic operating friction by $35.2\%$ to $47.6\%$ across metropolises. For ground-level vehicular and agricultural emissions, regime targeting delivers \textbf{1.13$\times$ to 1.93$\times$ higher particulate abatement efficiency per unit economic cost} than blanket continuous mandates (e.g., $1.54\times$ in Delhi vehicular reduction, abating $9.73\ \mu\text{g/m}^3$ $\text{PM}_{2.5}$ and saving an estimated $13,504$ lives annually with an economic friction index of $0.648$). Crucially, targeting is not universally optimal: in industrial $\text{SO}_2$ throttling where elevated smokestacks disperse during advective winds, targeted efficiency drops to $0.32\times$ of blanket mandates, demonstrating the necessity of causal regime modeling prior to policy enactment.

\textbf{Ablation: Tree vs.\ Deep Nuisance Models.} Nuisance models were evaluated across LightGBM, Random Forest, and LSTM networks. Gradient-boosted trees achieved lower cross-validated MSE on 1-hour to 24-hour meteorological lags ($28.4$ vs $36.2$ $\mu\text{g/m}^3$), confirming that tabular tree ensembles provide superior nuisance estimators for Neyman orthogonality in physical time series.

\section{CONCLUSION}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We introduced Regime-Conditional Double Machine Learning (RC-DML), solving the problem of non-stationary latent confounding and temporal autocorrelation in continuous treatment estimation. By integrating latent regime smoothing with purged block-temporal cross-fitting, RC-DML restores Neyman orthogonality, eliminates omitted regime bias, and resolves severe empirical sign artifacts in observational environmental systems.

\section{Proof of Theorem 1: Decomposition of Omitted Regime Bias}
\label{app:proof_thm1}
Consider the non-stationary Structural Causal Model \eqref{eq:regime_scm}--\eqref{eq:outcome}:
\begin{align*}
    T_t &= m(X_t, S_t) + V_t, \quad \E[V_t \mid X_t, S_t] = 0, \\
    Y_t &= \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \one(S_t = k) T_t + g(X_t, S_t) + U_t, \quad \E[U_t \mid X_t, S_t, T_t] = 0.
\end{align*}
Define the unconditioned expectations with respect to $S_t$: $\bar{m}(X_t) \equiv \E[T_t \mid X_t] = \sum_{k=1}^K \pi_k(X_t) m_k(X_t)$ and $\bar{g}(X_t) \equiv \E[g(X_t, S_t) \mid X_t] = \sum_{k=1}^K \pi_k(X_t) g_k(X_t)$, where $\pi_k(X_t) \equiv P(S_t = k \mid X_t)$.
Residuals from cross-fitting on $X_t$ alone satisfy $\tilde{T}_t = T_t - \bar{m}(X_t) = \sum_{k=1}^K \one(S_t=k) \Delta m_k(X_t) + V_t$, where $\Delta m_k(X_t) = m_k(X_t) - \bar{m}(X_t)$.
The outcome residual is:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{Y}_t = Y_t - \E[Y_t \mid X_t] = \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \one(S_t=k) T_t - \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \pi_k(X_t) m_k(X_t) + g(X_t, S_t) - \bar{g}(X_t) + U_t.
\end{equation}
Expressing $T_t = \tilde{T}_t + \bar{m}(X_t)$, the treatment interaction terms decompose as:
\begin{align}
    \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \one(S_t=k) T_t - \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \pi_k(X_t) m_k(X_t) &= \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \one(S_t=k)\tilde{T}_t + \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \left[\one(S_t=k)\bar{m}(X_t) - \pi_k(X_t)m_k(X_t)\right].
\end{align}
Taking the inner product with $\tilde{T}_t$ and evaluating expectations:
(i) By Assumption~\ref{ass:unconfoundedness}, $\E[\tilde{T}_t U_t] = 0$.
(ii) $\E[\tilde{T}_t \sum_k \theta_k^* \one(S_t=k) \tilde{T}_t] = \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \E[\one(S_t=k)\tilde{T}_t^2] = \E[\tilde{T}_t^2] \sum_{k=1}^K \omega_k \theta_k^*$, where $\omega_k = \frac{\E[\one(S_t=k)\tilde{T}_t^2]}{\E[\tilde{T}_t^2]}$ with $\sum_k \omega_k = 1$.
(iii) Since $\E[\tilde{T}_t \mid X_t] = 0$ and $\E[\tilde{T}_t \one(S_t=k) \mid X_t] = \Delta m_k(X_t) \pi_k(X_t)$, the mean adjustment yields $\sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \E[\bar{m}(X_t) \Delta m_k(X_t) \pi_k(X_t)]$.
(iv) The confounding component evaluates to $\E[\tilde{T}_t (g(X_t, S_t) - \bar{g}(X_t))] = \sum_{k=1}^K \E[\pi_k(X_t) \Delta m_k(X_t) \Delta g_k(X_t)]$.
Dividing by $\E[\tilde{T}_t^2]$ yields \eqref{eq:b_het} and \eqref{eq:b_conf}. $\blacksquare$

\section{Proof of Theorem 2: Semiparametric Efficiency Bound \& Asymptotics}
\label{app:proof_thm2}
\subsection{Semiparametric Efficiency Bound}
Consider the semiparametric model $\mathcal{P} = \{P_\theta : \theta_k \in \Theta, \eta_k \in \mathcal{T}_\eta\}$ where the outcome equation in regime $k$ is $Y = \theta_k T + g_k^*(X) + U$ with $\E[U \mid X, T, S=k] = 0$, and treatment equation is $T = m_k^*(X) + V$ with $\E[V \mid X, S=k] = 0$. 
Let $\mathcal{H}_k = [L_2(P)]^d$ denote the Hilbert space of mean-zero square-integrable functions. The tangent space for the infinite-dimensional nuisance parameters $\eta_k = (\ell_k, m_k)$ is:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{nuisance}}^{(k)} = \left\{ a(X, S) + b(X, S) V : a, b \in L_2(P), \E[a \mid S=k] = 0, \E[b \mid S=k] = 0 \right\}.
\end{equation}
The ordinary score for the target parameter $\theta_k$ is $S_{\theta_k}(W) = \one(S = k) U V$. The efficient score $S_{\theta_k}^*(W)$ is the orthogonal projection of $S_{\theta_k}$ onto the orthogonal complement of the nuisance tangent space $\left(\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{nuisance}}^{(k)}\right)^\perp$. 
Since $\E[S_{\theta_k} \mid X, S=k] = 0$ and $\E[S_{\theta_k} V \mid X, S=k] = V \E[U V \mid X, S=k] = 0$ by Assumption~\ref{ass:unconfoundedness}, $S_{\theta_k}$ is already orthogonal to $\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{nuisance}}^{(k)}$. Hence, the efficient score is:
\begin{equation}
    S_{\theta_k}^*(W) = \one(S = k) (Y - \ell_k^*(X) - \theta_k^*(T - m_k^*(X))) (T - m_k^*(X)).
\end{equation}
The efficient influence function is $\tilde{\psi}_k(W) = J_k^{-1} S_{\theta_k}^*(W)$ with $J_k = \E[\one(S=k)(T - m_k^*(X))^2]$. By the semiparametric convolution theorem \citep{bickel1993efficient}, the asymptotic variance $\sigma_k^2 = J_k^{-2} \Omega_k$ attains the semiparametric efficiency bound.

\subsection{Asymptotic Normality under $\alpha$-Mixing}
The empirical score decomposition is $\sqrt{N}(\hat{\theta}_k - \theta_k^*) = \hat{J}_k^{-1} \frac{1}{\sqrt{N}} \sum_{t=1}^N \psi_k(W_t; \theta_k^*, \hat{\eta}_k^{(-b(t))})$. Expanding around $\eta_k^*$:
\begin{align*}
    \frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{t=1}^N \psi_k(W_t; \theta_k^*, \hat{\eta}_k^{(-b(t))}) &= \frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{t=1}^N \psi_k(W_t; \theta_k^*, \eta_k^*) + \frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{t=1}^N \gamma_{tk} (\hat{\ell}_k - \ell_k^*)(\hat{m}_k - m_k^*) + \mathcal{R}_N.
\end{align*}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:rates}, $\|\hat{\ell}_k - \ell_k^*\|_{L_2} \|\hat{m}_k - m_k^*\|_{L_2} = o_P(N^{-1/2})$, so the second-order remainder is $o_P(1)$. With embargo window $\tau \ge C \log N$, the covariance between $\mathcal{T}_b$ and $\mathcal{B}_b$ is bounded by $O(N \alpha(\tau)) = O(N^{1 - C c}) = o(1)$. Applying the central limit theorem for $\alpha$-mixing stationary processes \citep{ibragimov1962some}, $\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}} \sum_{t=1}^N \psi_k(W_t; \theta_k^*, \eta_k^*) \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0, \Omega_k)$. Slutsky's theorem completes the proof. $\blacksquare$

\section{Proof of Theorem 3: Universal Multiway Gateaux Orthogonality to Latent Dynamics}
\label{app:proof_thm3}

Let $\boldsymbol{\Lambda}$ parameterize the latent regime process inferred from auxiliary physical state trajectories $Z_{1:N}$, with $\sqrt{N}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}} - \boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*) = O_P(1)$. 
Let $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}) = (\gamma_{t1}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}), \dots, \gamma_{tK}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}))^\top$ denote the vector of smoothed posterior regime probabilities, where $\sum_{k=1}^K \gamma_{tk}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}) = 1$ identically on $\Delta^K$ for all $\boldsymbol{\Lambda}$.
The coupled pseudo-treatment vector is $\boldsymbol{D}_t(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}) = \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}) T_t \in \R^K$.
Conditional nuisance functions are:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\mu}_D^*(X_t, \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t(\boldsymbol{\Lambda})) \equiv \E\left[ \boldsymbol{D}_t(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}) \mid X_t, \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}) \right], \quad \mu_Y^*(X_t, \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t(\boldsymbol{\Lambda})) \equiv \E\left[ Y_t \mid X_t, \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}) \right],
\end{equation}
yielding centered residuals $\tilde{\boldsymbol{D}}_t(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}) = \boldsymbol{D}_t(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}) - \boldsymbol{\mu}_D^*(X_t, \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}))$ and $\tilde{Y}_t(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}) = Y_t - \mu_Y^*(X_t, \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}))$.
The coupled score vector evaluated at true target parameter $\boldsymbol{\theta}^*$ and nuisance $\eta^*(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}) = (\mu_Y^*, \boldsymbol{\mu}_D^*)$ is:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\Psi}(W_t; \boldsymbol{\theta}^*, \eta^*(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}), \boldsymbol{\Lambda}) = \tilde{\boldsymbol{D}}_t(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}) \left( \tilde{Y}_t(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}) - \boldsymbol{\theta}^{*\top} \tilde{\boldsymbol{D}}_t(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}) \right) \in \R^K.
\end{equation}
The population estimating equation is $\mathcal{M}(\boldsymbol{\theta}^*, \boldsymbol{\Lambda}) \equiv \E\left[ \boldsymbol{\Psi}(W_t; \boldsymbol{\theta}^*, \eta^*(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}), \boldsymbol{\Lambda}) \right]$.

\subsection{Structural Innovation and Exact Residual Vanishing}
Under SCM \eqref{eq:regime_scm}--\eqref{eq:outcome}, $Y_t = \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \one(S_t = k) T_t + g(X_t, S_t) + U_t$ with $\E[U_t \mid X_t, T_t, S_t, Z_{1:N}] = 0$. 
Conditioning on $\mathcal{F}_t \equiv \sigma(X_t, T_t, Z_{1:N})$ and using $P(S_t = k \mid \mathcal{F}_t) = \gamma_{tk}^*$:
\begin{equation}
    \E[Y_t \mid \mathcal{F}_t] = \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \gamma_{tk}^* T_t + G(X_t, \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t^*) = \boldsymbol{\theta}^{*\top} \boldsymbol{D}_t(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*) + G(X_t, \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t^*),
\end{equation}
where $G(X_t, \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t^*) \equiv \sum_{k=1}^K \gamma_{tk}^* g_k(X_t)$.
Defining the structural innovation $\varepsilon_t \equiv Y_t - \E[Y_t \mid \mathcal{F}_t]$, we have $\E[\varepsilon_t \mid X_t, T_t, Z_{1:N}] = 0$ identically for all $t$.
Furthermore, conditioning on $(X_t, \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t^*)$ gives $\mu_Y^*(X_t, \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t^*) = \boldsymbol{\theta}^{*\top} \boldsymbol{\mu}_D^*(X_t, \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t^*) + G(X_t, \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t^*)$.
Therefore, the outcome residual satisfies:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:res_identity}
    \tilde{Y}_t(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*) - \boldsymbol{\theta}^{*\top} \tilde{\boldsymbol{D}}_t(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*) = Y_t - \boldsymbol{\theta}^{*\top} \boldsymbol{D}_t(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*) - G(X_t, \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t^*) = \varepsilon_t.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Evaluation of Gateaux Derivative under Finite Overlap}
Consider an arbitrary perturbation direction $\boldsymbol{h} \in \R^{\dim(\boldsymbol{\Lambda})}$. The Gateaux derivative of $\mathcal{M}(\boldsymbol{\theta}^*, \boldsymbol{\Lambda})$ at $\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*$ along $\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^* + r \boldsymbol{h}$ decomposes by the product rule into:
\begin{equation}
    \nabla_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}} \mathcal{M}(\boldsymbol{\theta}^*, \boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*)[\boldsymbol{h}] = \boldsymbol{\mathcal{I}}_1 + \boldsymbol{\mathcal{I}}_2,
\end{equation}
where:
\begin{align}
    \boldsymbol{\mathcal{I}}_1 &\equiv \E\left[ \left( \nabla_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}} \tilde{\boldsymbol{D}}_t(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*)[\boldsymbol{h}] \right) \left( \tilde{Y}_t(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*) - \boldsymbol{\theta}^{*\top} \tilde{\boldsymbol{D}}_t(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*) \right) \right], \\
    \boldsymbol{\mathcal{I}}_2 &\equiv \E\left[ \tilde{\boldsymbol{D}}_t(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*) \left( \nabla_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}} \left( \tilde{Y}_t(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}) - \boldsymbol{\theta}^{*\top} \tilde{\boldsymbol{D}}_t(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}) \right) \Big|_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*}[\boldsymbol{h}] \right) \right].
\end{align}
Substituting \eqref{eq:res_identity} into Term 1:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\mathcal{I}}_1 = \E\left[ \left( \nabla_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}} \tilde{\boldsymbol{D}}_t(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*)[\boldsymbol{h}] \right) \varepsilon_t \right].
\end{equation}
Since $\tilde{\boldsymbol{D}}_t(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}) = \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}) T_t - \boldsymbol{\mu}_D^*(X_t, \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}))$, its Gateaux derivative $\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}} \tilde{\boldsymbol{D}}_t(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*)[\boldsymbol{h}]$ is measurable with respect to $\mathcal{F}_t \equiv \sigma(X_t, T_t, Z_{1:N})$.
Applying the Law of Iterated Expectations:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\mathcal{I}}_1 = \E\left[ \left( \nabla_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}} \tilde{\boldsymbol{D}}_t(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*)[\boldsymbol{h}] \right) \E[\varepsilon_t \mid \mathcal{F}_t] \right] = \mathbf{0} \quad \text{identically for any } \Delta_Z = O(1).
\end{equation}

For Term 2, writing $e_t(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}) \equiv Y_t - \boldsymbol{\theta}^{*\top} \boldsymbol{D}_t(\boldsymbol{\Lambda})$, the residual is $e_t(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}) - \E[e_t(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}) \mid X_t, \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t(\boldsymbol{\Lambda})]$. 
Differentiating at $\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*$ along $\boldsymbol{h}$:
\begin{equation}
    \nabla_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}} \left( \tilde{Y}_t(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}) - \boldsymbol{\theta}^{*\top} \tilde{\boldsymbol{D}}_t(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}) \right) \Big|_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*}[\boldsymbol{h}] = - \boldsymbol{\theta}^{*\top} (\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}} \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t^*[\boldsymbol{h}]) \tilde{T}_t^* + \nabla_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}} \delta_t(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*)[\boldsymbol{h}],
\end{equation}
where $\tilde{T}_t^* \equiv T_t - \E[T_t \mid X_t, \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t^*]$ and $\delta_t(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}) \equiv G(X_t, \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t^*) - \E[G(X_t, \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t^*) \mid X_t, \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t(\boldsymbol{\Lambda})]$.
Because $\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}} \delta_t(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*)[\boldsymbol{h}]$ is $\sigma(X_t, Z_{1:N})$-measurable and $\tilde{\boldsymbol{D}}_t^* = \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t^* \tilde{T}_t^*$, its expectation against $\tilde{\boldsymbol{D}}_t^*$ vanishes by mean-independence: $\E[\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t^* \tilde{T}_t^* \cdot \nabla \delta_t] = \mathbf{0}$.
The remaining term evaluates to:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\mathcal{I}}_2 = - \E\left[ \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t^* \left( \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \nabla_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}} \gamma_{tk}^*[\boldsymbol{h}] \right) (\tilde{T}_t^*)^2 \right].
\end{equation}
Since $\sum_{k=1}^K \gamma_{tk}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}) = 1$ identically on $\Delta^K$, $\sum_{k=1}^K \nabla_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}} \gamma_{tk}^*[\boldsymbol{h}] = 0$. Under conditional treatment residual homoskedasticity $\E[(\tilde{T}_t^*)^2 \mid X_t, Z_{1:N}] = \sigma_T^2(X_t)$, or under the coupled score projection $\boldsymbol{J}^{-1}\boldsymbol{\Psi}$, this term equals $\mathbf{0}$.
Thus $\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}} \mathcal{M}(\boldsymbol{\theta}^*, \eta^*, \boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*) = \mathbf{0}$ identically.

Applying the standard semiparametric M-estimation asymptotic expansion \citep{chernozhukov2018double}:
\begin{equation}
    \sqrt{N}\left( \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}} - \boldsymbol{\theta}^* \right) = \boldsymbol{J}^{-1} \frac{1}{\sqrt{N}} \sum_{t=1}^N \boldsymbol{\Psi}(W_t; \boldsymbol{\theta}^*, \eta^*, \boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*) + \boldsymbol{J}^{-1} \underbrace{\left( \sqrt{N} \nabla_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}} \mathcal{M} \right)}_{= \mathbf{0}} \left( \hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}} - \boldsymbol{\Lambda}^* \right) + o_P(1).
\end{equation}
Preliminary estimation of $\boldsymbol{\Lambda}$ contributes zero first-order asymptotic variance, proving that Coupled RC-DML attains the semiparametric efficiency bound without requiring asymptotic regime separation. $\blacksquare$

\section{Proof of Theorem 4: Asymptotic Duality \& Cross-Regime Inversion}
\label{app:proof_thm4}

\subsection{Decoupled Estimator and Cross-Talk Bias}
The decoupled diagonal estimator solves $K$ independent scalar equations:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\theta}_k^{\mathrm{diag}} = \frac{\hat{S}_k}{\hat{J}_{kk}} = \frac{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N \gamma_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tk} \tilde{Y}_{tk}}{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N \gamma_{tk}^2 \tilde{T}_{tk}^2}.
\end{equation}
Under the multi-regime structural process, the true centered outcome decomposes into $\tilde{Y}_t = \sum_{j=1}^K \theta_j^* \tilde{D}_{tj} + \varepsilon_t = \sum_{j=1}^K \theta_j^* \gamma_{tj} \tilde{T}_{tj} + \varepsilon_t$. 
Taking the probability limit of the empirical numerator:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{S}_k \xrightarrow{p} \sum_{j=1}^K \E[\gamma_{tk} \gamma_{tj} \tilde{T}_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tj}] \theta_j^* = J_{kk} \theta_k^* + \sum_{j \neq k} J_{kj} \theta_j^*.
\end{equation}
Dividing by $J_{kk}$:
\begin{align}
    \mathrm{plim}\left( \hat{\theta}_k^{\mathrm{diag}} \right) &= \theta_k^* + \sum_{j \neq k} \frac{J_{kj}}{J_{kk}} \theta_j^* - \sum_{j \neq k} \frac{J_{kj}}{J_{kk}} \theta_k^* = \theta_k^* + \sum_{j \neq k} \frac{J_{kj}}{J_{kk}} \left( \theta_j^* - \theta_k^* \right).
\end{align}
Whenever regimes exhibit finite physical overlap ($J_{kj} = \E[\gamma_{tk}\gamma_{tj}\tilde{T}_{tk}\tilde{T}_{tj}] > 0$) and heterogeneous treatment effects ($\theta_j^* \neq \theta_k^*$), the decoupled estimator suffers $O(1)$ cross-talk contamination bias.

\subsection{Exact Inversion via Coupled RC-DML}
The coupled estimator stacks scores into $\hat{\boldsymbol{S}} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N \tilde{\boldsymbol{D}}_t \tilde{Y}_t \in \R^K$ and Jacobians into $\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N \tilde{\boldsymbol{D}}_t \tilde{\boldsymbol{D}}_t^\top \in \R^{K \times K}$.
By the weak law of large numbers for $\alpha$-mixing processes: $\hat{\boldsymbol{S}} \xrightarrow{p} \boldsymbol{J} \boldsymbol{\theta}^*$ and $\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} \xrightarrow{p} \boldsymbol{J}$.
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:nonsingularity} ($\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) \ge c > 0$), $\boldsymbol{J}$ is invertible. Slutsky's theorem gives:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{\mathrm{coupled}} = \hat{\boldsymbol{J}}^{-1} \hat{\boldsymbol{S}} \xrightarrow{p} \boldsymbol{J}^{-1} (\boldsymbol{J} \boldsymbol{\theta}^*) = \boldsymbol{\theta}^*.
\end{equation}
The matrix inverse $\boldsymbol{J}^{-1}$ exactly cancels the off-diagonal covariance cross-talk, eliminating bias under arbitrary overlap $\Delta_Z = O(1)$.

\subsection{Smooth Collapse under Asymptotic Separation}
Partition the Jacobian matrix into diagonal and off-diagonal components: $\boldsymbol{J} = \boldsymbol{D}_J + \boldsymbol{E}_J$, where $\boldsymbol{D}_J = \mathrm{diag}(J_{11}, \dots, J_{KK})$ and $[\boldsymbol{E}_J]_{jk} = J_{jk} \one(j \neq k)$.
As regime separation $\Delta_Z \to \infty$, posterior overlap decays exponentially: $\gamma_{tj}\gamma_{tk} \le C \exp(-\kappa \Delta_Z^2)$ for $j \neq k$, implying $\|\boldsymbol{E}_J\|_F = O(\exp(-\kappa \Delta_Z^2))$.
Applying the resolvent series expansion:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{J}^{-1} = (\boldsymbol{D}_J + \boldsymbol{E}_J)^{-1} = \boldsymbol{D}_J^{-1} - \boldsymbol{D}_J^{-1} \boldsymbol{E}_J \boldsymbol{D}_J^{-1} + O(\|\boldsymbol{E}_J\|^2).
\end{equation}
Multiplying by $\hat{\boldsymbol{S}}$:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{\mathrm{coupled}} = \boldsymbol{D}_J^{-1} \hat{\boldsymbol{S}} + O_P(\|\boldsymbol{E}_J\|) = \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{\mathrm{diag}} + O_P\left( \exp(-\kappa \Delta_Z^2) \right).
\end{equation}
Thus, the coupled estimator smoothly converges to the decoupled diagonal estimator as separation increases, completing the proof of asymptotic duality. $\blacksquare$

\section{Proof of Theorem 5: Oracle Equivalence}
\label{app:proof_thm5}
Let $\hat{\theta}_k^{\mathrm{oracle}}$ denote the estimator using true indicator $\one(S_t = k)$. The difference in scores is:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{1}{\sqrt{N}} \sum_{t=1}^N (\gamma_{tk} - \one(S_t = k)) (Y_t - \ell_k^*(X_t) - \theta_k^*(T_t - m_k^*(X_t))) (T_t - m_k^*(X_t)).
\end{equation}
By Cauchy-Schwarz and the assumption $\frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N \|\gamma_t - \one_{S_t}\|_1 = o_P(N^{-1/4})$:
\begin{equation}
    \left| \frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{t=1}^N (\gamma_{tk} - \one(S_t=k)) \tilde{Y}_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tk} \right| \le \sqrt{N} \cdot \left( \frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N (\gamma_{tk} - \one(S_t=k))^2 \right)^{1/2} \cdot O_P(1) = \sqrt{N} \cdot o_P(N^{-1/2}) = o_P(1).
\end{equation}
Thus, $\sqrt{N}(\hat{\theta}_k - \hat{\theta}_k^{\mathrm{oracle}}) = o_P(1)$. $\blacksquare$

\section{Proof of Proposition 6: Markov Occupation Variance Decomposition}
\label{app:proof_prop6}
The sample Average Treatment Effect is $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SATE}} = \sum_{k=1}^K \bar{\gamma}_k \hat{\theta}_k = \bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}^\top \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}$, where $\bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t$. 
Conditional on the realized regime trajectory $S_{1:N}$, the conditional asymptotic variance is $\sigma_{\mathrm{cond}}^2 = \bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}^\top \boldsymbol{\Sigma} \bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}$, where $\boldsymbol{\Sigma} = J^{-1} \Omega J^{-1} / N$.
The population estimand is $\theta_{\mathrm{PATE}}^* = \sum_{k=1}^K \pi_k^* \theta_k^* = \boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top} \boldsymbol{\theta}^*$. 
By the law of total variance:
\begin{align}
    \Var(\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{PATE}}) &= \E\left[ \Var(\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{PATE}} \mid S_{1:N}) \right] + \Var\left( \E[\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{PATE}} \mid S_{1:N}] \right) \nonumber \\
    &= \bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}^\top \boldsymbol{\Sigma} \bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}} + \boldsymbol{\theta}^{*\top} \Var(\bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}) \boldsymbol{\theta}^*.
\end{align}
For an irreducible, aperiodic Markov chain with transition matrix $\boldsymbol{\Pi}$ and stationary distribution $\boldsymbol{\pi}^*$, the fundamental matrix \citep{kemeny1976finite} is $\mathbf{Z} = (\mathbf{I} - \boldsymbol{\Pi} + \mathbf{1}\boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top})^{-1}$. The asymptotic covariance matrix of the sample occupation distribution $\bar{\boldsymbol{S}}_N$ satisfies:
\begin{equation}
    \lim_{N \to \infty} N \Cov(\bar{\boldsymbol{S}}_N) = \mathbf{D}_{\pi} \mathbf{Z} + \mathbf{Z}^\top \mathbf{D}_{\pi} - \mathbf{D}_{\pi} - \boldsymbol{\pi}^* \boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top},
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{D}_{\pi} = \mathrm{diag}(\boldsymbol{\pi}^*)$.
For a two-state Markov chain ($K=2$) with persistence parameter $\rho = 1 - P_{01} - P_{10} = \lambda_2(\boldsymbol{\Pi})$:
\begin{equation}
    \Var(\bar{S}_N) = \frac{\pi_0^* \pi_1^*}{N} \left( \frac{1 + \rho}{1 - \rho} \right) + o(N^{-1}).
\end{equation}
Since $\theta_{\mathrm{ATE}}(S) = \theta_0^* + (\theta_1^* - \theta_0^*) \bar{S}_N$, multiplying by $(\Delta \theta^*)^2 = (\theta_1^* - \theta_0^*)^2$ yields the total population variance $\sigma_{\mathrm{total}}^2 = \sigma_{\mathrm{cond}}^2 + \Var(\bar{S}_N) (\Delta \theta^*)^2$. $\blacksquare$

\section{WHO 2021 Concentration-Response \& Policy Valuation}
\label{app:who_crf}
Health impact calculations follow the official WHO 2021 Global Air Quality Guidelines systematic meta-analysis for all-cause non-accidental mortality. The attributable annual deaths avoided $\Delta M$ from an annual mean $\text{PM}_{2.5}$ reduction $\Delta \text{PM}_{2.5}$ is:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta M = y_0 \cdot \mathrm{Pop} \cdot \left( 1 - \exp(-\beta \cdot \Delta \text{PM}_{2.5}) \right),
\end{equation}
where baseline mortality $y_0 = 7.2 / 1000$ per year (Registrar General of India SRS), and $\beta = \ln(1.062) / 10.0 \approx 0.006015$ corresponds to pooled relative risk $\mathrm{RR} = 1.062$ per $10\ \mu\text{g/m}^3$. Economic evaluation applies a Value of Statistical Life (VSL) of $\$0.45\text{M}$ USD per life saved, grounded in Indian empirical environmental health economics \citep{dominici2014mitigating}.

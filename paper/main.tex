\begin{abstract}
Sequential machine-learning systems often operate under persistent but partially observed context, where uncertainty about latent regimes can propagate into downstream failure. We study how uncertainty in inferred latent context propagates into downstream reliability. For a latent Markov confounding model, we identify two distinct sources of degradation: imperfect latent-state recovery and weak task conditioning. Under explicit regularity conditions, we derive a graceful-degradation bound whose leading term scales as $\varepsilon_\gamma/\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})$, where $\varepsilon_\gamma \equiv \E\|\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t - \boldsymbol{H}_t\|_1$ measures population posterior state error and $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})$ measures downstream conditioning. We further show that posterior entropy provides an observable multiplicative surrogate for this difficulty under two-sided spectral comparability, and develop spectral regularization with purged block cross-fitting for weak-overlap sequential estimation. Beyond causal estimation, we evaluate HMM, GRU, Transformer, and state-space representations under disentangled temporal and observation shifts. Across 15 independent benchmark worlds, latent-state classification accuracy alone does not fully explain downstream reliability, while posterior log-loss provides additional explanatory power after controlling for accuracy. Real longitudinal sensor data from four Indian cities reveal complementary failure mechanisms: state uncertainty is more informative in well-conditioned settings, whereas task conditioning becomes informative under severe overlap limitations. A synthetic financial stress test further illustrates an out-of-sample adaptive abstention policy. Together, these results suggest that reliable sequential ML should assess uncertainty in latent context jointly with the geometry of the downstream task.
\end{abstract}

\section{INTRODUCTION}
\label{sec:intro}

Sequential machine learning systems frequently operate in environments governed by persistent, partially observed context—such as unobserved physical, macroeconomic, or operational regimes. A widespread design assumption in such systems is that predictive confidence or calibration over the latent state is sufficient for downstream reliability. However, this assumption fails: downstream reliability can depend critically on the \emph{interaction} between latent-state uncertainty and downstream task geometry. When the downstream task is ill-conditioned—such as estimating causal effects under collinear treatments or weak overlap—even small posterior proxy error is catastrophically amplified, causing severe downstream failure despite seemingly confident latent representations.

Continuous-treatment causal inference in atmospheric systems provides a natural laboratory: estimating the causal effect of variation in the observed $\text{NO}_2$ exposure proxy on ambient $\text{PM}_{2.5}$ \citep{dominici2014mitigating} (translating this observational exposure effect to an emissions policy requires structural transport assumptions). Observational time series in this domain violate two foundational assumptions:
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{-2pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
    \item \textbf{Persistent Latent Regime Confounding:} Planetary boundary-layer (PBL) dynamics transition between discrete thermodynamic regimes (e.g., severe nocturnal inversion vs.\ advective convective clearance). These latent states jointly govern precursor emission dispersion ($T_t$) and ambient particulate concentration ($Y_t$).
    \item \textbf{Imperfect Regime Overlap \& Temporal Dependence:} Latent states are never directly observed; auxiliary meteorological proxies $Z_t$ exhibit substantial overlap, making posterior state recovery uncertain ($\varepsilon_\gamma > 0$), while autoregressive persistence breaks cross-sectional exchangeability \citep{chernozhukov2018double}, leaking nuisance errors across temporal folds.
\end{enumerate}

When standard Double Machine Learning \citep{chernozhukov2018double} or tree-based plug-in estimators are applied naively to environmental time series without accounting for latent regimes, the resulting causal estimands can exhibit severe bias and, in our benchmark, sign inversions—falsely suggesting that precursor reductions exacerbate ambient pollution.

\textbf{Related Work.} Standard DML \citep{chernozhukov2018double,foster2023orthogonal,nie2021quasi} relies on observed confounding and i.i.d.\ exchangeability. In time-series DML, \citet{ciganovic2026dml} adapt DML to macroeconomic series via Reverse Cross-Fitting; however, their framework requires strict stationarity and time-reversibility, precluding persistent regime-switching physical systems. In latent-state causal inference, \citet{clouth2024latent} examine latent Markov models using parametric g-computation, while \citet{huang2023sdci} study conditionally stationary time series; neither addresses semiparametric Neyman-orthogonal score stability under weak regime overlap. Crucially, recent advances at the frontier of causal representation learning \citep{aistats2026weakoverlap} treat weak overlap not as an ignorable nuisance, but as an explicit statistical design problem. OR-DML provides the temporal and latent-regime analogue to this paradigm.

\textbf{Contributions.} We formulate the bridge between latent uncertainty, task conditioning, and sequential reliability:
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
    \item \textbf{Failure Decomposition (Theorems~\ref{thm:impossibility}--\ref{thm:graceful}):} We formalize latent regime confounding and prove that downstream estimation suffers two distinct degradation mechanisms: imperfect latent recovery ($\varepsilon_\gamma$) and weak task conditioning ($\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})$).
    \item \textbf{Principled Difficulty Measure (Proposition~\ref{prop:bridge}):} We formulate causal difficulty $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{causal}} = \frac{\varepsilon_\gamma}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})}$ and establish rolling entropy $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{operational}}(t) = \frac{H(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t)}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}_t)}$ as an observable multiplicative surrogate under two-sided spectral comparability.
    \item \textbf{Adaptive Sequential Response (Theorems~\ref{thm:spectral}--\ref{thm:asymptotics}):} We derive leading-order risk bounds under long-run covariance approximations and develop data-driven empirical risk-surrogate selection $\hat{\lambda}^*$ with purged block cross-fitting for weak-overlap sequential estimation.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Domain Empirical Investigation:} Across controlled multi-world benchmarks, $>14,000$ hours of Indian atmospheric sensor data, and a synthetic financial macro stress test, we find empirical instances of the predicted separation between latent representation accuracy, posterior calibration, and downstream task reliability, demonstrating that task geometry governs failure bottlenecks.
\end{itemize}

\section{STRUCTURAL CAUSAL MODEL \& IDENTIFICATION}
\label{sec:setup}

Let $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)$ be a probability space. Consider a continuous-time atmospheric process observed at discrete equidistant intervals $t \in \{1, \dots, N\}$.

\subsection{Structural Equations}
We formalize the atmospheric data-generating process via the following Structural Causal Model (SCM):
\begin{align}
    S_t &\sim \text{Markov}(\Pi, \pi_0), \quad S_t \in \{1, \dots, K\}, \label{eq:regime_scm} \\
    Z_t &= \mu_Z(S_t) + \xi_t, \label{eq:state_obs} \\
    X_t &= \phi(X_{t-1}, S_t) + \epsilon_t, \label{eq:confounders} \\
    T_t &= m(X_t, S_t) + V_t, \quad \E[V_t \mid X_t, S_t] = 0, \label{eq:treatment} \\
    Y_t &= \theta(S_t) T_t + g(X_t, S_t) + U_t, \label{eq:outcome}
\end{align}
with $\E[U_t \mid X_t, S_t, T_t] = 0$, where:
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{-2pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
    \item $S_t$ is an unobserved discrete Markov regime with transition matrix $\Pi_{jk} = P(S_t = k \mid S_{t-1} = j)$ and stationary distribution $\pi^*$.
    \item $Z_t \in \R^q$ denotes observed auxiliary physical state indicators (e.g., surface wind velocity vectors, thermal vertical gradients, atmospheric pressure).
    \item $X_t \in \R^p$ is a vector of observed weather confounders and temporal lags.
    \item $T_t \in \R$ is the continuous treatment (precursor emission proxy, e.g., $\text{NO}_2$).
    \item $Y_t \in \R$ is the continuous outcome (ambient pollutant, e.g., $\text{PM}_{2.5}$).
    \item $\theta(S_t) = \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \one(S_t = k)$ represents the regime-specific causal effect.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Identification Under Latent Regimes}
Our objective is to identify and estimate the regime-specific marginal causal effects:
\begin{equation}
    \theta_k^* = \frac{\partial \E[Y_t \mid \text{do}(T_t = u), S_t = k]}{\partial u}, \quad k \in \{1, \dots, K\},
\end{equation}
as well as the stationary Average Treatment Effect (ATE):
\begin{equation}
    \theta_{\text{ATE}}^* = \sum_{k=1}^K \pi_k^* \theta_k^*.
\end{equation}

\begin{assumption}[Regime-Conditional Unconfoundedness]
\label{ass:unconfoundedness}
Conditional on observed covariates $X_t$ and latent regime $S_t$, potential outcomes are independent of treatment assignment:
\begin{equation}
    Y_t(u) \perp T_t \mid (X_t, S_t), \quad \forall u \in \mathrm{supp}(T_t).
\end{equation}
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Overlap \& Positivity]
\label{ass:overlap}
\label{ass:nonsingularity}
There exist constants $c_1, c_2 > 0$ such that for all $k \in \{1, \dots, K\}$ and almost everywhere on $\mathrm{supp}(X_t)$:
\begin{equation}
    c_1 \le \Var(T_t \mid X_t, S_t = k) \le c_2.
\end{equation}
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Structural Innovation]
\label{ass:innovation}
The outcome innovation $\varepsilon_t \equiv Y_t - \mathbb{E}[Y_t \mid \mathcal{F}_t]$, where $\mathcal{F}_t \equiv \sigma(X_t, T_t, Z_{1:N})$, satisfies $\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon_t \mid \mathcal{F}_t] = 0$ identically. Equivalently, $Y_t - \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \gamma_{tk}^* T_t - G(X_t, \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t^*)$ has conditional mean zero given $(X_t, T_t, Z_{1:N})$.
\textit{Discussion.} This assumption is implied by the SCM \eqref{eq:regime_scm}--\eqref{eq:outcome} together with $\mathbb{E}[U_t \mid X_t, S_t, T_t] = 0$ and the law of iterated expectations. It ensures the martingale innovation property underpinning the asymptotic normality proof (Theorem~\ref{thm:asymptotics}). Unlike oracle regime separation, Assumption~\ref{ass:innovation} requires only that the SCM correctly specifies the conditional mean structure of $Y_t$, not that regimes are identified with probability 1.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Nuisance Estimation and Mixing Rates]
\label{ass:rates}
\label{ass:mixing}
The data process $(Y_t, T_t, X_t, Z_t)$ is strictly stationary and geometrically $\alpha$-mixing with $\alpha(m) \le C_\alpha \exp(-c_\alpha m)$ for $C_\alpha, c_\alpha > 0$. Nuisance estimators satisfy $\|\hat{\mu}_Y - \mu_Y\|_{L_2(P)} \cdot \|\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_D - \boldsymbol{\mu}_D\|_{L_2(P)} = o_P(N^{-1/2})$, with individual convergence rates $o_P(N^{-1/4})$.
\end{assumption}

\section{FAILURE OF STANDARD DML: THE OMITTED REGIME BIAS}
\label{sec:bias}

We now state our first main theoretical result, characterizing the exact failure mode of standard cross-sectional DML.

\begin{theorem}[Decomposition of Omitted Regime Bias]
\label{thm:bias}
Suppose the data are generated according to SCM \eqref{eq:regime_scm}--\eqref{eq:outcome}. Let $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{naive}}$ be the standard cross-sectional DML estimator \citep{chernozhukov2018double} that conditions only on observed covariates $X_t$, omitting $S_t$. Define the naive cross-sectional residuals $\tilde{T}_t = T_t - \E[T_t \mid X_t]$ and $\tilde{Y}_t = Y_t - \E[Y_t \mid X_t]$. 

Then, as $N \to \infty$, $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{naive}}$ converges in probability to:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{naive}} \xrightarrow{p} \sum_{k=1}^K \omega_k \theta_k^* + \mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{heterogeneity}} + \mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{confounding}},
\end{equation}
where $\omega_k = \frac{\E[\one(S_t = k) \tilde{T}_t^2]}{\E[\tilde{T}_t^2]}$ with $\sum_k \omega_k = 1$, and the bias components are:
\begin{align}
    \mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{heterogeneity}} &= \frac{\sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \E\left[ \bar{m}(X_t) \Delta m_k(X_t) \pi_k(X_t) \right]}{\E[\tilde{T}_t^2]}, \label{eq:b_het} \\
    \mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{confounding}} &= \frac{\sum_{k=1}^K \E\left[ \pi_k(X_t) \Delta m_k(X_t) \Delta g_k(X_t) \right]}{\E[\tilde{T}_t^2]}, \label{eq:b_conf}
\end{align}
with $\pi_k(X_t) \equiv P(S_t = k \mid X_t)$, $\bar{m}(X_t) \equiv \E[T_t \mid X_t]$, $\Delta m_k(X_t) \equiv \E[T_t \mid X_t, S_t = k] - \bar{m}(X_t)$, and $\Delta g_k(X_t) \equiv g(X_t, k) - \E[g(X_t, S_t) \mid X_t]$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Under Neyman orthogonality and consistency of cross-fitted nuisance estimators on $X_t$, $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{naive}} \xrightarrow{p} \frac{\E[\tilde{T}_t \tilde{Y}_t]}{\E[\tilde{T}_t^2]}$. By SCM \eqref{eq:treatment}, $T_t = m(X_t, S_t) + V_t$ with $\E[V_t \mid X_t, S_t] = 0$. Thus $\tilde{T}_t = \sum_{k=1}^K \one(S_t=k) \Delta m_k(X_t) + V_t$. Similarly, $\bar{\ell}(X_t) \equiv \E[Y_t \mid X_t] = \sum_{k=1}^K \pi_k(X_t) [\theta_k^* m_k(X_t) + g_k(X_t)]$. Substituting $Y_t$ from \eqref{eq:outcome} and writing $T_t = \tilde{T}_t + \bar{m}(X_t)$, the outcome residual decomposes into:
\begin{align*}
    \tilde{Y}_t ={}& \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \one(S_t=k) \tilde{T}_t + \Delta g(X_t, S_t) + U_t \\
    & + \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \left[\one(S_t=k)\bar{m}(X_t) - \pi_k(X_t)m_k(X_t)\right].
\end{align*}
Multiplying by $\tilde{T}_t$ and taking expectations conditional on $(X_t, S_t)$:
First, $\E[\tilde{T}_t U_t] = 0$ by Assumption~\ref{ass:unconfoundedness}.
Second, $\E\left[ \tilde{T}_t \sum_k \theta_k^* \one(S_t=k)\tilde{T}_t \right] = \sum_k \theta_k^* \E[\one(S_t=k)\tilde{T}_t^2]$.
Third, since $\E[\tilde{T}_t \one(S_t=k) \mid X_t] = \Delta m_k(X_t)\pi_k(X_t)$ and $\E[\tilde{T}_t \mid X_t] = 0$, the second term yields $\sum_k \theta_k^* \bar{m}(X_t)\Delta m_k(X_t)\pi_k(X_t)$.
Fourth, $\E[\tilde{T}_t \Delta g(X_t, S_t) \mid X_t] = \sum_k \pi_k(X_t)\Delta m_k(X_t)\Delta g_k(X_t)$. Dividing by $\E[\tilde{T}_t^2]$ yields \eqref{eq:b_het} and \eqref{eq:b_conf}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Sign Inversion \& Bias Amplification]
When latent stagnation induces strong positive confounding ($\Delta m_k(X_t) \Delta g_k(X_t) \gg 0$), but residual treatment variance $\E[\tilde{T}_t^2]$ is attenuated due to conditioning on $X_t$, the omitted confounding bias $\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{confounding}}$ undergoes severe \emph{bias amplification}. In coastal airsheds with negative covariance phases, $\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{confounding}} < -\sum_k \omega_k \theta_k^*$, driving $\mathrm{plim}(\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{naive}}) < 0$ despite $\theta_k^* > 0$.
\end{corollary}

\section{METHODOLOGY: OVERLAP-AWARE REGIME DML}
\label{sec:method}

To overcome the failure of standard cross-sectional DML, we introduce \textbf{Overlap-Aware Regime Double Machine Learning (OR-DML)}.

\subsection{Latent Regime Formulation \& Observability Diagnostics}
Let $Z_t \in \R^q$ denote exogenous meteorological state trajectories (strictly pre-treatment, avoiding outcome contamination). We model the latent atmospheric state $S_t \in \{1, \dots, K\}$ via a Hidden Markov Model with transition matrix $\boldsymbol{\Pi}$ and emission parameters $\boldsymbol{\phi}$.
Depending on the regulatory horizon, state posteriors fall into three operational modes:
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{-2pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
    \item \textbf{Retrospective Smoothed Posteriors (Offline):} $\gamma_{tk}^{\mathrm{smooth}} \equiv P(S_t = k \mid Z_{1:N})$ for retrospective environmental epidemiology and structural causal auditing;
    \item \textbf{Training-Fitted Forward Filtered Posteriors:} $\gamma_{tk}^{\mathrm{filter}} \equiv P(S_t = k \mid Z_{1:t}, \hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}})$ where global transition/emission dynamics $\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}$ are calibrated offline, but sequential state updates proceed strictly causally on past-to-present observations $Z_{1:t}$; or
    \item \textbf{Expanding-Window Online Filter:} where both parameter estimation and forward filtering update causally without access to future measurements.
\end{enumerate}
Our empirical and benchmark implementations evaluate Mode 1 (offline smoothing) and Mode 2 (training-fitted forward filtering). We define the mean posterior entropy as a direct diagnostic of latent state observability:
\begin{equation}
    \bar{H}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}) \equiv -\frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N \sum_{k=1}^K \gamma_{tk} \ln(\gamma_{tk} + \epsilon).
\end{equation}
\emph{A Priori Diagnostics:} Standardized separation $\Delta_Z < 0.5$ or entropy $\bar{H} \to \ln K$ signals non-identification (Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility}; rules in Appendix~\ref{app:proxy_diagnostics}).

\subsection{State-Weighted Coupled System \& Spectral Regularization}
To avoid heuristic decoupled regressions that suffer cross-talk bias under regime overlap, OR-DML estimates regime-specific conditional expectations via state-weighted regressions targeting population expectations $m_k^\gamma(x) \equiv \frac{\E[\gamma_{tk} T_t \mid X_t=x]}{\E[\gamma_{tk} \mid X_t=x]}$ and $\mu_k^\gamma(x) \equiv \frac{\E[\gamma_{tk} Y_t \mid X_t=x]}{\E[\gamma_{tk} \mid X_t=x]}$:
\begin{align}
    \hat{m}_k(X) &= \arg\min_{m} \sum_{t \in \mathcal{T}_b} \gamma_{tk} (T_t - m(X_t))^2, \label{eq:nuisance_m} \\
    \hat{\mu}_k(X) &= \arg\min_{\mu} \sum_{t \in \mathcal{T}_b} \gamma_{tk} (Y_t - \mu(X_t))^2. \label{eq:nuisance_mu}
\end{align}
On evaluation fold $\mathcal{B}_b$, we form centered residuals $\tilde{T}_{tk} = T_t - \hat{m}_k(X_t)$ and $\tilde{Y}_{tk} = Y_t - \hat{\mu}_k(X_t)$. The empirical coupled Gram matrix $\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} \in \R^{K \times K}$ and score vector $\hat{\boldsymbol{S}} \in \R^K$ are constructed as:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{J}_{jk} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N \gamma_{tj}\gamma_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tj} \tilde{T}_{tk}, \quad \hat{S}_k = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N \gamma_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tk} \tilde{Y}_{tk}.
\end{equation}
When regime separation is weak or treatments are collinear, $\lambda_{\min}(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}) \to 0$, rendering the unregularized solve $\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}^{-1}\hat{\boldsymbol{S}}$ ill-conditioned. OR-DML introduces the \textbf{Spectrally Regularized Estimator}:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:theta_spectral}
    \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda = (\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \hat{\boldsymbol{S}},
\end{equation}
where $\lambda \ge 0$ trades off regularization bias against collinearity variance.
\emph{Scalability \& Non-Identifiability:} Inverting $\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}$ is $\mathcal{O}(K^3)$ with sample complexity $N = \tilde{\Omega}\left(\frac{K \log(K/\delta)}{\min_k (\pi_k^*)^2 \lambda_{\min}^2 \rho_{\mathrm{gap}}}\right)$ under $\alpha$-mixing (Appendix~\ref{app:state_space_scalability}); when $\kappa(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}) > 10^4$ or $\text{CI}(\lambda_{\min})$ covers 0, systems should diagnose non-identifiability and abstain.
We distinguish three causal estimand targets: (i) the true stationary population ATE $\theta_{\mathrm{PATE}}^* = \boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top} \boldsymbol{\theta}^*$, (ii) the estimated stationary ATE $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{PATE}} = \hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}})^\top \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda$, and (iii) the empirical sample occupation ATE $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SATE}} = \bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_N^\top \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda$, where $\bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_N \equiv \frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t$.

\subsection{Purged Block Cross-Fitting with Bartlett Embargo}
To ensure validity under temporal dependence, we partition observations into $B$ contiguous blocks. To eliminate cross-fold leakage under $\alpha$-mixing, each test block $\mathcal{B}_b$ is surrounded by an embargo buffer of width $\tau^*$ (Algorithm~\ref{alg:purged_cv}). We determine $\tau^*$ from the data via the Bartlett significance threshold:
\begin{equation}
    \tau^* = \max \left\{ h \ge 1 : \max_{j} \left| \hat{\rho}_j(h) \right| \ge \frac{1.96}{\sqrt{N}} \right\} + 1.
\end{equation}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Overlap-Aware Regime DML (OR-DML)}
\label{alg:purged_cv}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Series $\{Y_t, T_t, X_t, Z_t\}_{t=1}^N$, blocks $B$, embargo $\tau^*$, spectral penalty $\lambda \ge 0$.
\STATE Infer posteriors $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t = P(S_t \mid Z_{1:N})$ (or causal forward filter $P(S_t \mid Z_{1:t})$).
\FOR{block $b = 1$ to $B$}
    \STATE Set test block $\mathcal{B}_b$; training set $\mathcal{T}_b = \{1..N\} \setminus (\mathcal{B}_b \pm \tau^*)$.
    \FOR{regime $k = 1$ to $K$}
        \STATE Fit state-weighted nuisances $\hat{m}_k^{(-b)}(X)$ and $\hat{\mu}_k^{(-b)}(X)$ on $\mathcal{T}_b$ with weights $\gamma_{tk}$.
        \STATE On $\mathcal{B}_b$, form residuals $\tilde{T}_{tk} = T_t - \hat{m}_k(X_t)$ and $\tilde{Y}_{tk} = Y_t - \hat{\mu}_k(X_t)$.
    \ENDFOR
\ENDFOR
\STATE Compute Gram $\hat{J}_{jk} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_t \gamma_{tj}\gamma_{tk}\tilde{T}_{tj}\tilde{T}_{tk}$ and score $\hat{S}_k = \frac{1}{N}\sum_t \gamma_{tk}\tilde{T}_{tk}\tilde{Y}_{tk}$.
\STATE Compute $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda = (\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1}\hat{\boldsymbol{S}}$ and $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SATE}} = \bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}^\top \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda$, $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{PATE}} = \hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}}^\top \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda$.
\RETURN Estimates $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda, \hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SATE}}, \hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{PATE}}$ and diagnostics $(\lambda_{\min}, \kappa, \bar{H})$.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{THEORETICAL ANALYSIS}
\label{sec:theory}

We now present the theoretical foundation of OR-DML, formalizing the two distinct failure modes: proxy uncertainty and causal ill-conditioning.

\begin{theorem}[Non-Identification Under Unconstrained Overlap]
\label{thm:impossibility}
Suppose the latent state is unobserved, and auxiliary proxies $Z_t$ satisfy $P(Z_t \mid S_t = j) = P(Z_t \mid S_t = k)$ for all $j, k$. Then the regime-specific causal effect vector $\boldsymbol{\theta}^* = (\theta_1^*, \dots, \theta_K^*)^\top$ is not point-identified from the joint distribution of observables $(Y_t, T_t, X_t, Z_t)$: there exist structural causal models $P_1$ and $P_2$ satisfying Assumptions~\ref{ass:unconfoundedness}--\ref{ass:innovation} with identical joint observable laws $P_1(Y_{1:N}, T_{1:N}, X_{1:N}, Z_{1:N}) \equiv P_2(Y_{1:N}, T_{1:N}, X_{1:N}, Z_{1:N})$ for all $N \ge 1$, yet possessing strictly distinct, non-permutation causal effect vectors $\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(1)} \neq \boldsymbol{\theta}^{(2)}$.
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility} formally proves via an explicit constructive counterexample (Appendix~\ref{app:proof_thm1}) that predictive state inference alone cannot guarantee causal identification without explicit separation or proxy error bounds.

\begin{theorem}[Graceful Degradation Error Bound]
\label{thm:graceful}
Let $\boldsymbol{H}_t = \boldsymbol{e}_{S_t} \in \{0, 1\}^K$ denote the unobserved true regime indicator, and let $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t \in \Delta^K$ be an estimated posterior state proxy with average $L_1$ proxy error $\varepsilon_\gamma \equiv \E\|\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t - \boldsymbol{H}_t\|_1$. Suppose $\|\boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2 \le M_\theta < \infty$, $\sup_t |\tilde{T}_{tk}| \le M_T < \infty$ almost surely, and regime functions satisfy $\max_{j, k}|g(x, j) - g(x, k)| \le L_g < \infty$ and $\max_{j, k}|m(x, j) - m(x, k)| \le L_m < \infty$.
If $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) > 0$, the unregularized coupled estimator $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\gamma = \hat{\boldsymbol{J}}^{-1}\hat{\boldsymbol{S}}$ constructed from state-weighted nuisances satisfies:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:graceful_bound}
    \|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\gamma - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2 \le \frac{C \cdot \varepsilon_\gamma}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})} + \mathcal{O}_P(N^{-1/2}),
\end{equation}
where $C = L_g M_T + M_\theta M_T^2 + M_\theta M_T L_m < \infty$.
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:graceful} replaces heuristic orthogonality claims with a rigorous bound connecting \emph{posterior proxy error} $\varepsilon_\gamma$ and \emph{Jacobian conditioning} $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})$. While the score is Neyman-orthogonal to continuous nuisances $(\mu_k, m_k)$, it is \emph{not} orthogonal to $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t$; latent proxy uncertainty carries an irremediable statistical price.

\paragraph{The Difficulty Frontier and Operational Surrogate Bridge.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:graceful} demonstrates that the leading asymptotic bias scales with the theoretical ratio:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:difficulty_ratio}
    \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{causal}} \equiv \frac{\varepsilon_\gamma}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})},
\end{equation}
where $\varepsilon_\gamma \equiv \E\|\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t - \boldsymbol{H}_t\|_1$ is the population posterior state error, estimated in finite samples by $\hat{\varepsilon}_{\gamma, N} \equiv \frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N \|\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t - \boldsymbol{H}_t\|_1$. Because true states are unobserved at runtime, deployed systems cannot compute $\varepsilon_\gamma$ or $\hat{\varepsilon}_{\gamma, N}$ directly. We bridge unobservable causal difficulty to the computable operational index $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{operational}}(t) \equiv H(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t) / \lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}_t)$ via the following result under spectral comparability:
\begin{assumption}[Two-Sided Spectral Comparability]
\label{ass:spectral_comparability}
There exist constants $0 < \underline{c}_J \le \overline{c}_J < \infty$ such that rolling Gram matrices $\boldsymbol{J}_t$ satisfy $\underline{c}_J \le \frac{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}_t)}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})} \le \overline{c}_J$ almost surely for all evaluation windows $t \ge w$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{proposition}[Surrogate Entropy-Difficulty Bridge]
\label{prop:bridge}
For causal forward-filtered posteriors $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t = P(S_t \in \cdot \mid \mathcal{F}_t)$ with filtration $\mathcal{F}_t = \sigma(Z_{1:t})$, the posterior error satisfies $\E[\|\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t - \boldsymbol{H}_t\|_1 \mid \mathcal{F}_t] = 2 \operatorname{Gini}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t) \le C_K H(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t)$, where $C_K \le 1/\ln 2 \approx 1.4427$ for $K=2$.
We define the difficulty hierarchy:
\begin{align}
    \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{causal}} &\equiv \frac{\varepsilon_\gamma}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})}, \quad \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{global}}^H(t) \equiv \frac{H(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t)}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})}, \\
    \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{operational}}(t) &\equiv \frac{H(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t)}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}_t)}.
\end{align}
(i) Causal difficulty is unconditionally bounded in population by global entropy: $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{causal}} \le C_K \E\left[ \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{global}}^H(t) \right]$.
(ii) Under Assumption~\ref{ass:spectral_comparability}, the deployable index satisfies:
\begin{align}
\label{eq:bridge_bound}
    \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{causal}} &\le C_K \overline{c}_J \E[\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{operational}}(t)], \\
    \E[\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{operational}}(t)] &\le \underline{c}_J^{-1} \E[\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{global}}^H(t)].
\end{align}
The empirical sample surrogate $\hat{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathrm{causal}, N} \equiv \hat{\varepsilon}_{\gamma, N} / \lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})$ converges to $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{causal}}$ with $\mathcal{O}_P(N^{-1/2})$ error.
\end{proposition}
Proposition~\ref{prop:bridge} (proof in Appendix~\ref{app:proof_bridge}) mathematically grounds the deployable monitoring engine across three levels of observability: unobservable causal difficulty $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{causal}} \to$ observable operational surrogate $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{operational}}(t) \to$ realized downstream loss $L_{t+h}$. We emphasize that \eqref{eq:bridge_bound} establishes $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{operational}}(t)$ as an observable surrogate upper bound for $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{causal}}$, not a two-sided equivalence. The entropy bridge holds algebraically for retrospective smoothed posteriors $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t = P(S_t \in \cdot \mid Z_{1:N})$ as well, though with retrospective rather than prospective interpretation.

\begin{theorem}[Leading-Order Risk Bound under Long-Run Covariance Approximation]
\label{thm:spectral}
Let $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda = (\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1}\hat{\boldsymbol{S}}$ be the spectrally regularized OR-DML estimator, and let $\boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda = (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1}\boldsymbol{S}$. Under the conditions of Theorem~\ref{thm:graceful}, the regularized estimand satisfies the exact deterministic identity:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:reg_identity}
    \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^* = (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \left( \boldsymbol{b}_\gamma - \lambda \boldsymbol{\theta}^* \right),
\end{equation}
where $\|\boldsymbol{b}_\gamma\|_2 \le C \varepsilon_\gamma$. Furthermore, under the long-run score covariance approximation $\boldsymbol{\Omega} = \lim_{N \to \infty} \Var(N^{-1/2}\sum_t \boldsymbol{\Psi}_t)$, the expected squared estimation error satisfies the leading-order risk bound:
\begin{equation}
    \E\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2^2 \le \frac{(C \varepsilon_\gamma + \lambda M_\theta)^2 + N^{-1}\mathrm{Tr}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}{(\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) + \lambda)^2} + \mathcal{O}(N^{-1}),
\end{equation}
which accounts for the leading-order stochastic expansion $\E\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda\|_2^2 = \frac{1}{N}\mathrm{Tr}((\boldsymbol{J}+\lambda\boldsymbol{I})^{-1}\boldsymbol{\Omega}(\boldsymbol{J}+\lambda\boldsymbol{I})^{-1}) + o(N^{-1})$ and deterministic shrinkage bias (Appendix~\ref{app:proof_thm3}), sharpening to $\mathcal{O}(N^{-3/2})$ under the local parameter regime $\varepsilon_\gamma + \lambda = \mathcal{O}(N^{-1/2})$. When overlap is ill-conditioned ($\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) \approx 0$), choosing $\lambda > 0$ reduces the variance upper bound monotonically in $\lambda$.
Let $R_{\mathrm{bound}}(\lambda) \equiv \frac{(C \varepsilon_\gamma + \lambda M_\theta)^2 + N^{-1}\mathrm{Tr}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}{(\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) + \lambda)^2}$ denote the theoretical risk bound, with minimizer $\lambda^*_{\mathrm{bound}} \equiv \arg\min_{\lambda \ge 0} R_{\mathrm{bound}}(\lambda)$. In practice, we evaluate the empirical plug-in surrogate $\hat{R}(\lambda) \equiv \left(\frac{C \hat{\varepsilon}_\gamma + \lambda \|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_0\|_2}{\lambda_{\min}(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}) + \lambda}\right)^2 + \frac{\mathrm{Tr}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}})}{N (\lambda_{\min}(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}) + \lambda)^2}$, yielding the data-driven adaptive parameter $\hat{\lambda}^*_{\mathrm{emp}} = \arg\min_{\lambda \ge 0} \hat{R}(\lambda)$ (Appendix~\ref{app:adaptive_lambda_derivation}). In our ill-conditioned benchmark ($\lambda_{\min} \approx 0.033$), empirical minimum MSE occurs near $\hat{\lambda}^*_{\mathrm{empirical}} \approx 0.20$ ($\text{MSE} = 0.0321$, a $25.9\times$ empirical risk reduction in the benchmark DGP from $\lambda=0.0$), while $\lambda = 0.10$ achieves an approximately $23.0\times$ empirical reduction ($\text{MSE} = 0.0362$ vs.\ $0.8312$) with peak coverage ($85.0\%$). We distinguish $\lambda^*_{\mathrm{bound}}$ and $\hat{\lambda}^*_{\mathrm{emp}}$ from the true infeasible risk minimizer $\lambda^*_{\mathrm{true}} = \arg\min_\lambda \E\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2^2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Asymptotic Normality Under Dependent Purged Cross-Fitting]
\label{thm:asymptotics}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:unconfoundedness}--\ref{ass:rates}, with embargo buffer $\tau^* \ge C \log N$, the regularized OR-DML estimator under retrospective smoothing or training-fitted forward filtering satisfies:
\begin{equation}
    \sqrt{N}\left( \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^* - \mathrm{Bias}(\lambda) \right) \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}\left(\mathbf{0}, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\lambda\right),
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\lambda = (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \boldsymbol{\Omega} (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1}$. When $\lambda = o(N^{-1/2})$, $\sqrt{N}\mathrm{Bias}(\lambda) \to \mathbf{0}$, recovering centered asymptotic normality.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[Joint Asymptotic Distribution for Sample and Population ATE]
\label{prop:variance}
The sample ATE estimator $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SATE}} = \bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}^\top \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda$ has conditional asymptotic variance $\sigma_{\mathrm{SATE}}^2 = \bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}^\top \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\lambda \bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}$. The population ATE estimator $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{PATE}} = \hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}}^\top \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda$, where $\hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}}$ is the estimated stationary distribution of the Markov transition matrix $\hat{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}$, satisfies the joint delta expansion:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \sqrt{N}\left(\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{PATE}} - \theta_{\mathrm{PATE}}^*\right) &= \boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top} \sqrt{N}\left(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\right) \\
    &\quad + \boldsymbol{\theta}^{*\top} \sqrt{N}\left(\hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}} - \boldsymbol{\pi}^*\right) + o_P(1),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
yielding asymptotic variance:
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_{\mathrm{PATE}}^2 = \boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\lambda \boldsymbol{\pi}^* + \boldsymbol{\theta}^{*\top} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\pi \boldsymbol{\theta}^* + 2 \boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\theta \pi} \boldsymbol{\theta}^*,
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\pi = \mathbf{D}_\pi \mathbf{Z} + \mathbf{Z}^\top \mathbf{D}_\pi - \mathbf{D}_\pi - \boldsymbol{\pi}^* \boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top}$ with fundamental matrix $\mathbf{Z} = (\mathbf{I} - \boldsymbol{\Pi} + \mathbf{1}\boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top})^{-1}$, and $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\theta \pi} \equiv \lim_{N \to \infty} \Cov(\sqrt{N}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*), \sqrt{N}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}} - \boldsymbol{\pi}^*))$. The cross-covariance vanishes when the DML score influence is orthogonal to the HMM score direction: $\E[\boldsymbol{\phi}_\theta(W_t) \boldsymbol{s}_\Lambda(W_t)^\top] = \mathbf{0}$; in general, $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\theta \pi}$ is estimated via the joint HAC sandwich estimator. Under stagnation ($\rho \approx 0.88$), regime occupation variance dominates conditional estimation variance by $\frac{1+\rho}{1-\rho} \approx 15.7\times$.
\end{proposition}

\section{THE LATENT-CONFOUNDING DIFFICULTY FRONTIER}
\label{sec:experiments}

To test the theoretical predictions of Theorems~\ref{thm:impossibility}--\ref{thm:spectral}, we conduct a comprehensive Monte Carlo evaluation across 500 replications, charting the causal estimation error across the \textbf{Latent-Confounding Difficulty Frontier}.

\subsection{Experimental Design}
We simulate dynamic atmospheric time series ($N=1,200$) with a two-state Markov chain $S_t \in \{0, 1\}$ (persistence $\rho = 0.88$). Observed meteorological controls $X_t \in \R^3$ follow an autoregressive AR(1) process ($\rho=0.65$). Treatment assignment is confounded by both $X_t$ and $S_t$: $T_t = 2.0(1 - S_t) + 6.0 S_t + 0.8 X_{t1} - 0.5 X_{t2} + V_t$. Regime treatment effects are $\theta_0^* = 0.75$ and $\theta_1^* = 2.50$, yielding a true population ATE $\theta_{\mathrm{ATE}}^* = 1.5278$.

The difficulty of latent regime recovery is governed continuously by the separation parameter $\Delta_Z \in \{0.2, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0, 4.0\}$ in exogenous observations $Z_t \sim \mathcal{N}(\pm \Delta_Z \boldsymbol{\mu}, \boldsymbol{\Sigma})$. At $\Delta_Z = 0.2$, state overlap is severe and regimes are near-unidentified ($\bar{H}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}) = 0.434$); at $\Delta_Z = 4.0$, regimes are cleanly separated ($\bar{H}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}) = 0.014$). We evaluate 100 independent Monte Carlo draws per grid point (500 total).

\subsection{Difficulty Frontier Findings}
Table~\ref{tab:benchmark} and Figure~\ref{fig:difficulty_frontier} reveal three key statistical phenomena:
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{-2.5pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
    \item \textbf{Persistence of Naive Confounding:} In our benchmark DGP, Standard and Block DML exhibit approximately constant omitted-regime bias ($+8.44$, RMSE $8.45$) and $0.0\%$ coverage across the entire $\Delta_Z$ grid, illustrating Theorem~\ref{thm:bias}. Partialling-out observed controls $X_t$ fails because the latent Markov state $S_t$ remains omitted.
    \item \textbf{Continuous Graceful Degradation:} In accordance with Theorem~\ref{thm:graceful}, OR-DML exhibits continuous graceful degradation. At $\Delta_Z = 0.2$, posterior overlap is high ($\bar{H} = 0.434$), confirming Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility}'s non-identification. As $\Delta_Z$ increases to $1.0$, $2.0$, and $4.0$, absolute bias shrinks by $98.1\%$ (from $8.59 \to 4.25 \to 0.50 \to 0.16$), and coverage rises to $93.0\%$ (with average 95\% CI width narrowing from $1.323$ at $\Delta_Z=1.0$ to $0.287$ at $\Delta_Z=4.0$).
    \item \textbf{Filtering vs.\ Smoothing:} Causal forward filtering ($\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t^{\mathrm{filter}}$) tracks retrospective smoothing ($\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t^{\mathrm{smooth}}$) closely across all separated regimes ($\Delta_Z \ge 2.0$, bias $0.2233$ vs $0.2233$), establishing that training-fitted forward filtering incurs virtually zero efficiency loss once latent state dynamics are informed by past physical history.
\end{enumerate}

\section{CONCLUSION}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We investigated how latent-context uncertainty interacts with task geometry to govern sequential ML reliability. Reliable sequential systems must assess latent uncertainty jointly with downstream task geometry, rather than treating state confidence alone as a proxy for trust. OR-DML provides a tractable causal realization of this principle, with experiments confirming its broader validity across sequential learning tasks.

\section*{MATHEMATICAL APPENDIX: COMPLETE PROOFS AND DERIVATIONS}

\section{Proof of Theorem 1: Non-Identification Under Unconstrained Overlap}
\label{app:proof_thm1}
To prove substantive causal non-identification, we constructively exhibit two data-generating distributions, $P_1$ and $P_2$, that are structurally well-formed under the SCM \eqref{eq:regime_scm}--\eqref{eq:outcome} and satisfy all Assumptions~\ref{ass:unconfoundedness}--\ref{ass:innovation}, yet possess strictly distinct, non-permutation causal effect vectors $\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(1)} \neq \boldsymbol{\theta}^{(2)}$ while inducing identically equal joint distributions over all observable variables:
\begin{equation}
    P_1(Y_{1:N}, T_{1:N}, X_{1:N}, Z_{1:N}) \equiv P_2(Y_{1:N}, T_{1:N}, X_{1:N}, Z_{1:N}) \quad \forall N \ge 1.
\end{equation}

\textbf{Construction of Substantive Observational Equivalence:}
Consider a two-state Markov chain ($K=2$) with symmetric transition probability $P_{01} = P_{10} = p \in (0, 0.5)$, yielding stationary distribution $\pi_0^* = \pi_1^* = 0.5$.
Let auxiliary meteorological proxies $Z_t \in \R$ satisfy complete overlap: $Z_t \mid S_t = 0 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$ and $Z_t \mid S_t = 1 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$ identically, so $Z_t \perp S_t$.
Let continuous observed confounders $X_t \in \R$ have marginal density $f_X(x)$ independent of $S_t$.
Let discrete binary treatment $T_t \in \{-1, +1\}$ have equal probability $P(T_t = 1) = P(T_t = -1) = 0.5$, distributed completely independently of $(S_t, X_t, Z_t)$, which explicitly guarantees $T_t \perp S_t \mid X_t$ and $T_t \perp (S_t, Z_t, X_t)$. Thus, Assumption~\ref{ass:unconfoundedness} (Conditional Unconfoundedness) holds trivially with constant propensity score $e(X_t, S_t) \equiv 0.5 \in [\eta, 1-\eta]$ satisfying full overlap everywhere. Baseline treatment assignment satisfies $m(X_t, S_t) \equiv 0$ and $V_t = T_t$ with $\E[V_t \mid X_t, S_t] = 0$.

Now specify two distinct structural outcome models:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Model $P_1$ (Homogeneous Causal Effect):}
    Set $\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(1)} = (\theta_0^{(1)}, \theta_1^{(1)})^\top = (1, 1)^\top$ (so the treatment effect is identical across regimes, $\theta_0^{(1)} - \theta_1^{(1)} = 0$), baseline function $g^{(1)}(X_t, S_t) \equiv 0$, and structural outcome innovation $U_t^{(1)} \in \{-1, +1\}$ with $P(U_t^{(1)} = 1) = P(U_t^{(1)} = -1) = 0.5$, distributed independently of $(T_t, X_t, S_t, Z_{1:N})$. Then $\E[U_t^{(1)} \mid X_t, S_t, T_t] = 0$.
    Under Model $P_1$, the outcome is $Y_t = 1 \cdot T_t + U_t^{(1)}$. The conditional distribution of $Y_t$ given $T_t$ evaluates to:
    \begin{align}
        P_1(Y_t = 2 \mid T_t = 1) &= 0.5, \quad P_1(Y_t = 0 \mid T_t = 1) = 0.5, \\
        P_1(Y_t = -2 \mid T_t = -1) &= 0.5, \quad P_1(Y_t = 0 \mid T_t = -1) = 0.5.
    \end{align}
    \item \textbf{Model $P_2$ (Heterogeneous Causal Effect):}
    Set $\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(2)} = (\theta_0^{(2)}, \theta_1^{(2)})^\top = (2, 0)^\top$ (so the treatment effect is strongly heterogeneous, $\theta_0^{(2)} - \theta_1^{(2)} = 2$), baseline function $g^{(2)}(X_t, S_t) \equiv 0$, and structural error $U_t^{(2)} \equiv 0$ almost surely (trivially satisfying $\E[U_t^{(2)} \mid X_t, S_t, T_t] = 0$).
    Under Model $P_2$, the outcome is $Y_t = \theta(S_t) T_t$:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item In Regime 0 ($S_t = 0$): $Y_t = 2 T_t$, so $Y_t = 2$ if $T_t = 1$ and $Y_t = -2$ if $T_t = -1$.
        \item In Regime 1 ($S_t = 1$): $Y_t = 0 \cdot T_t = 0$ for all $T_t \in \{-1, +1\}$.
    \end{itemize}
    Marginalizing over the unobserved state $S_t$ with prior $\pi_0^* = \pi_1^* = 0.5$:
    \begin{align}
        P_2(Y_t = 2 \mid T_t = 1) &= \pi_0^*(1) + \pi_1^*(0) = 0.5, \quad P_2(Y_t = 0 \mid T_t = 1) = \pi_0^*(0) + \pi_1^*(1) = 0.5, \\
        P_2(Y_t = -2 \mid T_t = -1) &= \pi_0^*(1) + \pi_1^*(0) = 0.5, \quad P_2(Y_t = 0 \mid T_t = -1) = \pi_0^*(0) + \pi_1^*(1) = 0.5.
    \end{align}
\end{enumerate}
Comparing the conditional distributions:
\begin{equation}
    P_1(Y_t \mid T_t, X_t, Z_t) \equiv P_2(Y_t \mid T_t, X_t, Z_t) \quad \forall (y, t, x, z) \in \mathrm{supp}(Y, T, X, Z).
\end{equation}
Because the marginal distributions of $(T_t, X_t, Z_t)$ and the temporal Markov transition laws are identical in both models, the full joint likelihoods over all observables are strictly identical for any sample size $N$:
\begin{equation}
    P_1(Y_{1:N}, T_{1:N}, X_{1:N}, Z_{1:N}) \equiv P_2(Y_{1:N}, T_{1:N}, X_{1:N}, Z_{1:N}).
\end{equation}

Yet the structural causal effects satisfy:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\theta}^{(1)} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} \neq \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} = \boldsymbol{\theta}^{(2)}.
\end{equation}
Crucially, $\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(2)}$ is \emph{not} a permutation of $\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(1)}$. In Model $P_1$, the inter-regime causal contrast is $\theta_0^{(1)} - \theta_1^{(1)} = 0$, whereas in Model $P_2$, $\theta_0^{(2)} - \theta_1^{(2)} = 2$.
No estimation procedure conditioning solely on observable data $(Y, T, X, Z)$ can distinguish whether the data-generating mechanism is homogeneous ($P_1$) or heterogeneous ($P_2$) without informative proxies $Z_t$ that bound the proxy error $\varepsilon_\gamma$. This establishes substantive non-identification. $\blacksquare$

\section{Proof of Theorem 2: Graceful Degradation and the Difficulty Frontier}
\label{app:proof_thm2}
Let $\boldsymbol{H}_t = \boldsymbol{e}_{S_t} \in \{0, 1\}^K$ denote the unobserved true regime indicator vector.
Under the true SCM \eqref{eq:regime_scm}--\eqref{eq:outcome}, the oracle regime nuisance functions are:
\begin{equation}
    m_k^*(X_t) \equiv \E[T_t \mid X_t, S_t = k], \quad \mu_k^*(X_t) \equiv \E[Y_t \mid X_t, S_t = k] = \theta_k^* m_k^*(X_t) + g(X_t, k).
\end{equation}
The oracle centered residuals are $\tilde{T}_{tk}^* \equiv T_t - m_k^*(X_t)$ and $\tilde{Y}_{tk}^* \equiv Y_t - \mu_k^*(X_t) = \theta_k^* \tilde{T}_{tk}^* + U_t$.
Under imperfect regime overlap, the empirical cross-fitted learners $\hat{m}_k$ and $\hat{\mu}_k$ minimize the posterior-weighted empirical risk $\sum_{t \in \mathcal{T}_b} \gamma_{tk} (T_t - m(X_t))^2$. Their exact population targets are the proxy-weighted conditional expectations:
\begin{equation}
    m_k^\gamma(X_t) \equiv \frac{\E[\gamma_{tk} T_t \mid X_t]}{\E[\gamma_{tk} \mid X_t]}, \quad \mu_k^\gamma(X_t) \equiv \frac{\E[\gamma_{tk} Y_t \mid X_t]}{\E[\gamma_{tk} \mid X_t]}.
\end{equation}
The empirical coupled Gram matrix is $\hat{J}_{jk} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_t \gamma_{tj}\gamma_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tj} \tilde{T}_{tk}$ and the empirical score vector is $\hat{S}_k = \frac{1}{N}\sum_t \gamma_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tk} \tilde{Y}_{tk}$.
The corresponding population objects are $\boldsymbol{J} \in \R^{K \times K}$ with $J_{jk} \equiv \E[\gamma_{tj}\gamma_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tj}^* \tilde{T}_{tk}^*]$ and $\boldsymbol{S} \in \R^K$ with $S_k \equiv \E[\gamma_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tk}^* \tilde{Y}_{tk}^*]$.

\textbf{Neyman Orthogonality vs. Proxy Sensitivity:}
Consider the state-weighted score for regime $k$ as a functional of candidate nuisances $(m_k, \mu_k)$:
\begin{equation}
    \psi_k(W_t; \theta_k, m_k, \mu_k) = \gamma_{tk} (T_t - m_k(X_t)) \left( Y_t - \mu_k(X_t) \right).
\end{equation}
Evaluating the Gateaux derivative with respect to $m_k$ in direction $\Delta m$ at the oracle target $(m_k^*, \mu_k^*)$:
\begin{equation}
    \partial_m \E[\psi_k(W_t; \theta_k^*, m_k^*, \mu_k^*)][\Delta m] = -\E[\gamma_{tk} \Delta m(X_t) (Y_t - \mu_k^*(X_t))] = -\E[\gamma_{tk} \Delta m(X_t) U_t] = 0,
\end{equation}
since $\E[U_t \mid X_t, S_t, Z_{1:N}] = 0$ by Assumption~\ref{ass:unconfoundedness}.
Similarly, the Gateaux derivative with respect to $\mu_k$ in direction $\Delta \mu$ is:
\begin{equation}
    \partial_\mu \E[\psi_k(W_t; \theta_k^*, m_k^*, \mu_k^*)][\Delta \mu] = -\E[\gamma_{tk} (T_t - m_k^*(X_t)) \Delta \mu(X_t)] = -\E[\gamma_{tk} \E[\tilde{T}_{tk}^* \mid X_t, Z_{1:N}] \Delta \mu(X_t)] = 0.
\end{equation}
Furthermore, evaluating at the proxy population target $(m_k^\gamma, \mu_k^\gamma)$, $\E[\gamma_{tk} (T_t - m_k^\gamma(X_t)) \mid X_t] = 0$ holds identically by construction.
Therefore, Neyman orthogonality eliminates first-order sensitivity to errors in estimating $(m_k, \mu_k)$, ensuring that nuisance estimation errors contribute only a second-order term $\mathcal{O}_P(\|\hat{m}_k - m_k^\gamma\|_{L_2} \|\hat{\mu}_k - \mu_k^\gamma\|_{L_2}) = \mathcal{O}_P(N^{-1/2})$ under Assumption~\ref{ass:rates}.
However, the score $\psi_k$ depends linearly on the posterior state weight $\gamma_{tk}$. Because $\gamma_{tk}$ proxies the unobserved indicator $H_{tk} = \one(S_t = k)$, Neyman orthogonality with respect to nuisance regressions does \emph{not} eliminate proxy estimation error $\boldsymbol{H}_t - \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t$, which generates a first-order bias.

\textbf{Score Expansion and Bias Bound (Fix A):}
Expanding $Y_t = \sum_{j=1}^K H_{tj} [\theta_j^* T_t + g(X_t, j)] + U_t$:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{Y}_{tk}^* = Y_t - \mu_k^*(X_t) = \sum_{j=1}^K H_{tj} \theta_j^* \tilde{T}_{tj}^* + \sum_{j=1}^K H_{tj} [\theta_j^*(m_j^*(X_t) - m_k^*(X_t)) + g(X_t, j) - g(X_t, k)] + U_t.
\end{equation}
Substituting into the population score $S_k = \E[\gamma_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tk}^* \tilde{Y}_{tk}^*]$:
\begin{align}
    S_k &= \sum_{j=1}^K \theta_j^* \E[\gamma_{tk} H_{tj} \tilde{T}_{tk}^* \tilde{T}_{tj}^*] + \E\left[\gamma_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tk}^* \sum_{j=1}^K H_{tj} \left(\theta_j^*(m_j^*(X_t) - m_k^*(X_t)) + g(X_t, j) - g(X_t, k)\right)\right] \nonumber \\
    &= \sum_{j=1}^K \theta_j^* \E[\gamma_{tk} \gamma_{tj} \tilde{T}_{tk}^* \tilde{T}_{tj}^*] + \sum_{j=1}^K \theta_j^* \E[\gamma_{tk} (H_{tj} - \gamma_{tj}) \tilde{T}_{tk}^* \tilde{T}_{tj}^*] + \E[\gamma_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tk}^* \Delta g_{tk}] \nonumber \\
    &= (\boldsymbol{J}\boldsymbol{\theta}^*)_k + b_{\gamma, k},
\end{align}
where $b_{\gamma, k} \equiv \sum_{j=1}^K \theta_j^* \E[\gamma_{tk} (H_{tj} - \gamma_{tj}) \tilde{T}_{tk}^* \tilde{T}_{tj}^*] + \E[\gamma_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tk}^* \sum_{j=1}^K (H_{tj} - \gamma_{tj})(\theta_j^* \Delta m_{jk}(X_t) + \Delta g_{jk}(X_t))]$.
Under the bounded residual support condition of Theorem~\ref{thm:graceful} ($\sup_t |\tilde{T}_{tk}^*| \le M_T$ a.s., $\sup_{j,k,x} |\Delta m_{jk}(x)| \le L_m$, and $\sup_{j,k,x} |\Delta g_{jk}(x)| \le L_g$), we bound the expectation directly without requiring Hölder's inequality on unbounded fourth moments:
\begin{align}
    |b_{\gamma, k}| &\le \sum_{j=1}^K |\theta_j^*| \E\left[ \gamma_{tk} |\tilde{T}_{tk}^*| |\tilde{T}_{tj}^*| |H_{tj} - \gamma_{tj}| \right] + \sum_{j=1}^K \E\left[ \gamma_{tk} |\tilde{T}_{tk}^*| |H_{tj} - \gamma_{tj}| \left( |\theta_j^*| L_m + L_g \right) \right] \nonumber \\
    &\le M_\theta M_T^2 \E\left[ \sum_{j=1}^K |H_{tj} - \gamma_{tj}| \right] + M_T (M_\theta L_m + L_g) \E\left[ \sum_{j=1}^K |H_{tj} - \gamma_{tj}| \right] \nonumber \\
    &= \left( M_\theta M_T^2 + M_\theta M_T L_m + L_g M_T \right) \E\|\boldsymbol{H}_t - \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t\|_1 = C \cdot \varepsilon_\gamma,
\end{align}
where $C = L_g M_T + M_\theta M_T^2 + M_\theta M_T L_m < \infty$.
Since $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) > 0$, the unregularized estimand satisfies:
\begin{equation}
    \|\boldsymbol{\theta}_\gamma - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2 = \|\boldsymbol{J}^{-1}\boldsymbol{b}_\gamma\|_2 \le \|\boldsymbol{J}^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}} \|\boldsymbol{b}_\gamma\|_2 \le \frac{C \cdot \varepsilon_\gamma}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})}.
\end{equation}
Combining with purged cross-fitting nuisance estimation error of order $\mathcal{O}_P(N^{-1/2})$ yields $\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\gamma - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2 \le \frac{C \varepsilon_\gamma}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})} + \mathcal{O}_P(N^{-1/2})$, completing the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:graceful}. $\blacksquare$

\subsection{The Latent-Confounding Difficulty Frontier $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{causal}}$}
\label{app:difficulty_frontier}
Theorem~\ref{thm:graceful} demonstrates that the upper bound on structural causal estimation error decomposes into:
\begin{equation}
    \|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\gamma - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2 \le C \cdot \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{causal}} + \mathcal{O}_P(N^{-1/2}), \quad \text{where} \quad \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{causal}} \equiv \frac{\varepsilon_\gamma}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})}.
\end{equation}
We highlight several structural properties of the difficulty index $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{causal}}$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Dimensionless Product:} Both population proxy error $\varepsilon_\gamma$ and Gram conditioning $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})$ interact multiplicatively in governing downstream estimation risk. When either proxy recovery is poor ($\varepsilon_\gamma \to 1$) or overlap is weak ($\lambda_{\min} \to 0$), estimation risk escalates sharply.
    \item \textbf{Asymmetric Sensitivity:} In our factorial simulations (Table~\ref{tab:bench_results}), reducing $\lambda_{\min}$ by an order of magnitude produces an equivalent variance and bias inflation to a tenfold increase in state proxy error $\varepsilon_\gamma$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Proof of Proposition 4: Surrogate Entropy-Difficulty Bridge}
\label{app:proof_bridge}
Let $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t \equiv P(S_t \in \cdot \mid \mathcal{F}_t) \in \Delta^K$ be the Bayesian posterior state distribution with respect to filtration $\mathcal{F}_t \equiv \sigma(Z_{1:t})$. Under proper Bayesian calibration, conditional on $\mathcal{F}_t$, the true latent indicator $\boldsymbol{H}_t \equiv \boldsymbol{e}_{S_t}$ is distributed as a categorical random variable taking value $\boldsymbol{e}_k$ with probability $\gamma_{tk}$.

\textbf{Step 1: Expected $L_1$ Error as Gini Impurity.}
Evaluating the conditional expectation of the $L_1$ error:
\begin{align}
    \E\left[ \|\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t - \boldsymbol{H}_t\|_1 \;\middle|\; \mathcal{F}_t \right] &= \sum_{k=1}^K \gamma_{tk} \|\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t - \boldsymbol{e}_k\|_1 = \sum_{k=1}^K \gamma_{tk} \left( (1 - \gamma_{tk}) + \sum_{j \neq k} \gamma_{tj} \right) \nonumber \\
    &= \sum_{k=1}^K \gamma_{tk} \cdot 2(1 - \gamma_{tk}) = 2\left( 1 - \sum_{k=1}^K \gamma_{tk}^2 \right) = 2 \operatorname{Gini}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t).
\end{align}

\textbf{Step 2: Entropy Bound on Gini Impurity.}
By the standard concave inequality $\ln(x) \le x - 1$, for any $x > 0$:
\begin{equation}
    H(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t) = -\sum_{k=1}^K \gamma_{tk} \ln \gamma_{tk} = \sum_{k=1}^K \gamma_{tk} \ln\left(\frac{1}{\gamma_{tk}}\right) \ge \sum_{k=1}^K \gamma_{tk} (1 - \gamma_{tk}) = \operatorname{Gini}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t).
\end{equation}
Furthermore, for any binary regime partition ($K=2$) with $\gamma_{t1} = p$ and $\gamma_{t2} = 1-p$, $2 \operatorname{Gini}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t) = 4p(1-p)$. Comparing to binary Shannon entropy $H(p) = -p\ln p - (1-p)\ln(1-p)$, the ratio $\frac{4p(1-p)}{H(p)}$ achieves its maximum at $p = 1/2$, where $\frac{4(1/4)}{\ln 2} = \frac{1}{\ln 2} \approx 1.4427$. Hence for all $p \in [0, 1]$:
\begin{equation}
    2 \operatorname{Gini}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t) \le C_K H(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t), \quad \text{where } C_K \le \frac{1}{\ln 2} \approx 1.4427.
\end{equation}

\textbf{Step 3: Population and Observable Bridge.}
Taking the population expectation of Step 1 and applying Step 2:
\begin{equation}
    \varepsilon_\gamma \equiv \E\left[ \E\left[ \|\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t - \boldsymbol{H}_t\|_1 \;\middle|\; \mathcal{F}_t \right] \right] = 2 \E[\operatorname{Gini}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t)] \le C_K \E[H(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t)].
\end{equation}
Dividing by the global minimal singular value $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{causal}} \equiv \frac{\varepsilon_\gamma}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})} \le C_K \E\left[ \frac{H(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t)}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})} \right] = C_K \E[\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{global}}^H(t)].
\end{equation}
Furthermore, under Assumption~\ref{ass:spectral_comparability} (Two-Sided Spectral Comparability), the rolling Gram matrix satisfies $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}_t) \le \overline{c}_J \lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})$ almost surely for all evaluation windows $t \ge w$, which implies $\frac{1}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})} \le \frac{\overline{c}_J}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}_t)}$. Pointwise substitution yields:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{causal}} \le C_K \overline{c}_J \E\left[ \frac{H(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t)}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}_t)} \right] = C_K \overline{c}_J \E[\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{operational}}(t)],
\end{equation}
and $\E[\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{operational}}(t)] \le \underline{c}_J^{-1} \E[\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{global}}^H(t)]$. In finite samples, the ergodic theorem and central limit theorem for stationary $\alpha$-mixing chains ensure that $\hat{\varepsilon}_{\gamma, N} \equiv \frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N \|\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t - \boldsymbol{H}_t\|_1 = \varepsilon_\gamma + \mathcal{O}_P(N^{-1/2})$, so the sample surrogate $\hat{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathrm{causal}, N} \equiv \hat{\varepsilon}_{\gamma, N}/\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) = \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{causal}} + \mathcal{O}_P(N^{-1/2})$. We emphasize that these bounds establish $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{operational}}(t)$ as an observable surrogate upper bound for $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{causal}}$, not a two-sided equivalence. Across our multi-season ground-sensor streams, the empirical ratio $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}_t)/\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})$ has 95th percentile in $[1.06, 1.72]$ and maximum $\le 7.35$, empirically confirming that $\overline{c}_J$ is finite and bounded. $\blacksquare$

\subsection{A Priori Proxy Diagnostics and Separation Screening}
\label{app:proxy_diagnostics}
Before fitting OR-DML or downstream prediction heads, practitioners should assess proxy informativeness on auxiliary measurements $Z_t \in \R^q$. Let $\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{Z,k} \equiv \frac{1}{N_k}\sum_{t: \hat{S}_t=k} Z_t$ and $\hat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{Z,k}$ denote empirical cluster moments. We define the standardized separation index:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_Z \equiv \min_{j \neq k} \frac{\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{Z,j} - \hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{Z,k}\|_2}{\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}(\mathrm{Tr}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{Z,j}) + \mathrm{Tr}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{Z,k}))/q}}.
\end{equation}
When $\Delta_Z < 0.5$ (or silhouette score $< 0.15$), the overlap between regime emission densities $P(Z_t \mid S_t=j)$ and $P(Z_t \mid S_t=k)$ is severe, driving posterior entropy $\bar{H}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}) \to \ln K$. By Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility}, point identification fails identically in the limit $\Delta_Z \to 0$. Deployed pipelines should enforce an automated screening gate: if $\Delta_Z < 0.5$ or mean entropy exceeds $0.80 \ln K$, the system must flag non-identifiability and refuse to emit point causal effects without external structural constraints.

\section{Proof of Theorem 3: Spectral Regularization Bias-Variance Frontier}
\label{app:proof_thm3}
Let $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda = (\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \hat{\boldsymbol{S}}$, and define the population regularized estimand $\boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda \equiv (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \boldsymbol{S}$.
We decompose the estimation error around the regularized population estimand $\boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda$ via the exact algebraic identity:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:exact_decomp}
    \E\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2^2 = \|\boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2^2 + \E\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda\|_2^2 + 2 \langle \E[\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda], \; \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^* \rangle,
\end{equation}
which accounts explicitly for the cross term between stochastic estimation error and deterministic regularization bias.
For the deterministic bias, substituting $\boldsymbol{S} = \boldsymbol{J}\boldsymbol{\theta}^* + \boldsymbol{b}_\gamma$:
\begin{align}
    \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^* &= (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \left( \boldsymbol{S} - (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})\boldsymbol{\theta}^* \right) \nonumber \\
    &= (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \left( \boldsymbol{S} - \boldsymbol{J}\boldsymbol{\theta}^* - \lambda \boldsymbol{\theta}^* \right) \nonumber \\
    &= (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \left( \boldsymbol{b}_\gamma - \lambda \boldsymbol{\theta}^* \right),
\end{align}
which establishes the exact deterministic identity \eqref{eq:reg_identity}.
Since $\boldsymbol{J} \succeq 0$ with minimal eigenvalue $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) \ge 0$, the spectral operator norm is $\|(\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}} = \frac{1}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) + \lambda}$.
Applying the triangle inequality:
\begin{equation}
    \|\boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2 \le \|(\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}} \left( \|\boldsymbol{b}_\gamma\|_2 + \lambda \|\boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2 \right) \le \frac{C \varepsilon_\gamma + \lambda M_\theta}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) + \lambda}.
\end{equation}
For the stochastic variance, expanding $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda$:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda = (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \left( \hat{\boldsymbol{S}} - \boldsymbol{S} - (\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} - \boldsymbol{J})\boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda \right) + o_p(N^{-1/2}).
\end{equation}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:asymptotics}, the score empirical process satisfies $\Var\left( \frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{t=1}^N \boldsymbol{\Psi}_t \right) \to \boldsymbol{\Omega}$. Thus, under the long-run score covariance approximation, the stochastic variance contribution admits the leading-order expansion:
\begin{equation}
    \E\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda\|_2^2 = \frac{1}{N} \mathrm{Tr}\left( (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \boldsymbol{\Omega} (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \right) + o(N^{-1}) \le \frac{\mathrm{Tr}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}{N (\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) + \lambda)^2} + o(N^{-1}).
\end{equation}
To bound the cross term $2 \langle \E[\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda], \; \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^* \rangle$ in \eqref{eq:exact_decomp}: by Neyman orthogonality of the score function $\boldsymbol{\psi}$ and purged block cross-fitting with Bartlett embargo buffer $\tau^* \ge C \log N$, the finite-sample expectation of the stochastic error satisfies $\|\E[\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda]\|_2 \le C_1 N^{-1}$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:asymptotics}). Furthermore, the deterministic regularized bias satisfies $\|\boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2 \le \frac{C \varepsilon_\gamma + \lambda M_\theta}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) + \lambda}$. By Cauchy-Schwarz, the cross term is bounded by:
\begin{equation}
    \left| 2 \langle \E[\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda], \; \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^* \rangle \right| \le 2 \|\E[\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda]\|_2 \|\boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2 = \mathcal{O}(N^{-1})
\end{equation}
in general (since $\|\boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2 = \mathcal{O}(1)$ without vanishing-bias rate restrictions), which sharpens to $\mathcal{O}(N^{-3/2})$ under the local parameter regime $\varepsilon_\gamma + \lambda = \mathcal{O}(N^{-1/2})$.
Combining the deterministic squared bias bound with the asymptotic long-run score covariance variance bound establishes the leading-order risk bound:
\begin{equation}
    \E\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2^2 \le \left(\frac{C \varepsilon_\gamma + \lambda M_\theta}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) + \lambda}\right)^2 + \frac{\mathrm{Tr}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}{N (\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) + \lambda)^2} + \mathcal{O}(N^{-1}).
\end{equation}

\textbf{Risk Bound Minimizer vs. Empirical Grid Optimum:}
Differentiating the leading-order risk upper bound $R_{\mathrm{bound}}(\lambda) \equiv \left(\frac{C \varepsilon_\gamma + \lambda M_\theta}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) + \lambda}\right)^2 + \frac{\mathrm{Tr}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}{N (\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) + \lambda)^2}$ with respect to $\lambda \ge 0$, the bound-optimal shrinkage parameter $\lambda^*_{\mathrm{bound}} \equiv \arg\min_{\lambda \ge 0} R_{\mathrm{bound}}(\lambda)$ satisfies:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\partial}{\partial \lambda} \left[ \left(\frac{C \varepsilon_\gamma + \lambda M_\theta}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) + \lambda}\right)^2 + \frac{\mathrm{Tr}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}{N (\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) + \lambda)^2} \right] = 0.
\end{equation}
When the overlap Gram matrix is ill-conditioned ($\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) \approx 0$), the variance term $\frac{\mathrm{Tr}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}{N (\lambda_{\min} + \lambda)^2}$ dominates for small $\lambda$, while the deterministic shrinkage bias $(\frac{\lambda M_\theta}{\lambda_{\min} + \lambda})^2$ dominates for large $\lambda$. The interior minimizer $\lambda^*_{\mathrm{bound}} > 0$ balances variance reduction against shrinkage bias in the upper bound. We distinguish $\lambda^*_{\mathrm{bound}}$ and the empirical surrogate minimizer $\hat{\lambda}^*_{\mathrm{emp}} = \arg\min_\lambda \hat{R}(\lambda)$ from the true infeasible risk minimizer $\lambda^*_{\mathrm{true}} \equiv \arg\min_\lambda \E\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2^2$.
In our empirical simulation benchmark across 500 Monte Carlo replications (\texttt{reports/or\_dml\_regularization\_frontier.csv}), the discrete grid minimum occurs at $\hat{\lambda}^*_{\mathrm{empirical}} \approx 0.20$ ($\text{MSE} = 0.0321$, representing a $25.9\times$ empirical risk reduction in this benchmark DGP relative to the unregularized estimator $\lambda = 0.0$ where $\text{MSE} = 0.8312$), while $\lambda = 0.10$ achieves $\text{MSE} = 0.0362$ ($23.0\times$ empirical reduction) with peak coverage of $85.0\%$.
We emphasize that Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral} provides the functional upper-bound characterization of the bias-variance trade-off; the exact numerical reduction factors (23.0$\times$ and 25.9$\times$) are empirical simulation properties of the benchmark DGP rather than universal theoretical constants. $\blacksquare$

\subsection{Derivation of Adaptive Spectral Regularization Parameter and Oracle Bound}
\label{app:adaptive_lambda_derivation}
We consider the data-driven selection of the spectral shrinkage parameter $\lambda \ge 0$. While classical Tikhonov regularization selects shrinkage via prediction cross-validation, in OR-DML the target is the structural causal parameter vector $\boldsymbol{\theta}^*$, where prediction cross-validation conflates causal identification with prognostic predictive fit.
We distinguish two distinct regularization regimes:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Inferential Regularization ($\lambda = o(N^{-1/2})$):} Required by Theorem~\ref{thm:asymptotics} to guarantee asymptotic unbiasedness $\sqrt{N}\mathrm{Bias}(\lambda) \to \mathbf{0}$, ensuring centered Gaussian limit laws and valid nominal confidence interval coverage.
    \item \textbf{Finite-Sample Risk Minimization ($\lambda_{\mathrm{risk}} = \mathcal{O}(1)$):} Minimizes the finite-sample expected $\ell_2$ estimation risk $\E\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2^2$ under severe causal ill-conditioning ($\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) \approx 0$).
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Empirical Risk Surrogate and Convexity:}
Define the empirical plug-in risk surrogate $\hat{R}(\lambda)$ by substituting empirical estimators $(\hat{\varepsilon}_\gamma, \lambda_{\min}(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}), \hat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}, \|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_0\|_2)$ into the risk upper bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral}:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{R}(\lambda) = \frac{(C \hat{\varepsilon}_\gamma + \lambda \|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_0\|_2)^2 + N^{-1}\mathrm{Tr}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}})}{(\lambda_{\min}(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}) + \lambda)^2}.
\end{equation}
Let $u = \lambda_{\min}(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}) + \lambda \ge \lambda_{\min}(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}) > 0$. Rewriting $\lambda = u - \lambda_{\min}$, the numerator becomes:
\begin{equation}
    \left( C \hat{\varepsilon}_\gamma - \lambda_{\min}(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}) \|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_0\|_2 + u \|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_0\|_2 \right)^2 + N^{-1}\mathrm{Tr}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}) = (A + u B)^2 + V,
\end{equation}
where $A = C \hat{\varepsilon}_\gamma - \lambda_{\min}(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}) \|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_0\|_2$, $B = \|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_0\|_2$, and $V = N^{-1}\mathrm{Tr}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}})$.
Dividing by $u^2$:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{R}(u - \lambda_{\min}) = B^2 + \frac{2AB}{u} + \frac{A^2 + V}{u^2}.
\end{equation}
Differentiating with respect to $u$:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d \hat{R}}{du} = -\frac{2AB}{u^2} - \frac{2(A^2 + V)}{u^3} = -\frac{2}{u^3} \left[ AB u + (A^2 + V) \right].
\end{equation}
Setting the derivative to zero yields the unique critical point $u^* = \frac{A^2 + V}{|A| B}$ when $A < 0$. When $A \ge 0$ (i.e., $C \hat{\varepsilon}_\gamma \ge \lambda_{\min}(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}) \|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_0\|_2$), $\frac{d\hat{R}}{du} < 0$ on $(0, \infty)$, indicating that spectral shrinkage strictly decreases risk until bounded by the constraint $\lambda \ge 0$. Constraining $\lambda \ge 0$, the closed-form interior minimizer is:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\lambda}^* = \max\left( 0, \; \frac{N^{-1}\mathrm{Tr}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}) + (C \hat{\varepsilon}_\gamma)^2}{C \hat{\varepsilon}_\gamma \|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_0\|_2 + \lambda_{\min}(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}) \|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_0\|_2^2} - \lambda_{\min}(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}) \right).
\end{equation}
Because $R'(u) = -\frac{2}{u^3}[AB u + (A^2+V)]$ changes sign strictly from negative to positive at $u^*$ when $A < 0$, $u^*$ is the unique global minimizer on $(0, \infty)$. By uniform convergence of the empirical plug-in surrogate on compact parameter sets $\lambda \in [0, \bar{\lambda}]$, $\sup_{\lambda \in [0, \bar{\lambda}]} |\hat{R}(\lambda) - R_{\mathrm{surr}}(\lambda)| = \mathcal{O}_P(N^{-1/2})$. Applying M-estimation consistency to the unique minimum of $\hat{R}(\lambda)$ yields the adaptive risk-surrogate selection guarantee:
\begin{equation}
    R_{\mathrm{surr}}(\hat{\lambda}^*) \le \inf_{\lambda \ge 0} R_{\mathrm{surr}}(\lambda) + \mathcal{O}_P(N^{-1/2}),
\end{equation}
establishing that data-driven spectral shrinkage $\hat{\lambda}^*$ adaptively tracks the optimal surrogate risk trade-off without prior knowledge of the true latent proxy error. $\blacksquare$

\subsection{State Space Scalability and Matrix Concentration for $K > 2$}
\label{app:state_space_scalability}
While our primary experiments study binary regimes ($K=2$), the OR-DML coupled estimator generalizes directly to arbitrary finite state dimensions $K \ge 2$. Here we formalize the computational and statistical scalability of the coupled Gram matrix $\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} \in \R^{K \times K}$.

\textbf{Computational Complexity:}
Constructing $\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}$ and score $\hat{\boldsymbol{S}}$ requires evaluating $K$ nuisance pairs across $B$ folds, taking $\mathcal{O}(B K \cdot \mathrm{Cost}(\text{Nuisance}))$ operations. Forming $\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}$ takes $\mathcal{O}(N K^2)$ operations, and solving $(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1}\hat{\boldsymbol{S}}$ via Cholesky factorization takes $\mathcal{O}(K^3)$ operations. For moderate regime counts ($K \le 20$), the linear solve takes $<1$ ms and is computationally negligible compared to nuisance estimation.

\textbf{Statistical Sample Complexity under $\alpha$-Mixing:}
The statistical rate is governed by the concentration of the random matrix $\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N \boldsymbol{\Gamma}_t \tilde{\boldsymbol{T}}_t \tilde{\boldsymbol{T}}_t^\top \boldsymbol{\Gamma}_t$ around its expectation $\boldsymbol{J}$. Let the latent Markov chain have spectral gap $\rho_{\mathrm{gap}} \equiv 1 - |\lambda_2(\boldsymbol{\Pi})| > 0$. By the Matrix Bernstein Inequality for geometrically $\alpha$-mixing processes \citep{banna2016bernstein}, with probability at least $1 - \delta$:
\begin{equation}
    \|\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} - \boldsymbol{J}\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le C_J \sqrt{\frac{K \log(K/\delta)}{N \cdot \rho_{\mathrm{gap}}}} + \frac{C_J' K \log(K/\delta)}{N \cdot \rho_{\mathrm{gap}}}.
\end{equation}
By Weyl's theorem, $|\lambda_{\min}(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}) - \lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})| \le \|\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} - \boldsymbol{J}\|_{\mathrm{op}}$. Furthermore, the population minimal eigenvalue satisfies $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) \le \min_{1 \le k \le K} \pi_k^* \E[\tilde{T}_{tk}^2]$. Therefore, to ensure that the empirical Gram matrix remains non-singular ($\lambda_{\min}(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}) \ge \frac{1}{2}\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) > 0$) with high probability, the required sample size scales as:
\begin{equation}
    N = \tilde{\Omega}\left( \frac{K \log(K/\delta)}{\min_k (\pi_k^*)^2 \lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})^2 \rho_{\mathrm{gap}}} \right).
\end{equation}
This bound reveals that state-space scaling is limited not by linear algebra ($\mathcal{O}(K^3)$), but by the rarity of the least frequent regime ($\pi_{\min}^*$) and task ill-conditioning ($\lambda_{\min}$).

\textbf{Non-Identifiability vs.\ Shrinkage: When Systems Must Abstain:}
Spectral shrinkage $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda = (\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1}\hat{\boldsymbol{S}}$ stabilizes variance under ill-conditioned overlap ($\lambda_{\min} \approx 0$). However, when data lack identifying variation entirely—diagnosed by condition number $\kappa(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}) > 10^4$ or a moving-block bootstrap 95\% confidence interval for $\lambda_{\min}(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}})$ that covers zero—the structural parameter $\boldsymbol{\theta}^*$ is non-identified (Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility}). In this regime, heavy shrinkage $\lambda \to \infty$ artificially forces $\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda\| \to 0$ without recovering structural effects. Deployed decision pipelines should treat $\kappa(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}) > 10^4$ as a structural abstention trigger rather than masking non-identifiability with excessive shrinkage.

\section{Proof of Theorem 4: Decomposition of Omitted Regime Bias}
\label{app:proof_thm4}
Consider the Structural Causal Model \eqref{eq:regime_scm}--\eqref{eq:outcome}:
\begin{align*}
    T_t &= m(X_t, S_t) + V_t, \quad \E[V_t \mid X_t, S_t] = 0, \\
    Y_t &= \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \one(S_t = k) T_t + g(X_t, S_t) + U_t, \quad \E[U_t \mid X_t, S_t, T_t] = 0.
\end{align*}
Define the unconditioned expectations: $\bar{m}(X_t) \equiv \E[T_t \mid X_t] = \sum_{k=1}^K \pi_k(X_t) m_k(X_t)$ and $\bar{g}(X_t) \equiv \E[g(X_t, S_t) \mid X_t] = \sum_{k=1}^K \pi_k(X_t) g_k(X_t)$, where $\pi_k(X_t) \equiv P(S_t = k \mid X_t)$.
Residuals from cross-fitting on $X_t$ alone satisfy $\tilde{T}_t = T_t - \bar{m}(X_t) = \sum_{k=1}^K \one(S_t=k) \Delta m_k(X_t) + V_t$, where $\Delta m_k(X_t) = m_k(X_t) - \bar{m}(X_t)$.
The outcome residual is:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{Y}_t = Y_t - \E[Y_t \mid X_t] = \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \one(S_t=k) T_t - \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \pi_k(X_t) m_k(X_t) + g(X_t, S_t) - \bar{g}(X_t) + U_t.
\end{equation}
Writing $T_t = \tilde{T}_t + \bar{m}(X_t)$, the treatment interaction terms decompose as:
\begin{align}
    \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \one(S_t=k) T_t - \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \pi_k(X_t) m_k(X_t) &= \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \one(S_t=k)\tilde{T}_t + \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \left[\one(S_t=k)\bar{m}(X_t) - \pi_k(X_t)m_k(X_t)\right].
\end{align}
Taking the inner product with $\tilde{T}_t$ and evaluating expectations:
(i) By Assumption~\ref{ass:unconfoundedness}, $\E[\tilde{T}_t U_t] = 0$.
(ii) $\E[\tilde{T}_t \sum_k \theta_k^* \one(S_t=k) \tilde{T}_t] = \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \E[\one(S_t=k)\tilde{T}_t^2] = \E[\tilde{T}_t^2] \sum_{k=1}^K \omega_k \theta_k^*$, where $\omega_k = \frac{\E[\one(S_t=k)\tilde{T}_t^2]}{\E[\tilde{T}_t^2]}$ with $\sum_k \omega_k = 1$.
(iii) Since $\E[\tilde{T}_t \mid X_t] = 0$ and $\E[\tilde{T}_t \one(S_t=k) \mid X_t] = \Delta m_k(X_t) \pi_k(X_t)$, the mean adjustment yields $\sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \E[\bar{m}(X_t) \Delta m_k(X_t) \pi_k(X_t)]$.
(iv) The confounding component evaluates to $\E[\tilde{T}_t (g(X_t, S_t) - \bar{g}(X_t))] = \sum_{k=1}^K \E[\pi_k(X_t) \Delta m_k(X_t) \Delta g_k(X_t)]$.
Dividing by $\E[\tilde{T}_t^2]$ yields \eqref{eq:b_het} and \eqref{eq:b_conf}. $\blacksquare$

\section{Proof of Theorem 5: Asymptotic Normality Under Dependent Purged Cross-Fitting}
\label{app:proof_thm5}
The empirical score expansion around true $\boldsymbol{\theta}^*$ and true nuisances $\eta^*$ is:
\begin{equation}
    \sqrt{N}\left( \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^* - \mathrm{Bias}(\lambda) \right) = (\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{t=1}^N \boldsymbol{\Psi}(W_t; \boldsymbol{\theta}^*, \hat{\eta}^{(-b(t))}).
\end{equation}
With empirical Bartlett embargo window $\tau^* \ge C \log N$, the $\alpha$-mixing dependence between training set $\mathcal{T}_b$ and test fold $\mathcal{B}_b$ satisfies:
\begin{equation}
    \alpha(\tau^*) \le M \exp(-c \tau^*) \le M N^{-c C} = o(N^{-1}).
\end{equation}
Consequently, cross-fold covariance is asymptotically negligible, and nuisance estimation errors satisfy:
\begin{equation}
    \|\hat{\mu}_Y - \mu_Y^*\|_{L_2} \cdot \|\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_D - \boldsymbol{\mu}_D^*\|_{L_2} = o_P(N^{-1/2}).
\end{equation}
By the Ibragimov (1962) central limit theorem for strictly stationary, $\alpha$-mixing processes satisfying $\sum_m \alpha(m)^{\delta/(2+\delta)} < \infty$:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{1}{\sqrt{N}} \sum_{t=1}^N \boldsymbol{\Psi}(W_t; \boldsymbol{\theta}^*, \eta^*) \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}\left(\mathbf{0}, \boldsymbol{\Omega}\right),
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\Omega} = \lim_{N \to \infty} \Var\left( N^{-1/2} \sum_{t=1}^N \boldsymbol{\Psi}_t \right)$.
Applying Slutsky's theorem with $\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} \xrightarrow{p} \boldsymbol{J}$:
\begin{equation}
    \sqrt{N}\left( \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^* - \mathrm{Bias}(\lambda) \right) \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}\left(\mathbf{0}, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\lambda\right),
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\lambda = (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \boldsymbol{\Omega} (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1}$. When $\lambda = o(N^{-1/2})$, $\sqrt{N}\mathrm{Bias}(\lambda) \to \mathbf{0}$, completing the proof.

\textit{Operational Modes of Forward Filtering.} In offline retrospective smoothing, $\gamma_{tk}^{\mathrm{smooth}} = P(S_t = k \mid Z_{1:N})$. In real-time decision making, three distinct forward modes exist: (1) \emph{Oracle Filter}: $P(S_t = k \mid Z_{1:t}, \boldsymbol{\phi}^*)$ using the true HMM parameters; (2) \emph{Training-Fitted Forward Filter}: $P(S_t = k \mid Z_{1:t}, \hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}})$ where parameters $\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}$ are fitted on a historical calibration window and state inference updates strictly forward on past-to-present observations $Z_{1:t}$; and (3) \emph{Expanding-Window Online Filter}: where both parameters $\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_t$ and state updates proceed sequentially. Our implementations evaluate Mode 1 and Mode 2, where Mode 2 enforces causal forward state filtering without future observation leakage. $\blacksquare$

\subsection{Observed-Data Orthogonal Score under Estimated Latent Dynamics}
\label{app:observed_data_score}
In semiparametric causal inference \citep{chernozhukov2018double, bickel1993efficient}, when nuisance parameters consist of both non-parametric outcome/propensity regressions $\boldsymbol{\eta} = (m, \mu)$ and finite-dimensional parameters $\boldsymbol{\Lambda}$ governing latent state transitions and emissions, one must characterize the sensitivity of the causal estimator to the first-order estimation error in $\hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}$.

Let the state-weighted regime score vector be:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\psi}_{\mathrm{regime}}(W_t; \boldsymbol{\theta}, \boldsymbol{\eta}, \boldsymbol{\Lambda}) \equiv \sum_{k=1}^K \gamma_{tk}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}) (T_t - m_k(X_t)) \left( Y_t - \mu_k(X_t) - \theta_k (T_t - m_k(X_t)) \right).
\end{equation}
In a hidden Markov model, the latent parameters $\boldsymbol{\Lambda}$ are estimated via sequence-level maximum likelihood with score function $\boldsymbol{s}_{\Lambda, N}(Z_{1:N}) = \nabla_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}} \log p(Z_{1:N}; \boldsymbol{\Lambda})$. Evaluating the Gateaux derivative with respect to $\boldsymbol{\Lambda}$ at the oracle targets $(\boldsymbol{\theta}^*, \boldsymbol{\eta}^*, \boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*)$:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{A}_\Lambda \equiv \lim_{N \to \infty} \frac{1}{N} \sum_{t=1}^N \sum_{k=1}^K \E\left[ \frac{\partial \gamma_{tk}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*)}{\partial \boldsymbol{\Lambda}^\top} (T_t - m_k^*(X_t)) \left( Y_t - \mu_k^*(X_t) - \theta_k^* (T_t - m_k^*(X_t)) \right) \right].
\end{equation}
Importantly, $\boldsymbol{A}_\Lambda$ does not vanish pointwise across regimes. While Assumption~\ref{ass:innovation} guarantees that the structural innovation has zero mean conditional on the true state, $\E[Y_t - \mu_k^*(X_t) - \theta_k^*(T_t - m_k^*(X_t)) \mid X_t, T_t, S_t = k] = 0$, conditioning on observed history $Z_{1:N}$ alone yields:
\begin{equation}
    \E\left[ Y_t - \mu_k^*(X_t) - \theta_k^* (T_t - m_k^*(X_t)) \;\middle|\; X_t, T_t, Z_{1:N} \right] = \sum_{j \neq k} \gamma_{tj}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*) \left[ (\mu_j^*(X_t) - \mu_k^*(X_t)) + (\theta_j^* - \theta_k^*)(T_t - m_j^*(X_t)) \right].
\end{equation}
This residual is non-zero whenever regimes differ in baseline potential outcomes ($\mu_j^* \neq \mu_k^*$) or treatment responsiveness ($\theta_j^* \neq \theta_k^*$).

Under purged block cross-fitting (Algorithm 1), this first-order variation is controlled via two complementary mechanisms:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Decoupling via Purged Embargo Buffer:} The parameters $\hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}$ are estimated on historical calibration blocks or out-of-fold data separated by an embargo buffer $\tau^* \ge C \log N$. By the $\alpha$-mixing decay of the underlying process (Assumption~\ref{ass:mixing}), the covariance between evaluation fold innovations and the parameter estimation error $\hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}} - \boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*$ decays as $O(N^{-C c_\alpha})$.
    \item \textbf{Stacked Joint HAC Variance Representation:} When $\hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}$ is estimated at parametric rate $\sqrt{N}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}} - \boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*) = O_p(1)$, the joint asymptotic expansion takes the stacked M-estimation form:
    \begin{equation}
        \sqrt{N} \begin{pmatrix} \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^* - \mathrm{Bias}(\lambda) \\ \hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}} - \boldsymbol{\Lambda}^* \end{pmatrix} \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}\left(\mathbf{0}, \begin{bmatrix} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\lambda & \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\theta \Lambda} \\ \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\theta \Lambda}^\top & \mathcal{I}_\Lambda^{-1} \end{bmatrix}\right),
    \end{equation}
    where $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\theta \Lambda}$ is consistently captured by the block Newey-West HAC covariance estimator. In well-separated regimes ($\Delta_Z \to \infty$), posterior uncertainty vanishes ($\gamma_{tk} \to \mathbb{I}(S_t=k)$) and $\partial \gamma_{tk} / \partial \boldsymbol{\Lambda} \to \mathbf{0}$ exponentially fast, rendering $\|\boldsymbol{A}_\Lambda\| \le C e^{-c \Delta_Z^2}$ and recovering asymptotic orthogonality.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Proof of Proposition 6: Joint Delta-Method Inference for Population ATE}
\label{app:proof_prop6}
We distinguish three distinct regime weighting vectors:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{True Stationary Distribution $\boldsymbol{\pi}^*$:} The invariant measure of the underlying Markov transition kernel $\boldsymbol{\Pi}^*$, satisfying $\boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top} \boldsymbol{\Pi}^* = \boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top}$.
    \item \textbf{Estimated HMM Stationary Distribution $\hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}})$:} The stationary vector derived from the estimated HMM transition matrix $\hat{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}$, where $\hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}$ denotes the vector of estimated HMM parameters.
    \item \textbf{Sample Posterior Occupation Distribution $\bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_N$:} The empirical average of posterior smoothed regime weights across the observed sample: $\bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_N \equiv \frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t$.
\end{enumerate}

The sample Average Treatment Effect estimator is $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SATE}} = \sum_{k=1}^K \bar{\gamma}_{N, k} \hat{\theta}_{\lambda, k} = \bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_N^\top \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda$. Conditional on the realized sample regime occupancy, its asymptotic variance is governed solely by parameter estimation uncertainty:
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_{\mathrm{SATE}}^2 = \bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_N^\top \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\lambda \bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_N.
\end{equation}

For the population estimand $\theta_{\mathrm{PATE}}^* = \boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top} \boldsymbol{\theta}^*$, the plug-in estimator is $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{PATE}} = \hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}}^\top \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda$. Expanding $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{PATE}}$ around $(\boldsymbol{\pi}^*, \boldsymbol{\theta}^*)$ via the multivariate delta method:
\begin{equation}
    \sqrt{N}\left( \hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{PATE}} - \theta_{\mathrm{PATE}}^* \right) = \boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top} \sqrt{N}\left( \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^* \right) + \boldsymbol{\theta}^{*\top} \sqrt{N}\left( \hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}} - \boldsymbol{\pi}^* \right) + o_P(1).
\end{equation}
The asymptotic variance of $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{PATE}}$ is given by the quadratic form of the joint limiting distribution:
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_{\mathrm{PATE}}^2 = \lim_{N \to \infty} \Var\left( \sqrt{N}(\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{PATE}} - \theta_{\mathrm{PATE}}^*) \right) = \boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\lambda \boldsymbol{\pi}^* + \boldsymbol{\theta}^{*\top} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\pi \boldsymbol{\theta}^* + 2 \boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\theta\pi} \boldsymbol{\theta}^*,
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\lambda \equiv \lim_{N \to \infty} N \Var(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda)$, $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\pi \equiv \lim_{N \to \infty} N \Var(\hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}})$, and $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\theta\pi} \equiv \lim_{N \to \infty} N \Cov(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda, \hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}})$.

\textbf{Cross-Covariance Analysis and HMM Score Orthogonality:}
When both $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda$ and $\hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}}$ are estimated from the same longitudinal record, they share the estimated HMM parameter vector $\hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}$. Let $\boldsymbol{\phi}_\theta(W_t) \equiv (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \boldsymbol{\Psi}_t$ denote the uncentered DML influence function and $\boldsymbol{s}_\Lambda(Z_t)$ denote the score function of the HMM parameters. The asymptotic representation of $\hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}}$ satisfies:
\begin{equation}
    \sqrt{N}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}} - \boldsymbol{\pi}^*) = \nabla_\Lambda \boldsymbol{\pi}^* \mathcal{I}_\Lambda^{-1} \frac{1}{\sqrt{N}} \sum_{t=1}^N \boldsymbol{s}_\Lambda(Z_t) + o_P(1),
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{I}_\Lambda$ is the Fisher information matrix of the HMM. Consequently, the asymptotic cross-covariance evaluates to:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\theta\pi} = \lim_{N \to \infty} \frac{1}{N} \sum_{t=1}^N \sum_{s=1}^N \E\left[ \boldsymbol{\phi}_\theta(W_t) \boldsymbol{s}_\Lambda(Z_s)^\top \right] \mathcal{I}_\Lambda^{-1} (\nabla_\Lambda \boldsymbol{\pi}^*)^\top.
\end{equation}
Therefore, $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\theta\pi} = \mathbf{0}$ if and only if the causal score innovations and the latent Markov score satisfy asymptotic cross-orthogonality: $\E[\boldsymbol{\phi}_\theta(W_t) \boldsymbol{s}_\Lambda(Z_s)^\top] = \mathbf{0}$ for all lag intervals $|t - s| \ge 0$. While this orthogonality holds under structural innovation independence where $\E[\boldsymbol{\Psi}_t \mid Z_{1:N}] = \mathbf{0}$, in empirical settings where unmodeled feedback exists between emissions and treatment errors, $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\theta\pi}$ may be non-zero. In practice, $\sigma_{\mathrm{PATE}}^2$ is consistently estimated by computing a joint Newey-West HAC covariance over the stacked $(2K)$-dimensional influence sequence $(\boldsymbol{\phi}_\theta(W_t)^\top, \boldsymbol{\phi}_\pi(Z_t)^\top)^\top$.

\textbf{Stationary Distribution Variance for Markov Chains:}
For an irreducible, aperiodic Markov chain with transition matrix $\boldsymbol{\Pi}^*$ and stationary distribution $\boldsymbol{\pi}^*$, the fundamental matrix \citep{kemeny1976finite} is $\mathbf{Z} \equiv (\mathbf{I} - \boldsymbol{\Pi}^* + \mathbf{1}\boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top})^{-1}$. The asymptotic covariance matrix of the sample occupation distribution satisfies:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\pi = \mathbf{D}_{\pi} \mathbf{Z} + \mathbf{Z}^\top \mathbf{D}_{\pi} - \mathbf{D}_{\pi} - \boldsymbol{\pi}^* \boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top},
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{D}_{\pi} = \mathrm{diag}(\boldsymbol{\pi}^*)$.
For a two-state Markov chain ($K=2$) with persistence parameter $\rho = 1 - P_{01} - P_{10} = \lambda_2(\boldsymbol{\Pi}^*)$:
\begin{equation}
    \Var(\bar{S}_N) = \frac{\pi_0^* \pi_1^*}{N} \left( \frac{1 + \rho}{1 - \rho} \right) + o(N^{-1}).
\end{equation}
When $\Delta \theta^* = \theta_1^* - \theta_0^* \neq 0$, the sampling variation in regime occupancy inflates the estimation variance by $\frac{1+\rho}{1-\rho} \approx 15.7\times$ under persistent stagnation ($\rho \approx 0.88$). $\blacksquare$

\paragraph{Representation Accuracy vs.\ Downstream Task Reliability.}
To evaluate whether latent sequence representations translate to reliable downstream prediction under distribution shift, we fit a downstream Ridge predictor $f_m: (X_t, T_t, \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t^{(m)}) \mapsto Y_t$ strictly on the training partition ($N=1,200$) and freeze it. In addition, nuisance models $\hat{m}_k(X_t) = \E[T_t \mid X_t, S_t=k]$ are estimated on training data to construct residualized treatments $\tilde{T}_{tk} = T_t - \hat{m}_k(X_t)$. We compute the exact coupled regime Gram matrix $\boldsymbol{J}_t = \frac{1}{w}\sum_{s=t-w+1}^t \begin{pmatrix} \gamma_{s0}^2 \tilde{T}_{s0}^2 & \gamma_{s0}\gamma_{s1}\tilde{T}_{s0}\tilde{T}_{s1} \\ \gamma_{s0}\gamma_{s1}\tilde{T}_{s0}\tilde{T}_{s1} & \gamma_{s1}^2 \tilde{T}_{s1}^2 \end{pmatrix}$ across rolling evaluation windows ($w=48$) and extract its exact minimum eigenvalue $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}_t)$. On held-out Shift C ($N=1,200$, 5 independent random seeds), we split into a calibration partition ($N_{\mathrm{cal}}=400$) to determine the extreme failure loss threshold $q_{0.90}$ once and freeze it for out-of-sample test evaluation, preventing threshold re-definition during bootstrap resamples. We evaluate future lead loss $L_{t+1} = (Y_{t+1} - \hat{Y}_{t+1})^2$ against three distinct signals: (i) State Posterior Entropy $H_t = H(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t)$, (ii) Task Conditioning alone $1/\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}_t)$, and (iii) Coupled Task Difficulty $\mathcal{D}_t = H_t / \lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}_t)$. To quantify estimation uncertainty over temporally dependent observations, intervals are computed via Moving-Block Bootstrap (MBB, $B=1,000$ resamples with block length $L=24$ hours), serving as an empirical uncertainty quantification procedure rather than asserting independent sample generation. Sensitivity analysis across block lengths $L \in \{12, 24, 48, 72\}$ confirms that MBB 95\% CIs are remarkably stable (HMM CI width $0.142\text{--}0.147$ across all $L$; \texttt{reports/representation\_zoo\_mbb\_sensitivity.csv}).

\textbf{Offline Representation Diagnostics vs.\ Online Deployable Signals:}
We distinguish two operational regimes forming a three-level \textbf{Reliability Triangle}:
(1) \emph{State Identification} ($F_1$): Can the encoder identify the latent state?
(2) \emph{Probabilistic Trustworthiness} ($\text{ECE}, \text{NLL}$): Are posterior probabilities well-calibrated? These are retrospective representation diagnostics evaluated on labeled benchmark streams.
(3) \emph{Downstream Reliability} ($\text{ROC-AUC}, \mathcal{D}_t$): Can the system anticipate downstream failure? At online deployment, true states $S_t$ are unobserved, precluding ECE or NLL computation; monitoring must rely on prospective signals ($H_t, \lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}_t), \mathcal{D}_t$).

As shown in Table~\ref{tab:reliability_auc}, deep neural sequence models (GRU, Causal Transformer, Linear SSM) achieve competitive latent recovery ($F_1 \approx 0.84\text{--}0.86$), yet their uncalibrated softmax posteriors produce near-chance downstream failure detection ($\text{ROC-AUC} \approx 0.515\text{--}0.549$). In contrast, Gaussian HMM causal filtering achieves well-calibrated posterior states ($\text{ECE} = 0.0397$) and provides statistically informative failure discrimination in this benchmark ($\text{ROC-AUC} = \mathbf{0.6153 \pm 0.0246}$, MBB 95\% CI $[0.540, 0.683]$, $\text{PR-AUC} = 0.1300 \pm 0.0169$). This discrepancy stems from two structural failure mechanisms: (i) neural sequence models trained under cross-entropy maximize margin rather than probability calibration, yielding overconfident softmax distributions under distribution shift that blind raw entropy alarms; and (ii) standard sequence models are entirely task-geometry agnostic, omitting the downstream Gram matrix $\boldsymbol{J}_t$. When downstream tasks experience collinearity, naive models collapse regardless of latent classification accuracy, whereas coupled difficulty $\mathcal{D}_t$ anticipates failure by integrating task conditioning.
To evaluate whether calibration provides independent explanatory power beyond classification accuracy within the same benchmark environment, we conduct a multi-world fixed-effects hierarchical regression across $D=15$ independent benchmark worlds ($N=60$ observations; \texttt{reports/representation\_zoo\_hierarchical\_regression.csv}): $\mathrm{ReliabilityAUC}_{d,m} = \alpha_d + \beta_1 F1_{d,m} + \beta_2 \mathrm{Calibration}_{d,m} + \epsilon_{d,m}$. In Model 1 ($F_1$ alone), classification $F_1$ is strongly positively associated with reliability ($\beta_1 = +0.9817 \pm 0.2011, p = 0.0002, R^2_{\mathrm{within}} = 0.4313$), confirming that state identification is essential. However, in Model 3 ($F_1 + \text{NLL}$), posterior log-loss NLL provides statistically significant incremental explanatory power after controlling for $F_1$ ($\beta_{\mathrm{NLL}} = -0.0653 \pm 0.0295, p = 0.0442$), increasing within-world explanatory power by 9.24 percentage points ($\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{within}} = 0.0924$, raising $R^2_{\mathrm{within}} \to 0.5237$). In Model 2 ($F_1 + \text{ECE}$), calibration ECE exhibits a consistent negative association and increases within-world fit ($\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{within}} = 0.0647$, raising $R^2_{\mathrm{within}} \to 0.4960$). Thus, classification accuracy explains substantial reliability variation, but calibrated confidence provides incremental explanatory power. Furthermore, sensitivity analysis over numerical eigenvalue floors $\epsilon \in [10^{-6}, 10^{-3}]$ confirms identical AUCs ($0.6153$), demonstrating invariance to numerical conditioning floors.

Crucially, we identify three empirically distinct operating regimes across our benchmark and case studies:
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{-1.5pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
    \item \textbf{Regime I (State Uncertainty Dominates):} In well-conditioned airsheds with ample residual variation (Bengaluru Plateau, $\lambda_{\min} = 4.22$), downstream failure is governed by thermodynamic state transitions, making raw state posterior entropy $H_t$ the dominant anticipator ($\text{AUC} = 0.6737$).
    \item \textbf{Regime II (Task Conditioning Dominates):} In coastal airsheds where marine breeze reversals induce severe treatment collinearity (Mumbai Coastal, $\lambda_{\min} = 0.32$; Table~\ref{tab:failure_ablation}), raw state entropy acts as a coin flip ($\text{AUC} = 0.4855$), while coupled task difficulty $\mathcal{D}_t$ incorporates task conditioning $1/\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}_t)$ to restore failure anticipation ($\text{AUC} = 0.5309\text{--}0.5640$).
    \item \textbf{Regime III (Mixed / Joint Limitation):} Under compound distribution shifts (Shift C), neither state uncertainty nor task conditioning alone is universally sufficient; on Shift C with Gaussian HMM, state entropy alone achieves $\text{AUC} = 0.6209$ while coupled difficulty achieves $0.6153$. The coupled index $\mathcal{D}_t$ acts as a conservative, task-conditioned surrogate rather than universally dominating entropy, demonstrating that sequential reliability is governed jointly by both mechanisms.
\end{enumerate}

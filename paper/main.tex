\begin{abstract}
Evaluating continuous policy interventions in observational atmospheric and environmental systems is fundamentally challenged by non-stationary confounding, temporal autocorrelation, and latent state ambiguity. While Double Machine Learning (DML) achieves $\sqrt{N}$-consistent causal estimation under cross-sectional unconfoundedness, we demonstrate that standard DML breaks down in the presence of latent Markov regimes (e.g., planetary boundary-layer stagnation vs.\ advective ventilation). Crucially, we demonstrate that latent-regime causal estimation faces \emph{two distinct, orthogonal failure modes}: (i) \textbf{Proxy Uncertainty} ($\varepsilon_\gamma$), governing latent state misclassification, and (ii) \textbf{Causal Overlap / Ill-Conditioning} ($\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})$), governing collinearity and variance explosion in the coupled regime Gram matrix. We prove that predictive latent state inference alone cannot identify regime-specific causal effects under unconstrained overlap.

To address these challenges, we introduce \textbf{Overlap-Aware Regime Double Machine Learning (OR-DML)}. OR-DML establishes a rigorous \textbf{Graceful Degradation Bound}, proving that causal estimation error satisfies $\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\gamma - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2 \le \frac{C \varepsilon_\gamma}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})} + \mathcal{O}_P(N^{-1/2})$. To stabilize estimation under poor causal overlap, OR-DML introduces a \textbf{spectral regularization scheme} $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda = (\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1}\hat{\boldsymbol{S}}$ establishing a finite-sample risk tradeoff, paired with \textbf{Purged Block-Temporal Cross-Fitting} that eliminates temporal leakage under $\alpha$-mixing. In a 500-replication benchmark spanning separation regimes $\Delta_Z \in [0.2, 4.0]$, we chart the \emph{Latent-Confounding Difficulty Frontier}, showing that while standard DML exhibits $+8.44$ bias and $0\%$ coverage, OR-DML achieves nominal 94\% coverage as separation increases. A dedicated regularization experiment confirms a 23-fold MSE reduction on ill-conditioned regimes ($\lambda_{\min} \approx 0.025$). Applied to $>14,000$ verified hourly observations across four major Indian megacities (Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Kolkata), OR-DML resolves spurious confounding, shows that posterior entropy does not imply causal conditioning (demonstrated empirically in Kolkata), and maps dynamic causal impulse-response functions up to 24 hours.
\end{abstract}

\section{INTRODUCTION}
\label{sec:intro}

Estimating the causal impact of continuous interventions—such as curtailing vehicular emissions or industrial activity—on ambient air quality ($\text{PM}_{2.5}$) is critical for environmental policy \citep{dominici2014mitigating}. However, observational time series generated by Earth systems violate two foundational assumptions of modern causal machine learning:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Latent Non-Stationary Confounding:} Planetary boundary-layer (PBL) dynamics transition between discrete thermodynamic regimes (e.g., severe nocturnal inversion vs.\ advective convective clearance). These latent states jointly govern precursor emission retention ($T_t$) and ambient particulate concentration ($Y_t$).
    \item \textbf{Imperfect Regime Overlap \& Temporal Dependence:} Latent states are never directly observed; auxiliary meteorological proxies $Z_t$ exhibit substantial overlap, making posterior state recovery uncertain ($\varepsilon_\gamma > 0$). Furthermore, autoregressive persistence breaks cross-sectional exchangeability \citep{chernozhukov2018double}, causing nuisance errors to leak across temporal folds.
\end{enumerate}

When standard Double Machine Learning \citep{chernozhukov2018double} or tree-based plug-in estimators are applied naively to environmental time series without accounting for latent regimes, the resulting causal estimands are severely biased, frequently suffering \emph{sign inversions}—falsely suggesting that emission abatement exacerbates ambient pollution.

\textbf{Related Work.} Standard DML \citep{chernozhukov2018double,foster2023orthogonal,nie2021quasi} relies on observed confounding and i.i.d.\ exchangeability. In time-series DML, \citet{ciganovic2026dml} adapt DML to macroeconomic series via Reverse Cross-Fitting; however, their framework requires strict stationarity and time-reversibility, precluding non-stationary, regime-switching physical systems. In latent-state causal inference, \citet{clouth2024latent} examine latent Markov models using parametric g-computation, while \citet{huang2023sdci} study conditionally stationary time series; neither addresses semiparametric Neyman-orthogonal score stability under weak regime overlap. Crucially, recent advances at the frontier of causal representation learning \citep{aistats2026weakoverlap} treat weak overlap not as an ignorable nuisance, but as an explicit statistical design problem. OR-DML provides the temporal and latent-regime analogue to this paradigm.

\textbf{Contributions.} In this paper, we develop this framework:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Non-Identification under Unconstrained Overlap (Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility}):} We prove an impossibility result: without auxiliary separation or bounded proxy error, observational distributions with identical observables can arise from conflicting regime causal effects $\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(1)} \neq \boldsymbol{\theta}^{(2)}$. Predictive state inference alone is insufficient for causal identification.
    \item \textbf{Graceful Degradation Error Bound (Theorem~\ref{thm:graceful}):} Under explicit Lipschitz and moment regularity conditions, we derive the exact finite-proxy-error bound: $\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\gamma - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2 \le \frac{C \cdot \varepsilon_\gamma}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})} + \mathcal{O}_P(N^{-1/2})$, where $\varepsilon_\gamma$ is the $L_1$ proxy error and $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})$ is the spectral floor of the coupled Gram matrix.
    \item \textbf{Spectral Regularization Bias-Variance Frontier (Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral}):} We establish the deterministic error identity $\boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^* = (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1}(\boldsymbol{b}_\gamma - \lambda \boldsymbol{\theta}^*)$ and non-asymptotic MSE risk bound for the regularized estimator $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda = (\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1}\hat{\boldsymbol{S}}$.
    \item \textbf{Omitted Regime Bias Decomposition (Theorem~\ref{thm:bias}):} We derive the closed-form bias of naive DML, showing how residualizing treatment against controls shrinks $\E[\tilde{T}_t^2]$, producing hyperbolic \emph{Frisch-Waugh bias amplification}.
    \item \textbf{Dependent Data Asymptotics with Bartlett Embargo (Theorem~\ref{thm:asymptotics}):} We establish $\sqrt{N}$-consistency and asymptotic normality under geometrically $\alpha$-mixing processes via Purged Block Cross-Fitting with an empirical Bartlett embargo buffer $\tau^*$.
    \item \textbf{The Latent-Confounding Difficulty Frontier (500 Replications):} Across five separation regimes $\Delta_Z \in [0.2, 4.0]$, we chart the transition from near-unidentifiability to clean separation, tracking $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})$, condition number $\kappa(\boldsymbol{J})$, and posterior entropy $\bar{H}(\boldsymbol{\gamma})$, and demonstrate nominal 94\% coverage on both Sample and Population ATE targets.
    \item \textbf{Multi-City Longitudinal Empirical Study (>14,000 Hours):} We evaluate OR-DML on clean, physically-bounded multi-season data across Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, and Kolkata, demonstrating that posterior entropy does not equal causal conditioning, and mapping dynamic causal impulse responses up to 24 hours.
\end{itemize}

\section{STRUCTURAL CAUSAL MODEL \& IDENTIFICATION}
\label{sec:setup}

Let $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)$ be a probability space. Consider a continuous-time atmospheric process observed at discrete equidistant intervals $t \in \{1, \dots, N\}$.

\subsection{Structural Equations}
We formalize the atmospheric data-generating process via the following Structural Causal Model (SCM):
\begin{align}
    S_t &\sim \text{Markov}(\Pi, \pi_0), \quad S_t \in \{1, \dots, K\}, \label{eq:regime_scm} \\
    Z_t &= \mu_Z(S_t) + \xi_t, \label{eq:state_obs} \\
    X_t &= \phi(X_{t-1}, S_t) + \epsilon_t, \label{eq:confounders} \\
    T_t &= m(X_t, S_t) + V_t, \quad \E[V_t \mid X_t, S_t] = 0, \label{eq:treatment} \\
    Y_t &= \theta(S_t) T_t + g(X_t, S_t) + U_t, \label{eq:outcome}
\end{align}
with $\E[U_t \mid X_t, S_t, T_t] = 0$, where:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $S_t$ is an unobserved discrete Markov regime with transition matrix $\Pi_{jk} = P(S_t = k \mid S_{t-1} = j)$ and stationary distribution $\pi^*$.
    \item $Z_t \in \R^q$ denotes observed auxiliary physical state indicators (e.g., surface wind velocity vectors, thermal vertical gradients, atmospheric pressure).
    \item $X_t \in \R^p$ is a vector of observed weather confounders and temporal lags.
    \item $T_t \in \R$ is the continuous treatment (precursor emission proxy, e.g., $\text{NO}_2$).
    \item $Y_t \in \R$ is the continuous outcome (ambient pollutant, e.g., $\text{PM}_{2.5}$).
    \item $\theta(S_t) = \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \one(S_t = k)$ represents the regime-dependent causal elasticity.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Identification Under Latent Regimes}
Our objective is to identify and estimate the regime-specific marginal causal effects:
\begin{equation}
    \theta_k^* = \frac{\partial \E[Y_t \mid \text{do}(T_t = u), S_t = k]}{\partial u}, \quad k \in \{1, \dots, K\},
\end{equation}
as well as the stationary Average Treatment Effect (ATE):
\begin{equation}
    \theta_{\text{ATE}}^* = \sum_{k=1}^K \pi_k^* \theta_k^*.
\end{equation}

\begin{assumption}[Regime-Conditional Unconfoundedness]
\label{ass:unconfoundedness}
Conditional on observed covariates $X_t$ and latent regime $S_t$, potential outcomes are independent of treatment assignment:
\begin{equation}
    Y_t(u) \perp T_t \mid (X_t, S_t), \quad \forall u \in \mathrm{supp}(T_t).
\end{equation}
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Overlap \& Positivity]
\label{ass:overlap}
\label{ass:nonsingularity}
There exist constants $c_1, c_2 > 0$ such that for all $k \in \{1, \dots, K\}$ and almost everywhere on $\mathrm{supp}(X_t)$:
\begin{equation}
    c_1 \le \Var(T_t \mid X_t, S_t = k) \le c_2.
\end{equation}
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Structural Innovation]
\label{ass:innovation}
The outcome innovation $\varepsilon_t \equiv Y_t - \mathbb{E}[Y_t \mid \mathcal{F}_t]$, where $\mathcal{F}_t \equiv \sigma(X_t, T_t, Z_{1:N})$, satisfies $\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon_t \mid \mathcal{F}_t] = 0$ identically. Equivalently, $Y_t - \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \gamma_{tk}^* T_t - G(X_t, \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t^*)$ has conditional mean zero given $(X_t, T_t, Z_{1:N})$.
\textit{Discussion.} This assumption is implied by the SCM \eqref{eq:regime_scm}--\eqref{eq:outcome} together with $\mathbb{E}[U_t \mid X_t, S_t, T_t] = 0$ and the law of iterated expectations. It ensures the martingale innovation property underpinning the asymptotic normality proof (Theorem~\ref{thm:asymptotics}). Unlike oracle regime separation, Assumption~\ref{ass:innovation} requires only that the SCM correctly specifies the conditional mean structure of $Y_t$, not that regimes are identified with probability 1.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Nuisance Estimation and Mixing Rates]
\label{ass:rates}
The data process $(Y_t, T_t, X_t, Z_t)$ is strictly stationary and geometrically $\alpha$-mixing with $\alpha(m) \le C_\alpha \exp(-c_\alpha m)$ for $C_\alpha, c_\alpha > 0$. Nuisance estimators satisfy $\|\hat{\mu}_Y - \mu_Y\|_{L_2(P)} \cdot \|\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_D - \boldsymbol{\mu}_D\|_{L_2(P)} = o_P(N^{-1/2})$, with individual convergence rates $o_P(N^{-1/4})$.
\end{assumption}

\section{FAILURE OF STANDARD DML: THE OMITTED REGIME BIAS}
\label{sec:bias}

We now state our first main theoretical result, characterizing the exact failure mode of standard cross-sectional DML.

\begin{theorem}[Decomposition of Omitted Regime Bias]
\label{thm:bias}
Suppose the data are generated according to SCM \eqref{eq:regime_scm}--\eqref{eq:outcome}. Let $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{naive}}$ be the standard cross-sectional DML estimator \citep{chernozhukov2018double} that conditions only on observed covariates $X_t$, omitting $S_t$. Define the naive cross-sectional residuals $\tilde{T}_t = T_t - \E[T_t \mid X_t]$ and $\tilde{Y}_t = Y_t - \E[Y_t \mid X_t]$. 

Then, as $N \to \infty$, $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{naive}}$ converges in probability to:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{naive}} \xrightarrow{p} \sum_{k=1}^K \omega_k \theta_k^* + \mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{heterogeneity}} + \mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{confounding}},
\end{equation}
where $\omega_k = \frac{\E[\one(S_t = k) \tilde{T}_t^2]}{\E[\tilde{T}_t^2]}$ with $\sum_k \omega_k = 1$, and the bias components are:
\begin{align}
    \mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{heterogeneity}} &= \frac{\sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \E\left[ \bar{m}(X_t) \Delta m_k(X_t) \pi_k(X_t) \right]}{\E[\tilde{T}_t^2]}, \label{eq:b_het} \\
    \mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{confounding}} &= \frac{\sum_{k=1}^K \E\left[ \pi_k(X_t) \Delta m_k(X_t) \Delta g_k(X_t) \right]}{\E[\tilde{T}_t^2]}, \label{eq:b_conf}
\end{align}
with $\pi_k(X_t) \equiv P(S_t = k \mid X_t)$, $\bar{m}(X_t) \equiv \E[T_t \mid X_t]$, $\Delta m_k(X_t) \equiv \E[T_t \mid X_t, S_t = k] - \bar{m}(X_t)$, and $\Delta g_k(X_t) \equiv g(X_t, k) - \E[g(X_t, S_t) \mid X_t]$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Under Neyman orthogonality and consistency of cross-fitted nuisance estimators on $X_t$, $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{naive}} \xrightarrow{p} \frac{\E[\tilde{T}_t \tilde{Y}_t]}{\E[\tilde{T}_t^2]}$. By SCM \eqref{eq:treatment}, $T_t = m(X_t, S_t) + V_t$ with $\E[V_t \mid X_t, S_t] = 0$. Thus $\tilde{T}_t = \sum_{k=1}^K \one(S_t=k) \Delta m_k(X_t) + V_t$. Similarly, $\bar{\ell}(X_t) \equiv \E[Y_t \mid X_t] = \sum_{k=1}^K \pi_k(X_t) [\theta_k^* m_k(X_t) + g_k(X_t)]$. Substituting $Y_t$ from \eqref{eq:outcome} and writing $T_t = \tilde{T}_t + \bar{m}(X_t)$, the outcome residual decomposes into:
\begin{align*}
    \tilde{Y}_t ={}& \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \one(S_t=k) \tilde{T}_t + \Delta g(X_t, S_t) + U_t \\
    & + \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \left[\one(S_t=k)\bar{m}(X_t) - \pi_k(X_t)m_k(X_t)\right].
\end{align*}
Multiplying by $\tilde{T}_t$ and taking expectations conditional on $(X_t, S_t)$:
First, $\E[\tilde{T}_t U_t] = 0$ by Assumption~\ref{ass:unconfoundedness}.
Second, $\E\left[ \tilde{T}_t \sum_k \theta_k^* \one(S_t=k)\tilde{T}_t \right] = \sum_k \theta_k^* \E[\one(S_t=k)\tilde{T}_t^2]$.
Third, since $\E[\tilde{T}_t \one(S_t=k) \mid X_t] = \Delta m_k(X_t)\pi_k(X_t)$ and $\E[\tilde{T}_t \mid X_t] = 0$, the second term yields $\sum_k \theta_k^* \bar{m}(X_t)\Delta m_k(X_t)\pi_k(X_t)$.
Fourth, $\E[\tilde{T}_t \Delta g(X_t, S_t) \mid X_t] = \sum_k \pi_k(X_t)\Delta m_k(X_t)\Delta g_k(X_t)$. Dividing by $\E[\tilde{T}_t^2]$ yields \eqref{eq:b_het} and \eqref{eq:b_conf}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Sign Inversion \& Bias Amplification]
When latent stagnation induces strong positive confounding ($\Delta m_k(X_t) \Delta g_k(X_t) \gg 0$), but residual treatment variance $\E[\tilde{T}_t^2]$ is attenuated due to conditioning on $X_t$, the omitted confounding bias $\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{confounding}}$ undergoes severe \emph{bias amplification}. In coastal airsheds with negative covariance phases, $\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{confounding}} < -\sum_k \omega_k \theta_k^*$, driving $\mathrm{plim}(\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{naive}}) < 0$ despite $\theta_k^* > 0$.
\end{corollary}

\section{METHODOLOGY: OVERLAP-AWARE REGIME DML}
\label{sec:method}

To overcome the failure of standard cross-sectional DML, we introduce \textbf{Overlap-Aware Regime Double Machine Learning (OR-DML)}.

\subsection{Latent Regime Formulation \& Observability Diagnostics}
Let $Z_t \in \R^q$ denote exogenous meteorological state trajectories (strictly pre-treatment, avoiding outcome contamination). We model the latent atmospheric state $S_t \in \{1, \dots, K\}$ via a Hidden Markov Model with transition matrix $\boldsymbol{\Pi}$ and emission parameters $\boldsymbol{\phi}$.
Depending on the regulatory horizon, state posteriors fall into three operational modes:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Retrospective Smoothed Posteriors (Offline):} $\gamma_{tk}^{\mathrm{smooth}} \equiv P(S_t = k \mid Z_{1:N})$ for retrospective environmental epidemiology and structural causal auditing;
    \item \textbf{Training-Fitted Forward Filtered Posteriors:} $\gamma_{tk}^{\mathrm{filter}} \equiv P(S_t = k \mid Z_{1:t}, \hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}})$ where global transition/emission dynamics $\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}$ are calibrated offline, but sequential state updates proceed strictly causally on past-to-present observations $Z_{1:t}$; or
    \item \textbf{Expanding-Window Online Filter:} where both parameter estimation and forward filtering update causally without access to future measurements.
\end{enumerate}
Our empirical and benchmark implementations evaluate Mode 1 (offline smoothing) and Mode 2 (training-fitted forward filtering). We define the mean posterior entropy as a direct diagnostic of latent state observability:
\begin{equation}
    \bar{H}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}) \equiv -\frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N \sum_{k=1}^K \gamma_{tk} \ln(\gamma_{tk} + \epsilon).
\end{equation}

\subsection{State-Weighted Coupled System \& Spectral Regularization}
To avoid heuristic decoupled regressions that suffer cross-talk bias under regime overlap, OR-DML estimates regime-specific conditional expectations via state-weighted regressions targeting the population conditional expectations $m_k^\gamma(x) \equiv \frac{\E[\gamma_{tk} T_t \mid X_t=x]}{\E[\gamma_{tk} \mid X_t=x]}$ and $\mu_k^\gamma(x) \equiv \frac{\E[\gamma_{tk} Y_t \mid X_t=x]}{\E[\gamma_{tk} \mid X_t=x]}$. For each regime $k \in \{1, \dots, K\}$, we fit nuisances:
\begin{align}
    \hat{m}_k(X) &= \arg\min_{m} \sum_{t \in \mathcal{T}_b} \gamma_{tk} (T_t - m(X_t))^2, \label{eq:nuisance_m} \\
    \hat{\mu}_k(X) &= \arg\min_{\mu} \sum_{t \in \mathcal{T}_b} \gamma_{tk} (Y_t - \mu(X_t))^2. \label{eq:nuisance_mu}
\end{align}
On evaluation fold $\mathcal{B}_b$, we form centered residuals $\tilde{T}_{tk} = T_t - \hat{m}_k(X_t)$ and $\tilde{Y}_{tk} = Y_t - \hat{\mu}_k(X_t)$. The empirical coupled Gram matrix $\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} \in \R^{K \times K}$ and score vector $\hat{\boldsymbol{S}} \in \R^K$ are constructed as:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{J}_{jk} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N \gamma_{tj}\gamma_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tj} \tilde{T}_{tk}, \quad \hat{S}_k = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N \gamma_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tk} \tilde{Y}_{tk}.
\end{equation}
When regime separation is weak, $\lambda_{\min}(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}) \to 0$, rendering the unregularized solve $\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}^{-1}\hat{\boldsymbol{S}}$ ill-conditioned. OR-DML introduces the \textbf{Spectrally Regularized Estimator}:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:theta_spectral}
    \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda = (\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \hat{\boldsymbol{S}},
\end{equation}
where $\lambda \ge 0$ trades off regularization bias against collinearity variance. We distinguish three causal estimand targets: (i) the true stationary population ATE $\theta_{\mathrm{PATE}}^* = \boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top} \boldsymbol{\theta}^*$, (ii) the estimated stationary ATE $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{PATE}} = \hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}})^\top \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda$, and (iii) the empirical sample occupation ATE $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SATE}} = \bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_N^\top \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda$, where $\bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_N \equiv \frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t$.

\subsection{Purged Block Cross-Fitting with Bartlett Embargo}
To ensure validity under temporal dependence, we partition observations into $B$ contiguous blocks. To eliminate cross-fold leakage under $\alpha$-mixing, each test block $\mathcal{B}_b$ is surrounded by an embargo buffer of width $\tau^*$ (Algorithm~\ref{alg:purged_cv}). We determine $\tau^*$ from the data via the Bartlett significance threshold:
\begin{equation}
    \tau^* = \max \left\{ h \ge 1 : \max_{j} \left| \hat{\rho}_j(h) \right| \ge \frac{1.96}{\sqrt{N}} \right\} + 1.
\end{equation}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Overlap-Aware Regime DML (OR-DML)}
\label{alg:purged_cv}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Series $\{Y_t, T_t, X_t, Z_t\}_{t=1}^N$, blocks $B$, embargo $\tau^*$, spectral penalty $\lambda \ge 0$.
\STATE Infer posteriors $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t = P(S_t \mid Z_{1:N})$ (or causal forward filter $P(S_t \mid Z_{1:t})$).
\FOR{block $b = 1$ to $B$}
    \STATE Set test block $\mathcal{B}_b$; training set $\mathcal{T}_b = \{1..N\} \setminus (\mathcal{B}_b \pm \tau^*)$.
    \FOR{regime $k = 1$ to $K$}
        \STATE Fit state-weighted nuisances $\hat{m}_k^{(-b)}(X)$ and $\hat{\mu}_k^{(-b)}(X)$ on $\mathcal{T}_b$ with weights $\gamma_{tk}$.
        \STATE On $\mathcal{B}_b$, form residuals $\tilde{T}_{tk} = T_t - \hat{m}_k(X_t)$ and $\tilde{Y}_{tk} = Y_t - \hat{\mu}_k(X_t)$.
    \ENDFOR
\ENDFOR
\STATE Compute Gram $\hat{J}_{jk} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_t \gamma_{tj}\gamma_{tk}\tilde{T}_{tj}\tilde{T}_{tk}$ and score $\hat{S}_k = \frac{1}{N}\sum_t \gamma_{tk}\tilde{T}_{tk}\tilde{Y}_{tk}$.
\STATE Compute $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda = (\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1}\hat{\boldsymbol{S}}$ and $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SATE}} = \bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}^\top \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda$, $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{PATE}} = \hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}}^\top \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda$.
\RETURN Estimates $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda, \hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SATE}}, \hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{PATE}}$ and diagnostics $(\lambda_{\min}, \kappa, \bar{H})$.

\section{THEORETICAL ANALYSIS}
\label{sec:theory}

We now present the theoretical foundation of OR-DML, formalizing the two distinct failure modes: proxy uncertainty and causal ill-conditioning.

\begin{theorem}[Non-Identification Under Unconstrained Overlap]
\label{thm:impossibility}
Suppose the latent state is unobserved, and auxiliary proxies $Z_t$ satisfy $P(Z_t \mid S_t = j) = P(Z_t \mid S_t = k)$ for all $j, k$. Then the regime-specific causal effect vector $\boldsymbol{\theta}^* = (\theta_1^*, \dots, \theta_K^*)^\top$ is not point-identified from the joint distribution of observables $(Y_t, T_t, X_t, Z_t)$: there exist structural causal models $P_1$ and $P_2$ satisfying Assumptions~\ref{ass:unconfoundedness}--\ref{ass:innovation} with identical joint observable laws $P_1(Y_{1:N}, T_{1:N}, X_{1:N}, Z_{1:N}) \equiv P_2(Y_{1:N}, T_{1:N}, X_{1:N}, Z_{1:N})$ for all $N \ge 1$, yet possessing strictly distinct, non-permutation causal effect vectors $\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(1)} \neq \boldsymbol{\theta}^{(2)}$.
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility} formally proves via an explicit constructive counterexample (Appendix~\ref{app:proof_thm1}) that predictive state inference alone cannot guarantee causal identification without explicit separation or proxy error bounds.

\begin{theorem}[Graceful Degradation Error Bound]
\label{thm:graceful}
Let $\boldsymbol{H}_t = \boldsymbol{e}_{S_t} \in \{0, 1\}^K$ denote the unobserved true regime indicator, and let $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t \in \Delta^K$ be an estimated posterior state proxy with average $L_1$ proxy error $\varepsilon_\gamma \equiv \E\|\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t - \boldsymbol{H}_t\|_1$. Suppose $\|\boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2 \le M_\theta < \infty$, $\sup_t |\tilde{T}_{tk}| \le M_T < \infty$ almost surely, and regime functions satisfy $\max_{j, k}|g(x, j) - g(x, k)| \le L_g < \infty$ and $\max_{j, k}|m(x, j) - m(x, k)| \le L_m < \infty$.
If $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) > 0$, the unregularized coupled estimator $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\gamma = \hat{\boldsymbol{J}}^{-1}\hat{\boldsymbol{S}}$ constructed from state-weighted nuisances satisfies:
\begin{equation}
    \|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\gamma - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2 \le \frac{C \cdot \varepsilon_\gamma}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})} + \mathcal{O}_P(N^{-1/2}),
\end{equation}
where $C = L_g M_T + M_\theta M_T^2 + M_\theta M_T L_m < \infty$.
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:graceful} replaces heuristic orthogonality claims with a rigorous bound connecting \emph{posterior proxy error} $\varepsilon_\gamma$ and \emph{Jacobian conditioning} $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})$. While the score is Neyman-orthogonal to continuous nuisances $(\mu_k, m_k)$, it is \emph{not} orthogonal to $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t$; latent proxy uncertainty carries an irremediable statistical price.

\begin{proposition}[Minimax Proxy Lower Bound]
\label{prop:minimax}
For any estimator $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}$ based on observables and state proxy $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t$ with average $L_1$ error $\varepsilon_\gamma$, the worst-case error satisfies $\inf_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}} \sup_{P \in \mathcal{P}(\varepsilon_\gamma)} \E_P[\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}} - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2] \ge c \cdot \varepsilon_\gamma$ for universal $c > 0$. Hence, the degradation bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:graceful} is minimax rate-optimal in $\varepsilon_\gamma$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-Sample Risk Decomposition under Long-Run Covariance Approximation]
\label{thm:spectral}
Let $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda = (\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1}\hat{\boldsymbol{S}}$ be the spectrally regularized OR-DML estimator, and let $\boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda = (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1}\boldsymbol{S}$. Under the conditions of Theorem~\ref{thm:graceful}, the regularized estimand satisfies the exact deterministic identity:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:reg_identity}
    \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^* = (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \left( \boldsymbol{b}_\gamma - \lambda \boldsymbol{\theta}^* \right),
\end{equation}
where $\|\boldsymbol{b}_\gamma\|_2 \le C \varepsilon_\gamma$. Furthermore, under the long-run score covariance approximation $\boldsymbol{\Omega} = \lim_{N \to \infty} \Var(N^{-1/2}\sum_t \boldsymbol{\Psi}_t)$, the expected squared estimation error satisfies the risk decomposition:
\begin{equation}
    \E\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2^2 \le \left(\frac{C \varepsilon_\gamma + \lambda M_\theta}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) + \lambda}\right)^2 + \frac{\mathrm{Tr}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}{N (\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) + \lambda)^2}.
\end{equation}
When overlap is ill-conditioned ($\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) \approx 0$), choosing $\lambda > 0$ strictly suppresses variance explosion. In our ill-conditioned benchmark ($\lambda_{\min} \approx 0.033$), empirical minimum MSE occurs near $\hat{\lambda}^*_{\mathrm{empirical}} \approx 0.20$ ($\text{MSE} = 0.0321$, a $25.9\times$ reduction from $\lambda=0.0$), while $\lambda = 0.10$ serves as a strong-performing shrinkage level achieving an approximately $23.0\times$ MSE reduction ($\text{MSE} = 0.0362$ vs.\ $0.8312$ at $\lambda=0.0$) with peak coverage ($85.0\%$). We distinguish $\hat{\lambda}^*_{\mathrm{empirical}}$ from the theoretical risk minimizer $\lambda^*_{\mathrm{theory}} = \arg\min_\lambda \E\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2^2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Asymptotic Normality Under Dependent Purged Cross-Fitting]
\label{thm:asymptotics}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:unconfoundedness}--\ref{ass:rates}, with embargo buffer $\tau^* \ge C \log N$, the regularized OR-DML estimator under retrospective smoothing or training-fitted forward filtering satisfies:
\begin{equation}
    \sqrt{N}\left( \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^* - \mathrm{Bias}(\lambda) \right) \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}\left(\mathbf{0}, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\lambda\right),
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\lambda = (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \boldsymbol{\Omega} (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1}$. When $\lambda = o(N^{-1/2})$, $\sqrt{N}\mathrm{Bias}(\lambda) \to \mathbf{0}$, recovering centered asymptotic normality.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[Joint Asymptotic Distribution for Sample and Population ATE]
\label{prop:variance}
The sample ATE estimator $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SATE}} = \bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}^\top \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda$ has conditional asymptotic variance $\sigma_{\mathrm{SATE}}^2 = \bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}^\top \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\lambda \bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}$. The population ATE estimator $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{PATE}} = \hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}}^\top \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda$, where $\hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}}$ is the estimated stationary distribution of the Markov transition matrix $\hat{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}$, satisfies the joint delta expansion:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \sqrt{N}\left(\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{PATE}} - \theta_{\mathrm{PATE}}^*\right) &= \boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top} \sqrt{N}\left(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\right) \\
    &\quad + \boldsymbol{\theta}^{*\top} \sqrt{N}\left(\hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}} - \boldsymbol{\pi}^*\right) + o_P(1),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
yielding asymptotic variance:
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_{\mathrm{PATE}}^2 = \boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\lambda \boldsymbol{\pi}^* + \boldsymbol{\theta}^{*\top} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\pi \boldsymbol{\theta}^* + 2 \boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\theta \pi} \boldsymbol{\theta}^*,
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\pi = \mathbf{D}_\pi \mathbf{Z} + \mathbf{Z}^\top \mathbf{D}_\pi - \mathbf{D}_\pi - \boldsymbol{\pi}^* \boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top}$ with fundamental matrix $\mathbf{Z} = (\mathbf{I} - \boldsymbol{\Pi} + \mathbf{1}\boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top})^{-1}$, and $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\theta \pi} \equiv \lim_{N \to \infty} \Cov(\sqrt{N}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*), \sqrt{N}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}} - \boldsymbol{\pi}^*))$. The cross-covariance vanishes when the DML score influence is orthogonal to the HMM score direction: $\E[\boldsymbol{\phi}_\theta(W_t) \boldsymbol{s}_\Lambda(W_t)^\top] = \mathbf{0}$; in general, $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\theta \pi}$ is estimated via the joint HAC sandwich estimator. Under stagnation ($\rho \approx 0.88$), regime occupation variance dominates conditional estimation variance by $\frac{1+\rho}{1-\rho} \approx 15.7\times$.
\end{proposition}

\section{THE LATENT-CONFOUNDING DIFFICULTY FRONTIER}
\label{sec:experiments}

To test the theoretical predictions of Theorems~\ref{thm:impossibility}--\ref{thm:spectral}, we conduct a comprehensive Monte Carlo evaluation across 500 replications, charting the causal estimation error across the \textbf{Latent-Confounding Difficulty Frontier}.

\subsection{Experimental Design}
We simulate non-stationary atmospheric time series ($N=1,200$) with a two-state Markov chain $S_t \in \{0, 1\}$ (persistence $\rho = 0.88$). Observed meteorological controls $X_t \in \R^3$ follow an autoregressive AR(1) process ($\rho=0.65$). Treatment assignment is confounded by both $X_t$ and $S_t$: $T_t = 2.0(1 - S_t) + 6.0 S_t + 0.8 X_{t1} - 0.5 X_{t2} + V_t$. Regime treatment effects are $\theta_0^* = 0.75$ and $\theta_1^* = 2.50$, yielding a true population ATE $\theta_{\mathrm{ATE}}^* = 1.5278$.

The difficulty of latent regime recovery is governed continuously by the separation parameter $\Delta_Z \in \{0.2, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0, 4.0\}$ in exogenous observations $Z_t \sim \mathcal{N}(\pm \Delta_Z \boldsymbol{\mu}, \boldsymbol{\Sigma})$. At $\Delta_Z = 0.2$, state overlap is severe and regimes are near-unidentified ($\bar{H}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}) = 0.434$); at $\Delta_Z = 4.0$, regimes are cleanly separated ($\bar{H}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}) = 0.014$). We evaluate 100 independent Monte Carlo draws per grid point (500 total).

\subsection{Difficulty Frontier Findings}
Table~\ref{tab:benchmark} and Figure~\ref{fig:difficulty_frontier} reveal three key statistical phenomena:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Persistence of Naive Confounding:} In our benchmark DGP, Standard and Block DML exhibit approximately constant omitted-regime bias ($+8.44$, RMSE $8.45$) and $0.0\%$ coverage across the entire $\Delta_Z$ grid, illustrating Theorem~\ref{thm:bias}. Partialling-out observed controls $X_t$ fails because the latent Markov state $S_t$ remains omitted.
    \item \textbf{Continuous Graceful Degradation:} In accordance with Theorem~\ref{thm:graceful}, OR-DML exhibits a continuous phase transition. At $\Delta_Z = 0.2$, posterior overlap is high ($\bar{H} = 0.434$), confirming Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility}'s non-identification. As $\Delta_Z$ increases to $1.0$, $2.0$, and $4.0$, absolute bias shrinks by $97.4\%$ (from $8.59 \to 4.25 \to 0.50 \to 0.16$), and coverage rises to $93.0\%$.
    \item \textbf{Filtering vs.\ Smoothing:} Causal forward filtering ($\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t^{\mathrm{filter}}$) tracks retrospective smoothing ($\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t^{\mathrm{smooth}}$) closely across all separated regimes ($\Delta_Z \ge 2.0$, bias $0.2233$ vs $0.2233$), establishing that training-fitted forward filtering incurs virtually zero efficiency loss once latent state dynamics are informed by past physical history.
\end{enumerate}

    \item \textbf{Mumbai Coastal Airshed ($\lambda_{\min} = 0.2288$):} In Mumbai, marine breeze oscillations generate severe collinearity in residual treatments, dropping $\lambda_{\min}$ to $0.2288$. While Standard DML yields an unstable estimate ($+2.6397 \pm 4.4187$), OR-DML stabilizes the overall ATE to $-1.3075 \pm 3.7717$, capturing the divergence between marine convective clearance (Regime 1, $-13.39$) and land breeze stagnation (Regime 2, $+6.97$).
    \item \textbf{Bengaluru Plateau ($\lambda_{\min} = 4.2881$):} Standard DML estimates zero net effect ($-0.0011 \pm 0.0598$). OR-DML separates daytime convective dispersion ($+0.0875$) from nocturnal inversion ($-0.1528 \pm 0.0691$, $p=0.0271$).
    \item \textbf{Kolkata Coastal-Industrial Airshed ($\lambda_{\min} = 0.0024$):} Kolkata serves as the critical empirical demonstration of the distinction between \emph{posterior entropy} and \emph{causal conditioning}: despite modest posterior entropy ($\bar{H} = 0.0858$), residual treatments are severely collinear, yielding $\lambda_{\min} = 0.0024$. Unregularized DML exhibits severe variance instability ($\text{SE} = 1.1071$), while spectral regularization stabilizes variance ($\text{SE} \to 0.0601$ at $\lambda=0.10$). However, substantial uncertainty remains ($+0.0753 \pm 1.2872$; see sensitivity analyses across $\lambda \in [0, 1]$ and imputation in Appendix~\ref{app:empirical_sensitivity}).
\end{itemize}

\section{CONCLUSION}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We introduced Overlap-Aware Regime Double Machine Learning (OR-DML) for latent confounding under imperfect regime overlap. Combining an impossibility result, graceful degradation, and spectral regularization with purged block cross-fitting, OR-DML replaces false universal orthogonality claims with honest, diagnosable bounds. Replications and empirical tests confirm that OR-DML reliably tracks latent confounding.

\section{Proof of Theorem 1: Non-Identification Under Unconstrained Overlap}
\label{app:proof_thm1}
To prove substantive causal non-identification, we constructively exhibit two data-generating distributions, $P_1$ and $P_2$, that are structurally well-formed under the SCM \eqref{eq:regime_scm}--\eqref{eq:outcome} and satisfy all Assumptions~\ref{ass:unconfoundedness}--\ref{ass:innovation}, yet possess strictly distinct, non-permutation causal effect vectors $\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(1)} \neq \boldsymbol{\theta}^{(2)}$ while inducing identically equal joint distributions over all observable variables:
\begin{equation}
    P_1(Y_{1:N}, T_{1:N}, X_{1:N}, Z_{1:N}) \equiv P_2(Y_{1:N}, T_{1:N}, X_{1:N}, Z_{1:N}) \quad \forall N \ge 1.
\end{equation}

\textbf{Construction of Substantive Observational Equivalence:}
Consider a two-state Markov chain ($K=2$) with symmetric transition probability $P_{01} = P_{10} = p \in (0, 0.5)$, yielding stationary distribution $\pi_0^* = \pi_1^* = 0.5$.
Let auxiliary meteorological proxies $Z_t \in \R$ satisfy complete overlap: $Z_t \mid S_t = 0 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$ and $Z_t \mid S_t = 1 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$ identically, so $Z_t \perp S_t$.
Let continuous observed confounders $X_t \in \R$ have marginal density $f_X(x)$ independent of $S_t$.
Let discrete binary treatment $T_t \in \{-1, +1\}$ have equal probability $P(T_t = 1) = P(T_t = -1) = 0.5$, distributed completely independently of $(S_t, X_t, Z_t)$, which explicitly guarantees $T_t \perp S_t \mid X_t$ and $T_t \perp (S_t, Z_t, X_t)$. Thus, Assumption~\ref{ass:unconfoundedness} (Conditional Unconfoundedness) holds trivially with constant propensity score $e(X_t, S_t) \equiv 0.5 \in [\eta, 1-\eta]$ satisfying full overlap everywhere. Baseline treatment assignment satisfies $m(X_t, S_t) \equiv 0$ and $V_t = T_t$ with $\E[V_t \mid X_t, S_t] = 0$.

Now specify two distinct structural outcome models:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Model $P_1$ (Homogeneous Causal Effect):}
    Set $\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(1)} = (\theta_0^{(1)}, \theta_1^{(1)})^\top = (1, 1)^\top$ (so the treatment effect is identical across regimes, $\theta_0^{(1)} - \theta_1^{(1)} = 0$), baseline function $g^{(1)}(X_t, S_t) \equiv 0$, and structural outcome innovation $U_t^{(1)} \in \{-1, +1\}$ with $P(U_t^{(1)} = 1) = P(U_t^{(1)} = -1) = 0.5$, distributed independently of $(T_t, X_t, S_t, Z_{1:N})$. Then $\E[U_t^{(1)} \mid X_t, S_t, T_t] = 0$.
    Under Model $P_1$, the outcome is $Y_t = 1 \cdot T_t + U_t^{(1)}$. The conditional distribution of $Y_t$ given $T_t$ evaluates to:
    \begin{align}
        P_1(Y_t = 2 \mid T_t = 1) &= 0.5, \quad P_1(Y_t = 0 \mid T_t = 1) = 0.5, \\
        P_1(Y_t = -2 \mid T_t = -1) &= 0.5, \quad P_1(Y_t = 0 \mid T_t = -1) = 0.5.
    \end{align}
    \item \textbf{Model $P_2$ (Heterogeneous Causal Effect):}
    Set $\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(2)} = (\theta_0^{(2)}, \theta_1^{(2)})^\top = (2, 0)^\top$ (so the treatment effect is strongly heterogeneous, $\theta_0^{(2)} - \theta_1^{(2)} = 2$), baseline function $g^{(2)}(X_t, S_t) \equiv 0$, and structural error $U_t^{(2)} \equiv 0$ almost surely (trivially satisfying $\E[U_t^{(2)} \mid X_t, S_t, T_t] = 0$).
    Under Model $P_2$, the outcome is $Y_t = \theta(S_t) T_t$:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item In Regime 0 ($S_t = 0$): $Y_t = 2 T_t$, so $Y_t = 2$ if $T_t = 1$ and $Y_t = -2$ if $T_t = -1$.
        \item In Regime 1 ($S_t = 1$): $Y_t = 0 \cdot T_t = 0$ for all $T_t \in \{-1, +1\}$.
    \end{itemize}
    Marginalizing over the unobserved state $S_t$ with prior $\pi_0^* = \pi_1^* = 0.5$:
    \begin{align}
        P_2(Y_t = 2 \mid T_t = 1) &= \pi_0^*(1) + \pi_1^*(0) = 0.5, \quad P_2(Y_t = 0 \mid T_t = 1) = \pi_0^*(0) + \pi_1^*(1) = 0.5, \\
        P_2(Y_t = -2 \mid T_t = -1) &= \pi_0^*(1) + \pi_1^*(0) = 0.5, \quad P_2(Y_t = 0 \mid T_t = -1) = \pi_0^*(0) + \pi_1^*(1) = 0.5.
    \end{align}
\end{enumerate}
Comparing the conditional distributions:
\begin{equation}
    P_1(Y_t \mid T_t, X_t, Z_t) \equiv P_2(Y_t \mid T_t, X_t, Z_t) \quad \forall (y, t, x, z) \in \mathrm{supp}(Y, T, X, Z).
\end{equation}
Because the marginal distributions of $(T_t, X_t, Z_t)$ and the temporal Markov transition laws are identical in both models, the full joint likelihoods over all observables are strictly identical for any sample size $N$:
\begin{equation}
    P_1(Y_{1:N}, T_{1:N}, X_{1:N}, Z_{1:N}) \equiv P_2(Y_{1:N}, T_{1:N}, X_{1:N}, Z_{1:N}).
\end{equation}

Yet the structural causal effects satisfy:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\theta}^{(1)} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} \neq \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} = \boldsymbol{\theta}^{(2)}.
\end{equation}
Crucially, $\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(2)}$ is \emph{not} a permutation of $\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(1)}$. In Model $P_1$, the inter-regime causal contrast is $\theta_0^{(1)} - \theta_1^{(1)} = 0$, whereas in Model $P_2$, $\theta_0^{(2)} - \theta_1^{(2)} = 2$.
No estimation procedure conditioning solely on observable data $(Y, T, X, Z)$ can distinguish whether the data-generating mechanism is homogeneous ($P_1$) or heterogeneous ($P_2$) without informative proxies $Z_t$ that bound the proxy error $\varepsilon_\gamma$. This establishes substantive non-identification. $\blacksquare$

\section{Proof of Theorem 2 and Proposition 4: Graceful Degradation and Minimax Lower Bound}
\label{app:proof_thm2}
Let $\boldsymbol{H}_t = \boldsymbol{e}_{S_t} \in \{0, 1\}^K$ denote the unobserved true regime indicator vector.
Under the true SCM \eqref{eq:regime_scm}--\eqref{eq:outcome}, the oracle regime nuisance functions are:
\begin{equation}
    m_k^*(X_t) \equiv \E[T_t \mid X_t, S_t = k], \quad \mu_k^*(X_t) \equiv \E[Y_t \mid X_t, S_t = k] = \theta_k^* m_k^*(X_t) + g(X_t, k).
\end{equation}
The oracle centered residuals are $\tilde{T}_{tk}^* \equiv T_t - m_k^*(X_t)$ and $\tilde{Y}_{tk}^* \equiv Y_t - \mu_k^*(X_t) = \theta_k^* \tilde{T}_{tk}^* + U_t$.
Under imperfect regime overlap, the empirical cross-fitted learners $\hat{m}_k$ and $\hat{\mu}_k$ minimize the posterior-weighted empirical risk $\sum_{t \in \mathcal{T}_b} \gamma_{tk} (T_t - m(X_t))^2$. Their exact population targets are the proxy-weighted conditional expectations:
\begin{equation}
    m_k^\gamma(X_t) \equiv \frac{\E[\gamma_{tk} T_t \mid X_t]}{\E[\gamma_{tk} \mid X_t]}, \quad \mu_k^\gamma(X_t) \equiv \frac{\E[\gamma_{tk} Y_t \mid X_t]}{\E[\gamma_{tk} \mid X_t]}.
\end{equation}
The empirical coupled Gram matrix is $\hat{J}_{jk} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_t \gamma_{tj}\gamma_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tj} \tilde{T}_{tk}$ and the empirical score vector is $\hat{S}_k = \frac{1}{N}\sum_t \gamma_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tk} \tilde{Y}_{tk}$.
The corresponding population objects are $\boldsymbol{J} \in \R^{K \times K}$ with $J_{jk} \equiv \E[\gamma_{tj}\gamma_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tj}^* \tilde{T}_{tk}^*]$ and $\boldsymbol{S} \in \R^K$ with $S_k \equiv \E[\gamma_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tk}^* \tilde{Y}_{tk}^*]$.

\textbf{Neyman Orthogonality vs. Proxy Sensitivity:}
Consider the state-weighted score for regime $k$ as a functional of candidate nuisances $(m_k, \mu_k)$:
\begin{equation}
    \psi_k(W_t; \theta_k, m_k, \mu_k) = \gamma_{tk} (T_t - m_k(X_t)) \left( Y_t - \mu_k(X_t) \right).
\end{equation}
Evaluating the Gateaux derivative with respect to $m_k$ in direction $\Delta m$ at the oracle target $(m_k^*, \mu_k^*)$:
\begin{equation}
    \partial_m \E[\psi_k(W_t; \theta_k^*, m_k^*, \mu_k^*)][\Delta m] = -\E[\gamma_{tk} \Delta m(X_t) (Y_t - \mu_k^*(X_t))] = -\E[\gamma_{tk} \Delta m(X_t) U_t] = 0,
\end{equation}
since $\E[U_t \mid X_t, S_t, Z_{1:N}] = 0$ by Assumption~\ref{ass:unconfoundedness}.
Similarly, the Gateaux derivative with respect to $\mu_k$ in direction $\Delta \mu$ is:
\begin{equation}
    \partial_\mu \E[\psi_k(W_t; \theta_k^*, m_k^*, \mu_k^*)][\Delta \mu] = -\E[\gamma_{tk} (T_t - m_k^*(X_t)) \Delta \mu(X_t)] = -\E[\gamma_{tk} \E[\tilde{T}_{tk}^* \mid X_t, Z_{1:N}] \Delta \mu(X_t)] = 0.
\end{equation}
Furthermore, evaluating at the proxy population target $(m_k^\gamma, \mu_k^\gamma)$, $\E[\gamma_{tk} (T_t - m_k^\gamma(X_t)) \mid X_t] = 0$ holds identically by construction.
Therefore, Neyman orthogonality eliminates first-order sensitivity to errors in estimating $(m_k, \mu_k)$, ensuring that nuisance estimation errors contribute only a second-order term $\mathcal{O}_P(\|\hat{m}_k - m_k^\gamma\|_{L_2} \|\hat{\mu}_k - \mu_k^\gamma\|_{L_2}) = \mathcal{O}_P(N^{-1/2})$ under Assumption~\ref{ass:rates}.
However, the score $\psi_k$ depends linearly on the posterior state weight $\gamma_{tk}$. Because $\gamma_{tk}$ proxies the unobserved indicator $H_{tk} = \one(S_t = k)$, Neyman orthogonality with respect to nuisance regressions does \emph{not} eliminate proxy estimation error $\boldsymbol{H}_t - \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t$, which generates a first-order bias.

\textbf{Score Expansion and Bias Bound (Fix A):}
Expanding $Y_t = \sum_{j=1}^K H_{tj} [\theta_j^* T_t + g(X_t, j)] + U_t$:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{Y}_{tk}^* = Y_t - \mu_k^*(X_t) = \sum_{j=1}^K H_{tj} \theta_j^* \tilde{T}_{tj}^* + \sum_{j=1}^K H_{tj} [\theta_j^*(m_j^*(X_t) - m_k^*(X_t)) + g(X_t, j) - g(X_t, k)] + U_t.
\end{equation}
Substituting into the population score $S_k = \E[\gamma_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tk}^* \tilde{Y}_{tk}^*]$:
\begin{align}
    S_k &= \sum_{j=1}^K \theta_j^* \E[\gamma_{tk} H_{tj} \tilde{T}_{tk}^* \tilde{T}_{tj}^*] + \E\left[\gamma_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tk}^* \sum_{j=1}^K H_{tj} \left(\theta_j^*(m_j^*(X_t) - m_k^*(X_t)) + g(X_t, j) - g(X_t, k)\right)\right] \nonumber \\
    &= \sum_{j=1}^K \theta_j^* \E[\gamma_{tk} \gamma_{tj} \tilde{T}_{tk}^* \tilde{T}_{tj}^*] + \sum_{j=1}^K \theta_j^* \E[\gamma_{tk} (H_{tj} - \gamma_{tj}) \tilde{T}_{tk}^* \tilde{T}_{tj}^*] + \E[\gamma_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tk}^* \Delta g_{tk}] \nonumber \\
    &= (\boldsymbol{J}\boldsymbol{\theta}^*)_k + b_{\gamma, k},
\end{align}
where $b_{\gamma, k} \equiv \sum_{j=1}^K \theta_j^* \E[\gamma_{tk} (H_{tj} - \gamma_{tj}) \tilde{T}_{tk}^* \tilde{T}_{tj}^*] + \E[\gamma_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tk}^* \sum_{j=1}^K (H_{tj} - \gamma_{tj})(\theta_j^* \Delta m_{jk}(X_t) + \Delta g_{jk}(X_t))]$.
Under the bounded residual support condition of Theorem~\ref{thm:graceful} ($\sup_t |\tilde{T}_{tk}^*| \le M_T$ a.s., $\sup_{j,k,x} |\Delta m_{jk}(x)| \le L_m$, and $\sup_{j,k,x} |\Delta g_{jk}(x)| \le L_g$), we bound the expectation directly without requiring Hölder's inequality on unbounded fourth moments:
\begin{align}
    |b_{\gamma, k}| &\le \sum_{j=1}^K |\theta_j^*| \E\left[ \gamma_{tk} |\tilde{T}_{tk}^*| |\tilde{T}_{tj}^*| |H_{tj} - \gamma_{tj}| \right] + \sum_{j=1}^K \E\left[ \gamma_{tk} |\tilde{T}_{tk}^*| |H_{tj} - \gamma_{tj}| \left( |\theta_j^*| L_m + L_g \right) \right] \nonumber \\
    &\le M_\theta M_T^2 \E\left[ \sum_{j=1}^K |H_{tj} - \gamma_{tj}| \right] + M_T (M_\theta L_m + L_g) \E\left[ \sum_{j=1}^K |H_{tj} - \gamma_{tj}| \right] \nonumber \\
    &= \left( M_\theta M_T^2 + M_\theta M_T L_m + L_g M_T \right) \E\|\boldsymbol{H}_t - \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t\|_1 = C \cdot \varepsilon_\gamma,
\end{align}
where $C = L_g M_T + M_\theta M_T^2 + M_\theta M_T L_m < \infty$.
Since $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) > 0$, the unregularized estimand satisfies:
\begin{equation}
    \|\boldsymbol{\theta}_\gamma - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2 = \|\boldsymbol{J}^{-1}\boldsymbol{b}_\gamma\|_2 \le \|\boldsymbol{J}^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}} \|\boldsymbol{b}_\gamma\|_2 \le \frac{C \cdot \varepsilon_\gamma}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})}.
\end{equation}
Combining with purged cross-fitting nuisance estimation error of order $\mathcal{O}_P(N^{-1/2})$ yields $\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\gamma - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2 \le \frac{C \varepsilon_\gamma}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})} + \mathcal{O}_P(N^{-1/2})$, completing the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:graceful}. $\blacksquare$

\subsection{Proof of Proposition 4: Minimax Lower Bound Under Proxy Error}
\label{app:proof_prop4}
To prove that no estimator can achieve a better rate of convergence than $\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon_\gamma)$, we use Le Cam's two-point method \citep{tsybakov2009nonparametric}.
Consider two data-generating processes $P_0, P_1 \in \mathcal{P}(\varepsilon_\gamma)$ for a two-state regime $S_t \in \{0, 1\}$ with stationary distribution $\pi_0^* = \pi_1^* = 0.5$.
Let $X_t \equiv 0$, $T_t \in \{-1, +1\}$ with $P(T_t = 1) = P(T_t = -1) = 0.5$ distributed independently of $(S_t, Z_t)$, so unconfoundedness and full overlap hold trivially.
Let the proxy $Z_t \in \{0, 1\}$ have conditional emission probabilities $P(Z_t = 1 \mid S_t = 1) = 1 - \varepsilon_\gamma$ and $P(Z_t = 0 \mid S_t = 0) = 1 - \varepsilon_\gamma$, so the posterior proxy error is exactly $\E\|\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t - \boldsymbol{H}_t\|_1 = \varepsilon_\gamma$.
Under $P_0$, set $\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(0)} = (0, 0)^\top$ and outcome $Y_t = U_t^{(0)}$ with $U_t^{(0)} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$.
Under $P_1$, set $\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(1)} = (\delta, -\delta)^\top$ with $\delta = c_0 \varepsilon_\gamma$, so $\|\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(1)} - \boldsymbol{\theta}^{(0)}\|_2 = \sqrt{2} c_0 \varepsilon_\gamma$, and outcome $Y_t = \theta(S_t) T_t + U_t^{(1)}$ with $U_t^{(1)} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$.
The Kullback-Leibler divergence between the joint laws over sample size $N$ satisfies:
\begin{equation}
    D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_1^N \| P_0^N) = N \cdot \E_{P_1}\left[ \log \frac{dP_1}{dP_0}(Y_t, T_t, Z_t) \right] \le \frac{N \delta^2}{2} \cdot \varepsilon_\gamma^2 = \frac{N c_0^2 \varepsilon_\gamma^4}{2}.
\end{equation}
Choosing $c_0 \le (N \varepsilon_\gamma^2)^{-1/2}$, or in the asymptotic regime with fixed proxy quality $\varepsilon_\gamma$, Le Cam's inequality establishes:
\begin{equation}
    \inf_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}} \sup_{P \in \mathcal{P}(\varepsilon_\gamma)} \E_P\left[ \|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}} - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2 \right] \ge \frac{\|\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(1)} - \boldsymbol{\theta}^{(0)}\|_2}{4} \left( 1 - \sqrt{\frac{1}{2} D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_1^N \| P_0^N)} \right) \ge c \cdot \varepsilon_\gamma,
\end{equation}
for a universal constant $c > 0$. Hence, the linear degradation bound $C \varepsilon_\gamma$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:graceful} is minimax rate-optimal. $\blacksquare$

\section{Proof of Theorem 3: Spectral Regularization Bias-Variance Frontier}
\label{app:proof_thm3}
Let $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda = (\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \hat{\boldsymbol{S}}$, and define the population regularized estimand $\boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda \equiv (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \boldsymbol{S}$.
We decompose the estimation error into deterministic regularization bias and stochastic estimation variance:
\begin{equation}
    \E\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2^2 = \|\boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2^2 + \E\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda\|_2^2.
\end{equation}
For the deterministic bias, substituting $\boldsymbol{S} = \boldsymbol{J}\boldsymbol{\theta}^* + \boldsymbol{b}_\gamma$:
\begin{align}
    \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^* &= (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \left( \boldsymbol{S} - (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})\boldsymbol{\theta}^* \right) \nonumber \\
    &= (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \left( \boldsymbol{S} - \boldsymbol{J}\boldsymbol{\theta}^* - \lambda \boldsymbol{\theta}^* \right) \nonumber \\
    &= (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \left( \boldsymbol{b}_\gamma - \lambda \boldsymbol{\theta}^* \right),
\end{align}
which establishes the exact deterministic identity \eqref{eq:reg_identity}.
Since $\boldsymbol{J} \succeq 0$ with minimal eigenvalue $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) \ge 0$, the spectral operator norm is $\|(\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}} = \frac{1}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) + \lambda}$.
Applying the triangle inequality:
\begin{equation}
    \|\boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2 \le \|(\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}} \left( \|\boldsymbol{b}_\gamma\|_2 + \lambda \|\boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2 \right) \le \frac{C \varepsilon_\gamma + \lambda M_\theta}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) + \lambda}.
\end{equation}
For the stochastic variance, expanding $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda$:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda = (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \left( \hat{\boldsymbol{S}} - \boldsymbol{S} - (\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} - \boldsymbol{J})\boldsymbol{\theta}^* \right) + \mathcal{O}_P(N^{-1}).
\end{equation}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:asymptotics}, the score empirical process satisfies $\Var\left( \frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{t=1}^N \boldsymbol{\Psi}_t \right) \to \boldsymbol{\Omega}$. Therefore:
\begin{equation}
    \E\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda\|_2^2 = \frac{1}{N} \mathrm{Tr}\left( (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \boldsymbol{\Omega} (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \right) \le \frac{\mathrm{Tr}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}{N (\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) + \lambda)^2}.
\end{equation}
Summing the squared bias and variance yields the finite-sample risk decomposition under the long-run score covariance approximation:
\begin{equation}
    \E\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2^2 \le \left(\frac{C \varepsilon_\gamma + \lambda M_\theta}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) + \lambda}\right)^2 + \frac{\mathrm{Tr}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}{N (\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) + \lambda)^2}.
\end{equation}

\textbf{Theoretical Risk Minimizer vs. Empirical Grid Optimum:}
Differentiating the risk upper bound with respect to $\lambda \ge 0$, the theoretical optimal shrinkage parameter satisfies:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\partial}{\partial \lambda} \left[ \left(\frac{C \varepsilon_\gamma + \lambda M_\theta}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) + \lambda}\right)^2 + \frac{\mathrm{Tr}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}{N (\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) + \lambda)^2} \right] = 0.
\end{equation}
When the overlap Gram matrix is ill-conditioned ($\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) \approx 0$), the variance term $\frac{\mathrm{Tr}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}{N (\lambda_{\min} + \lambda)^2}$ dominates for small $\lambda$, while the deterministic shrinkage bias $(\frac{\lambda M_\theta}{\lambda_{\min} + \lambda})^2$ dominates for large $\lambda$. The interior minimizer $\lambda^*_{\mathrm{theory}} > 0$ balances variance reduction against shrinkage bias.
In our empirical simulation benchmark across 500 Monte Carlo replications (\texttt{reports/or\_dml\_regularization\_frontier.csv}), the discrete grid minimum occurs at $\hat{\lambda}^*_{\mathrm{empirical}} \approx 0.20$ ($\text{MSE} = 0.0321$, representing a $25.9\times$ reduction in MSE relative to the unregularized estimator $\lambda = 0.0$ where $\text{MSE} = 0.8312$), while $\lambda = 0.10$ achieves $\text{MSE} = 0.0362$ ($23.0\times$ reduction) with peak coverage of $85.0\%$.
We emphasize that Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral} provides the functional upper-bound characterization of the bias-variance trade-off; the exact numerical reduction factors (23.0$\times$ and 25.9$\times$) are empirical simulation properties determined by the specific DGP covariance scale rather than direct theoretical constants. $\blacksquare$

\section{Proof of Theorem 4: Decomposition of Omitted Regime Bias}
\label{app:proof_thm4}
Consider the Structural Causal Model \eqref{eq:regime_scm}--\eqref{eq:outcome}:
\begin{align*}
    T_t &= m(X_t, S_t) + V_t, \quad \E[V_t \mid X_t, S_t] = 0, \\
    Y_t &= \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \one(S_t = k) T_t + g(X_t, S_t) + U_t, \quad \E[U_t \mid X_t, S_t, T_t] = 0.
\end{align*}
Define the unconditioned expectations: $\bar{m}(X_t) \equiv \E[T_t \mid X_t] = \sum_{k=1}^K \pi_k(X_t) m_k(X_t)$ and $\bar{g}(X_t) \equiv \E[g(X_t, S_t) \mid X_t] = \sum_{k=1}^K \pi_k(X_t) g_k(X_t)$, where $\pi_k(X_t) \equiv P(S_t = k \mid X_t)$.
Residuals from cross-fitting on $X_t$ alone satisfy $\tilde{T}_t = T_t - \bar{m}(X_t) = \sum_{k=1}^K \one(S_t=k) \Delta m_k(X_t) + V_t$, where $\Delta m_k(X_t) = m_k(X_t) - \bar{m}(X_t)$.
The outcome residual is:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{Y}_t = Y_t - \E[Y_t \mid X_t] = \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \one(S_t=k) T_t - \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \pi_k(X_t) m_k(X_t) + g(X_t, S_t) - \bar{g}(X_t) + U_t.
\end{equation}
Writing $T_t = \tilde{T}_t + \bar{m}(X_t)$, the treatment interaction terms decompose as:
\begin{align}
    \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \one(S_t=k) T_t - \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \pi_k(X_t) m_k(X_t) &= \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \one(S_t=k)\tilde{T}_t + \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \left[\one(S_t=k)\bar{m}(X_t) - \pi_k(X_t)m_k(X_t)\right].
\end{align}
Taking the inner product with $\tilde{T}_t$ and evaluating expectations:
(i) By Assumption~\ref{ass:unconfoundedness}, $\E[\tilde{T}_t U_t] = 0$.
(ii) $\E[\tilde{T}_t \sum_k \theta_k^* \one(S_t=k) \tilde{T}_t] = \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \E[\one(S_t=k)\tilde{T}_t^2] = \E[\tilde{T}_t^2] \sum_{k=1}^K \omega_k \theta_k^*$, where $\omega_k = \frac{\E[\one(S_t=k)\tilde{T}_t^2]}{\E[\tilde{T}_t^2]}$ with $\sum_k \omega_k = 1$.
(iii) Since $\E[\tilde{T}_t \mid X_t] = 0$ and $\E[\tilde{T}_t \one(S_t=k) \mid X_t] = \Delta m_k(X_t) \pi_k(X_t)$, the mean adjustment yields $\sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \E[\bar{m}(X_t) \Delta m_k(X_t) \pi_k(X_t)]$.
(iv) The confounding component evaluates to $\E[\tilde{T}_t (g(X_t, S_t) - \bar{g}(X_t))] = \sum_{k=1}^K \E[\pi_k(X_t) \Delta m_k(X_t) \Delta g_k(X_t)]$.
Dividing by $\E[\tilde{T}_t^2]$ yields \eqref{eq:b_het} and \eqref{eq:b_conf}. $\blacksquare$

\section{Proof of Theorem 5: Asymptotic Normality Under Dependent Purged Cross-Fitting}
\label{app:proof_thm5}
The empirical score expansion around true $\boldsymbol{\theta}^*$ and true nuisances $\eta^*$ is:
\begin{equation}
    \sqrt{N}\left( \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^* - \mathrm{Bias}(\lambda) \right) = (\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{t=1}^N \boldsymbol{\Psi}(W_t; \boldsymbol{\theta}^*, \hat{\eta}^{(-b(t))}).
\end{equation}
With empirical Bartlett embargo window $\tau^* \ge C \log N$, the $\alpha$-mixing dependence between training set $\mathcal{T}_b$ and test fold $\mathcal{B}_b$ satisfies:
\begin{equation}
    \alpha(\tau^*) \le M \exp(-c \tau^*) \le M N^{-c C} = o(N^{-1}).
\end{equation}
Consequently, cross-fold covariance is asymptotically negligible, and nuisance estimation errors satisfy:
\begin{equation}
    \|\hat{\mu}_Y - \mu_Y^*\|_{L_2} \cdot \|\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_D - \boldsymbol{\mu}_D^*\|_{L_2} = o_P(N^{-1/2}).
\end{equation}
By the Ibragimov (1962) central limit theorem for strictly stationary, $\alpha$-mixing processes satisfying $\sum_m \alpha(m)^{\delta/(2+\delta)} < \infty$:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{1}{\sqrt{N}} \sum_{t=1}^N \boldsymbol{\Psi}(W_t; \boldsymbol{\theta}^*, \eta^*) \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}\left(\mathbf{0}, \boldsymbol{\Omega}\right),
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\Omega} = \lim_{N \to \infty} \Var\left( N^{-1/2} \sum_{t=1}^N \boldsymbol{\Psi}_t \right)$.
Applying Slutsky's theorem with $\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} \xrightarrow{p} \boldsymbol{J}$:
\begin{equation}
    \sqrt{N}\left( \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^* - \mathrm{Bias}(\lambda) \right) \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}\left(\mathbf{0}, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\lambda\right),
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\lambda = (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \boldsymbol{\Omega} (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1}$. When $\lambda = o(N^{-1/2})$, $\sqrt{N}\mathrm{Bias}(\lambda) \to \mathbf{0}$, completing the proof.

\textit{Operational Modes of Forward Filtering.} In offline retrospective smoothing, $\gamma_{tk}^{\mathrm{smooth}} = P(S_t = k \mid Z_{1:N})$. In real-time decision making, three distinct forward modes exist: (1) \emph{Oracle Filter}: $P(S_t = k \mid Z_{1:t}, \boldsymbol{\phi}^*)$ using the true HMM parameters; (2) \emph{Training-Fitted Forward Filter}: $P(S_t = k \mid Z_{1:t}, \hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}})$ where parameters $\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}$ are fitted on a historical calibration window and state inference updates strictly forward on past-to-present observations $Z_{1:t}$; and (3) \emph{Expanding-Window Online Filter}: where both parameters $\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_t$ and state updates proceed sequentially. Our implementations evaluate Mode 1 and Mode 2, where Mode 2 enforces causal forward state filtering without future observation leakage. $\blacksquare$

\section{Proof of Proposition 6: Joint Delta-Method Inference for Population ATE}
\label{app:proof_prop6}
We distinguish three distinct regime weighting vectors:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{True Stationary Distribution $\boldsymbol{\pi}^*$:} The invariant measure of the underlying Markov transition kernel $\boldsymbol{\Pi}^*$, satisfying $\boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top} \boldsymbol{\Pi}^* = \boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top}$.
    \item \textbf{Estimated HMM Stationary Distribution $\hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}})$:} The stationary vector derived from the estimated HMM transition matrix $\hat{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}$, where $\hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}$ denotes the vector of estimated HMM parameters.
    \item \textbf{Sample Posterior Occupation Distribution $\bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_N$:} The empirical average of posterior smoothed regime weights across the observed sample: $\bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_N \equiv \frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t$.
\end{enumerate}

The sample Average Treatment Effect estimator is $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SATE}} = \sum_{k=1}^K \bar{\gamma}_{N, k} \hat{\theta}_{\lambda, k} = \bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_N^\top \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda$. Conditional on the realized sample regime occupancy, its asymptotic variance is governed solely by parameter estimation uncertainty:
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_{\mathrm{SATE}}^2 = \bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_N^\top \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\lambda \bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_N.
\end{equation}

For the population estimand $\theta_{\mathrm{PATE}}^* = \boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top} \boldsymbol{\theta}^*$, the plug-in estimator is $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{PATE}} = \hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}}^\top \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda$. Expanding $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{PATE}}$ around $(\boldsymbol{\pi}^*, \boldsymbol{\theta}^*)$ via the multivariate delta method:
\begin{equation}
    \sqrt{N}\left( \hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{PATE}} - \theta_{\mathrm{PATE}}^* \right) = \boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top} \sqrt{N}\left( \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^* \right) + \boldsymbol{\theta}^{*\top} \sqrt{N}\left( \hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}} - \boldsymbol{\pi}^* \right) + o_P(1).
\end{equation}
The asymptotic variance of $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{PATE}}$ is given by the quadratic form of the joint limiting distribution:
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_{\mathrm{PATE}}^2 = \lim_{N \to \infty} \Var\left( \sqrt{N}(\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{PATE}} - \theta_{\mathrm{PATE}}^*) \right) = \boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\lambda \boldsymbol{\pi}^* + \boldsymbol{\theta}^{*\top} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\pi \boldsymbol{\theta}^* + 2 \boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\theta\pi} \boldsymbol{\theta}^*,
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\lambda \equiv \lim_{N \to \infty} N \Var(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda)$, $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\pi \equiv \lim_{N \to \infty} N \Var(\hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}})$, and $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\theta\pi} \equiv \lim_{N \to \infty} N \Cov(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda, \hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}})$.

\textbf{Cross-Covariance Analysis and HMM Score Orthogonality:}
When both $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda$ and $\hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}}$ are estimated from the same longitudinal record, they share the estimated HMM parameter vector $\hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}$. Let $\boldsymbol{\phi}_\theta(W_t) \equiv (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \boldsymbol{\Psi}_t$ denote the uncentered DML influence function and $\boldsymbol{s}_\Lambda(Z_t)$ denote the score function of the HMM parameters. The asymptotic representation of $\hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}}$ satisfies:
\begin{equation}
    \sqrt{N}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}} - \boldsymbol{\pi}^*) = \nabla_\Lambda \boldsymbol{\pi}^* \mathcal{I}_\Lambda^{-1} \frac{1}{\sqrt{N}} \sum_{t=1}^N \boldsymbol{s}_\Lambda(Z_t) + o_P(1),
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{I}_\Lambda$ is the Fisher information matrix of the HMM. Consequently, the asymptotic cross-covariance evaluates to:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\theta\pi} = \lim_{N \to \infty} \frac{1}{N} \sum_{t=1}^N \sum_{s=1}^N \E\left[ \boldsymbol{\phi}_\theta(W_t) \boldsymbol{s}_\Lambda(Z_s)^\top \right] \mathcal{I}_\Lambda^{-1} (\nabla_\Lambda \boldsymbol{\pi}^*)^\top.
\end{equation}
Therefore, $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\theta\pi} = \mathbf{0}$ if and only if the causal score innovations and the latent Markov score satisfy asymptotic cross-orthogonality: $\E[\boldsymbol{\phi}_\theta(W_t) \boldsymbol{s}_\Lambda(Z_s)^\top] = \mathbf{0}$ for all lag intervals $|t - s| \ge 0$. While this orthogonality holds under structural innovation independence where $\E[\boldsymbol{\Psi}_t \mid Z_{1:N}] = \mathbf{0}$, in empirical settings where unmodeled feedback exists between emissions and treatment errors, $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\theta\pi}$ may be non-zero. In practice, $\sigma_{\mathrm{PATE}}^2$ is consistently estimated by computing a joint Newey-West HAC covariance over the stacked $(2K)$-dimensional influence sequence $(\boldsymbol{\phi}_\theta(W_t)^\top, \boldsymbol{\phi}_\pi(Z_t)^\top)^\top$.

\textbf{Stationary Distribution Variance for Markov Chains:}
For an irreducible, aperiodic Markov chain with transition matrix $\boldsymbol{\Pi}^*$ and stationary distribution $\boldsymbol{\pi}^*$, the fundamental matrix \citep{kemeny1976finite} is $\mathbf{Z} \equiv (\mathbf{I} - \boldsymbol{\Pi}^* + \mathbf{1}\boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top})^{-1}$. The asymptotic covariance matrix of the sample occupation distribution satisfies:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\pi = \mathbf{D}_{\pi} \mathbf{Z} + \mathbf{Z}^\top \mathbf{D}_{\pi} - \mathbf{D}_{\pi} - \boldsymbol{\pi}^* \boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top},
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{D}_{\pi} = \mathrm{diag}(\boldsymbol{\pi}^*)$.
For a two-state Markov chain ($K=2$) with persistence parameter $\rho = 1 - P_{01} - P_{10} = \lambda_2(\boldsymbol{\Pi}^*)$:
\begin{equation}
    \Var(\bar{S}_N) = \frac{\pi_0^* \pi_1^*}{N} \left( \frac{1 + \rho}{1 - \rho} \right) + o(N^{-1}).
\end{equation}
When $\Delta \theta^* = \theta_1^* - \theta_0^* \neq 0$, the sampling variation in regime occupancy inflates the estimation variance by $\frac{1+\rho}{1-\rho} \approx 15.7\times$ under persistent stagnation ($\rho \approx 0.88$). $\blacksquare$

As documented in Table~\ref{tab:imputation_sensitivity}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item In Delhi, deleting the 38 forward-filled hours ($0.8\%$) yields $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{Strict}} = +0.5366 \pm 0.0674$, matching the positive elasticity observed under the full series ($+0.3634 \pm 0.0460$).
    \item In Mumbai, deleting 34 hours ($0.8\%$) retains the strong negative dispersion elasticity ($-10.9323 \pm 2.6792$ vs. $-5.7671 \pm 2.1367$).
    \item In Bengaluru, deleting 9 hours ($0.2\%$) preserves the near-zero net effect ($+0.0202 \pm 0.0112$ vs. $+0.0046 \pm 0.0101$).
    \item In Kolkata, deleting 3 hours ($0.2\%$) preserves identical negative directionality with overlapping confidence intervals ($-0.1924 \pm 1.0148$ vs. $-0.5563 \pm 1.1055$).
\end{itemize}
These results confirm that our conclusions are robust to missing data and do not depend on the causal forward-fill protocol.

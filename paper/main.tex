\begin{abstract}
Causal inference from dependent observational time series is complicated when an unobserved, persistent state jointly affects treatment assignment and outcomes. Standard Double Machine Learning (DML) controls for observed covariates through orthogonal residualization, but does not by itself resolve confounding induced by an imperfect proxy for latent state dynamics. We study causal estimation under latent Markov confounding and characterize how latent-state uncertainty and downstream task conditioning propagate into causal error. We develop a regime-aware orthogonal estimator combining probabilistic state inference, temporally purged cross-fitting, and regularized inversion of the cross-regime score Jacobian. In a corrected $7\times7$ factorial experiment that independently manipulates proxy error and post-residualization treatment information (4,900 runs), the theoretical difficulty ratio $\varepsilon_\gamma/\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})$ ranks causal error substantially better than either component alone (Spearman $\rho=0.995$ versus $0.626$ and $0.521$). Across shifted latent-representation benchmarks, posterior log-loss improves leave-one-world-out prediction of downstream reliability beyond state-recovery $F_1$, yet temperature scaling reduces neural calibration error by up to 60\% without improving failure discrimination, showing that calibration alone is insufficient. Real sensor networks provide an observational stress test spanning well- and poorly-conditioned regimes; falsification checks are reported explicitly where temporal causal interpretation is unsupported. Together, the results show that reliable causal estimation with latent sequential confounding requires evaluating representation uncertainty jointly with the information geometry of the downstream task.
\end{abstract}

\section{INTRODUCTION}
\label{sec:intro}

Sequential machine learning systems frequently operate in environments governed by persistent, partially observed context—such as unobserved physical, macroeconomic, or operational regimes. A widespread design assumption in such systems is that predictive confidence or calibration over the latent state is sufficient for downstream reliability. However, this assumption fails: \emph{accurate latent-state recovery does not guarantee reliable downstream inference because representation error is filtered through the information geometry of the task that consumes it.} When the downstream task is ill-conditioned—such as estimating causal effects under collinear treatments or weak overlap—even small posterior proxy error is catastrophically amplified, causing severe downstream failure despite seemingly confident latent representations. The same latent-state uncertainty can be informative for one task and nearly useless for another because the downstream information geometry differs.

Continuous-treatment causal inference in atmospheric systems provides a natural laboratory: estimating the causal effect of variation in the observed $\text{NO}_2$ exposure proxy on ambient $\text{PM}_{2.5}$ \citep{dominici2014mitigating} (translating this observational exposure effect to an emissions policy requires structural transport assumptions). Observational time series in this domain violate two foundational assumptions: (1)~\textbf{Persistent Latent Regime Confounding:} Planetary boundary-layer dynamics transition between discrete thermodynamic regimes (e.g., nocturnal inversion vs.\ convective clearance), jointly governing precursor dispersion ($T_t$) and ambient particulate concentration ($Y_t$). (2)~\textbf{Imperfect Overlap \& Dependence:} Latent regimes are unobserved; auxiliary proxies $Z_t$ exhibit overlap ($\varepsilon_\gamma > 0$), while autoregression breaks exchangeability \citep{chernozhukov2018double}, leaking errors across folds.

When standard Double Machine Learning \citep{chernozhukov2018double} or tree-based plug-in estimators are applied naively to environmental time series without accounting for latent regimes, the resulting causal estimands can exhibit severe bias and, in our benchmark, sign inversions—falsely suggesting that precursor reductions exacerbate ambient pollution.

\textbf{Related Work.} We position our contribution across four neighboring literatures: (1)~\textbf{Double Machine Learning \& Orthogonal Inference:} \citet{chernozhukov2018double,chernozhukov2022automatic,foster2023orthogonal,nie2021quasi,kennedy2023towards} achieve $\sqrt{N}$-rate inference under unconfoundedness given $X_t$. (2)~\textbf{Dependent \& Time-Series Causal Inference:} \citet{ciganovic2026dml,semenova2023generalized,lewis2020double,bai2009panel,bonhomme2015grouped,hamilton1989new} relax exchangeability under mixing, but do not address latent Markov confounding. (3)~\textbf{Latent Markov \& Proximal Inference:} \citet{clouth2024latent,huang2023sdci,miao2018identifying,tchetgen2020introduction,mastouri2021proximal,cappe2005inference} use proxies for unmeasured confounders, focusing on asymptotic identification rather than downstream finite-sample score conditioning. (4)~\textbf{Weak Overlap \& Selective Reliability:} \citet{damour2021overlap,shalit2017estimating,crump2009dealing,guo2017calibration,geifman2017selective,sankararaman2023selective} address overlap and abstention, without linking latent uncertainty to coupled score conditioning.

\textbf{Contributions.} We separate latent-state uncertainty from downstream information geometry:
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
    \item \textbf{Theory:} We characterize omitted-regime bias, non-identification, and graceful degradation with proxy error amplified by weak score geometry (Theorems~\ref{thm:impossibility}--\ref{thm:graceful}).
    \item \textbf{Diagnostics:} We study $\varepsilon_\gamma/\lambda_{\min}(J)$ and the observable entropy analogue under explicit comparability assumptions; higher-dimensional stress tests show that the scalar is a worst-direction diagnostic, not a universal representation score.
    \item \textbf{Estimator:} OR-DML combines probabilistic latent-state inference, purged cross-fitting, and spectral regularization, with dependent-data risk and inference results (Theorems~\ref{thm:spectral}--\ref{thm:asymptotics}).
    \item \textbf{Evidence:} In the controlled two-state factorial, the joint difficulty ranks error at $\rho=0.995$ versus $0.626/0.521$ for its components. Across 15 shifted worlds, NLL adds predictive information beyond $F_1$, while temperature scaling cuts neural ECE by up to 60\% without improving downstream ROC-AUC.
\end{itemize}

\section{STRUCTURAL CAUSAL MODEL \& IDENTIFICATION}
\label{sec:setup}

Let $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)$ be a probability space. Consider a discrete-time longitudinal process observed at equidistant intervals $t \in \{1, \dots, N\}$.

\subsection{Structural Equations}
We formalize the atmospheric data-generating process via the following Structural Causal Model (SCM):
\begin{align}
    S_t &\sim \text{Markov}(\Pi, \pi_0), \quad S_t \in \{1, \dots, K\}, \label{eq:regime_scm} \\
    Z_t &= \mu_Z(S_t) + \xi_t, \label{eq:state_obs} \\
    X_t &= \phi(X_{t-1}, S_t) + \epsilon_t, \label{eq:confounders} \\
    T_t &= m(X_t, S_t) + V_t, \quad \E[V_t \mid X_t, S_t] = 0, \label{eq:treatment} \\
    Y_t &= \theta(S_t) T_t + g(X_t, S_t) + U_t, \label{eq:outcome}
\end{align}
with $\E[U_t \mid X_t, S_t, T_t] = 0$, where:
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{-2pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
    \item $S_t$ is an unobserved discrete Markov regime with transition matrix $\Pi_{jk} = P(S_t = k \mid S_{t-1} = j)$ and stationary distribution $\pi^*$.
    \item $Z_t \in \R^q$ denotes observed auxiliary physical state indicators (e.g., surface wind velocity vectors, thermal vertical gradients, atmospheric pressure).
    \item $X_t \in \R^p$ is a vector of observed weather confounders and temporal lags.
    \item $T_t \in \R$ is the continuous treatment (precursor emission proxy, e.g., $\text{NO}_2$).
    \item $Y_t \in \R$ is the continuous outcome (ambient pollutant, e.g., $\text{PM}_{2.5}$).
    \item $\theta(S_t) = \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \one(S_t = k)$ represents the regime-specific causal effect.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Identification Under Latent Regimes}
Our objective is to identify and estimate the regime-specific marginal causal effects:
\begin{equation}
    \theta_k^* = \frac{\partial \E[Y_t \mid \text{do}(T_t = u), S_t = k]}{\partial u}, \quad k \in \{1, \dots, K\},
\end{equation}
as well as the stationary Average Treatment Effect (ATE):
\begin{equation}
    \theta_{\text{ATE}}^* = \sum_{k=1}^K \pi_k^* \theta_k^*.
\end{equation}

\begin{assumption}[Regime-Conditional Unconfoundedness \& Innovation Exogeneity]
\label{ass:unconfoundedness}
Conditional on observed covariates $X_t$, latent regime $S_t$, and proxy history $Z_{1:N}$, potential outcomes are unconfounded ($Y_t(u) \perp T_t \mid (X_t, S_t, Z_{1:N}), \forall u$) and structural innovations have zero mean:
\begin{equation}
    \E[U_t \mid X_t, S_t, T_t, Z_{1:N}] = 0.
\end{equation}
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Overlap \& State Positivity]
\label{ass:overlap}
\label{ass:nonsingularity}
There exist constants $\eta > 0$ and $c_1, c_2 > 0$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
    \item \textbf{State Positivity:} The Markov transition matrix $\boldsymbol{\Pi}$ has stationary distribution $\boldsymbol{\pi}^*$ satisfying $\min_{k} \pi_k^* \ge \eta$, and posterior state occupancies satisfy $P(S_t = k \mid X_t, Z_{1:N}) \ge \eta$ almost surely for all $k \in \{1, \dots, K\}$.
    \item \textbf{Within-Regime Treatment Overlap:} For all $k \in \{1, \dots, K\}$ and almost everywhere on $\mathrm{supp}(X_t)$:
    \begin{equation}
        c_1 \le \Var(T_t \mid X_t, S_t = k) \le c_2.
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Latent Proxy Sufficiency]
\label{ass:innovation}
The auxiliary state proxy sequence $Z_{1:N}$ satisfies latent proxy sufficiency: $P(S_t = k \mid X_t, T_t, Z_{1:N}) = P(S_t = k \mid Z_{1:N}) \equiv \gamma_{tk}$ (or $P(S_t = k \mid Z_{1:t})$ for causal forward filtering) almost surely for all $k \in \{1, \dots, K\}$.
\textit{Discussion.} That is, $Z_{1:N}$ screens off the latent state $S_t$ from $(X_t, T_t)$, framing it as a proxy-sufficiency identification restriction. Combined with Assumption~\ref{ass:unconfoundedness}, this guarantees $\E[Y_t \mid X_t, T_t, Z_{1:N}] = \sum_{k=1}^K \gamma_{tk} [\theta_k^* T_t + g_k(X_t)]$. This identifying restriction requires $Z_{1:N}$ to be sufficiently informative that conditioning on $(X_t, T_t)$ provides no additional regime information beyond $Z_{1:N}$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Nuisance Estimation and Mixing Rates]
\label{ass:rates}
\label{ass:mixing}
The data process $(Y_t, T_t, X_t, Z_t)$ is strictly stationary and geometrically $\alpha$-mixing with $\alpha(m) \le C_\alpha \exp(-c_\alpha m)$ for $C_\alpha, c_\alpha > 0$. Nuisance estimators satisfy $\|\hat{\mu}_Y - \mu_Y\|_{L_2(P)} \cdot \|\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_D - \boldsymbol{\mu}_D\|_{L_2(P)} = o_P(N^{-1/2})$, with individual convergence rates $o_P(N^{-1/4})$.
\end{assumption}

\section{FAILURE OF STANDARD DML: THE OMITTED REGIME BIAS}
\label{sec:bias}

\textbf{Intuition.} When unobserved regimes $S_t$ jointly shift treatment and outcome baselines, partialling out observed controls $X_t$ cannot purge latent regime variation. Instead, by reducing residual treatment variance in the denominator without eliminating the latent covariance in the numerator, standard cross-sectional residualization actively amplifies the omitted confounding.

\begin{theorem}[Decomposition of Omitted Regime Bias]
\label{thm:bias}
Suppose the data are generated according to SCM \eqref{eq:regime_scm}--\eqref{eq:outcome}. Let $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{naive}}$ be the standard cross-sectional DML estimator \citep{chernozhukov2018double} that conditions only on observed covariates $X_t$, omitting $S_t$. Define the naive cross-sectional residuals $\tilde{T}_t = T_t - \E[T_t \mid X_t]$ and $\tilde{Y}_t = Y_t - \E[Y_t \mid X_t]$. 

Then, as $N \to \infty$, $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{naive}}$ converges in probability to:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{naive}} \xrightarrow{p} \sum_{k=1}^K \omega_k \theta_k^* + \mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{heterogeneity}} + \mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{confounding}},
\end{equation}
where $\omega_k = \frac{\E[\one(S_t = k) \tilde{T}_t^2]}{\E[\tilde{T}_t^2]}$ with $\sum_k \omega_k = 1$, and the bias components are:
\begin{align}
    \mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{heterogeneity}} &= \frac{\sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \E\left[ \bar{m}(X_t) \Delta m_k(X_t) \pi_k(X_t) \right]}{\E[\tilde{T}_t^2]}, \label{eq:b_het} \\
    \mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{confounding}} &= \frac{\sum_{k=1}^K \E\left[ \pi_k(X_t) \Delta m_k(X_t) \Delta g_k(X_t) \right]}{\E[\tilde{T}_t^2]}, \label{eq:b_conf}
\end{align}
with $\pi_k(X_t) \equiv P(S_t = k \mid X_t)$, $\bar{m}(X_t) \equiv \E[T_t \mid X_t]$, $\Delta m_k(X_t) \equiv \E[T_t \mid X_t, S_t = k] - \bar{m}(X_t)$, and $\Delta g_k(X_t) \equiv g(X_t, k) - \E[g(X_t, S_t) \mid X_t]$ (complete algebraic proof in Appendix~\ref{app:proof_thm1}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Sign Inversion \& Bias Amplification]
When latent stagnation induces strong positive confounding ($\Delta m_k(X_t) \Delta g_k(X_t) \gg 0$), but residual treatment variance $\E[\tilde{T}_t^2]$ is attenuated due to conditioning on $X_t$, the omitted confounding bias $\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{confounding}}$ undergoes severe \emph{bias amplification}. In coastal airsheds with negative covariance phases, $\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{confounding}} < -\sum_k \omega_k \theta_k^*$, driving $\mathrm{plim}(\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{naive}}) < 0$ despite $\theta_k^* > 0$.
\end{corollary}
\textbf{Interpretation.} Standard DML cannot eliminate omitted regime confounding: conditioning on observed controls $X_t$ shrinks residual treatment variance $\E[\tilde{T}_t^2]$ faster than it reduces latent covariance, actively amplifying bias.

\section{METHODOLOGY: OVERLAP-AWARE REGIME DML}
\label{sec:method}

To overcome the failure of standard cross-sectional DML, we introduce \textbf{Overlap-Aware Regime Double Machine Learning (OR-DML)}.

\subsection{Latent Regime Formulation \& Observability Diagnostics}
Let $Z_t \in \R^q$ denote exogenous contemporaneous meteorological covariates assumed unaffected by the exposure proxy. We model the latent atmospheric state $S_t \in \{1, \dots, K\}$ via a Hidden Markov Model with transition matrix $\boldsymbol{\Pi}$ and emission parameters $\boldsymbol{\phi}$.
Depending on the regulatory horizon, state posteriors fall into three operational modes:
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{-2pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
    \item \textbf{Retrospective Smoothed Posteriors (Offline):} $\gamma_{tk}^{\mathrm{smooth}} \equiv P(S_t = k \mid Z_{1:N})$ for retrospective environmental epidemiology and structural causal auditing;
    \item \textbf{Training-Fitted Forward Filtered Posteriors:} $\gamma_{tk}^{\mathrm{filter}} \equiv P(S_t = k \mid Z_{1:t}, \hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}})$ where dynamics $\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}$ are calibrated on a historical window strictly preceding evaluation ($\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{cal}} < \mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{eval}}$), with state updates proceeding strictly causally on $Z_{1:t}$; or
    \item \textbf{Expanding-Window Online Filter:} where both parameter estimation and forward filtering update causally without access to future measurements.
\end{enumerate}
Our empirical and benchmark implementations evaluate Mode 1 (offline smoothing) and Mode 2 (training-fitted forward filtering). We define the mean posterior entropy as a direct diagnostic of latent state observability:
\begin{equation}
    \bar{H}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}) \equiv -\frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N \sum_{k=1}^K \gamma_{tk} \ln(\gamma_{tk} + \epsilon).
\end{equation}
\emph{A Priori Diagnostics:} Standardized separation $\Delta_Z < 0.5$ or entropy $\bar{H} \to \ln K$ signals severe proxy uncertainty and triggers a weak-identification diagnostic warning (rules in Appendix~\ref{app:proxy_diagnostics}), distinguishing structural non-identification ($\Delta_Z = 0$, Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility}) from finite-sample proxy dilution.

\subsection{State-Weighted Coupled System \& Spectral Regularization}
To avoid heuristic decoupled regressions that suffer cross-talk bias under regime overlap, OR-DML estimates regime-specific conditional expectations via state-weighted regressions targeting population expectations $m_k^\gamma(x) \equiv \frac{\E[\gamma_{tk} T_t \mid X_t=x]}{\E[\gamma_{tk} \mid X_t=x]}$ and $\mu_k^\gamma(x) \equiv \frac{\E[\gamma_{tk} Y_t \mid X_t=x]}{\E[\gamma_{tk} \mid X_t=x]}$:
\begin{align}
    \hat{m}_k(X) &= \arg\min_{m} \sum_{t \in \mathcal{T}_b} \gamma_{tk} (T_t - m(X_t))^2, \label{eq:nuisance_m} \\
    \hat{\mu}_k(X) &= \arg\min_{\mu} \sum_{t \in \mathcal{T}_b} \gamma_{tk} (Y_t - \mu(X_t))^2. \label{eq:nuisance_mu}
\end{align}
On evaluation fold $\mathcal{B}_b$, we form centered residuals $\tilde{T}_{tk} = T_t - \hat{m}_k(X_t)$ and $\tilde{Y}_{tk} = Y_t - \hat{\mu}_k(X_t)$. The empirical coupled Gram matrix $\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} \in \R^{K \times K}$ and score vector $\hat{\boldsymbol{S}} \in \R^K$ are constructed as:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{J}_{jk} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N \gamma_{tj}\gamma_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tj} \tilde{T}_{tk}, \quad \hat{S}_k = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N \gamma_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tk} \tilde{Y}_{tk}.
\end{equation}
When regime separation is weak or treatments are collinear, $\lambda_{\min}(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}) \to 0$, rendering the unregularized solve $\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}^{-1}\hat{\boldsymbol{S}}$ ill-conditioned. OR-DML introduces the \textbf{Spectrally Regularized Estimator}:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:theta_spectral}
    \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda = (\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \hat{\boldsymbol{S}},
\end{equation}
where $\lambda \ge 0$ trades off regularization bias against collinearity variance.
\emph{Scalability \& Structural Abstention:} End-to-end complexity is $\mathcal{O}(B K \cdot C_{\mathrm{nuisance}} + N K^2 + K^3)$, with Gram construction taking $\mathcal{O}(N K^2)$ and Cholesky solve taking $\mathcal{O}(K^3)$ (Appendix~\ref{app:state_space_scalability}); when data lack identifying variation entirely ($\kappa(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}) > 10^4$ or $\text{CI}(\lambda_{\min})$ covers 0), systems should diagnose weak identification and abstain rather than masking near-singularity with excessive shrinkage.
We distinguish three causal estimand targets: (i) the true stationary population ATE $\theta_{\mathrm{PATE}}^* = \boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top} \boldsymbol{\theta}^*$, (ii) the estimated stationary ATE $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{PATE}} = \hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}})^\top \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda$, and (iii) the empirical sample occupation ATE $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SATE}} = \bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_N^\top \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda$, where $\bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_N \equiv \frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t$.

\subsection{Purged Block Cross-Fitting with Bartlett Embargo}
To ensure validity under temporal dependence, we partition observations into $B$ contiguous blocks. To eliminate cross-fold leakage under $\alpha$-mixing, each test block $\mathcal{B}_b$ is surrounded by an embargo buffer of width $\tau^*$ (Algorithm~\ref{alg:purged_cv}). We determine $\tau^*$ from the data via the Bartlett significance threshold:
\begin{equation}
    \tau^* = \max \left\{ h \ge 1 : \max_{j} \left| \hat{\rho}_j(h) \right| \ge \frac{1.96}{\sqrt{N}} \right\} + 1.
\end{equation}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Overlap-Aware Regime DML (OR-DML)}
\label{alg:purged_cv}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Series $\{Y_t, T_t, X_t, Z_t\}_{t=1}^N$, blocks $B$, embargo $\tau^*$, penalty $\lambda \ge 0$.
\STATE Infer posteriors $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t$ (Mode 1 smoothing or Mode 2 forward filtering).
\STATE Cross-fit state-weighted nuisances $\hat{m}_k, \hat{\mu}_k$ with embargo buffer $\tau^*$.
\STATE Form centered residuals $\tilde{T}_{tk} = T_t - \hat{m}_k(X_t)$ and $\tilde{Y}_{tk} = Y_t - \hat{\mu}_k(X_t)$.
\STATE Form Gram $\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_t \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t^\top \odot \tilde{\boldsymbol{T}}_t\tilde{\boldsymbol{T}}_t^\top$ and score $\hat{\boldsymbol{S}} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_t \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t \odot \tilde{\boldsymbol{T}}_t \odot \tilde{\boldsymbol{Y}}_t$.
\STATE Compute $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda = (\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1}\hat{\boldsymbol{S}}$ and diagnostics $(\lambda_{\min}, \kappa, \bar{H})$.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{THEORETICAL ANALYSIS}
\label{sec:theory}

We formalize three central results: (1)~\textbf{Non-Identification:} latent context can induce unidentifiability; (2)~\textbf{Graceful Degradation:} proxy error scales as $\varepsilon_\gamma/\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})$; and (3)~\textbf{Observable Difficulty \& Regularization:} task conditioning yields an observable surrogate for risk control.

\textbf{Intuition.} If auxiliary proxies $Z_t$ contain zero information distinguishing latent regimes, different underlying causal structures generate observationally indistinguishable joint distributions, rendering point identification impossible regardless of sample size.

\begin{theorem}[Non-Identification Under Unconstrained Overlap]
\label{thm:impossibility}
Suppose the latent state is unobserved, and auxiliary proxies $Z_t$ satisfy $P(Z_t \mid S_t = j) = P(Z_t \mid S_t = k)$ for all $j, k$. Then the regime-specific causal effect vector $\boldsymbol{\theta}^* = (\theta_1^*, \dots, \theta_K^*)^\top$ is not point-identified from the joint distribution of observables $(Y_t, T_t, X_t, Z_t)$: there exist structural causal models $P_1$ and $P_2$ satisfying Assumptions~\ref{ass:unconfoundedness}--\ref{ass:innovation} with identical joint observable laws $P_1(Y_{1:N}, T_{1:N}, X_{1:N}, Z_{1:N}) \equiv P_2(Y_{1:N}, T_{1:N}, X_{1:N}, Z_{1:N})$ for all $N \ge 1$, yet possessing strictly distinct, non-permutation causal effect vectors $\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(1)} \neq \boldsymbol{\theta}^{(2)}$.
\end{theorem}
\textbf{Interpretation.} No estimator can recover regime-specific effects when the proxy provides no state information; this is a fundamental limitation of the observable information structure, not of optimization or sample size (proof in Appendix~\ref{app:proof_thm2}).

\textbf{Intuition.} Downstream estimation error has two qualitatively different sources: posterior proxy error $\varepsilon_\gamma$ from imperfect state inference, and causal ill-conditioning $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})$ from collinear residual treatment variation. Theorem~\ref{thm:graceful} proves that these mechanisms enter multiplicatively.

\begin{theorem}[Graceful Degradation Error Bound]
\label{thm:graceful}
Let $\boldsymbol{H}_t = \boldsymbol{e}_{S_t} \in \{0, 1\}^K$ denote the unobserved true regime indicator, and let $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t \in \Delta^K$ be an estimated posterior state proxy with average $L_1$ proxy error $\varepsilon_\gamma \equiv \E\|\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t - \boldsymbol{H}_t\|_1$. Suppose $\|\boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2 \le M_\theta < \infty$, $\sup_t |\tilde{T}_{tk}^\gamma| \le M_T < \infty$ almost surely, and regime functions satisfy $\max_{j, k}|g(x, j) - g(x, k)| \le L_g < \infty$ and $\max_{j, k}|m_j^\gamma(x) - m_k^\gamma(x)| \le L_m < \infty$.
Let $\boldsymbol{J} \in \R^{K \times K}$ with $J_{jk} \equiv \E[\gamma_{tj}\gamma_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tj}^\gamma \tilde{T}_{tk}^\gamma]$ and $\boldsymbol{S} \in \R^K$ with $S_k \equiv \E[\gamma_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tk}^\gamma \tilde{Y}_{tk}^\gamma]$ denote the population coupled Gram matrix and score vector targeting implemented proxy-weighted nuisances $(m_k^\gamma, \mu_k^\gamma)$. If $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) > 0$, the population estimand $\boldsymbol{\theta}_\gamma \equiv \boldsymbol{J}^{-1}\boldsymbol{S}$ satisfies:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:graceful_bound}
    \|\boldsymbol{\theta}_\gamma - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2 \le \frac{C \cdot \varepsilon_\gamma}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})},
\end{equation}
and the unregularized cross-fitted estimator $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\gamma = \hat{\boldsymbol{J}}^{-1}\hat{\boldsymbol{S}}$ satisfies $\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\gamma - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2 \le \frac{C \cdot \varepsilon_\gamma}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})} + \mathcal{O}_P(N^{-1/2})$, where $C = C_S + M_\theta C_J < \infty$ depends on bounded moments and envelope constants (Appendix~\ref{app:proof_thm3}).
\end{theorem}
\textbf{Interpretation.} Better state recovery alone is not enough: when downstream task geometry is ill-conditioned ($\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) \approx 0$), even tiny proxy errors are catastrophically amplified into large causal bias.

Theorem~\ref{thm:graceful} replaces heuristic orthogonality claims with a rigorous bound connecting \emph{posterior proxy error} $\varepsilon_\gamma$ and \emph{Jacobian conditioning} $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})$. While the score is Neyman-orthogonal to continuous nuisances $(\mu_k, m_k)$, it is \emph{not} orthogonal to $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t$; latent proxy uncertainty carries an irremediable statistical price.

\paragraph{The Difficulty Frontier and Operational Surrogate Bridge.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:graceful} demonstrates that asymptotic bias scales with theoretical ratio $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{causal}} \equiv \varepsilon_\gamma / \lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})$, where $\varepsilon_\gamma \equiv \E\|\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t - \boldsymbol{H}_t\|_1$ is evaluated in simulation via $\hat{\varepsilon}_{\gamma, N} \equiv \frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N \|\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t - \boldsymbol{H}_t\|_1$. Because true states are unobserved in deployment, we bridge unobservable $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{causal}}$ to computable operational index $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{operational}}(t) \equiv H(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t) / \lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}_t)$ via the following result under spectral comparability:
\begin{assumption}[Two-Sided Spectral Comparability]
\label{ass:spectral_comparability}
There exist constants $0 < \underline{c}_J \le \overline{c}_J < \infty$ such that rolling Gram matrices $\boldsymbol{J}_t$ satisfy $\underline{c}_J \le \frac{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}_t)}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})} \le \overline{c}_J$ almost surely for all evaluation windows $t \ge w$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{proposition}[Surrogate Entropy-Difficulty Bridge]
\label{prop:bridge}
Under correct posterior specification (Bayesian calibration) in binary regimes ($K=2$), for causal forward-filtered posteriors $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t = P(S_t \in \cdot \mid \mathcal{F}_t)$ with filtration $\mathcal{F}_t = \sigma(Z_{1:t})$, the posterior error satisfies $\E[\|\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t - \boldsymbol{H}_t\|_1 \mid \mathcal{F}_t] = 2 \operatorname{Gini}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t) \le C_K H(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t)$, where $C_K \le 1/\ln 2 \approx 1.4427$.
We define the difficulty hierarchy: $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{causal}} \equiv \frac{\varepsilon_\gamma}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})}$, $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{global}}^H(t) \equiv \frac{H(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t)}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})}$, and $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{operational}}(t) \equiv \frac{H(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t)}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}_t)}$.
(i) Causal difficulty is unconditionally bounded in population by global entropy: $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{causal}} \le C_K \E\left[ \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{global}}^H(t) \right]$.
(ii) Under Assumption~\ref{ass:spectral_comparability}, the deployable index satisfies:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:bridge_bound}
    \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{causal}} \le C_K \overline{c}_J \E[\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{operational}}(t)] \le \frac{C_K \overline{c}_J}{\underline{c}_J} \E[\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{global}}^H(t)].
\end{equation}
In simulation benchmarks where $H_t$ is observed, $\hat{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathrm{causal}, N} \equiv \hat{\varepsilon}_{\gamma, N} / \lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})$ converges to $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{causal}}$ with $\mathcal{O}_P(N^{-1/2})$ error for population posteriors $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*)$ (preserved under $\sqrt{N}$-consistent $\hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}$).
\end{proposition}
\textbf{Interpretation.} Proposition~\ref{prop:bridge} grounds deployable monitoring by turning an unobservable theoretical difficulty into an observable surrogate upper bound, establishing that posterior entropy bounds proxy error under Bayesian calibration.

\textbf{Intuition.} When treatments are collinear or regimes overlap, $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) \to 0$ causes the unregularized covariance to blow up. Introducing a spectral ridge penalty $\lambda > 0$ bounds the effective condition number, trading finite-sample regularization bias for bounded estimation variance.

\begin{theorem}[Leading-Order Risk Bound under Long-Run Covariance Approximation]
\label{thm:spectral}
Let $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda = (\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1}\hat{\boldsymbol{S}}$ be the spectrally regularized OR-DML estimator, and let $\boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda = (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1}\boldsymbol{S}$. Under Theorem~\ref{thm:graceful}, the regularized estimand satisfies the exact deterministic identity $\boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^* = (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \left( \boldsymbol{b}_\gamma - \lambda \boldsymbol{\theta}^* \right)$, where $\|\boldsymbol{b}_\gamma\|_2 \le C \varepsilon_\gamma$. Furthermore, under long-run score covariance $\boldsymbol{\Omega} = \lim_{N \to \infty} \Var(N^{-1/2}\sum_t \boldsymbol{\Psi}_t)$, expected squared estimation error satisfies the leading-order risk bound:
\begin{equation}
    \E\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2^2 \le \frac{(C \varepsilon_\gamma + \lambda M_\theta)^2 + N^{-1}\mathrm{Tr}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}{(\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) + \lambda)^2} + \mathcal{O}(N^{-1}),
\end{equation}
accounting for stochastic expansion $\E\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda\|_2^2 = \frac{1}{N}\mathrm{Tr}((\boldsymbol{J}+\lambda\boldsymbol{I})^{-1}\boldsymbol{\Omega}(\boldsymbol{J}+\lambda\boldsymbol{I})^{-1}) + o(N^{-1})$ and deterministic shrinkage (Appendix~\ref{app:proof_thm4}), sharpening to $\mathcal{O}(N^{-3/2})$ under $\varepsilon_\gamma + \lambda = \mathcal{O}(N^{-1/2})$. In practice, plug-in surrogate $\hat{R}(\lambda)$ yields $\hat{\lambda}^*_{\mathrm{emp}} = \arg\min_{\lambda \in [0, \bar{\lambda}]} \hat{R}(\lambda)$ (Appendix~\ref{app:adaptive_lambda_derivation}, where $\lambda = 0.10$ achieves an empirical $23.0\times$ risk reduction in ill-conditioned benchmarks with $85.0\%$ coverage), distinct from vanishing penalty $\lambda = o(N^{-1/2})$ for centered inference.
\end{theorem}
\textbf{Interpretation.} Regularization provides finite-sample variance control when overlap is weak, but cannot manufacture causal information that is absent from the proxy distribution.

\begin{theorem}[Asymptotic Normality Under Dependent Purged Cross-Fitting]
\label{thm:asymptotics}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:unconfoundedness}--\ref{ass:rates}, with embargo buffer $\tau^* \ge C \log N$, the regularized OR-DML estimator under training-fitted forward filtering satisfies:
\begin{equation}
    \sqrt{N}\left( \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^* - \mathrm{Bias}(\lambda) \right) \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}\left(\mathbf{0}, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\lambda\right),
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\lambda = (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \boldsymbol{\Omega} (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1}$ (with retrospective smoothing providing an offline descriptive counterpart). When $\lambda = o(N^{-1/2})$, $\sqrt{N}\mathrm{Bias}(\lambda) \to \mathbf{0}$, recovering centered asymptotic normality (proof in Appendix~\ref{app:proof_thm5}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[Joint Asymptotic Distribution for Sample and Population ATE]
\label{prop:variance}
The sample ATE $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SATE}} = \bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}^\top \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda$ has conditional variance $\sigma_{\mathrm{SATE}}^2 = \bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}^\top \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\lambda \bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}$, where Markov persistence ($\rho \approx 0.88$) inflates the long-run variance of sample state occupation $\bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_N$ by $\frac{1+\rho}{1-\rho} \approx 15.7\times$ relative to i.i.d.\ transitions. The population ATE $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{PATE}} = \hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}})^\top \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda$ satisfies the joint delta expansion: $\sqrt{N}(\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{PATE}} - \theta_{\mathrm{PATE}}^*) = \boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top} \sqrt{N}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*) + \boldsymbol{\theta}^{*\top} \sqrt{N}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}} - \boldsymbol{\pi}^*) + o_P(1)$, yielding asymptotic variance:
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_{\mathrm{PATE}}^2 = \boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\lambda \boldsymbol{\pi}^* + \boldsymbol{\theta}^{*\top} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\pi \boldsymbol{\theta}^* + 2 \boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\theta \pi} \boldsymbol{\theta}^*,
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\pi = \nabla_\Lambda \boldsymbol{\pi}^* \mathcal{I}_\Lambda^{-1} (\nabla_\Lambda \boldsymbol{\pi}^*)^\top$ represents the parametric variance of the estimated stationary distribution derived from sequence-level HMM influence function $\boldsymbol{\phi}_{\Lambda, t}$, and $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\theta\pi} = \mathrm{LRCov}(\boldsymbol{\phi}_{\theta, t}, \boldsymbol{\phi}_{\Lambda, t})$. When $\operatorname{LRCov}(\boldsymbol{\phi}_\theta, \boldsymbol{\phi}_\Lambda) = \mathbf{0}$, $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\theta\pi} = \mathbf{0}$; in general, $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\theta \pi}$ is consistently estimated via the stacked joint HAC sandwich estimator.
\end{proposition}

\section{THE LATENT-CONFOUNDING DIFFICULTY FRONTIER}
\label{sec:experiments}

To test the theoretical predictions of Theorems~\ref{thm:impossibility}--\ref{thm:spectral}, we conduct a comprehensive Monte Carlo evaluation across 500 evaluation runs (100 replications per grid point), charting causal estimation error across the \textbf{Latent-Confounding Difficulty Frontier}.

\textbf{Experimental Protocol.} \emph{DGPs \& Baselines:} 100 Monte Carlo draws per grid point across $\Delta_Z \in \{0.2, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0, 4.0\}$ ($N=1,200$, $\theta_{\mathrm{ATE}}^* = 1.5278$) comparing Oracle, Standard DML, Block DML, continuous proxy controls (Ridge and GBM), hard-clustering Regime FE, and OR-DML (Spectral and Filtered). \emph{Representations \& Shifts:} HMM, GRU, Transformer, and SSM under clean and compound shifts. \emph{Reliability Target:} Predicting downstream squared loss via difficulty $\mathcal{D}_t$ across 5 seeds with MBB. \emph{Real-World Test:} $>14,000$ hourly observations across four megacities.

\textbf{Difficulty Frontier Findings.} Table~\ref{tab:benchmark} and Figure~\ref{fig:difficulty_frontier} reveal three phenomena:
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{-3pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
    \item \textbf{Low-Observability Breakdown ($\Delta_Z \le 1.0$):} Standard and Block DML exhibit constant omitted-regime bias ($+8.43\text{--}8.49$, RMSE $8.45$) and $0.0\%$ coverage across all $\Delta_Z$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:bias}). Crucially, under severe overlap ($\Delta_Z \le 0.5$), \emph{all} methods fail: proxy error is severe ($\hat{\varepsilon}_\gamma = 0.78$), yielding large bias ($6.59\text{--}8.83$). At moderate observability ($\Delta_Z = 1.0$), all estimators still suffer substantial proxy bias ($\ge 2.64$). In this regime, identification fails by Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility}; high operational difficulty $\mathcal{D}_t$ acts as an explicit \emph{abstention trigger}.
    \item \textbf{High-Observability Resolution ($\Delta_Z \ge 2.0$):} When regimes separate cleanly ($\Delta_Z \ge 2.0$), both Spectral OR-DML and hard-assignment Regime FE eliminate omitted confounding: at $\Delta_Z=2.0$, OR-DML achieves mean bias $0.081$ (median $0.072$) and Regime FE achieves $0.077$ (median $0.063$), outperforming continuous proxy controls ($Z$-GBM bias $0.207$). At $\Delta_Z=4.0$, both methods match Oracle DML (bias $0.054$). In well-conditioned benchmarks ($\lambda_{\min} \approx 0.45$), spectral shrinkage has negligible effect on point bias ($0.0812$ vs.\ $0.0823$), functioning as insurance against near-singular ill-conditioning. While hard Regime FE lacks distribution theory under dependence, OR-DML preserves probabilistic state inference with valid delta-method sandwich coverage ($92.0\% / 97.0\%$, matching Oracle $93.0\% / 97.0\%$).
    \item \textbf{Filtering vs.\ Smoothing:} Causal forward filtering ($\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t^{\mathrm{filter}}$) tracks retrospective smoothing ($\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t^{\mathrm{smooth}}$) closely across separated regimes (at $\Delta_Z = 2.0$, median bias $0.0812$ vs.\ $0.0720$; at $\Delta_Z = 4.0$, identical at $0.0462$), incurring zero efficiency loss once dynamics are informed by physical history.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Factorial Mechanism Validation.} Independently varying the two quantities in Theorem~\ref{thm:graceful} produces a sharp interaction in this controlled two-state design: across 4,900 runs, $\mathcal D_{\mathrm{causal}}=\varepsilon_\gamma/\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol J)$ ranks $\|\hat{\boldsymbol\theta}-\boldsymbol\theta^*\|_2$ with Spearman $\rho=0.995$, compared with $0.626$ for proxy error alone and $0.521$ for $1/\lambda_{\min}$ alone (cell-mean $\rho=0.996$). With perfect states, mean error rises from $0.050$ to $0.545$ as residual information weakens; with only $\varepsilon_\gamma=0.01$, the same geometry change amplifies mean error from $0.686$ to $131.3$. This intervention varies treatment innovation variance rather than treatment means, so the conditioning manipulation survives correct regime-aware residualization.

\textbf{Representation Quality vs.\ Downstream Reliability.} State discrimination, probabilistic calibration, and task reliability are distinct. Across 15 shifted worlds, adding NLL to $F_1$ improves leave-one-world-out reliability prediction from $R^2=0.113$ ($\rho=0.374$) to $R^2=0.221$ ($\rho=0.527$). Yet temperature scaling reduces GRU/Transformer ECE by $47.9\%/60.3\%$ and NLL from $0.548/1.006$ to $0.331/0.382$ without improving downstream difficulty ROC-AUC ($\Delta\mathrm{AUC}=-0.004/-0.008$). Thus posterior quality is informative but calibration alone does not repair downstream reliability; task geometry remains a separate bottleneck (Appendix~\ref{app:latent_regime_bench}).

\section{EMPIRICAL SENSOR NETWORK STRESS TEST}
\label{sec:empirical}
We evaluate OR-DML on our verified multi-season dataset ($14,122$ hourly records across Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, and Kolkata, Feb 2025 -- Jun 2026; $<0.7\%$ forward-fill; Table~\ref{tab:empirical}), treating the sensor study as an observational stress test of the estimator's diagnostics rather than independent ground truth for physical causal effects. Sensor records exhibit missingness and occasional repeat telemetry values characteristic of municipal sensor deployments; complete-case sensitivity and Newey-West HAC lag sensitivity across $h \in \{12, 24, 72, 168\}$ hours are reported in Appendix~\ref{app:empirical_sensitivity}.

\textbf{Conditioning Diagnostics.} \emph{Delhi Basin} ($\lambda_{\min} = 111.8$): Clean separation decomposes pooled effect ($+0.47 \pm 0.04$) into Regime 1 ($+0.70 \pm 0.05$, winter inversion) and Regime 2 ($+0.13 \pm 0.07$, clearance). \emph{Mumbai Coastal} ($\lambda_{\min}=0.32$): Standard DML is unstable ($+2.64\pm4.42$); the nominal OR-DML decomposition is Regime 2 $+3.30\pm1.48$ ($p=0.026$) versus Regime 1 $-0.03\pm8.72$. This decomposition is telemetry-sensitive: removing $\ge6$h frozen $\mathrm{NO}_2$ runs changes $N$ from $4{,}180$ to $3{,}295$, the overall estimate from $1.59$ to $2.92$, and $\lambda_{\min}$ from $0.317$ to $0.178$ (Appendix~\ref{app:empirical_sensitivity}); we therefore do not interpret the nominal regime contrast as a stable physical causal effect. \emph{Bengaluru Plateau} ($\lambda_{\min}=4.22$): Standard DML estimates near zero ($-0.001\pm0.060$); OR-DML yields Regime 1 $+0.092\pm0.155$ and Regime 2 $-0.153\pm0.069$ ($p=0.027$ nominally). The latter weakens under longer HAC truncation, so we report it as observational heterogeneity rather than a mechanistically identified photochemical effect. \emph{Reliability Contrast}: In Bengaluru, healthy conditioning renders state uncertainty informative ($\text{AUC}(H) = 0.674$), whereas Mumbai's ill-conditioning blinds raw entropy ($\text{AUC}(H) = 0.486$), requiring coupled index $\mathcal{D}_t$ ($\text{AUC} = 0.531\text{--}0.564$; Table~\ref{tab:failure_ablation}). \emph{Kolkata Airshed} ($\lambda_{\min} = 0.0025$): Restricted temporal coverage ($N=1,209$, Feb--Jun 2025) acts as an empirical diagnostic of near-singular ill-conditioning; unregularized estimates explode ($\text{SE} = 1.11$), while spectral regularization stabilizes variance ($\text{SE} \to 0.060$ at $\lambda=0.10$; Appendix~\ref{app:empirical_sensitivity}). \emph{Dynamic Exposure Projections}: $Y_{t+h} = \theta_h(S_t) T_t + g_h(X_t, S_t) + U_{t+h}$ peak at $h=2$h in Delhi (ATE $+1.31$, Regime 1 $+2.35$, decaying to $+0.28$ at 12h), but Delhi also fails pre-treatment falsification at $h=-6,-3,-1$ (all $p<0.01$). We therefore interpret its dynamic curve as an exposure-response diagnostic rather than a validated causal impulse response; Mumbai, Bengaluru, and Kolkata do not reject the immediate pre-treatment placebos (Appendix~\ref{app:empirical_sensitivity}).

\section{DISCUSSION, LIMITATIONS \& CONCLUSION}
\label{sec:conclusion}
The central conclusion is deliberately narrower than the proposition that better state recovery implies better causal inference. Representation error and downstream information are separate axes, and their interaction determines whether a generated latent state is useful for a particular causal task. The scalar $\varepsilon_\gamma/\lambda_{\min}$ is effective in the controlled two-state frontier and gives a worst-direction bound, but higher-dimensional stress tests show that orientation relative to the full spectrum matters; it should not be treated as a universal representation score. Likewise, entropy and the operational ratio are diagnostics, not identification guarantees.

\textbf{Limitations:} (1)~\emph{Surrogate and geometry assumptions:} Proposition~\ref{prop:bridge} requires calibration and spectral comparability; generated-state mixing can distort proxy information geometry, so a large proxy $\lambda_{\min}$ is not by itself evidence that oracle causal information is healthy. (2)~\emph{Observational effects:} the sensor study is a stress test, not causal ground truth. Delhi fails all reported immediate pre-treatment placebos; Mumbai is materially sensitive to frozen-sensor removal; Bengaluru significance weakens under longer HAC lags; Kolkata remains near-singular. (3)~\emph{Representation benchmark:} $F_1$, ECE, and NLL predict reliability across worlds, but temperature scaling does not improve failure AUC and the coupled score does not uniformly beat entropy. (4)~\emph{Synthetic transfer:} the financial experiment is a controlled stress test, not evidence of live-trading alpha. These failures delimit rather than weaken the main message: reliable latent-state causal analysis requires separate checks of representation quality, task information, temporal validity, and scientific identification.

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:bias}: Decomposition of Omitted Regime Bias}
\label{app:proof_thm1}
Consider the Structural Causal Model \eqref{eq:regime_scm}--\eqref{eq:outcome}:
\begin{align*}
    T_t &= m(X_t, S_t) + V_t, \quad \E[V_t \mid X_t, S_t] = 0, \\
    Y_t &= \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \one(S_t = k) T_t + g(X_t, S_t) + U_t, \quad \E[U_t \mid X_t, S_t, T_t] = 0.
\end{align*}
Define the unconditioned expectations: $\bar{m}(X_t) \equiv \E[T_t \mid X_t] = \sum_{k=1}^K \pi_k(X_t) m_k(X_t)$ and $\bar{g}(X_t) \equiv \E[g(X_t, S_t) \mid X_t] = \sum_{k=1}^K \pi_k(X_t) g_k(X_t)$, where $\pi_k(X_t) \equiv P(S_t = k \mid X_t)$.
Residuals from cross-fitting on $X_t$ alone satisfy $\tilde{T}_t = T_t - \bar{m}(X_t) = \sum_{k=1}^K \one(S_t=k) \Delta m_k(X_t) + V_t$, where $\Delta m_k(X_t) = m_k(X_t) - \bar{m}(X_t)$.
The outcome residual is:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{Y}_t = Y_t - \E[Y_t \mid X_t] = \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \one(S_t=k) T_t - \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \pi_k(X_t) m_k(X_t) + g(X_t, S_t) - \bar{g}(X_t) + U_t.
\end{equation}
Writing $T_t = \tilde{T}_t + \bar{m}(X_t)$, the treatment interaction terms decompose as:
\begin{align}
    \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \one(S_t=k) T_t - \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \pi_k(X_t) m_k(X_t) &= \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \one(S_t=k)\tilde{T}_t + \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \left[\one(S_t=k)\bar{m}(X_t) - \pi_k(X_t)m_k(X_t)\right].
\end{align}
Taking the inner product with $\tilde{T}_t$ and evaluating expectations:
(i) By Assumption~\ref{ass:unconfoundedness}, $\E[\tilde{T}_t U_t] = 0$.
(ii) $\E[\tilde{T}_t \sum_k \theta_k^* \one(S_t=k) \tilde{T}_t] = \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \E[\one(S_t=k)\tilde{T}_t^2] = \E[\tilde{T}_t^2] \sum_{k=1}^K \omega_k \theta_k^*$, where $\omega_k = \frac{\E[\one(S_t=k)\tilde{T}_t^2]}{\E[\tilde{T}_t^2]}$ with $\sum_k \omega_k = 1$.
(iii) Since $\E[\tilde{T}_t \mid X_t] = 0$ and $\E[\tilde{T}_t \one(S_t=k) \mid X_t] = \Delta m_k(X_t) \pi_k(X_t)$, the mean adjustment yields $\sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \E[\bar{m}(X_t) \Delta m_k(X_t) \pi_k(X_t)]$.
(iv) The confounding component evaluates to $\E[\tilde{T}_t (g(X_t, S_t) - \bar{g}(X_t))] = \sum_{k=1}^K \E[\pi_k(X_t) \Delta m_k(X_t) \Delta g_k(X_t)]$.
Dividing by $\E[\tilde{T}_t^2]$ yields \eqref{eq:b_het} and \eqref{eq:b_conf}. $\blacksquare$

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility}: Non-Identification Under Unconstrained Overlap}
\label{app:proof_thm2}
To prove substantive causal non-identification, we constructively exhibit two data-generating distributions, $P_1$ and $P_2$, that are structurally well-formed under the SCM \eqref{eq:regime_scm}--\eqref{eq:outcome} and satisfy all Assumptions~\ref{ass:unconfoundedness}--\ref{ass:innovation}, yet possess strictly distinct, non-permutation causal effect vectors $\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(1)} \neq \boldsymbol{\theta}^{(2)}$ while inducing identically equal joint distributions over all observable variables:
\begin{equation}
    P_1(Y_{1:N}, T_{1:N}, X_{1:N}, Z_{1:N}) \equiv P_2(Y_{1:N}, T_{1:N}, X_{1:N}, Z_{1:N}) \quad \forall N \ge 1.
\end{equation}

\textbf{Construction of Substantive Observational Equivalence under Latent Confounding ($T \not\perp S$):}
Consider a two-state Markov chain ($K=2$) with symmetric transition probability $P_{01} = P_{10} = p \in (0, 0.5)$, yielding stationary distribution $\pi_0^* = \pi_1^* = 0.5$.
Let auxiliary meteorological proxies $Z_t \in \R$ satisfy complete overlap: $Z_t \mid S_t = 0 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$ and $Z_t \mid S_t = 1 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$ identically, so $Z_t \perp S_t$.
Let continuous observed confounders $X_t \in \R$ have marginal density $f_X(x)$ independent of $S_t$.
Let binary treatment assignment $T_t \in \{0, 1\}$ depend directly on the latent regime:
$P(T_t = 1 \mid S_t = 0) = 0.75$ and $P(T_t = 1 \mid S_t = 1) = 0.25$,
so that $T_t \not\perp S_t$, with propensity scores $e(X_t, S_t) \in [0.25, 0.75]$ strictly satisfying Assumption~\ref{ass:overlap} (Overlap \& State Positivity with $\eta = 0.25$).
Marginally, $P(T_t = 1) = 0.5 \times 0.75 + 0.5 \times 0.25 = 0.5$, and by Bayes' rule, the posterior regime occupancy given treatment satisfies $P(S_t = 0 \mid T_t = 1) = 0.75$, $P(S_t = 1 \mid T_t = 1) = 0.25$, $P(S_t = 0 \mid T_t = 0) = 0.25$, and $P(S_t = 1 \mid T_t = 0) = 0.75$.

Now consider structural outcome equation $Y_t \in \{0, 1\}$ with $P(Y_t = 1 \mid T_t = t, S_t = s) = \theta_s t + g_s$ and unconfounded innovations $\E[U_t \mid X_t, S_t, T_t] = 0$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Model $P_1$ (Homogeneous Causal Effect):}
    Set $\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(1)} = (0.20, 0.20)^\top$ ($\theta_0^{(1)} - \theta_1^{(1)} = 0$), baselines $g_0^{(1)} = 0.30, g_1^{(1)} = 0.50$.
    Then $P_1(Y_t = 1 \mid T_t = 0) = 0.25(0.30) + 0.75(0.50) = 0.45$, and $P_1(Y_t = 1 \mid T_t = 1) = 0.75(0.20 + 0.30) + 0.25(0.20 + 0.50) = 0.75(0.50) + 0.25(0.70) = 0.55$.
    \item \textbf{Model $P_2$ (Heterogeneous Causal Effect):}
    Set $\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(2)} = (0.10, -0.30)^\top$ ($\theta_0^{(2)} - \theta_1^{(2)} = +0.40$), baselines $g_0^{(2)} = 0.60, g_1^{(2)} = 0.40$.
    Then $P_2(Y_t = 1 \mid T_t = 0) = 0.25(0.60) + 0.75(0.40) = 0.45$, and $P_2(Y_t = 1 \mid T_t = 1) = 0.75(0.10 + 0.60) + 0.25(-0.30 + 0.40) = 0.75(0.70) + 0.25(0.10) = 0.525 + 0.025 = 0.55$.
\end{enumerate}
Comparing observable conditionals, $P_1(Y_t \mid T_t, X_t, Z_t) \equiv P_2(Y_t \mid T_t, X_t, Z_t)$ for all $(y, t, x, z)$, yielding identical joint laws $P_1(Y_{1:N}, T_{1:N}, X_{1:N}, Z_{1:N}) \equiv P_2(Y_{1:N}, T_{1:N}, X_{1:N}, Z_{1:N})$ for all $N \ge 1$.
Yet structural effects satisfy $\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(1)} = (0.20, 0.20)^\top \neq (0.10, -0.30)^\top = \boldsymbol{\theta}^{(2)}$, which is not a permutation.
No estimator conditioning solely on observables can distinguish whether the data-generating mechanism is homogeneous ($P_1$) or heterogeneous ($P_2$) without informative proxies $Z_t$. This establishes substantive non-identification under latent confounding. $\blacksquare$

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:graceful}: Graceful Degradation and the Difficulty Frontier}
\label{app:proof_thm3}
Let $\boldsymbol{H}_t = \boldsymbol{e}_{S_t} \in \{0, 1\}^K$ denote the unobserved true regime indicator vector.
Under SCM \eqref{eq:regime_scm}--\eqref{eq:outcome}, the oracle regime nuisance functions are:
\begin{equation}
    m_k^*(X_t) \equiv \E[T_t \mid X_t, S_t = k], \quad \mu_k^*(X_t) \equiv \E[Y_t \mid X_t, S_t = k] = \theta_k^* m_k^*(X_t) + g(X_t, k).
\end{equation}
\textbf{Step 1: Define proxy-weighted targets and population estimand.}
The empirical cross-fitted learners $\hat{m}_k$ and $\hat{\mu}_k$ minimize the posterior-weighted empirical risk targeting the proxy-weighted conditional expectations:
\begin{equation}
    m_k^\gamma(X_t) \equiv \frac{\E[\gamma_{tk} T_t \mid X_t]}{\E[\gamma_{tk} \mid X_t]}, \quad \mu_k^\gamma(X_t) \equiv \frac{\E[\gamma_{tk} Y_t \mid X_t]}{\E[\gamma_{tk} \mid X_t]}.
\end{equation}
Define the centered residuals at proxy targets: $\tilde{T}_{tk}^\gamma \equiv T_t - m_k^\gamma(X_t)$, $\tilde{Y}_{tk}^\gamma \equiv Y_t - \mu_k^\gamma(X_t)$, and at oracle targets: $\tilde{T}_{tk}^* \equiv T_t - m_k^*(X_t)$, $\tilde{Y}_{tk}^* \equiv Y_t - \mu_k^*(X_t)$.
The population proxy Gram matrix $\boldsymbol{J} \in \R^{K \times K}$ and score vector $\boldsymbol{S} \in \R^K$ are:
\begin{equation}
    J_{jk} \equiv \E[\gamma_{tj}\gamma_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tj}^\gamma \tilde{T}_{tk}^\gamma], \qquad S_k \equiv \E[\gamma_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tk}^\gamma \tilde{Y}_{tk}^\gamma],
\end{equation}
and their oracle counterparts $\boldsymbol{J}^*, \boldsymbol{S}^*$ are defined by:
\begin{equation}
    J_{jk}^* \equiv \E[H_{tj} H_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tj}^* \tilde{T}_{tk}^*] = \delta_{jk} \E[H_{tk} (\tilde{T}_{tk}^*)^2], \qquad S_k^* \equiv \E[H_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tk}^* \tilde{Y}_{tk}^*],
\end{equation}
where $H_{tj} H_{tk} = \delta_{jk} H_{tk}$ because latent regimes are mutually exclusive.

\textbf{Step 2: Establish Neyman nuisance orthogonality.}
Consider the state-weighted score for regime $k$ as a functional of candidate nuisances $(m_k, \mu_k)$:
\begin{equation}
    \psi_k(W_t; \theta_k, m_k, \mu_k) = \gamma_{tk} (T_t - m_k(X_t)) \left( Y_t - \mu_k(X_t) \right).
\end{equation}
Evaluating the Gateaux derivative with respect to $\mu_k$ in direction $\Delta \mu$ at $(m_k^\gamma, \mu_k^\gamma)$:
\begin{equation}
    \partial_\mu \E[\psi_k(W_t; \theta_k^*, m_k^\gamma, \mu_k^\gamma)][\Delta \mu] = -\E\left[ \gamma_{tk} (T_t - m_k^\gamma(X_t)) \Delta \mu(X_t) \right] = -\E\left[ \E\left[ \gamma_{tk} (T_t - m_k^\gamma(X_t)) \;\middle|\; X_t \right] \Delta \mu(X_t) \right] = 0,
\end{equation}
since $\E[\gamma_{tk} (T_t - m_k^\gamma(X_t)) \mid X_t] \equiv 0$ identically by the definition of $m_k^\gamma(X_t)$.
Similarly, evaluating the Gateaux derivative with respect to $m_k$ in direction $\Delta m$:
\begin{equation}
    \partial_m \E[\psi_k(W_t; \theta_k^*, m_k^\gamma, \mu_k^\gamma)][\Delta m] = -\E\left[ \gamma_{tk} \Delta m(X_t) (Y_t - \mu_k^\gamma(X_t)) \right] = -\E\left[ \Delta m(X_t) \E\left[ \gamma_{tk} (Y_t - \mu_k^\gamma(X_t)) \;\middle|\; X_t \right] \right] = 0,
\end{equation}
since $\E[\gamma_{tk} (Y_t - \mu_k^\gamma(X_t)) \mid X_t] \equiv 0$ identically by the definition of $\mu_k^\gamma(X_t)$.
Therefore, Neyman orthogonality eliminates first-order sensitivity of the score to nuisance estimation errors $(\hat{m}_k - m_k^\gamma, \hat{\mu}_k - \mu_k^\gamma)$, contributing a second-order error $\mathcal{O}_P(\|\hat{m}_k - m_k^\gamma\|_{L_2} \|\hat{\mu}_k - \mu_k^\gamma\|_{L_2}) = \mathcal{O}_P(N^{-1/2})$ under Assumption~\ref{ass:rates}.
We emphasize that DML Neyman orthogonality protects against non-parametric nuisance estimation error, \emph{not} against latent proxy misspecification ($\boldsymbol{H}_t \neq \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t$).

\textbf{Step 3: Isolate proxy-induced bias via oracle score identity.}
Under SCM \eqref{eq:regime_scm}--\eqref{eq:outcome}, on the event $S_t = k$ ($H_{tk} = 1$), the oracle outcome residual is $\tilde{Y}_{tk}^* = Y_t - \mu_k^*(X_t) = \theta_k^* \tilde{T}_{tk}^* + U_t$.
Substituting into $S_k^*$:
\begin{equation}
    S_k^* = \E\left[ H_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tk}^* (\theta_k^* \tilde{T}_{tk}^* + U_t) \right] = \theta_k^* \E[H_{tk} (\tilde{T}_{tk}^*)^2] + \E\left[ H_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tk}^* \E[U_t \mid X_t, S_t, T_t, Z_{1:N}] \right] = \theta_k^* J_{kk}^*,
\end{equation}
where the innovation term vanishes by Assumption~\ref{ass:unconfoundedness}. Because $\boldsymbol{J}^* = \operatorname{diag}(J_{11}^*, \dots, J_{KK}^*)$, this yields the exact vector identity:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{S}^* = \boldsymbol{J}^* \boldsymbol{\theta}^*.
\end{equation}
Now consider the proxy score discrepancy $\boldsymbol{b}_\gamma \equiv \boldsymbol{S} - \boldsymbol{J}\boldsymbol{\theta}^*$. Expanding around the oracle identity:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{b}_\gamma = (\boldsymbol{S} - \boldsymbol{S}^*) - (\boldsymbol{J} - \boldsymbol{J}^*) \boldsymbol{\theta}^*.
\end{equation}

\textbf{Step 4: Bound score and Gram proxy discrepancies.}
We bound each component via telescoping expansions in $\varepsilon_\gamma \equiv \E\|\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t - \boldsymbol{H}_t\|_1$.
First, under positivity (Assumption~\ref{ass:overlap}), $\pi_k(x) \equiv \E[H_{tk} \mid X_t=x] \ge c_0 > 0$ and $\pi_k^\gamma(x) \equiv \E[\gamma_{tk} \mid X_t=x] \ge c_0 > 0$. For any bounded variable $V_t$ with $|V_t| \le M_V$ almost surely:
\begin{align}
    \left| \frac{\E[\gamma_{tk} V_t \mid X_t]}{\pi_k^\gamma(X_t)} - \frac{\E[H_{tk} V_t \mid X_t]}{\pi_k(X_t)} \right| 
    &\le \frac{\E[|\gamma_{tk} - H_{tk}| |V_t| \mid X_t]}{\pi_k^\gamma(X_t)} + \E[H_{tk}|V_t| \mid X_t] \left| \frac{\pi_k(X_t) - \pi_k^\gamma(X_t)}{\pi_k^\gamma(X_t) \pi_k(X_t)} \right| \nonumber \\
    &\le \frac{2 M_V}{c_0^2} \E[|\gamma_{tk} - H_{tk}| \mid X_t].
\end{align}
Applying this bound to $V_t = T_t$ and $V_t = Y_t$ yields $\E|m_k^\gamma(X_t) - m_k^*(X_t)| \le \tilde{C}_m \varepsilon_\gamma$ and $\E|\mu_k^\gamma(X_t) - \mu_k^*(X_t)| \le \tilde{C}_\mu \varepsilon_\gamma$.
Second, expanding the score difference $S_k - S_k^* = \E[\gamma_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tk}^\gamma \tilde{Y}_{tk}^\gamma - H_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tk}^* \tilde{Y}_{tk}^*]$:
\begin{align}
    \gamma_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tk}^\gamma \tilde{Y}_{tk}^\gamma - H_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tk}^* \tilde{Y}_{tk}^* &= (\gamma_{tk} - H_{tk}) \tilde{T}_{tk}^\gamma \tilde{Y}_{tk}^\gamma - H_{tk} (m_k^\gamma - m_k^*) \tilde{Y}_{tk}^\gamma - H_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tk}^* (\mu_k^\gamma - \mu_k^*).
\end{align}
Under bounded support ($\sup_t |\tilde{T}_{tk}^\gamma| \le M_T, \sup_t |\tilde{Y}_{tk}^\gamma| \le M_Y$), taking expectations yields $|S_k - S_k^*| \le (M_T M_Y + M_Y \tilde{C}_m + M_T \tilde{C}_\mu) \varepsilon_\gamma \equiv C_S \varepsilon_\gamma$.
Third, expanding the Gram difference $J_{jk} - J_{jk}^* = \E[\gamma_{tj}\gamma_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tj}^\gamma \tilde{T}_{tk}^\gamma - H_{tj}H_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tj}^* \tilde{T}_{tk}^*]$:
\begin{align}
    \gamma_{tj}\gamma_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tj}^\gamma \tilde{T}_{tk}^\gamma - H_{tj}H_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tj}^* \tilde{T}_{tk}^* &= (\gamma_{tj}\gamma_{tk} - H_{tj}H_{tk}) \tilde{T}_{tj}^\gamma \tilde{T}_{tk}^\gamma - H_{tj}H_{tk} (m_j^\gamma - m_j^*) \tilde{T}_{tk}^\gamma \nonumber \\
    &\quad - H_{tj}H_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tj}^* (m_k^\gamma - m_k^*).
\end{align}
Since $|\gamma_{tj}\gamma_{tk} - H_{tj}H_{tk}| \le |\gamma_{tj} - H_{tj}| + |\gamma_{tk} - H_{tk}|$, taking expectations gives $|J_{jk} - J_{jk}^*| \le (2 M_T^2 + 2 M_T \tilde{C}_m) \varepsilon_\gamma$, so $\|\boldsymbol{J} - \boldsymbol{J}^*\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le C_J \varepsilon_\gamma$.
Combining bounds yields:
\begin{equation}
    \|\boldsymbol{b}_\gamma\|_2 \le \|\boldsymbol{S} - \boldsymbol{S}^*\|_2 + \|\boldsymbol{J} - \boldsymbol{J}^*\|_{\mathrm{op}} \|\boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2 \le (C_S + C_J M_\theta) \varepsilon_\gamma \equiv C \cdot \varepsilon_\gamma.
\end{equation}

\textbf{Step 5: Apply spectral conditioning and conclude.}
Since $\boldsymbol{S} = \boldsymbol{J}\boldsymbol{\theta}^* + \boldsymbol{b}_\gamma$ and $\boldsymbol{\theta}_\gamma \equiv \boldsymbol{J}^{-1}\boldsymbol{S}$, we have $\boldsymbol{\theta}_\gamma - \boldsymbol{\theta}^* = \boldsymbol{J}^{-1}\boldsymbol{b}_\gamma$. Therefore:
\begin{equation}
    \|\boldsymbol{\theta}_\gamma - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2 \le \|\boldsymbol{J}^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}} \|\boldsymbol{b}_\gamma\|_2 = \frac{\|\boldsymbol{b}_\gamma\|_2}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})} \le \frac{C \cdot \varepsilon_\gamma}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})}.
\end{equation}
Combining with purged cross-fitting nuisance estimation error $\mathcal{O}_P(N^{-1/2})$ completes the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:graceful}. $\blacksquare$

\subsection{The Latent-Confounding Difficulty Frontier $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{causal}}$}
\label{app:difficulty_frontier}
Theorem~\ref{thm:graceful} demonstrates that the upper bound on structural causal estimation error decomposes into:
\begin{equation}
    \|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\gamma - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2 \le C \cdot \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{causal}} + \mathcal{O}_P(N^{-1/2}), \quad \text{where} \quad \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{causal}} \equiv \frac{\varepsilon_\gamma}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})}.
\end{equation}
We highlight several structural properties of the difficulty index $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{causal}}$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Scale-Dependent Difficulty Ratio:} Both population proxy error $\varepsilon_\gamma$ and Gram conditioning $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})$ interact multiplicatively in governing downstream estimation risk (dimensionless under standardized treatment units). When either proxy recovery is poor ($\varepsilon_\gamma \to 1$) or overlap is weak ($\lambda_{\min} \to 0$), estimation risk escalates sharply.
    \item \textbf{Asymmetric Sensitivity:} In our factorial simulations (Table~\ref{tab:bench_results}), reducing $\lambda_{\min}$ by an order of magnitude produces an equivalent variance and bias inflation to a tenfold increase in state proxy error $\varepsilon_\gamma$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:bridge}: Surrogate Entropy-Difficulty Bridge}
\label{app:proof_bridge}
Let $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t \equiv P(S_t \in \cdot \mid \mathcal{F}_t) \in \Delta^K$ be the Bayesian posterior state distribution with respect to filtration $\mathcal{F}_t \equiv \sigma(Z_{1:t})$. Under proper Bayesian calibration, conditional on $\mathcal{F}_t$, the true latent indicator $\boldsymbol{H}_t \equiv \boldsymbol{e}_{S_t}$ is distributed as a categorical random variable taking value $\boldsymbol{e}_k$ with probability $\gamma_{tk}$.

\textbf{Step 1: Expected $L_1$ Error as Gini Impurity.}
Evaluating the conditional expectation of the $L_1$ error:
\begin{align}
    \E\left[ \|\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t - \boldsymbol{H}_t\|_1 \;\middle|\; \mathcal{F}_t \right] &= \sum_{k=1}^K \gamma_{tk} \|\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t - \boldsymbol{e}_k\|_1 = \sum_{k=1}^K \gamma_{tk} \left( (1 - \gamma_{tk}) + \sum_{j \neq k} \gamma_{tj} \right) \nonumber \\
    &= \sum_{k=1}^K \gamma_{tk} \cdot 2(1 - \gamma_{tk}) = 2\left( 1 - \sum_{k=1}^K \gamma_{tk}^2 \right) = 2 \operatorname{Gini}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t).
\end{align}

\textbf{Step 2: Entropy Bound on Gini Impurity.}
By the standard concave inequality $\ln(x) \le x - 1$, for any $x > 0$:
\begin{equation}
    H(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t) = -\sum_{k=1}^K \gamma_{tk} \ln \gamma_{tk} = \sum_{k=1}^K \gamma_{tk} \ln\left(\frac{1}{\gamma_{tk}}\right) \ge \sum_{k=1}^K \gamma_{tk} (1 - \gamma_{tk}) = \operatorname{Gini}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t).
\end{equation}
Furthermore, for any binary regime partition ($K=2$) with $\gamma_{t1} = p$ and $\gamma_{t2} = 1-p$, $2 \operatorname{Gini}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t) = 4p(1-p)$. Comparing to binary Shannon entropy $H(p) = -p\ln p - (1-p)\ln(1-p)$, the ratio $\frac{4p(1-p)}{H(p)}$ achieves its maximum at $p = 1/2$, where $\frac{4(1/4)}{\ln 2} = \frac{1}{\ln 2} \approx 1.4427$. Hence for all $p \in [0, 1]$:
\begin{equation}
    2 \operatorname{Gini}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t) \le C_K H(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t), \quad \text{where } C_K \le \frac{1}{\ln 2} \approx 1.4427.
\end{equation}

\textbf{Step 3: Population and Observable Bridge.}
Taking the population expectation of Step 1 and applying Step 2:
\begin{equation}
    \varepsilon_\gamma \equiv \E\left[ \E\left[ \|\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t - \boldsymbol{H}_t\|_1 \;\middle|\; \mathcal{F}_t \right] \right] = 2 \E[\operatorname{Gini}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t)] \le C_K \E[H(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t)].
\end{equation}
Dividing by the global minimal singular value $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{causal}} \equiv \frac{\varepsilon_\gamma}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})} \le C_K \E\left[ \frac{H(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t)}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})} \right] = C_K \E[\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{global}}^H(t)].
\end{equation}
Furthermore, under Assumption~\ref{ass:spectral_comparability} (Two-Sided Spectral Comparability), the rolling Gram matrix satisfies $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}_t) \le \overline{c}_J \lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})$ almost surely for all evaluation windows $t \ge w$, which implies $\frac{1}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})} \le \frac{\overline{c}_J}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}_t)}$. Pointwise substitution yields:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{causal}} \le C_K \overline{c}_J \E\left[ \frac{H(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t)}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}_t)} \right] = C_K \overline{c}_J \E[\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{operational}}(t)],
\end{equation}
and $\E[\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{operational}}(t)] \le \underline{c}_J^{-1} \E[\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{global}}^H(t)]$. In simulation benchmarks where true states are known and posteriors are evaluated at population parameters $\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*$, the ergodic theorem and central limit theorem for stationary $\alpha$-mixing chains ensure that $\hat{\varepsilon}_{\gamma, N} \equiv \frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N \|\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t - \boldsymbol{H}_t\|_1 = \varepsilon_\gamma + \mathcal{O}_P(N^{-1/2})$ (preserved under $\sqrt{N}$-consistent estimation of $\boldsymbol{\Lambda}$), providing an evaluation-only benchmark surrogate $\hat{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathrm{causal}, N} \equiv \hat{\varepsilon}_{\gamma, N}/\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) = \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{causal}} + \mathcal{O}_P(N^{-1/2})$. We emphasize that these bounds establish $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{operational}}(t)$ as an observable surrogate upper bound for $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{causal}}$, not a two-sided equivalence. Across our multi-season ground-sensor streams, the empirical ratio $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}_t)/\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})$ has 95th percentile in $[1.06, 1.72]$ and maximum $\le 7.35$, providing empirical support for the spectral-comparability assumption with finite $\overline{c}_J$. $\blacksquare$

\subsection{A Priori Proxy Diagnostics and Separation Screening}
\label{app:proxy_diagnostics}
Before fitting OR-DML or downstream prediction heads, practitioners should assess proxy informativeness on auxiliary measurements $Z_t \in \R^q$. Let $\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{Z,k} \equiv \frac{1}{N_k}\sum_{t: \hat{S}_t=k} Z_t$ and $\hat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{Z,k}$ denote empirical cluster moments. We define the standardized separation index:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_Z \equiv \min_{j \neq k} \frac{\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{Z,j} - \hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{Z,k}\|_2}{\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}(\mathrm{Tr}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{Z,j}) + \mathrm{Tr}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{Z,k}))/q}}.
\end{equation}
When $\Delta_Z < 0.5$ (or silhouette score $< 0.15$), the overlap between regime emission densities $P(Z_t \mid S_t=j)$ and $P(Z_t \mid S_t=k)$ is severe, driving posterior entropy $\bar{H}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}) \to \ln K$. By Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility}, point identification fails identically in the limit $\Delta_Z \to 0$. Deployed pipelines should enforce an automated screening gate: if $\Delta_Z < 0.5$ or mean entropy exceeds $0.80 \ln K$, the system must flag non-identifiability and refuse to emit point causal effects without external structural constraints.

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral}: Leading-Order Risk Bound and Spectral Regularization}
\label{app:proof_thm4}
Let $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda = (\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \hat{\boldsymbol{S}}$, and define the population regularized estimand $\boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda \equiv (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \boldsymbol{S}$.
We decompose the estimation error around the regularized population estimand $\boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda$ via the exact algebraic identity:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:exact_decomp}
    \E\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2^2 = \|\boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2^2 + \E\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda\|_2^2 + 2 \langle \E[\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda], \; \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^* \rangle,
\end{equation}
which accounts explicitly for the cross term between stochastic estimation error and deterministic regularization bias.
For the deterministic bias, substituting $\boldsymbol{S} = \boldsymbol{J}\boldsymbol{\theta}^* + \boldsymbol{b}_\gamma$:
\begin{align}
    \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^* &= (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \left( \boldsymbol{S} - (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})\boldsymbol{\theta}^* \right) \nonumber \\
    &= (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \left( \boldsymbol{S} - \boldsymbol{J}\boldsymbol{\theta}^* - \lambda \boldsymbol{\theta}^* \right) \nonumber \\
    &= (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \left( \boldsymbol{b}_\gamma - \lambda \boldsymbol{\theta}^* \right), \label{eq:reg_identity}
\end{align}
which establishes the exact deterministic identity \eqref{eq:reg_identity}.
Since $\boldsymbol{J} \succeq 0$ with minimal eigenvalue $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) \ge 0$, the spectral operator norm is $\|(\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}} = \frac{1}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) + \lambda}$.
Applying the triangle inequality:
\begin{equation}
    \|\boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2 \le \|(\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}} \left( \|\boldsymbol{b}_\gamma\|_2 + \lambda \|\boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2 \right) \le \frac{C \varepsilon_\gamma + \lambda M_\theta}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) + \lambda}.
\end{equation}
For the stochastic variance, expanding $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda$:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda = (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \left( \hat{\boldsymbol{S}} - \boldsymbol{S} - (\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} - \boldsymbol{J})\boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda \right) + o_p(N^{-1/2}).
\end{equation}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:asymptotics}, the score empirical process satisfies $\Var\left( \frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{t=1}^N \boldsymbol{\Psi}_t \right) \to \boldsymbol{\Omega}$. Thus, under the long-run score covariance approximation, the stochastic variance contribution admits the leading-order expansion:
\begin{equation}
    \E\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda\|_2^2 = \frac{1}{N} \mathrm{Tr}\left( (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \boldsymbol{\Omega} (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \right) + o(N^{-1}) \le \frac{\mathrm{Tr}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}{N (\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) + \lambda)^2} + o(N^{-1}),
\end{equation}
using $\mathrm{Tr}(\boldsymbol{A}^{-1}\boldsymbol{\Omega}\boldsymbol{A}^{-1}) = \mathrm{Tr}(\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{1/2}\boldsymbol{A}^{-2}\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{1/2}) \le \|\boldsymbol{A}^{-2}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\mathrm{Tr}(\boldsymbol{\Omega}) = \frac{\mathrm{Tr}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}{(\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})+\lambda)^2}$ for symmetric $\boldsymbol{A} = \boldsymbol{J}+\lambda\boldsymbol{I} \succ 0$ and $\boldsymbol{\Omega} \succeq 0$.
To bound the cross term $2 \langle \E[\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda], \; \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^* \rangle$ in \eqref{eq:exact_decomp}: by Neyman orthogonality of the score function $\boldsymbol{\psi}$ and purged block cross-fitting with Bartlett embargo buffer $\tau^* \ge C \log N$, the finite-sample expectation of the stochastic error satisfies $\|\E[\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda]\|_2 \le C_1 N^{-1}$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:asymptotics}). Furthermore, the deterministic regularized bias satisfies $\|\boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2 \le \frac{C \varepsilon_\gamma + \lambda M_\theta}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) + \lambda}$. By Cauchy-Schwarz, the cross term is bounded by:
\begin{equation}
    \left| 2 \langle \E[\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda], \; \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^* \rangle \right| \le 2 \|\E[\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda]\|_2 \|\boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2 = \mathcal{O}(N^{-1})
\end{equation}
in general (since $\|\boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2 = \mathcal{O}(1)$ without vanishing-bias rate restrictions), which sharpens to $\mathcal{O}(N^{-3/2})$ under the local parameter regime $\varepsilon_\gamma + \lambda = \mathcal{O}(N^{-1/2})$.
Combining the deterministic squared bias bound with the asymptotic long-run score covariance variance bound establishes the leading-order risk bound:
\begin{equation}
    \E\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2^2 \le \left(\frac{C \varepsilon_\gamma + \lambda M_\theta}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) + \lambda}\right)^2 + \frac{\mathrm{Tr}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}{N (\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) + \lambda)^2} + \mathcal{O}(N^{-1}).
\end{equation}

\textbf{Risk Bound Minimizer vs. Empirical Grid Optimum:}
Differentiating the leading-order risk upper bound $R_{\mathrm{bound}}(\lambda) \equiv \left(\frac{C \varepsilon_\gamma + \lambda M_\theta}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) + \lambda}\right)^2 + \frac{\mathrm{Tr}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}{N (\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) + \lambda)^2}$ with respect to $\lambda \ge 0$, the bound-optimal shrinkage parameter $\lambda^*_{\mathrm{bound}} \equiv \arg\min_{\lambda \ge 0} R_{\mathrm{bound}}(\lambda)$ satisfies:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\partial}{\partial \lambda} \left[ \left(\frac{C \varepsilon_\gamma + \lambda M_\theta}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) + \lambda}\right)^2 + \frac{\mathrm{Tr}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}{N (\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) + \lambda)^2} \right] = 0.
\end{equation}
When the overlap Gram matrix is ill-conditioned ($\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) \approx 0$), the variance term $\frac{\mathrm{Tr}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}{N (\lambda_{\min} + \lambda)^2}$ dominates for small $\lambda$, while the deterministic shrinkage bias $(\frac{\lambda M_\theta}{\lambda_{\min} + \lambda})^2$ dominates for large $\lambda$. The interior minimizer $\lambda^*_{\mathrm{bound}} > 0$ balances variance reduction against shrinkage bias in the upper bound. We distinguish $\lambda^*_{\mathrm{bound}}$ and the empirical surrogate minimizer $\hat{\lambda}^*_{\mathrm{emp}} = \arg\min_\lambda \hat{R}(\lambda)$ from the true infeasible risk minimizer $\lambda^*_{\mathrm{true}} \equiv \arg\min_\lambda \E\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2^2$.
In our empirical simulation benchmark across 500 Monte Carlo replications (\texttt{reports/or\_dml\_regularization\_frontier.csv}), the discrete grid minimum occurs at $\hat{\lambda}^*_{\mathrm{empirical}} \approx 0.20$ ($\text{MSE} = 0.0321$, representing a $25.9\times$ empirical risk reduction in this benchmark DGP relative to the unregularized estimator $\lambda = 0.0$ where $\text{MSE} = 0.8312$), while $\lambda = 0.10$ achieves $\text{MSE} = 0.0362$ ($23.0\times$ empirical reduction) with peak coverage of $85.0\%$.
We emphasize that Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral} provides the functional upper-bound characterization of the bias-variance trade-off; the exact numerical reduction factors (23.0$\times$ and 25.9$\times$) are empirical simulation properties of the benchmark DGP rather than universal theoretical constants. $\blacksquare$

\subsection{Derivation of Adaptive Spectral Regularization Parameter and Oracle Bound}
\label{app:adaptive_lambda_derivation}
We consider the data-driven selection of the spectral shrinkage parameter $\lambda \ge 0$. While classical Tikhonov regularization selects shrinkage via prediction cross-validation, in OR-DML the target is the structural causal parameter vector $\boldsymbol{\theta}^*$, where prediction cross-validation conflates causal identification with prognostic predictive fit.
We distinguish two distinct regularization regimes:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Inferential Regularization ($\lambda = o(N^{-1/2})$):} Required by Theorem~\ref{thm:asymptotics} to guarantee asymptotic unbiasedness $\sqrt{N}\mathrm{Bias}(\lambda) \to \mathbf{0}$, ensuring centered Gaussian limit laws and valid nominal confidence interval coverage.
    \item \textbf{Finite-Sample Risk Minimization ($\lambda_{\mathrm{risk}} = \mathcal{O}(1)$):} Minimizes the finite-sample expected $\ell_2$ estimation risk $\E\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2^2$ under severe causal ill-conditioning ($\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) \approx 0$).
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Empirical Risk Surrogate and Convexity:}
Define the empirical plug-in risk surrogate $\hat{R}(\lambda)$ by substituting empirical estimators $(\hat{\varepsilon}_\gamma, \lambda_{\min}(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}), \hat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}, \|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_0\|_2)$ into the risk upper bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral}:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{R}(\lambda) = \frac{(C \hat{\varepsilon}_\gamma + \lambda \|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_0\|_2)^2 + N^{-1}\mathrm{Tr}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}})}{(\lambda_{\min}(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}) + \lambda)^2}.
\end{equation}
Let $u = \lambda_{\min}(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}) + \lambda \ge \lambda_{\min}(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}) > 0$. Rewriting $\lambda = u - \lambda_{\min}$, the numerator becomes:
\begin{equation}
    \left( C \hat{\varepsilon}_\gamma - \lambda_{\min}(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}) \|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_0\|_2 + u \|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_0\|_2 \right)^2 + N^{-1}\mathrm{Tr}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}) = (A + u B)^2 + V,
\end{equation}
where $A = C \hat{\varepsilon}_\gamma - \lambda_{\min}(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}) \|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_0\|_2$, $B = \|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_0\|_2$, and $V = N^{-1}\mathrm{Tr}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}})$.
Dividing by $u^2$:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{R}(u - \lambda_{\min}) = B^2 + \frac{2AB}{u} + \frac{A^2 + V}{u^2}.
\end{equation}
Differentiating with respect to $u$:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d \hat{R}}{du} = -\frac{2AB}{u^2} - \frac{2(A^2 + V)}{u^3} = -\frac{2}{u^3} \left[ AB u + (A^2 + V) \right].
\end{equation}
Setting the derivative to zero yields the unique critical point $u^* = \frac{A^2 + V}{|A| B}$ when $A < 0$. When $A \ge 0$ (i.e., $C \hat{\varepsilon}_\gamma \ge \lambda_{\min}(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}) \|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_0\|_2$), $\frac{d\hat{R}}{du} < 0$ on $(0, \infty)$, so that the objective is strictly decreasing toward $B^2$ as $\lambda \to \infty$. Constraining the search to a compact admissible set $\lambda \in [0, \bar{\lambda}]$ with upper bound $\bar{\lambda} \asymp N^{-1/2}$, the bounded empirical minimizer is:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\lambda}^* = \begin{cases} 
    \mathrm{clip}\left( \frac{(C\hat{\varepsilon}_\gamma - \lambda_{\min}\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_0\|_2)^2 + N^{-1}\mathrm{Tr}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}})}{(\lambda_{\min}\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_0\|_2 - C\hat{\varepsilon}_\gamma)\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_0\|_2} - \lambda_{\min}(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}), \; 0, \; \bar{\lambda} \right), & \text{if } A < 0, \\
    \bar{\lambda}, & \text{if } A \ge 0.
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Because $R'(u) = -\frac{2}{u^3}[AB u + (A^2+V)]$ changes sign strictly from negative to positive at $u^*$ when $A < 0$, $u^*$ is the unique global interior minimizer on $(0, \infty)$. By uniform convergence of the empirical plug-in surrogate on compact parameter sets $\lambda \in [0, \bar{\lambda}]$, $\sup_{\lambda \in [0, \bar{\lambda}]} |\hat{R}(\lambda) - R_{\mathrm{surr}}(\lambda)| = \mathcal{O}_P(N^{-1/2})$. Applying M-estimation consistency on $[0, \bar{\lambda}]$ yields the adaptive risk-surrogate selection guarantee:
\begin{equation}
    R_{\mathrm{surr}}(\hat{\lambda}^*) \le \inf_{\lambda \in [0, \bar{\lambda}]} R_{\mathrm{surr}}(\lambda) + \mathcal{O}_P(N^{-1/2}),
\end{equation}
establishing that data-driven spectral shrinkage $\hat{\lambda}^*$ adaptively tracks the optimal surrogate risk trade-off without prior knowledge of the true latent proxy error. $\blacksquare$

\subsection{State Space Scalability and Matrix Concentration for $K > 2$}
\label{app:state_space_scalability}
While our primary experiments study binary regimes ($K=2$), the OR-DML coupled estimator generalizes directly to arbitrary finite state dimensions $K \ge 2$. Here we formalize the computational and statistical scalability of the coupled Gram matrix $\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} \in \R^{K \times K}$.

\textbf{Computational Complexity and Matrix Concentration under $\alpha$-Mixing:}
Constructing $\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}$ and score $\hat{\boldsymbol{S}}$ requires evaluating $K$ nuisance pairs across $B$ folds ($\mathcal{O}(B K \cdot \mathrm{Cost}(\text{Nuisance}))$). Forming $\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}$ takes $\mathcal{O}(N K^2)$ operations, and solving $(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1}\hat{\boldsymbol{S}}$ via Cholesky factorization takes $\mathcal{O}(K^3)$ operations, taking $<1$ ms for $K \le 20$.
Statistically, the empirical Gram matrix $\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N \boldsymbol{\Gamma}_t \tilde{\boldsymbol{T}}_t \tilde{\boldsymbol{T}}_t^\top \boldsymbol{\Gamma}_t$ concentrates around $\boldsymbol{J}$ under geometric $\alpha$-mixing with spectral gap $\rho_{\mathrm{gap}} \equiv 1 - |\lambda_2(\boldsymbol{\Pi})| > 0$ via the Matrix Bernstein inequality \citep{banna2016bernstein}:
\begin{equation}
    \|\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} - \boldsymbol{J}\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le C_J \sqrt{\frac{K \log(K/\delta)}{N \cdot \rho_{\mathrm{gap}}}} + \frac{C_J' K \log(K/\delta)}{N \cdot \rho_{\mathrm{gap}}}.
\end{equation}
By Weyl's theorem, $|\lambda_{\min}(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}) - \lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})| \le \|\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} - \boldsymbol{J}\|_{\mathrm{op}}$, ensuring that $\lambda_{\min}(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}})$ concentrates at the standard $\mathcal{O}_P(N^{-1/2})$ rate whenever $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) > 0$.

\textbf{Non-Identifiability vs.\ Shrinkage: When Systems Must Abstain:}
Spectral shrinkage $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda = (\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1}\hat{\boldsymbol{S}}$ stabilizes variance under ill-conditioned overlap ($\lambda_{\min} \approx 0$). However, when data lack identifying variation entirely—diagnosed by condition number $\kappa(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}) > 10^4$ or a moving-block bootstrap 95\% confidence interval for $\lambda_{\min}(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}})$ that covers zero—the structural parameter $\boldsymbol{\theta}^*$ is non-identified (Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility}). In this regime, heavy shrinkage $\lambda \to \infty$ artificially forces $\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda\| \to 0$ without recovering structural effects. Deployed decision pipelines should treat $\kappa(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}) > 10^4$ as a structural abstention trigger rather than masking non-identifiability with excessive shrinkage.

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:asymptotics}: Asymptotic Normality Under Dependent Purged Cross-Fitting}
\label{app:proof_thm5}
The empirical score expansion around true $\boldsymbol{\theta}^*$ and true nuisances $\eta^*$ is:
\begin{equation}
    \sqrt{N}\left( \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^* - \mathrm{Bias}(\lambda) \right) = (\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{t=1}^N \boldsymbol{\Psi}(W_t; \boldsymbol{\theta}^*, \hat{\eta}^{(-b(t))}).
\end{equation}
With empirical Bartlett embargo window $\tau^* \ge C \log N$, the $\alpha$-mixing dependence between training set $\mathcal{T}_b$ and test fold $\mathcal{B}_b$ satisfies:
\begin{equation}
    \alpha(\tau^*) \le M \exp(-c \tau^*) \le M N^{-c C} = o(N^{-1}).
\end{equation}
Consequently, cross-fold covariance is asymptotically negligible, and nuisance estimation errors satisfy:
\begin{equation}
    \|\hat{\mu}_Y - \mu_Y^*\|_{L_2} \cdot \|\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_D - \boldsymbol{\mu}_D^*\|_{L_2} = o_P(N^{-1/2}).
\end{equation}
By the Ibragimov (1962) central limit theorem for strictly stationary, $\alpha$-mixing processes satisfying $\sum_m \alpha(m)^{\delta/(2+\delta)} < \infty$:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{1}{\sqrt{N}} \sum_{t=1}^N \boldsymbol{\Psi}(W_t; \boldsymbol{\theta}^*, \eta^*) \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}\left(\mathbf{0}, \boldsymbol{\Omega}\right),
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\Omega} = \lim_{N \to \infty} \Var\left( N^{-1/2} \sum_{t=1}^N \boldsymbol{\Psi}_t \right)$.
Applying Slutsky's theorem with $\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} \xrightarrow{p} \boldsymbol{J}$:
\begin{equation}
    \sqrt{N}\left( \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^* - \mathrm{Bias}(\lambda) \right) \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}\left(\mathbf{0}, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\lambda\right),
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\lambda = (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \boldsymbol{\Omega} (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1}$. When $\lambda = o(N^{-1/2})$, $\sqrt{N}\mathrm{Bias}(\lambda) \to \mathbf{0}$, completing the proof.

\textit{Operational Modes of Forward Filtering.} In offline retrospective smoothing, $\gamma_{tk}^{\mathrm{smooth}} = P(S_t = k \mid Z_{1:N})$. In real-time decision making, three distinct forward modes exist: (1) \emph{Oracle Filter}: $P(S_t = k \mid Z_{1:t}, \boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*)$ using the true HMM parameters; (2) \emph{Training-Fitted Forward Filter}: $P(S_t = k \mid Z_{1:t}, \hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}})$ where parameters $\hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}$ are fitted on a historical calibration window strictly preceding the evaluation window ($\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{cal}} < \mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{eval}}$) and state inference updates strictly forward on past-to-present observations $Z_{1:t}$; and (3) \emph{Expanding-Window Online Filter}: where both parameters $\hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}_t$ and state updates proceed sequentially. Our implementations evaluate Mode 1 and Mode 2, where Mode 2 enforces causal forward state filtering without future observation leakage (distinguishing forward state updating from online sequential parameter re-estimation). $\blacksquare$

\subsection{Observed-Data Orthogonal Score under Estimated Latent Dynamics}
\label{app:observed_data_score}
In semiparametric causal inference \citep{chernozhukov2018double, bickel1993efficient}, when nuisance parameters consist of both non-parametric outcome/propensity regressions $\boldsymbol{\eta} = (m, \mu)$ and finite-dimensional parameters $\boldsymbol{\Lambda}$ governing latent state transitions and emissions, one must characterize the sensitivity of the causal estimator to the first-order estimation error in $\hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}$.

Let the state-weighted regime score vector be:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\psi}_{\mathrm{regime}}(W_t; \boldsymbol{\theta}, \boldsymbol{\eta}, \boldsymbol{\Lambda}) \equiv \sum_{k=1}^K \gamma_{tk}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}) (T_t - m_k(X_t)) \left( Y_t - \mu_k(X_t) - \theta_k (T_t - m_k(X_t)) \right).
\end{equation}
In a hidden Markov model satisfying standard regularity and mixing conditions \citep{bickel1993efficient}, the latent parameters $\boldsymbol{\Lambda}$ are estimated via sequence-level maximum likelihood with sequence score $\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}} \log p(Z_{1:N}; \boldsymbol{\Lambda}) = \sum_{t=1}^N \boldsymbol{\phi}_{\Lambda, t} + o_P(\sqrt{N})$, where $\boldsymbol{\phi}_{\Lambda, t} \equiv \nabla_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}} \log p(Z_t \mid Z_{1:t-1}; \boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*)$ denotes the sequence-level prediction-error score increment satisfying $\E[\boldsymbol{\phi}_{\Lambda, t} \mid Z_{1:t-1}] = \mathbf{0}$ and $\frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N \E[\boldsymbol{\phi}_{\Lambda, t}\boldsymbol{\phi}_{\Lambda, t}^\top] \to \mathcal{I}_\Lambda$. Evaluating the Gateaux derivative with respect to $\boldsymbol{\Lambda}$ at the oracle targets $(\boldsymbol{\theta}^*, \boldsymbol{\eta}^*, \boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*)$:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{A}_\Lambda \equiv \lim_{N \to \infty} \frac{1}{N} \sum_{t=1}^N \sum_{k=1}^K \E\left[ \frac{\partial \gamma_{tk}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*)}{\partial \boldsymbol{\Lambda}^\top} (T_t - m_k^*(X_t)) \left( Y_t - \mu_k^*(X_t) - \theta_k^* (T_t - m_k^*(X_t)) \right) \right].
\end{equation}
Importantly, $\boldsymbol{A}_\Lambda$ does not vanish pointwise across regimes. While Assumption~\ref{ass:innovation} guarantees that the structural innovation has zero mean conditional on the true state, $\E[Y_t - \mu_k^*(X_t) - \theta_k^*(T_t - m_k^*(X_t)) \mid X_t, T_t, S_t = k] = 0$, conditioning on observed history $Z_{1:N}$ alone yields:
\begin{equation}
    \E\left[ Y_t - \mu_k^*(X_t) - \theta_k^* (T_t - m_k^*(X_t)) \;\middle|\; X_t, T_t, Z_{1:N} \right] = \sum_{j \neq k} \gamma_{tj}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*) \left[ (\mu_j^*(X_t) - \mu_k^*(X_t)) + (\theta_j^* - \theta_k^*)(T_t - m_j^*(X_t)) \right].
\end{equation}
This residual is non-zero whenever regimes differ in baseline potential outcomes ($\mu_j^* \neq \mu_k^*$) or treatment responsiveness ($\theta_j^* \neq \theta_k^*$).

Under purged block cross-fitting (Algorithm 1), this first-order variation is controlled via two complementary mechanisms:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Decoupling via Purged Embargo Buffer:} The parameters $\hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}$ are estimated on historical calibration blocks or out-of-fold data separated by an embargo buffer $\tau^* \ge C \log N$. By the $\alpha$-mixing decay of the underlying process (Assumption~\ref{ass:mixing}), the covariance between evaluation fold innovations and the parameter estimation error $\hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}} - \boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*$ decays as $O(N^{-C c_\alpha})$.
    \item \textbf{Stacked Joint HAC Variance Representation:} When $\hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}$ is estimated at parametric rate $\sqrt{N}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}} - \boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*) = \mathcal{I}_\Lambda^{-1} \frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{t=1}^N \boldsymbol{\phi}_{\Lambda, t} + o_P(1)$, first-order parameter variation is absorbed into the sequence-level influence representation $\boldsymbol{\phi}_t = \boldsymbol{\phi}_{\theta, t} + \boldsymbol{A}_\Lambda \mathcal{I}_\Lambda^{-1} \boldsymbol{\phi}_{\Lambda, t}$, yielding the stacked M-estimation limiting distribution:
    \begin{equation}
        \sqrt{N} \begin{pmatrix} \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^* - \mathrm{Bias}(\lambda) \\ \hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}} - \boldsymbol{\Lambda}^* \end{pmatrix} \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}\left(\mathbf{0}, \begin{bmatrix} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\lambda & \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\theta \Lambda} \\ \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\theta \Lambda}^\top & \mathcal{I}_\Lambda^{-1} \end{bmatrix}\right),
    \end{equation}
    where $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\theta \Lambda} \equiv \operatorname{LRCov}(\boldsymbol{\phi}_{\theta, t}, \boldsymbol{\phi}_{\Lambda, t})$, and the long-run covariance $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\lambda$ and cross-term $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\theta \Lambda}$ are consistently captured by the joint Newey-West HAC sandwich estimator. In well-separated regimes ($\Delta_Z \to \infty$), posterior uncertainty vanishes ($\gamma_{tk} \to \mathbb{I}(S_t=k)$) and $\partial \gamma_{tk} / \partial \boldsymbol{\Lambda} \to \mathbf{0}$ exponentially fast, rendering $\|\boldsymbol{A}_\Lambda\| \le C e^{-c \Delta_Z^2}$ and recovering asymptotic orthogonality.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:variance}: Joint Delta-Method Inference for Population ATE}
\label{app:proof_prop6}
We distinguish three distinct regime weighting vectors:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{True Stationary Distribution $\boldsymbol{\pi}^*$:} The invariant measure of the underlying Markov transition kernel $\boldsymbol{\Pi}^*$, satisfying $\boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top} \boldsymbol{\Pi}^* = \boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top}$.
    \item \textbf{Estimated HMM Stationary Distribution $\hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}})$:} The stationary vector derived from the estimated HMM transition matrix $\hat{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}$, where $\hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}$ denotes the vector of estimated HMM parameters.
    \item \textbf{Sample Posterior Occupation Distribution $\bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_N$:} The empirical average of posterior smoothed regime weights across the observed sample: $\bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_N \equiv \frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t$.
\end{enumerate}

The sample Average Treatment Effect estimator is $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SATE}} = \sum_{k=1}^K \bar{\gamma}_{N, k} \hat{\theta}_{\lambda, k} = \bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_N^\top \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda$. Conditional on the realized sample regime occupancy, its asymptotic variance is governed solely by parameter estimation uncertainty:
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_{\mathrm{SATE}}^2 = \bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_N^\top \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\lambda \bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_N.
\end{equation}

For the population estimand $\theta_{\mathrm{PATE}}^* = \boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top} \boldsymbol{\theta}^*$, the plug-in estimator is $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{PATE}} = \hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}}^\top \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda$. Expanding $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{PATE}}$ around $(\boldsymbol{\pi}^*, \boldsymbol{\theta}^*)$ via the multivariate delta method:
\begin{equation}
    \sqrt{N}\left( \hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{PATE}} - \theta_{\mathrm{PATE}}^* \right) = \boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top} \sqrt{N}\left( \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^* \right) + \boldsymbol{\theta}^{*\top} \sqrt{N}\left( \hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}} - \boldsymbol{\pi}^* \right) + o_P(1).
\end{equation}
The asymptotic variance of $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{PATE}}$ is given by the quadratic form of the joint limiting distribution:
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_{\mathrm{PATE}}^2 = \lim_{N \to \infty} \Var\left( \sqrt{N}(\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{PATE}} - \theta_{\mathrm{PATE}}^*) \right) = \boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\lambda \boldsymbol{\pi}^* + \boldsymbol{\theta}^{*\top} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\pi \boldsymbol{\theta}^* + 2 \boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\theta\pi} \boldsymbol{\theta}^*,
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\lambda \equiv \lim_{N \to \infty} N \Var(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda)$, $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\pi \equiv \lim_{N \to \infty} N \Var(\hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}})$, and $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\theta\pi} \equiv \lim_{N \to \infty} N \Cov(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda, \hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}})$.

\textbf{Cross-Covariance Analysis and HMM Influence Orthogonality:}
When both $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda$ and $\hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}}$ are estimated from the same longitudinal record, they share the estimated HMM parameter vector $\hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}$. Let $\boldsymbol{\phi}_\theta(W_t) \equiv (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \boldsymbol{\Psi}_t$ denote the uncentered DML influence function and $\boldsymbol{\phi}_{\Lambda, t} \equiv \nabla_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}} \log p(Z_t \mid Z_{1:t-1}; \boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*)$ denote the sequence-level HMM influence increment. The asymptotic representation of $\hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}}$ satisfies:
\begin{equation}
    \sqrt{N}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}} - \boldsymbol{\pi}^*) = \nabla_\Lambda \boldsymbol{\pi}^* \mathcal{I}_\Lambda^{-1} \frac{1}{\sqrt{N}} \sum_{t=1}^N \boldsymbol{\phi}_{\Lambda, t} + o_P(1),
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{I}_\Lambda$ is the asymptotic Fisher information matrix of the HMM. Consequently, the asymptotic cross-covariance evaluates to:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\theta\pi} = \lim_{N \to \infty} \frac{1}{N} \sum_{t=1}^N \sum_{s=1}^N \E\left[ \boldsymbol{\phi}_\theta(W_t) \boldsymbol{\phi}_{\Lambda, s}^\top \right] \mathcal{I}_\Lambda^{-1} (\nabla_\Lambda \boldsymbol{\pi}^*)^\top = \operatorname{LRCov}(\boldsymbol{\phi}_\theta, \boldsymbol{\phi}_\Lambda) \mathcal{I}_\Lambda^{-1} (\nabla_\Lambda \boldsymbol{\pi}^*)^\top.
\end{equation}
Therefore, a sufficient condition for $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\theta\pi} = \mathbf{0}$ is that the causal score innovations and the latent Markov sequence influence satisfy long-run cross-orthogonality: $\operatorname{LRCov}(\boldsymbol{\phi}_\theta, \boldsymbol{\phi}_\Lambda) = \mathbf{0}$. While this orthogonality holds under structural innovation independence where $\E[\boldsymbol{\Psi}_t \mid Z_{1:N}] = \mathbf{0}$, in empirical settings where unmodeled feedback exists between emissions and treatment errors, $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\theta\pi}$ may be non-zero. In practice, $\sigma_{\mathrm{PATE}}^2$ is consistently estimated by computing a joint Newey-West HAC covariance over the stacked $(2K)$-dimensional influence sequence $(\boldsymbol{\phi}_\theta(W_t)^\top, \boldsymbol{\phi}_\pi(Z_t)^\top)^\top$, where $\boldsymbol{\phi}_\pi(Z_t) \equiv \nabla_\Lambda \boldsymbol{\pi}^* \mathcal{I}_\Lambda^{-1} \boldsymbol{\phi}_{\Lambda, t}$.

\textbf{Stationary Distribution Variance vs.\ Sample Regime Occupancy:}
The parametric asymptotic variance of the estimated stationary distribution vector $\hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}})$ is $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\pi = \nabla_\Lambda \boldsymbol{\pi}^* \mathcal{I}_\Lambda^{-1} (\nabla_\Lambda \boldsymbol{\pi}^*)^\top$.
By contrast, for the sample occupancy process $\bar{\boldsymbol{S}}_N \equiv \frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N \boldsymbol{H}_t$, its asymptotic covariance is governed by the fundamental matrix $\mathbf{Z} \equiv (\mathbf{I} - \boldsymbol{\Pi}^* + \mathbf{1}\boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top})^{-1}$ \citep{kemeny1976finite}:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{occ}} = \mathbf{D}_{\pi} \mathbf{Z} + \mathbf{Z}^\top \mathbf{D}_{\pi} - \mathbf{D}_{\pi} - \boldsymbol{\pi}^* \boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top},
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{D}_{\pi} = \mathrm{diag}(\boldsymbol{\pi}^*)$.
For a two-state Markov chain ($K=2$) with persistence parameter $\rho = 1 - P_{01} - P_{10} = \lambda_2(\boldsymbol{\Pi}^*)$:
\begin{equation}
    \Var(\bar{S}_N) = \frac{\pi_0^* \pi_1^*}{N} \left( \frac{1 + \rho}{1 - \rho} \right) + o(N^{-1}).
\end{equation}
When $\Delta \theta^* = \theta_1^* - \theta_0^* \neq 0$, the temporal persistence of sample regime occupancy inflates the sampling variance of $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SATE}}$ by $\frac{1+\rho}{1-\rho} \approx 15.7\times$ under persistent stagnation ($\rho \approx 0.88$) relative to independent transitions. $\blacksquare$

\paragraph{Representation Accuracy vs.\ Downstream Task Reliability.}
To evaluate whether latent sequence representations translate to reliable downstream prediction under distribution shift, we fit a downstream Ridge predictor $f_m: (X_t, T_t, \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t^{(m)}) \mapsto Y_t$ strictly on the training partition ($N=1,200$) and freeze it. In addition, nuisance models $\hat{m}_k(X_t) = \E[T_t \mid X_t, S_t=k]$ are estimated on training data to construct residualized treatments $\tilde{T}_{tk} = T_t - \hat{m}_k(X_t)$. We compute the exact coupled regime Gram matrix $\boldsymbol{J}_t = \frac{1}{w}\sum_{s=t-w+1}^t \begin{pmatrix} \gamma_{s0}^2 \tilde{T}_{s0}^2 & \gamma_{s0}\gamma_{s1}\tilde{T}_{s0}\tilde{T}_{s1} \\ \gamma_{s0}\gamma_{s1}\tilde{T}_{s0}\tilde{T}_{s1} & \gamma_{s1}^2 \tilde{T}_{s1}^2 \end{pmatrix}$ across rolling evaluation windows ($w=48$) and extract its exact minimum eigenvalue $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}_t)$. On held-out Shift C ($N=1,200$, 5 independent random seeds), we split into a calibration partition ($N_{\mathrm{cal}}=400$) to determine the extreme failure loss threshold $q_{0.90}$ once and freeze it for out-of-sample test evaluation, preventing threshold re-definition during bootstrap resamples. We evaluate future lead loss $L_{t+1} = (Y_{t+1} - \hat{Y}_{t+1})^2$ against three distinct signals: (i) State Posterior Entropy $H_t = H(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t)$, (ii) Task Conditioning alone $1/\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}_t)$, and (iii) Coupled Task Difficulty $\mathcal{D}_t = H_t / \lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}_t)$. To quantify estimation uncertainty over temporally dependent observations, intervals are computed via Moving-Block Bootstrap (MBB, $B=1,000$ resamples with block length $L=24$ hours), serving as an empirical uncertainty quantification procedure rather than asserting independent sample generation. Sensitivity analysis across block lengths $L \in \{12, 24, 48, 72\}$ confirms that MBB 95\% CIs are remarkably stable (HMM CI width $0.142\text{--}0.147$ across all $L$; \texttt{reports/representation\_zoo\_mbb\_sensitivity.csv}).

\textbf{Offline Representation Diagnostics vs.\ Prospective Failure Signals.}
We formalize a three-level \textbf{Reliability Triangle}: (1)~\emph{State Identification} ($F_1$), (2)~\emph{Probabilistic Trustworthiness} ($\text{ECE}, \text{NLL}$), and (3)~\emph{Downstream Reliability} ($\text{ROC-AUC}, \mathcal{D}_t$). While retrospective diagnostics ($F_1, \text{ECE}$) require ground-truth state labels, online monitoring must rely on prospective signals ($H_t, \lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}_t), \mathcal{D}_t$). As shown in Table~\ref{tab:bench_models} (Panel~B), deep neural sequence models (GRU, Transformer, SSM) achieve competitive latent recovery ($F_1 \approx 0.84\text{--}0.86$), yet they produce near-chance downstream failure detection ($\text{ROC-AUC} \approx 0.515\text{--}0.549$). In contrast, Gaussian HMM causal filtering achieves well-calibrated states ($\text{ECE} = 0.0397$) and statistically informative failure anticipation ($\text{ROC-AUC} = \mathbf{0.6153 \pm 0.0246}$, MBB 95\% CI $[0.540, 0.683]$, $\text{PR-AUC} = 0.1300$). In multi-world fixed-effects regressions across 15 benchmark worlds ($N=60$; \texttt{reports/representation\_zoo\_hierarchical\_regression.csv}), classification $F_1$ explains substantial reliability ($\beta_1 = +0.9817 \pm 0.2011, p = 0.0002, R^2_{\mathrm{within}} = 0.4313$), but posterior log-loss provides incremental explanatory information beyond $F_1$ ($\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{within}} = +0.0924, \beta_{\mathrm{NLL}} = -0.0653 \pm 0.0295, p = 0.0442$), raising $R^2_{\mathrm{within}} \to 0.5237$. Calibration ECE exhibits consistent negative association ($\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{within}} = 0.0647$, raising $R^2_{\mathrm{within}} \to 0.4960$). Sensitivity analysis over numerical eigenvalue floors $\epsilon \in [10^{-6}, 10^{-3}]$ confirms identical AUCs ($0.6153$).

\section{Post-Audit Stress Tests: When Proxy Geometry Misleads}
\label{app:postaudit}
This appendix records adversarial tests added after the primary experiments. They are included to delimit the scope of the scalar difficulty diagnostic rather than to retroactively redefine the preregistered headline experiment.

\paragraph{Phantom causal resolution.}
Let $H_t$ be the one-hot true state and consider the oracle treatment-information matrix $J_{\rm true}=\E[\widetilde T_t^2 H_tH_t^\top]$. In a balanced two-state model with true residual treatment variances $a>b>0$, a symmetric hard classifier that flips labels with probability $p\in(0,1/2)$ yields inferred diagonal information proportional to $((1-p)a+pb,\;pa+(1-p)b)/2$. Hence the smaller inferred information is $[pa+(1-p)b]/2>b/2$: state confusion can \emph{increase} the apparent weakest information direction. At $p=1/2$ the apparent condition number is one even when the oracle condition number $a/b$ is arbitrarily large. In a three-state Monte Carlo with residual treatment standard deviations $(1,.3,.08)$ and emission separation $\Delta_Z=.5$, oracle $\lambda_{\min}\approx.0022$ while soft/hard generated-state values are approximately $.0246/.0457$ (11.4x/21.2x inflation). Thus a large generated-state $\lambda_{\min}$ is not automatically a conservative certificate of oracle causal resolution.

\paragraph{Why posterior means are insufficient for information moments.}
For one-hot $H_t$ and posterior $q_t=P(S_t\mid\mathcal I_t)$,
\[
\E[H_tH_t^\top\mid\mathcal I_t]=\operatorname{diag}(q_t),
\]
not $q_tq_t^\top$. For any weight $W_t$ measurable with respect to $\mathcal I_t$,
$\E[W_tH_tH_t^\top]=\E[W_t\operatorname{diag}(q_t)]$.
This identity can recover oracle second moments under a correctly specified posterior whose information set contains every state-informative variable entering $W_t$. It is not a free correction: misspecifying the state--treatment model can recreate phantom resolution. We therefore treat completed geometry as a sensitivity diagnostic rather than a point-identified certificate.

\paragraph{Higher-dimensional scope of the scalar ratio.}
The bound $\|b_\gamma\|/\lambda_{\min}(J)$ is worst-directional. In $K\ge3$, equal-magnitude representation perturbations can have very different consequences depending on their alignment with information eigenvectors. The local displacement is $J^{-1}b_\gamma$; for a scientific contrast $a$, the relevant first-order quantity is $|a^\top J^{-1}b_\gamma|$. This explains why the two-state factorial ratio can rank error extremely well while no task-agnostic scalar representation score is sufficient in general.

\paragraph{Dual resolution.}
Fine latent states may remain necessary for nuisance adjustment even when estimating a separate causal coefficient for every state is unsupported. If microstates share a scientific effect but differ in treatment/outcome baselines, coarse nuisance adjustment can reintroduce confounding, whereas fine adjustment followed by pooling of task-equivalent effect parameters can retain identification and aggregate treatment information. We therefore distinguish \emph{adjustment resolution} from \emph{target resolution}: retain enough latent detail to remove confounding, but estimate only effect contrasts supported by the scientific question and causal information.

\paragraph{Observational collision tests.}
The sensor study reinforces the need for separate diagnostics. Delhi has favorable generated-state conditioning but rejects all three reported immediate pre-treatment placebos. Mumbai becomes more certain in posterior entropy after removing long frozen-$\mathrm{NO}_2$ runs while its conditioning worsens and its regime decomposition changes materially. Kolkata remains near-singular after cleaning. These observations are not oracle validation of phantom resolution; they show that representation certainty, downstream information, data quality, temporal falsification, and scientific identification are distinct axes.

\begin{abstract}
Observational time series are often driven by an unobserved, persistent state, or regime (for example the mixing regime of the atmosphere), that affects both a treatment and an outcome. A common remedy infers the regime from noisy auxiliary measurements (proxies) with a hidden Markov model (HMM) and then applies Double Machine Learning (DML). Practitioners judge such pipelines by two signs: the inferred regime looks confident, and the matrix in the estimating equation looks well conditioned. We show that both signs can mislead: when the inferred regime is wrong, the conditioning can improve while the estimate gets worse (``phantom causal resolution''). We derive the exact bias of two-stage estimators, which is governed by the uncertainty about the regime that remains after inference, and identify an orthogonal design (OR-DML) that is the most accurate regime-aware two-stage variant. We also show that regime-specific effects are identified when the proxies are informative and affect treatment and outcome only through the regime, and give a deconvolution estimator based on this result. In simulations and a benchmark calibrated to four Indian cities, two-stage plug-in estimators, including ours, remain biased when proxies are weak, while a correctly specified joint model or the deconvolution estimator does better. An observational sensor study with falsification tests is reported as an association, not an emissions effect. We give a sensitivity bound for the weak-proxy case and do not claim nominal coverage for the plug-in estimators.
\end{abstract}

\section{INTRODUCTION}
\label{sec:intro}

\textbf{A motivating example.} Does reducing nitrogen dioxide (NO$_2$) reduce fine particulate matter (PM$_{2.5}$) in a city? Both are driven by the state of the atmosphere, for instance whether the air is stagnant or well ventilated, and that state is not measured directly. When stagnant air raises both pollutants, a naive comparison attributes the joint rise to NO$_2$. Adjusting for observed weather helps only partly, because the unobserved state is not a function of the weather we observe. The same structure appears in the liquidity regime of a market or the operating mode of a plant.

\textbf{The usual remedy and the usual check.} A standard remedy has two steps. First, infer the unobserved \emph{regime} (the value of the persistent latent state) from auxiliary measurements, or \emph{proxies}, with a hidden Markov model (HMM); this yields, for every time point, a \emph{posterior} probability for each regime. Second, use these probabilities in Double Machine Learning \citep[DML;][]{chernozhukov2018double}, which estimates a causal effect after removing the part of treatment and outcome that the observed variables explain. In practice such pipelines are judged by two signs: the posterior is confident, and the matrix DML inverts to obtain the effects (the \emph{score Jacobian}) is well conditioned, that is, its smallest eigenvalue $\lmin$ is not close to zero. Both quantities appear in error bounds of the form $C\eps/\lmin(\bJ)$, which we also prove (Theorem~\ref{thm:graceful}).

\textbf{What we show.} These two signs can mislead. When the inferred regime is wrong, the error does not only add noise: it leaks the between-regime difference in treatment into the variation that DML treats as exogenous. That can make the matrix look \emph{better} conditioned while the causal estimate gets \emph{worse}. We call this \emph{phantom causal resolution} (Figure~\ref{fig:phantom}). In a controlled two-regime design the smallest eigenvalue grows ${\CFLamInfl}\times$ while the error first rises and then falls, and with posteriors learned by EM the inferred-regime matrix looks better conditioned than the true-regime one in \LPFracInfl\% of runs.

\textbf{Why it happens.} For the coupled regime score we derive the exact population displacement under state confusion, meaning that the posterior puts mass on the wrong regime (Proposition~\ref{prop:exact}). It reproduces all \CFNCells\ cells of a 4,900-run factorial experiment (observed/predicted ratio \CFRatioMed, interquartile range \CFRatioLo--\CFRatioHi) and shows where the damage arises: \emph{weighting} regime-specific nuisance regressions by the posterior leaks regime contrasts into the score, whereas \emph{conditioning} the nuisances on the posterior makes the coupled score exactly Neyman-orthogonal for every posterior (Lemma~\ref{lem:orth}). What remains is an exact omitted-variable identity in the \emph{residual state variance} $v$ that the posterior leaves behind (Theorem~\ref{thm:law}); for a pooled effect it is $\Delta_T\Delta_g v/(\Delta_T^2v+\sigma^2)$. The conditioning diagnostic shares a factor with the bias numerator, so no function of $(\eps,\lmin)$ alone can certify reliability. Both ingredients are estimable up to one unidentified outcome shift, which supports a sensitivity analysis.

\textbf{Contributions.}
(i)~\emph{Failure and limits:} an exact decomposition of the omitted-regime bias of standard DML, including bias amplification, and non-identification when proxies carry no regime information (Theorems~\ref{thm:bias}--\ref{thm:impossibility}).
(ii)~\emph{Mechanism:} exact laws for how state confusion enters weighted and posterior-conditioned estimators, explaining phantom resolution, the non-monotone error, and why a posterior helps as an adjustment variable but hurts as a nuisance weight (Proposition~\ref{prop:exact}, Theorem~\ref{thm:law}).
(iii)~\emph{Identification:} regime-specific effects are identified when the proxies are informative and act on treatment and outcome only through the regime; Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility} is the singular case (Proposition~\ref{prop:ident}). The result yields a deconvolution estimator (DC-DML).
(iv)~\emph{Two-stage estimator:} overlap-aware regime DML (OR-DML), with posterior-adjusted nuisances, purged block cross-fitting and spectral regularization, exactly orthogonal and with a degradation bound separating nuisance from state-recovery error (Lemma~\ref{lem:orth}, Theorems~\ref{thm:graceful},~\ref{thm:asymptotics}). It is the most accurate regime-aware two-stage variant we test, but not the most accurate estimator overall (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}).
(v)~\emph{Evidence:} a factorial design checked against the closed form; learned-posterior experiments in which the proxy error is not set by hand; a benchmark built from real data in four Indian cities with real gaps and known causal effects; comparison with deconvolution and joint-likelihood estimators; and an audited sensor study with aligned placebos and robustness values.

\textbf{Related work.} Orthogonal and debiased estimation \citep{chernozhukov2018double,chernozhukov2022automatic,foster2023orthogonal,nie2021quasi} and its time-series extensions \citep{lewis2020double,semenova2023generalized,ciganovic2026dml} assume the confounders are observed. Sequential deconfounders learn latent confounders with neural representations \citep{bica2020time,hatt2021sequential}; our results caution that representation quality and downstream reliability are different objects. Latent-state and proximal approaches target identification \citep{miao2018identifying,tchetgen2020introduction,mastouri2021proximal,cui2024semiparametric,clouth2024latent}, and regime-switching models go back to \citet{hamilton1989new}. We instead quantify what a \emph{plug-in} posterior leaves behind, and compare against a joint likelihood and a deconvolution estimator. Our bias law is an omitted-variable identity \citep{greenland1980effect,cinelli2020making,chernozhukov2022long}, and its non-monotonicity is the algebra of bias amplification \citep{pearl2012bias,ding2017instrumental}; we claim neither as new. What is new is its calibrated, dependent-data and regime-coupled form, the exact law for regime-weighted scores, the orthogonal design that follows from them, and the consequence that the Gram-matrix diagnostics used for weak overlap \citep{crump2009dealing,damour2021overlap} can move in the wrong direction when the state is generated. Posterior-based adjustment is known to fail without identification \citep{wang2019blessings,damour2019multicause}; we characterize how it fails.

\section{SETTING AND THE FAILURE OF STANDARD DML}
\label{sec:setup}

We observe $(Y_t,T_t,X_t,Z_t)_{t=1}^N$ generated by
\begin{align}
S_t&\sim\mathrm{Markov}(\Pi,\pi_0),\quad Z_t=\mu_Z(S_t)+\xi_t, \label{eq:scm1}\\
T_t&=m(X_t,S_t)+V_t,\quad Y_t=\theta(S_t)T_t+g(X_t,S_t)+U_t, \label{eq:scm2}
\end{align}
with $S_t\in\{1,\dots,K\}$ latent, $\E[V_t\mid X_t,S_t]=0$, $\E[U_t\mid X_t,S_t,T_t]=0$ and $\theta(S_t)=\sum_k\theta_k^*\one(S_t=k)$. Targets are the regime effects $\theta_k^*$ and the stationary average $\theta^*_{\rm ATE}=\sum_k\pi_k^*\theta_k^*$. In our application $T$ is NO$_2$, $Y$ is PM$_{2.5}$, $X$ collects lagged pollutants and weather, and $Z$ is meteorology. Regime-conditional unconfoundedness, overlap and positivity, bounded variables, geometric $\alpha$-mixing and the usual $o_P(N^{-1/4})$ nuisance rates are stated as Assumptions~\ref{ass:unconfoundedness}--\ref{ass:rates} in Appendix~\ref{app:assumptions}.

\begin{theorem}[Omitted regime bias]
\label{thm:bias}
Standard DML that residualizes on $X_t$ alone satisfies $\hat\theta_{\rm naive}\to_p\sum_k\omega_k\theta_k^*+\mathcal B_{\rm het}+\mathcal B_{\rm conf}$, with $\omega_k=\E[\one(S_t{=}k)\tilde T_t^2]/\E\tilde T_t^2$ and
$\mathcal B_{\rm conf}=\sum_k\E[\pi_k(X)\Delta m_k(X)\Delta g_k(X)]/\E[\tilde T_t^2]$,
where $\Delta m_k,\Delta g_k$ are regime deviations of the treatment mean and outcome baseline (full statement and proof in Appendix~\ref{app:proof_thm1}).
\end{theorem}
Because $\E\tilde T_t^2$ shrinks as $X$ explains more of $T$ while the latent covariance in the numerator does not, residualizing on more observed controls can \emph{amplify} omitted-regime bias and flip its sign \citep{pearl2012bias}. In our synthetic frontier, standard DML has bias \FDStd\ on a true ATE of \FDTrueATE\ at every level of proxy quality.

\begin{theorem}[Non-identification without state information]
\label{thm:impossibility}
If $P(Z_t\mid S_t{=}j)=P(Z_t\mid S_t{=}k)$ for all $j,k$ and transitions are memoryless, there exist two models satisfying all assumptions, with identical laws of $(Y,T,X,Z)_{1:N}$ for every $N$ and distinct, non-permuted effect vectors (Appendix~\ref{app:proof_thm2}).
\end{theorem}
Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility} is the singular case of a more general fact. Let $\bgam_t=P(S_t\mid Z_{1:N})$ and assume: (a)~the proxy HMM is identified up to label permutation \citep{allman2009identifiability}, so $\bgam_t$ is a functional of the observed law; (b)~\emph{exclusion}, $(T_t,Y_t)\perp Z_{1:N}\mid(X_t,S_t)$; (c)~the controls carry no regime information beyond the proxies, $P(S_t\mid X_t,Z_{1:N})=\bgam_t$; (d)~$M(x)=\E[\bgam_t\bgam_t^\top\mid X_t=x]$ is nonsingular for almost every $x$.

\begin{proposition}[Identification with informative proxies]
\label{prop:ident}
Under (a)--(d) and Assumptions~\ref{ass:unconfoundedness}--\ref{ass:overlap}, for every integrable $h$, $\E[h(T_t,Y_t)\mid X_t,Z_{1:N}]=\bgam_t^\top\mu_h(X_t)$ with $\mu_h(x)=M(x)^{-1}\E[\bgam_th(T_t,Y_t)\mid X_t{=}x]$. Hence $\theta_k^*=\E[\Cov(T,Y\mid X,S{=}k)\mid S{=}k]\,/\,\E[\Var(T\mid X,S{=}k)\mid S{=}k]$ and $\theta^*_{\rm ATE}$ are identified, up to relabeling of the regimes. For $K=2$, (d) fails exactly when $\bgam_t$ is almost surely constant given $X_t$, which is the setting of Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility}.
\end{proposition}
The proof is in Appendix~\ref{app:ident}. Identification therefore rests on the exclusion restriction (b) and on $\lmin(M)>0$, not on proxy sufficiency (Assumption~\ref{ass:innovation}), and the quantity that certifies it is $\lmin(M)$ rather than $\lmin(\bJ)$. The proposition suggests \emph{deconvolution DML} (DC-DML): regress $T$, $T^2$, $Y$ and $TY$ on $\bgam_t\otimes\varphi(X_t)$ for a basis $\varphi$, read off the regime-conditional moments, and plug them into the expression for $\theta_k^*$. It requires (b) and (c), which fail in our real-data application, and its variance grows as $\lmin(M)\to0$. We compare it with two-stage designs and with a joint likelihood in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}. Between full identification and none lies the regime that matters in practice: proxies that are informative but imperfect, which is where two-stage plug-in estimators are used and where they are biased.

\section{OVERLAP-AWARE REGIME DML}
\label{sec:method}

Stepwise latent-model analysis is established, and classification error already motivates explicit corrections \citep{dimari2016bias}. We study the two-stage pipeline because it is the common practical recipe and accepts any state estimator; joint latent-model likelihoods and a deconvolution estimator are compared in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}. Proposition~\ref{prop:exact} isolates soft target weighting with oracle regime nuisances; Lemma~\ref{lem:orth} addresses nuisance estimation; Theorem~\ref{thm:law} characterizes the residual bias after posterior adjustment.

OR-DML infers posteriors $\gamma_{tk}=P(S_t{=}k\mid Z_{1:N})$ (smoothing) or $P(S_t{=}k\mid Z_{1:t})$ (causal filtering) with a Gaussian HMM. It cross-fits one pair of nuisances on the posterior-augmented features $F_t=(X_t,\bgam_t,\bgam_t\otimes X_t)$, $\hat m(F)\approx\E[T\mid F]$ and $\hat\mu(F)\approx\E[Y\mid F]$, on contiguous blocks separated by an embargo of $\tau^\ast$ steps. With $\tilde T_t=T_t-\hat m(F_t)$ and $\tilde Y_t=Y_t-\hat\mu(F_t)$ it solves the coupled system
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:coupled}
\hat J_{jk}=\tfrac1N\textstyle\sum_t\gamma_{tj}\gamma_{tk}\tilde T_t^2,\quad
\hat S_k=\tfrac1N\textstyle\sum_t\gamma_{tk}\tilde T_t\tilde Y_t,
\end{equation}
$\hat\bth_\lambda=(\hat\bJ+\lambda I)^{-1}\hat\bS$. This is an R-learner-type residual regression \citep{nie2021quasi} whose effect basis $\bgam_t$ is itself estimated; Theorems~\ref{thm:graceful}--\ref{thm:asymptotics} account for that estimation. The posterior enters twice: as an \emph{adjustment feature} in the nuisances (fine resolution) and as a \emph{weight} that defines the regime effects (coarse target). Bartlett bands (confidence bands for the residual autocorrelation) guide the choice of the embargo length; they do not estimate $c_\beta$ or certify independence. Theorem~\ref{thm:asymptotics} separately requires $\tau_N\ge C\log N$ and augmented-process mixing. Diurnal dependence motivates lag sensitivity.

\begin{lemma}[Exact orthogonality]
\label{lem:orth}
Let $\psi_k(\bth;m,\mu)=\gamma_k(T-m)\{Y-\mu-\sum_j\gamma_j\theta_j(T-m)\}$. At $m=\E[T\mid F]$, $\mu=\E[Y\mid F]$, the Gateaux derivative of $\E\psi_k$ in every direction $(\delta_m,\delta_\mu)$ of square-integrable functions of $F$ vanishes, for every $\bth$ and every posterior $\bgam$; so does that of $\E[\gamma_j\gamma_k(T-m)^2]$.
\end{lemma}
\emph{Proof.} Each derivative is an expectation of a product of an $F$-measurable factor (the weights $\gamma$ and $\delta$) and $T-m$ or $Y-\mu-\sum_j\gamma_j\theta_j(T-m)$, whose conditional mean given $F$ is zero. $\square$

The natural alternative, closest to regime fixed effects, fits regime-$k$ nuisances on $X$ with weights $\gamma_{\cdot k}$. Its Gram part is not orthogonal for soft weights: under a nuisance perturbation its score derivative is \OrthW\% of the Jacobian, against \OrthPA\% for Lemma~\ref{lem:orth} (Appendix~\ref{app:pa}). Section~\ref{sec:theory} shows why it fails, and we keep it as an ablation. Two simpler estimators on the same inputs are our main baselines: \emph{hard regime fixed effects} (one-hot $\arg\max$ weights) and \emph{posterior-feature DML}, a single-index DML with nuisances on $F_t$. Algorithm~\ref{alg:ordml} summarizes the procedure and the diagnostics we recommend reporting with it. Complexity is $\mathcal O(B\,C_{\rm nuis}+NK^2+K^3)$; Appendix~\ref{app:algorithm} checks $K\le5$.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{OR-DML with a reliability report}
\label{alg:ordml}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE $\{Y_t,T_t,X_t,Z_t\}_{t=1}^N$, folds $B$, penalty $\lambda\ge0$.
\STATE Fit a Gaussian HMM to $Z$ (multi-restart EM); compute posteriors $\bgam_t$ (smoothing or causal filter).
\STATE Embargo $\tau^*=\max\{h:\max_j|\hat\rho_j(h)|\ge1.96/\sqrt N\}+1$, at least $C\log N$.
\STATE Cross-fit $\hat m,\hat\mu$ on $F_t=(X_t,\bgam_t,\bgam_t\otimes X_t)$ over purged contiguous blocks.
\STATE Form $\hat\bJ,\hat\bS$ by~\eqref{eq:coupled}; return $\hat\bth_\lambda=(\hat\bJ+\lambda I)^{-1}\hat\bS$ with a HAC sandwich.
\STATE \textbf{Report:} hard-FE and posterior-feature baselines; $\hat v$, $\hat a$, $\widehat{\Var}(\tilde T)$ and the robustness value $b^\star$ (Section~\ref{sec:theory}); raw and standardized $\lmin$; lead placebos aligned to the outcome's information set.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{HOW STATE UNCERTAINTY AND TASK CONDITIONING PROPAGATE}
\label{sec:theory}

Let $\boldsymbol H_t=\boldsymbol e_{S_t}$, $\eps=\E\|\bgam_t-\boldsymbol H_t\|_1$, and let $\bJ,\bS$ be the population versions of the coupled system at the population nuisances of either design. Define $\bth_\gamma=\bJ^{-1}\bS$ and $\boldsymbol b_\gamma=\bS-\bJ\bth^*$, so $\bth_\gamma-\bth^*=\bJ^{-1}\boldsymbol b_\gamma$ exactly.

\begin{theorem}[Graceful degradation]
\label{thm:graceful}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:overlap} and~\ref{ass:rates}, suppose the nuisance targets are within $C_\eta\eps$ of the oracle regime nuisances in $L^1$. This holds for posterior-weighted nuisances, and for posterior-adjusted nuisances when the posterior is calibrated given $F$ and $Z$ is independent of $(T,Y)$ given $(X,S)$. If $\lmin(\bJ)>0$, then $\|\bth_\gamma-\bth^*\|_2\le C\eps/\lmin(\bJ)$ with explicit $C$, and
$\|\hat\bth_\gamma-\bth^*\|_2\le C\eps/\lmin(\bJ)+\mathcal O_P\big((N^{-1/2}+\rho_N)/\lmin(\bJ)\big)$,
with $\rho_N=r_N^2$ for posterior-adjusted nuisances (Lemma~\ref{lem:orth}) and $\rho_N=r_N$ for posterior-weighted ones, where $r_N$ is the nuisance rate (Appendices~\ref{app:proof_thm3},~\ref{app:pa}).
\end{theorem}
The bound separates the two errors the abstract names: state recovery ($\eps$) and nuisance estimation ($\rho_N$). In a controlled design $\eps/\lmin(\hat\bJ)$ ranks causal error almost perfectly (Spearman \FacRhoD\ over \FacNRuns\ runs, versus \FacRhoEps\ for $\eps$ and \FacRhoLam\ for $1/\lmin$). The next two results explain why that success is fragile: $\lmin(\bJ)$ is itself a function of the state error.

\textbf{The coupled score under state confusion.} Consider two regimes with stationary weights $\pi_s$, treatment means $m_s$, outcome means $\mu_s=\theta_sm_s+g_s$ and innovation scales $\sigma_s$. Take regime-wise nuisances (the posterior-weighted design with correct regime means) and a proxy that places weight $1-\alpha$ on the true regime and $\alpha=\eps/2$ on the other.

\begin{proposition}[Exact law for regime-weighted scores]
\label{prop:exact}
With $\gamma_j(s)=1-\alpha$ if $j=s$ and $\alpha$ otherwise,
\begin{align*}
J_{jk}&=\textstyle\sum_s\pi_s\gamma_j(s)\gamma_k(s)\big[(m_s{-}m_j)(m_s{-}m_k)+\sigma_s^2\big],\\
S_j&=\textstyle\sum_s\pi_s\gamma_j(s)\big[(m_s{-}m_j)(\mu_s{-}\mu_j)+\theta_s\sigma_s^2\big].
\end{align*}
In the symmetric case ($\pi_s=\tfrac12$, $\sigma_s=\sigma$, $\Delta=m_1-m_0$, $G=\mu_1-\mu_0$), $\bJ$ has eigenvectors $(1,\pm1)$ with eigenvalues $\lambda_+=\tfrac12\sigma^2+\tfrac12\alpha^2\Delta^2$ and $\lambda_-=\tfrac12\sigma^2(1-2\alpha)^2+\tfrac12\alpha^2\Delta^2$, and
\begin{align*}
\bth_\gamma-\bth^*&=\frac{\alpha\Delta G-\tfrac12\alpha^2\Delta^2(\theta_0{+}\theta_1)}{2\lambda_+}\binom{1}{1}\\
&\quad+\frac{(\theta_0{-}\theta_1)\big(\alpha(1{-}2\alpha)\sigma^2-\tfrac12\alpha^2\Delta^2\big)}{2\lambda_-}\binom{1}{-1}.
\end{align*}
\end{proposition}
\begin{corollary}[Phantom resolution and the error hump]
\label{cor:phantom}
In the symmetric case: (a) $\lmin(\bJ)=\lambda_-$ exceeds its oracle value $\sigma^2/2$ whenever $\eps>8\sigma^2/(\Delta^2+4\sigma^2)$, so a confused state can look \emph{better} conditioned than the true one; (b) when $|\Delta G|\gg\sigma^2|\theta_0-\theta_1|$, the error is $\approx\sqrt2\,\tfrac{\eps}{2}|\Delta G|/(\sigma^2+\eps^2\Delta^2/4)$, maximized at $\varepsilon^\star=2\sigma/|\Delta|$ with peak $|G|/(\sqrt2\sigma)$, and \emph{decreasing} in $\eps$ beyond it; (c) the dominant displacement lies on the $\lambda_+$ eigenvector, so the worst-direction bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:graceful} is loose by $\lambda_+/\lambda_-$.
\end{corollary}
Proposition~\ref{prop:exact} is exact (verified symbolically in the supplement) and involves no sampling. Applied to the asymmetric factorial design (which crosses proxy error with regime-specific treatment-noise scales, so the law can be checked cell by cell) it reproduces every cell's median error to within ${\CFRatioMin}$--${\CFRatioMax}\times$ and locates the error peaks at $\eps\in\{\CFPeakA,\CFPeakB,\CFPeakC\}$ for $\sigma_1\in\{0.08,0.15,0.30\}$, exactly where they are observed (Figure~\ref{fig:phantom}). The factorial's near-perfect rank correlation is therefore an algebraic identity of the design, not a statistical regularity. It works partly \emph{because} $\lmin(\hat\bJ)$ inflates: replacing it by the design value $\sigma_1^2$ lowers the cell-level correlation from \CFRhoProxy\ to \CFRhoDesign. The leading displacement $\alpha\Delta G$ is the product of state error with the regime contrasts that the weighted nuisances fail to remove.

\textbf{Posterior-adjusted designs.} Posterior-adjusted nuisances remove those contrasts up to what the posterior cannot explain. Let $v_t=\gamma_t(1-\gamma_t)$ be the posterior variance of the regime indicator, and let $T_t=m(X_t)+aS_t+V_t$ and $Y_t=\theta_{S_t}T_t+g(X_t)+bS_t+U_t$.

\begin{theorem}[Residual-state bias law]
\label{thm:law}
Let $K=2$ and suppose the posterior is calibrated given $F$ ($P(S_t{=}1\mid F_t)=\gamma_t$), $V_t$ is independent of $(S,Z,X)$ with variance $\sigma^2$, nuisances are cross-fitted at rates with $r_N^2=o(N^{-1/2})$, and the posterior forgets distant proxies (Appendix~\ref{app:pa}). With score weights $\boldsymbol w_t=(1-\gamma_t,\gamma_t)$ (OR-DML) or $w_t\equiv1$ (posterior-feature DML),
\begin{gather*}
\hat\bth\to_p\bar\bth+\bJ^{-1}\boldsymbol b_\gamma,\\
\bJ=\E\big[\boldsymbol w\boldsymbol w^\top(a^2v+\sigma^2)\big],\quad
\boldsymbol b_\gamma=a\,\E[\boldsymbol w\,c\,v],
\end{gather*}
with $c_t=b+(\theta_1-\theta_0)\{m(X_t)+a(1-\gamma_t)\}$, $\bar\bth=\bth^*$ for OR-DML and the $(a^2v+\sigma^2)$-weighted mean of $\theta_0+(\theta_1-\theta_0)\gamma$ for the pooled estimator. Hence: (a) with no treatment regime shift ($a=0$) both are consistent for any calibrated posterior, whatever $b$ and the effect heterogeneity; (b) for homogeneous effects the pooled bias is $abv/(a^2v+\sigma^2)$, hump-shaped in $a$ with peak at $|a|=\sigma/\sqrt v$ and $\sup|B|=|b|\sqrt v/(2\sigma)$, increasing in $v$, and a soft posterior feature is never worse than its hard label; (c) $\bJ$ grows with $a^2v$, so phantom resolution is not specific to the weighted design.
\end{theorem}
The law is exact up to sampling error: over \CLNConf\ configurations with $N=10^6$ the estimator and the law agree to within \CLMaxDev, and with $a=0$ the bias is \CLZeroShift\ (Appendix~\ref{app:pa}). The pooled form with $K>2$ regimes is $a^\top\Sigma b/(a^\top\Sigma a+\sigma^2)$ with $\Sigma$ the residual state covariance (Appendix~\ref{app:biaslaw}). If the innovation variance depends on the regime, $\sigma^2$ in the pooled law is replaced by its posterior average $\E[\sigma_S^2]$, and the plug-in $\widehat{\Var}(\tilde T)-\hat a^2\hat v$ still estimates it, so the sensitivity analysis is unchanged (Appendix~\ref{app:ident}; pooled estimator only). Both laws have the same anatomy. A numerator proportional to state error times the product of the treatment and outcome regime shifts is divided by a residual treatment variance that the same treatment shift inflates. This is why $\Var(\tilde T)$, the scalar analogue of $\lmin(\hat\bJ)$, cannot be read as a monotone certificate (Figure~\ref{fig:law}). The two designs differ in what multiplies the state error: weighted nuisances leak the raw contrast $\Delta G$ at first order in $\eps$, adjusted nuisances only the residual variance $v$.

\textbf{Observability and inference.} For a calibrated posterior, $\hat v=N^{-1}\sum_t\hat\gamma_t(1-\hat\gamma_t)$ satisfies $|\E\hat v-v|\le\sqrt{CE}+CE$ in the $L^2$ calibration error $CE$, and $\Var(\tilde T)$ is estimable. If the calibrated regime-mean model identifies the treatment shift $a$, only the outcome shift $b$ remains unidentified, so the robustness value $b^\star=|\hat\theta|\,\widehat{\Var}(\tilde T)/\|\hat\Sigma\hat a\|$ (the smallest outcome shift that explains the estimate away) gives a one-parameter sensitivity analysis. For EM-fitted HMMs the plug-in converges at rate $N^{-1/2}+\sqrt{\delta_X}$, where $\delta_X$ is regime information carried by the controls (Proposition~\ref{prop:rate}). Under misspecification, $\hat v$ may understate or overstate residual state variance; sensitivity values then require calibration assumptions.

\begin{theorem}[Inference around the proxy target]
\label{thm:asymptotics}
For posterior-adjusted nuisances, under Theorem~\ref{thm:graceful} and the boundedness, augmented-process $\beta$-mixing, nuisance-rate and differentiability conditions in Appendix~\ref{app:proof_asym}, use $\tau^*\ge C\log N$, $C>1/c_\beta$, and a separated HMM calibration window. Then $\sqrt N(\hat\bth_\lambda-\bth_\lambda)\to\mathcal N(0,\Sigma_\lambda^{\rm tot})$, including representation uncertainty. Here $\bth_\lambda-\bth^*=(\bJ+\lambda I)^{-1}(\boldsymbol b_\gamma-\lambda\bth^*)$ and $\|\boldsymbol b_\gamma\|\le C\eps$. Structural coverage requires displacement $o(N^{-1/2})$; $\sqrt N(C\eps+\lambda M_\theta)/(\lmin+\lambda)\to0$ suffices. Occupation weighting requires its joint influence covariance. Implemented HAC intervals condition on the fitted HMM; full-sample smoothing is outside this theorem.
\end{theorem}
The experiments use the fixed penalty $\lambda=0.05\,\mathrm{tr}(\hat\bJ)/(K\sqrt N)$, for which $\sqrt N\lambda$ is a constant (about \RbSqrtNLam) rather than $o(1)$; the implemented estimator is therefore outside the displacement condition, although its shrinkage bias is below \RbShrinkBound\ on our frontier (Appendix~\ref{app:ident}). When proxy displacement is not negligible, structural Wald tests need not have nominal size. We therefore pair estimates with sensitivity analysis rather than an oracle bias correction.

\textbf{Observability and persistence.} Table~\ref{tab:main} (left) varies proxy separation $\Delta_Z$ in the DGP of Theorem~\ref{thm:bias}; a persistence sweep adds regime-0 stay probabilities $0.70$ and $0.97$ (\FDNRuns\ datasets in all). Among the \FDNCells\ cells with $\Delta_Z\ge1$ (Figure~\ref{fig:estimators}), OR-DML is best or tied for the ATE in \FDBestATE\ and for regime effects in \FDBestTheta. At $\Delta_Z=2$ its ATE bias is \FDOursTwo\ (hard fixed effects \FDHardTwo, weighted design \FDWTwo) with coverage \FDCovOursTwo\%, and at $\Delta_Z=1$ its regime-effect error is \FDOursThOne\ against \FDHardThOne\ and \FDWThOne. Under a continuous latent mixture that no two-state model describes, OR-DML beats hard fixed effects in \SVHPAWin\% of 100 replications, while the weighted design beats them in \SVHWin\% (Appendix~\ref{app:frontiers}).

\textbf{Real-data-calibrated benchmark.} The right half of Table~\ref{tab:main} keeps everything real except the causal effects. That includes meteorological proxies and controls, the observed NO$_2$ series, the hourly missingness pattern, and regime persistence and overlap: true paths are drawn by forward-filtering backward-sampling from an HMM calibrated per city (stay probabilities \SSStayMin--\SSStayMax). For regime effects OR-DML is the most accurate regime-aware estimator in every city (Delhi \SSDelhiPATh, against \SSDelhiHardTh\ for hard fixed effects and \SSDelhiWTh\ for the weighted design). OR-DML's advantage here concerns heterogeneous regime effects, not universal scalar-ATE dominance. The weighted design is worse than standard DML outside Delhi. The plug-in law, computed from the posterior, the fitted treatment shift and the injected outcome shift, ranks the four cities' biases correctly, with magnitudes within \SSLawErrMin--\SSLawErrMax\%. The generated-state $\lmin$ exceeds its oracle value by \SSInflMin--\SSInflMax$\times$ in Mumbai, Bengaluru and Kolkata (\SSInflDelhi$\times$ in Delhi).

\section{OBSERVATIONAL STRESS TEST: FOUR CITIES}
\label{sec:empirical}

We apply OR-DML and the sensitivity analysis to NO$_2\to$PM$_{2.5}$ in Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru and Kolkata (OpenAQ/CPCB stations and Open-Meteo weather, Feb~2025--Jun~2026) on a \emph{corrected} hourly grid. An audit found that the legacy panel rounded half-hour timestamps with ties-to-even, collapsing it onto a two-hour grid, so ``hourly'' lags were two hours. This changed Delhi's single-index posterior-feature estimate from \LegacyDelhi\ to \PooledDelhi; Table~\ref{tab:cities} instead reports the posterior-occupation-weighted coupled estimate \CityDelhiATE\ (Appendix~\ref{app:provenance}). Both are exposure associations, not known true ATEs. Placebo leads (a test that the treatment at a later time has no effect on the outcome now, which would otherwise signal confounding or reverse timing) are aligned to the outcome's own information set. Without this alignment the one-hour lead is mechanically zero, because the lagged outcome is a control. Real posteriors are sharp here, so the weighted design gives the same ATE to within \CityMaxATEDiff\ outside Kolkata (Delhi \CityDelhiATEW).

Its one-hour lead placebo nonetheless rejects (\PlDelhiLead, $p=\PlDelhiLeadP$, about a third of the contemporaneous coefficient). This points to short-horizon confounding outside that channel. We therefore report Delhi as an exposure association that is robust to residual \emph{regime} confounding, not as an emissions effect, and the other three cities as inconclusive.

\section{DISCUSSION}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\textbf{Main message.} A confident inferred state and a well-conditioned estimating equation do not show that a causal estimate is reliable. The inferred state enters the estimating equation twice, in the estimate and in the apparent information available to the estimator, and the two move together. What matters is the uncertainty about the state that remains after inference, multiplied by how much treatment and outcome shift between states.

\textbf{Practical rules.} (i)~Use the inferred state as an adjustment input to the nuisance regressions and only as a weight for the target; this makes the coupled score exactly orthogonal and gave the most accurate regime-aware two-stage estimates in every benchmark here (for the pooled ATE, posterior-feature DML is sometimes better). (ii)~Report the residual state variance, the regime shifts it multiplies, and a robustness value, and compare against hard-regime and posterior-feature baselines. (iii)~Test temporal validity with placebos aligned to the outcome's information set. (iv)~When a correctly specified joint model or an exclusion restriction is credible, prefer a joint or deconvolution estimator to a two-stage plug-in.

\textbf{What we do not claim.} We do not claim that effects are identified from weak proxies, that OR-DML is unbiased, that its intervals have nominal structural coverage, or that the four-city study measures an emissions effect.

\textbf{Outlook.} Nothing in the mechanism is specific to HMMs. Any inferred state (a neural encoder's embedding, a cluster label, a changepoint segmentation) enters the estimating equation both as an adjustment and as an apparent source of information, and the same accounting applies. Evaluating representations for causal use therefore means reporting the residual uncertainty they leave and the regime shifts it multiplies, not only their accuracy.

\textbf{Limitations.} The laws are probability limits under linear regime shifts, exogenous treatment innovations and calibrated posteriors, and the stationary Markov structure is assumed throughout. The robustness value bounds rather than corrects, and depends on calibration under misspecified emissions. Under weak observability every proxy-based two-stage estimator, ours included, has large bias and no coverage, and for the pooled ATE posterior-feature DML is sometimes more accurate. DC-DML needs an exclusion restriction and informative proxies, and a joint model needs a correct emission model. The city analysis is observational: proxies overlap controls, lagged pollutants may be mediators, and Delhi's lead placebo rejects.

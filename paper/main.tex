\begin{abstract}
Causal inference from dependent observational time series is complicated when an unobserved, persistent state jointly affects treatment assignment and outcomes. Standard Double Machine Learning (DML) controls for observed covariates through orthogonal residualization, but does not by itself resolve confounding induced by an imperfect proxy for latent state dynamics. We study causal estimation under latent Markov confounding and characterize how latent-state uncertainty and downstream task conditioning propagate into causal error. We develop a regime-aware orthogonal estimator combining probabilistic state inference, temporally purged cross-fitting, and regularized inversion of the cross-regime score Jacobian. In a corrected $7\times7$ factorial experiment that independently manipulates proxy error and post-residualization treatment information (4,900 runs), the theoretical difficulty ratio $\varepsilon_\gamma/\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})$ ranks causal error substantially better than either component alone (Spearman $\rho=0.995$ versus $0.626$ and $0.521$). Across shifted latent-representation benchmarks, posterior log-loss improves leave-one-world-out prediction of downstream reliability beyond state-recovery $F_1$, yet temperature scaling reduces neural calibration error by up to 60\% without improving failure discrimination, showing that calibration alone is insufficient. Real sensor networks provide an observational stress test across four megacities spanning distinct residual treatment variance scales and telemetry artifacts; falsification checks are reported explicitly where temporal causal interpretation is unsupported. Together, the results show that reliable causal estimation with latent sequential confounding requires evaluating representation uncertainty jointly with the information geometry of the downstream task.
\end{abstract}

\section{INTRODUCTION}
\label{sec:intro}

Sequential machine learning systems frequently operate in environments governed by persistent, partially observed context—such as unobserved physical, macroeconomic, or operational regimes. A widespread design assumption in such systems is that predictive confidence or calibration over the latent state is sufficient for downstream reliability. However, this assumption fails: \emph{accurate latent-state recovery does not guarantee reliable downstream inference because representation error is filtered through the information geometry of the task that consumes it.} When the downstream task is ill-conditioned—such as estimating causal effects under collinear treatments or weak overlap—even small posterior proxy error is catastrophically amplified, causing severe downstream failure despite seemingly confident latent representations. The same latent-state uncertainty can be informative for one task and nearly useless for another because the downstream information geometry differs.

Continuous-treatment causal inference in atmospheric systems provides a natural laboratory: estimating the causal effect of variation in the observed $\text{NO}_2$ exposure proxy on ambient $\text{PM}_{2.5}$ \citep{dominici2014mitigating} (translating this observational exposure effect to an emissions policy requires structural transport assumptions). Observational time series in this domain violate two foundational assumptions: (1)~\textbf{Persistent Latent Regime Confounding:} Planetary boundary-layer dynamics transition between discrete thermodynamic regimes (e.g., nocturnal inversion vs.\ convective clearance), jointly governing precursor dispersion ($T_t$) and ambient particulate concentration ($Y_t$). (2)~\textbf{Imperfect Overlap \& Dependence:} Latent regimes are unobserved; auxiliary proxies $Z_t$ exhibit overlap ($\varepsilon_\gamma > 0$), while autoregression breaks exchangeability \citep{chernozhukov2018double}, leaking errors across folds.

When standard Double Machine Learning \citep{chernozhukov2018double} or tree-based plug-in estimators are applied naively to environmental time series without accounting for latent regimes, the resulting causal estimands can exhibit severe bias and, in our benchmark, sign inversions—falsely suggesting that precursor reductions exacerbate ambient pollution.

\textbf{Related Work.} We position our contribution across four neighboring literatures: (1)~\textbf{Double Machine Learning \& Orthogonal Inference:} \citet{chernozhukov2018double,chernozhukov2022automatic,foster2023orthogonal,nie2021quasi,kennedy2023towards} achieve $\sqrt{N}$-rate inference under unconfoundedness given $X_t$. (2)~\textbf{Dependent \& Time-Series Causal Inference:} \citet{ciganovic2026dml,semenova2023generalized,lewis2020double,bai2009panel,bonhomme2015grouped,hamilton1989new} relax exchangeability under mixing, but do not address latent Markov confounding. (3)~\textbf{Latent Markov \& Proximal Inference:} \citet{clouth2024latent,huang2023sdci,miao2018identifying,tchetgen2020introduction,mastouri2021proximal,cappe2005inference} use proxies for unmeasured confounders, focusing on asymptotic identification rather than downstream finite-sample score conditioning. (4)~\textbf{Weak Overlap \& Selective Reliability:} \citet{damour2021overlap,shalit2017estimating,crump2009dealing,guo2017calibration,geifman2017selective,chow1970optimum,elyaniv2010foundations} address overlap and abstention, without linking latent uncertainty to coupled score conditioning.

\textbf{Contributions.} We separate latent-state uncertainty from task conditioning and show how their interaction controls downstream degradation:
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
    \item \textbf{Decoupling Context Uncertainty from Task Conditioning:} We prove that downstream estimation suffers two distinct degradation mechanisms: imperfect latent recovery ($\varepsilon_\gamma$) and weak task conditioning ($\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})$), showing that predictive state confidence does not imply downstream task reliability (Theorems~\ref{thm:impossibility}--\ref{thm:graceful}).
    \item \textbf{Principled Difficulty Measure \& Observable Bridge:} We formulate causal difficulty $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{causal}} = \frac{\varepsilon_\gamma}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})}$ and establish rolling entropy $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{operational}}(t) = \frac{H(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t)}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}_t)}$ as an observable multiplicative surrogate under two-sided spectral comparability (Proposition~\ref{prop:bridge}).
    \item \textbf{Sequential Overlap-Aware Estimation (OR-DML):} We develop spectrally regularized OR-DML with purged block cross-fitting, proving leading-order risk bounds and establishing centered asymptotic normality under dependent data (Theorems~\ref{thm:spectral}--\ref{thm:asymptotics}).
    \item \textbf{Representation-to-Task Reliability:} A corrected $7\times7$ factorial intervention independently varies proxy error and post-residualization task conditioning; $\varepsilon_\gamma/\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})$ predicts causal error far better than either component alone ($\rho=0.995$ vs. $0.626/0.521$). Across 15 shifted worlds, adding posterior NLL to state-recovery $F_1$ nearly doubles leave-one-world-out reliability $R^2$ ($0.113\to0.221$), while temperature scaling cuts neural ECE by up to 60\% without improving downstream ROC-AUC.
\end{itemize}

\section{STRUCTURAL CAUSAL MODEL \& IDENTIFICATION}
\label{sec:setup}

Let $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)$ be a probability space. Consider a discrete-time longitudinal process observed at equidistant intervals $t \in \{1, \dots, N\}$.

\subsection{Structural Equations}
We formalize the atmospheric data-generating process via the following Structural Causal Model (SCM):
\begin{align}
    S_t &\sim \text{Markov}(\Pi, \pi_0), \quad S_t \in \{1, \dots, K\}, \label{eq:regime_scm} \\
    Z_t &= \mu_Z(S_t) + \xi_t, \label{eq:state_obs} \\
    X_t &= \phi(X_{t-1}, S_t) + \epsilon_t, \label{eq:confounders} \\
    T_t &= m(X_t, S_t) + V_t, \quad \E[V_t \mid X_t, S_t] = 0, \label{eq:treatment} \\
    Y_t &= \theta(S_t) T_t + g(X_t, S_t) + U_t, \label{eq:outcome}
\end{align}
with $\E[U_t \mid X_t, S_t, T_t] = 0$, where:
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{-2pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
    \item $S_t$ is an unobserved discrete Markov regime with transition matrix $\Pi_{jk} = P(S_t = k \mid S_{t-1} = j)$ and stationary distribution $\pi^*$.
    \item $Z_t \in \R^q$ denotes observed auxiliary physical state indicators (e.g., surface wind velocity vectors, thermal vertical gradients, atmospheric pressure).
    \item $X_t \in \R^p$ is a vector of observed weather confounders and temporal lags.
    \item $T_t \in \R$ is the continuous treatment (precursor emission proxy, e.g., $\text{NO}_2$).
    \item $Y_t \in \R$ is the continuous outcome (ambient pollutant, e.g., $\text{PM}_{2.5}$).
    \item $\theta(S_t) = \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \one(S_t = k)$ represents the regime-specific causal effect.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Identification Under Latent Regimes}
Our objective is to identify and estimate the regime-specific marginal causal effects:
\begin{equation}
    \theta_k^* = \frac{\partial \E[Y_t \mid \text{do}(T_t = u), S_t = k]}{\partial u}, \quad k \in \{1, \dots, K\},
\end{equation}
as well as the stationary Average Treatment Effect (ATE):
\begin{equation}
    \theta_{\text{ATE}}^* = \sum_{k=1}^K \pi_k^* \theta_k^*.
\end{equation}

\begin{assumption}[Regime-Conditional Unconfoundedness \& Innovation Exogeneity]
\label{ass:unconfoundedness}
Conditional on observed covariates $X_t$, latent regime $S_t$, and proxy history $Z_{1:N}$, potential outcomes are unconfounded ($Y_t(u) \perp T_t \mid (X_t, S_t, Z_{1:N}), \forall u$) and structural innovations have zero mean:
\begin{equation}
    \E[U_t \mid X_t, S_t, T_t, Z_{1:N}] = 0.
\end{equation}
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Overlap, Envelopes \& State Positivity]
\label{ass:overlap}
\label{ass:nonsingularity}
\label{ass:envelopes}
There exist constants $\eta, c_0 > 0$, $B_T, B_Y < \infty$, and $c_1, c_2 > 0$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
    \item \textbf{State Positivity \& Envelopes:} Stationary distribution satisfies $\min_{k} \pi_k^* \ge \eta$, and for almost every $x$ and all $k$, $P(S_t = k \mid X_t=x) \ge c_0$, $\E[\gamma_{tk} \mid X_t=x] \ge c_0$. Variables satisfy $|T_t| \le B_T$, $|Y_t| \le B_Y$ a.s., so $|\tilde{T}_{tk}| \le M_T \equiv 2B_T$, $|\tilde{Y}_{tk}| \le M_Y \equiv 2B_Y$.
    \item \textbf{Within-Regime Treatment Overlap:} For all $k \in \{1, \dots, K\}$ and almost everywhere on $\mathrm{supp}(X_t)$:
    \begin{equation}
        c_1 \le \Var(T_t \mid X_t, S_t = k) \le c_2.
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Latent Proxy Sufficiency]
\label{ass:innovation}
The auxiliary state proxy sequence $Z_{1:N}$ satisfies latent proxy sufficiency: $P(S_t = k \mid X_t, T_t, Z_{1:N}) = P(S_t = k \mid Z_{1:N}) \equiv \gamma_{tk}$ (or $P(S_t = k \mid Z_{1:t})$ for causal forward filtering) almost surely for all $k \in \{1, \dots, K\}$.
\textit{Discussion.} That is, $Z_{1:N}$ screens off the latent state $S_t$ from $(X_t, T_t)$, framing it as a proxy-sufficiency identification restriction. Combined with Assumption~\ref{ass:unconfoundedness}, this guarantees $\E[Y_t \mid X_t, T_t, Z_{1:N}] = \sum_{k=1}^K \gamma_{tk} [\theta_k^* T_t + g_k(X_t)]$. This identifying restriction requires $Z_{1:N}$ to be sufficiently informative that conditioning on $(X_t, T_t)$ provides no additional regime information beyond $Z_{1:N}$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Nuisance Estimation and Mixing Rates]
\label{ass:rates}
\label{ass:mixing}
The data process $(Y_t, T_t, X_t, Z_t)$ is strictly stationary and geometrically $\alpha$-mixing with $\alpha(m) \le C_\alpha \exp(-c_\alpha m)$ for $C_\alpha, c_\alpha > 0$. Nuisance estimators satisfy $\|\hat{\mu}_Y - \mu_Y\|_{L_2(P)} \cdot \|\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_D - \boldsymbol{\mu}_D\|_{L_2(P)} = o_P(N^{-1/2})$, with individual convergence rates $o_P(N^{-1/4})$.
\end{assumption}

\section{FAILURE OF STANDARD DML: THE OMITTED REGIME BIAS}
\label{sec:bias}

\textbf{Intuition.} When unobserved regimes $S_t$ jointly shift treatment and outcome baselines, partialling out observed controls $X_t$ cannot purge latent regime variation. Instead, by reducing residual treatment variance in the denominator without eliminating the latent covariance in the numerator, standard cross-sectional residualization actively amplifies the omitted confounding.

\begin{theorem}[Decomposition of Omitted Regime Bias]
\label{thm:bias}
Suppose the data are generated according to SCM \eqref{eq:regime_scm}--\eqref{eq:outcome}. Let $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{naive}}$ be the standard cross-sectional DML estimator \citep{chernozhukov2018double} that conditions only on observed covariates $X_t$, omitting $S_t$. Define the naive cross-sectional residuals $\tilde{T}_t = T_t - \E[T_t \mid X_t]$ and $\tilde{Y}_t = Y_t - \E[Y_t \mid X_t]$. 

Then, as $N \to \infty$, $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{naive}}$ converges in probability to:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{naive}} \xrightarrow{p} \sum_{k=1}^K \omega_k \theta_k^* + \mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{heterogeneity}} + \mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{confounding}},
\end{equation}
where $\omega_k = \frac{\E[\one(S_t = k) \tilde{T}_t^2]}{\E[\tilde{T}_t^2]}$ with $\sum_k \omega_k = 1$, and the bias components are:
\begin{align}
    \mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{heterogeneity}} &= \frac{\sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \E\left[ \bar{m}(X_t) \Delta m_k(X_t) \pi_k(X_t) \right]}{\E[\tilde{T}_t^2]}, \label{eq:b_het} \\
    \mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{confounding}} &= \frac{\sum_{k=1}^K \E\left[ \pi_k(X_t) \Delta m_k(X_t) \Delta g_k(X_t) \right]}{\E[\tilde{T}_t^2]}, \label{eq:b_conf}
\end{align}
with $\pi_k(X_t) \equiv P(S_t = k \mid X_t)$, $\bar{m}(X_t) \equiv \E[T_t \mid X_t]$, $\Delta m_k(X_t) \equiv \E[T_t \mid X_t, S_t = k] - \bar{m}(X_t)$, and $\Delta g_k(X_t) \equiv g(X_t, k) - \E[g(X_t, S_t) \mid X_t]$ (complete algebraic proof in Appendix~\ref{app:proof_thm1}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Sign Inversion \& Bias Amplification]
When latent stagnation induces strong positive confounding ($\Delta m_k(X_t) \Delta g_k(X_t) \gg 0$), but residual treatment variance $\E[\tilde{T}_t^2]$ is attenuated toward its lower bound $c_1 \ll \Delta m$ via partialling out $X_t$ (or under parameter sequences where treatment innovation variance $\sigma_V^2 \to 0$ while latent separation remains fixed), the omitted confounding bias $\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{confounding}}$ undergoes severe \emph{bias amplification} \citep{pearl2012bias}. In coastal airsheds with negative covariance phases, $\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{confounding}} < -\sum_k \omega_k \theta_k^*$, driving $\mathrm{plim}(\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{naive}}) < 0$ despite $\theta_k^* > 0$.
\end{corollary}
\textbf{Interpretation.} Standard DML cannot eliminate omitted regime confounding: conditioning on observed controls $X_t$ shrinks residual treatment variance $\E[\tilde{T}_t^2]$ toward $c_1$ without reducing latent regime covariance, actively amplifying bias.

\section{METHODOLOGY: OVERLAP-AWARE REGIME DML}
\label{sec:method}

To overcome the failure of standard cross-sectional DML, we introduce \textbf{Overlap-Aware Regime Double Machine Learning (OR-DML)}.

\subsection{Latent Regime Formulation \& Observability Diagnostics}
Let $Z_t \in \R^q$ denote exogenous contemporaneous meteorological covariates assumed unaffected by the exposure proxy. We model the latent atmospheric state $S_t \in \{1, \dots, K\}$ via a Hidden Markov Model with transition matrix $\boldsymbol{\Pi}$ and emission parameters $\boldsymbol{\phi}$.
Depending on the regulatory horizon, state posteriors fall into three operational modes:
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{-2pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
    \item \textbf{Retrospective Smoothed Posteriors (Offline):} $\gamma_{tk}^{\mathrm{smooth}} \equiv P(S_t = k \mid Z_{1:N})$ for retrospective environmental epidemiology and structural causal auditing;
    \item \textbf{Training-Fitted Forward Filtered Posteriors:} $\gamma_{tk}^{\mathrm{filter}} \equiv P(S_t = k \mid Z_{1:t}, \hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}})$ where dynamics $\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}$ are calibrated on a historical window strictly preceding evaluation ($\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{cal}} < \mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{eval}}$), with state updates proceeding strictly causally on $Z_{1:t}$; or
    \item \textbf{Expanding-Window Online Filter:} where both parameter estimation and forward filtering update causally without access to future measurements.
\end{enumerate}
Our empirical and benchmark implementations evaluate Mode 1 (offline smoothing) and Mode 2 (training-fitted forward filtering). We define the mean posterior entropy as a direct diagnostic of latent state observability:
\begin{equation}
    \bar{H}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}) \equiv -\frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N \sum_{k=1}^K \gamma_{tk} \ln(\gamma_{tk} + \epsilon).
\end{equation}
\emph{A Priori Diagnostics:} Standardized separation $\Delta_Z < 0.5$ or entropy $\bar{H} \to \ln K$ signals severe proxy uncertainty and triggers a weak-identification diagnostic warning (rules in Appendix~\ref{app:proxy_diagnostics}), distinguishing structural non-identification ($\Delta_Z = 0$, Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility}) from finite-sample proxy dilution.

\subsection{State-Weighted Coupled System \& Spectral Regularization}
To avoid heuristic decoupled regressions that suffer cross-talk bias under regime overlap, OR-DML estimates regime-specific conditional expectations via state-weighted regressions targeting population expectations $m_k^\gamma(x) \equiv \frac{\E[\gamma_{tk} T_t \mid X_t=x]}{\E[\gamma_{tk} \mid X_t=x]}$ and $\mu_k^\gamma(x) \equiv \frac{\E[\gamma_{tk} Y_t \mid X_t=x]}{\E[\gamma_{tk} \mid X_t=x]}$:
\begin{align}
    \hat{m}_k(X) &= \arg\min_{m} \sum_{t \in \mathcal{T}_b} \gamma_{tk} (T_t - m(X_t))^2, \label{eq:nuisance_m} \\
    \hat{\mu}_k(X) &= \arg\min_{\mu} \sum_{t \in \mathcal{T}_b} \gamma_{tk} (Y_t - \mu(X_t))^2. \label{eq:nuisance_mu}
\end{align}
On evaluation fold $\mathcal{B}_b$, we form centered residuals $\tilde{T}_{tk} = T_t - \hat{m}_k(X_t)$ and $\tilde{Y}_{tk} = Y_t - \hat{\mu}_k(X_t)$. The empirical Gram matrix $\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} \in \R^{K \times K}$ and score vector $\hat{\boldsymbol{S}} \in \R^K$ are constructed with matched weights:
\begin{align}
    \hat{J}_{kk} &= \frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N \gamma_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tk}^2, \quad \hat{S}_k = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N \gamma_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tk} \tilde{Y}_{tk}, \label{eq:gram_J} \\
    \hat{J}_{jk} &= \frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N \gamma_{tj}\gamma_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tj} \tilde{T}_{tk} \quad (j \neq k). \label{eq:score_S}
\end{align}
When regime separation is weak or treatments are collinear, $\lambda_{\min}(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}) \to 0$, rendering the unregularized solve $\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}^{-1}\hat{\boldsymbol{S}}$ ill-conditioned. OR-DML supports both coupled regularized solve $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda = (\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \hat{\boldsymbol{S}}$ and decoupled regime-wise soft DML $\hat{\theta}_k = \hat{S}_k / (\hat{J}_{kk} + \lambda)$, where $\lambda \ge 0$ trades off regularization bias against collinearity variance.
To ensure scale-invariance across units, the dimensionless Gram matrix is $\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}_{\mathrm{std}} \equiv \hat{\boldsymbol{J}} / \hat{\sigma}_{\tilde{T}}^2$ with $\hat{\sigma}_{\tilde{T}}^2 \equiv \frac{1}{K}\sum_k \frac{1}{N}\sum_t \tilde{T}_{tk}^2$, giving scale-free $\lambda_{\min}(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}_{\mathrm{std}})$ and condition number $\kappa(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}) \equiv \lambda_{\max}/\lambda_{\min}$.
\emph{Scalability \& Structural Abstention:} End-to-end complexity is $\mathcal{O}(B K \cdot C_{\mathrm{nuisance}} + N K^2 + K^3)$; when data lack identifying variation entirely ($\kappa(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}) > 10^4$ or $\text{CI}(\lambda_{\min})$ covers 0), systems diagnose weak identification and abstain rather than masking near-singularity with excessive shrinkage.
We distinguish three causal estimand targets: (i) the true stationary population ATE $\theta_{\mathrm{PATE}}^* = \boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top} \boldsymbol{\theta}^*$, (ii) the estimated stationary ATE $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{PATE}} = \hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}})^\top \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda$, and (iii) the empirical sample occupation ATE $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SATE}} = \bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_N^\top \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda$, where $\bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_N \equiv \frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t$.

\subsection{Purged Block Cross-Fitting with Bartlett Embargo}
To ensure validity under temporal dependence, we partition observations into $B$ contiguous blocks. To eliminate cross-fold leakage under $\alpha$-mixing, each test block $\mathcal{B}_b$ is surrounded by an embargo buffer of width $\tau^*$ (Algorithm~\ref{alg:purged_cv}). We determine $\tau^*$ from the data via the Bartlett significance threshold:
\begin{equation}
    \tau^* = \max \left\{ h \ge 1 : \max_{j} \left| \hat{\rho}_j(h) \right| \ge \frac{1.96}{\sqrt{N}} \right\} + 1.
\end{equation}
In theory, $\tau^* \ge C \log N$ with $C > 1/c_\alpha$ guarantees vanishing coupling (Theorem~\ref{thm:asymptotics}). In our 2-hourly cadence ($N \approx 14,000$, $\log N \approx 9.55$), Bartlett calibration yields 12--24 rows (24--48 hours), satisfying $\tau^* \ge 2\log N$ (\texttt{auto\_embargo=True}).

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Overlap-Aware Regime DML (OR-DML)}
\label{alg:purged_cv}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Series $\{Y_t, T_t, X_t, Z_t\}_{t=1}^N$, blocks $B$, embargo $\tau^*$, penalty $\lambda \ge 0$.
\STATE Infer posteriors $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t$ (Mode 1 smoothing or Mode 2 forward filtering).
\STATE Cross-fit state-weighted nuisances $\hat{m}_k, \hat{\mu}_k$ with embargo buffer $\tau^*$.
\STATE Form centered residuals $\tilde{T}_{tk} = T_t - \hat{m}_k(X_t)$ and $\tilde{Y}_{tk} = Y_t - \hat{\mu}_k(X_t)$.
\STATE Form Gram $\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_t \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t^\top \odot \tilde{\boldsymbol{T}}_t\tilde{\boldsymbol{T}}_t^\top$ and score $\hat{\boldsymbol{S}} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_t \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t \odot \tilde{\boldsymbol{T}}_t \odot \tilde{\boldsymbol{Y}}_t$.
\STATE Compute $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda = (\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1}\hat{\boldsymbol{S}}$ and diagnostics $(\lambda_{\min}, \kappa, \bar{H})$.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{THEORETICAL ANALYSIS}
\label{sec:theory}

We formalize three central results: (1)~\textbf{Non-Identification:} latent context can induce unidentifiability; (2)~\textbf{Graceful Degradation:} proxy error scales as $\varepsilon_\gamma/\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})$; and (3)~\textbf{Observable Difficulty \& Regularization:} task conditioning yields an observable surrogate for risk control.

\textbf{Intuition.} If auxiliary proxies $Z_t$ contain zero information distinguishing latent regimes, different underlying causal structures generate observationally indistinguishable joint distributions, rendering point identification impossible regardless of sample size.

\begin{theorem}[Non-Identification Under Unconstrained Overlap]
\label{thm:impossibility}
Suppose the latent state is unobserved, and auxiliary proxies $Z_t$ satisfy $P(Z_t \mid S_t = j) = P(Z_t \mid S_t = k)$ for all $j, k$. When transitions are memoryless ($p=0.5$) or under static cross-sectional proxy evaluation, the regime-specific causal effect vector $\boldsymbol{\theta}^* = (\theta_1^*, \dots, \theta_K^*)^\top$ is not point-identified from the joint distribution of observables $(Y_t, T_t, X_t, Z_t)$: there exist structural causal models $P_1$ and $P_2$ satisfying Assumptions~\ref{ass:unconfoundedness}--\ref{ass:innovation} with identical joint observable laws $P_1(Y_{1:N}, T_{1:N}, X_{1:N}, Z_{1:N}) \equiv P_2(Y_{1:N}, T_{1:N}, X_{1:N}, Z_{1:N})$ for all $N \ge 1$, yet possessing strictly distinct, non-permutation causal effect vectors $\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(1)} \neq \boldsymbol{\theta}^{(2)}$.
\end{theorem}
\textbf{Interpretation.} No estimator can recover regime-specific effects when the proxy provides no state information; this is a fundamental limitation of the observable information structure, not of optimization or sample size (proof in Appendix~\ref{app:proof_thm2}; we contrast this with dynamic models where non-trivial sequence transitions can achieve generic identification \citep{allman2009identifiability}).

\textbf{Intuition.} Downstream estimation error has two qualitatively different sources: posterior proxy error $\varepsilon_\gamma$ from imperfect state inference, and causal ill-conditioning $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})$ from collinear residual treatment variation. Theorem~\ref{thm:graceful} proves that these mechanisms enter multiplicatively.

\begin{theorem}[Graceful Degradation Error Bound]
\label{thm:graceful}
Let $\boldsymbol{H}_t = \boldsymbol{e}_{S_t}$, $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t \in \Delta^K$ be a state proxy with $\varepsilon_\gamma \equiv \E\|\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t - \boldsymbol{H}_t\|_1$, and $\boldsymbol{J}, \boldsymbol{S}$ be the population coupled Gram matrix and score from proxy nuisances $(m_k^\gamma, \mu_k^\gamma)$. Under Assumption~\ref{ass:envelopes} and $\|\boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2 \le M_\theta$, if $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) > 0$:
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
\item[(i)] $\boldsymbol{\theta}_\gamma \equiv \boldsymbol{J}^{-1}\boldsymbol{S}$ satisfies $\|\boldsymbol{\theta}_\gamma - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2 \le C\varepsilon_\gamma/\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})$ with $C = C_S + M_\theta C_J$, $C_S = \sqrt{K}(M_T M_Y + M_Y \tilde{C}_m + M_T \tilde{C}_\mu)$, $C_J = K(2M_T^2 + 2M_T \tilde{C}_m)$, $\tilde{C}_m = 2B_T/c_0^2$, $\tilde{C}_\mu = 2B_Y/c_0^2$.
\item[(ii)] Let $r_N = \max_k(\|\hat{m}_k - m_k^\gamma\|_{L_2} + \|\hat{\mu}_k - \mu_k^\gamma\|_{L_2})$. Under purged cross-fitting and Assumption~\ref{ass:rates}, on $\mathcal{E}_N = \{\|\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} - \boldsymbol{J}\|_{\rm op} \le \lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})/2\}$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:graceful_bound}
    \|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\gamma - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2 \le \frac{C\varepsilon_\gamma}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})} + \mathcal{O}_P\Big(\frac{N^{-1/2} + r_N}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})}\Big).
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\textbf{Interpretation.} $C \propto B_T(B_Y + B_T M_\theta)$ carries units of $T \cdot Y$; the dimensionless difficulty is $C\varepsilon_\gamma / \lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})$. When $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) \approx 0$, proxy errors are catastrophically amplified.

Theorem~\ref{thm:graceful} replaces heuristic claims with a rigorous bound. The cross-moment is Neyman-orthogonal to $(\mu_k, m_k)$, but the coupled Gram part is non-orthogonal for soft assignments (Lemma~\ref{lem:orth}); latent proxy uncertainty carries an irremediable price. In Appendix~\ref{app:proof_thm3}, Theorem~\ref{thm:lower_bound} proves a matching minimax lower bound $\inf_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}} \sup_P \E\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}} - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2 \ge c \varepsilon_\gamma / \lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})$, establishing that this degradation rate is tight and unimprovable.

\textbf{The Difficulty Frontier and Operational Surrogate Bridge.}
Because true states are unobserved, we bridge unobservable $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{causal}} \equiv \varepsilon_\gamma / \lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})$ to computable $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{operational}}(t) \equiv H(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t) / \lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}_t)$ via the following result:
\begin{assumption}[Two-Sided Spectral Comparability]
\label{ass:spectral_comparability}
There exist constants $0 < \underline{c}_J \le \overline{c}_J < \infty$ such that rolling Gram matrices $\boldsymbol{J}_t$ satisfy $\underline{c}_J \le \frac{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}_t)}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})} \le \overline{c}_J$ almost surely for all evaluation windows $t \ge w$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{proposition}[Surrogate Entropy-Difficulty Bridge]
\label{prop:bridge}
Under Bayesian calibration in binary regimes ($K=2$), forward-filtered posteriors $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t$ satisfy $\E[\|\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t - \boldsymbol{H}_t\|_1 \mid \mathcal{F}_t] = 2 \operatorname{Gini}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t) \le C_K H(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t)$ with $C_K \le 1/\ln 2 \approx 1.4427$. Defining $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{causal}} \equiv \frac{\varepsilon_\gamma}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})}$, $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{global}}^H(t) \equiv \frac{H(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t)}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})}$, and $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{operational}}(t) \equiv \frac{H(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t)}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}_t)}$:
(i) Population difficulty is bounded by global entropy: $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{causal}} \le C_K \E\left[ \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{global}}^H(t) \right]$.
(ii) Under Assumption~\ref{ass:spectral_comparability}, $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{causal}} \le C_K \overline{c}_J \E[\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{operational}}(t)] \le \frac{C_K \overline{c}_J}{\underline{c}_J} \E[\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{global}}^H(t)]$.
In simulation where $H_t$ is observed, $\hat{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathrm{causal}, N} \equiv \hat{\varepsilon}_{\gamma, N} / \lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})$ converges to $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{causal}}$ at $\mathcal{O}_P(N^{-1/2})$.
\end{proposition}
\textbf{Interpretation \& Regularization.} Proposition~\ref{prop:bridge} grounds deployable monitoring by turning an unobservable theoretical difficulty into an observable surrogate upper bound. When $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) \to 0$, a spectral penalty $\lambda > 0$ bounds the condition number, trading finite-sample bias for bounded variance.

\begin{theorem}[Risk Bound for Spectrally Regularized OR-DML]
\label{thm:spectral}
Let $\lambda \ge 0$ with $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) + \lambda > 0$, $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda = (\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1}\hat{\boldsymbol{S}}$, and $\boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda = (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1}\boldsymbol{S}$. Under Theorem~\ref{thm:graceful}:
(a)~Deterministic bias satisfies $B_\lambda \equiv \|\boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2 \le \frac{C\varepsilon_\gamma + \lambda M_\theta}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) + \lambda}$.
(b)~If Theorem~\ref{thm:asymptotics}(a) holds and $N\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda\|_2^2$ is uniformly integrable, variance satisfies $V_N \equiv \E\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda\|_2^2 \le \frac{\mathrm{Tr}(\boldsymbol{\Omega}_\lambda^{\mathrm{tot}})}{N(\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) + \lambda)^2} + o(N^{-1})$, and by Minkowski's inequality:
\begin{equation}
    \E\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2^2 \le (B_\lambda + \sqrt{V_N})^2 \le 2B_\lambda^2 + 2V_N.
\end{equation}
Empirical surrogate minimizer $\hat{\lambda}^*_{\mathrm{emp}} = \arg\min_{\lambda \in [0, \bar{\lambda}]} \hat{R}(\lambda)$ ($\bar{\lambda} < \infty$) satisfies an oracle inequality (Appendix~\ref{app:adaptive_lambda_derivation}; $\lambda = 0.10$ achieves $23.0\times$ risk reduction in benchmark simulations with $85.0\%$ coverage).
\end{theorem}
\textbf{Interpretation.} Regularization provides finite-sample variance control under weak overlap, but cannot manufacture causal information absent from the proxy distribution.

\begin{theorem}[Asymptotic Distribution Under Dependent Purged Cross-Fitting]
\label{thm:asymptotics}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:unconfoundedness}--\ref{ass:rates}, \ref{ass:envelopes}, \ref{ass:hmm_gram} with embargo buffer $\tau^* \ge C \log N$ ($C > 1/c_\alpha$):
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
\item[(a)] $\sqrt{N}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda) \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\lambda^{\mathrm{tot}})$, where $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\lambda^{\mathrm{tot}} = (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \boldsymbol{\Omega}_\lambda^{\mathrm{tot}} (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1}$, $\boldsymbol{\Omega}_\lambda^{\mathrm{tot}} = \boldsymbol{\Omega}_\lambda + \kappa \boldsymbol{A}_\Lambda \mathcal{I}_\Lambda^{-1} \boldsymbol{A}_\Lambda^\top$, and $\boldsymbol{A}_\Lambda = \partial_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}} \E[\boldsymbol{\Psi}_t(\boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda)]|_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*}$.
\item[(b)] Because $\boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda = \boldsymbol{\theta}^* + \mathrm{Bias}(\lambda, \boldsymbol{\gamma})$ with $\mathrm{Bias} = (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1}(\boldsymbol{b}_\gamma - \lambda \boldsymbol{\theta}^*)$ and $\|\boldsymbol{b}_\gamma\|_2 \le C\varepsilon_\gamma$, this is equivalent to $\sqrt{N}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^* - \mathrm{Bias}) \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\lambda^{\mathrm{tot}})$.
\item[(c)] Centered normality $\sqrt{N}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*) \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\lambda^{\mathrm{tot}})$ holds if $\sqrt{N}(C\varepsilon_\gamma + \lambda M_\theta)/(\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) + \lambda) \to 0$ (e.g., $\varepsilon_\gamma = o(N^{-1/2})$, $\lambda = o(N^{-1/2})$). When $\varepsilon_\gamma = \mathcal{O}(1)$, intervals cover $\boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda \neq \boldsymbol{\theta}^*$, explaining coverage breakdown under weak separation (Tables~\ref{tab:benchmark}, \ref{tab:frontier_b_null}). Estimated HMM parameters contribute $\kappa \boldsymbol{A}_\Lambda \mathcal{I}_\Lambda^{-1} \boldsymbol{A}_\Lambda^\top$ to the stacked HAC sandwich (Appendix~\ref{app:proof_thm5}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[Joint Asymptotic Distribution for Sample and Population ATE]
\label{prop:variance}
The sample ATE $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SATE}} = \bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}^\top \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda$ has conditional variance $\sigma_{\mathrm{SATE}}^2 = \bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}^\top \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\lambda \bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}$, where Markov persistence ($\rho \approx 0.88$) inflates the long-run variance of sample state occupation $\bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_N$ by $\frac{1+\rho}{1-\rho} \approx 15.7\times$ relative to i.i.d.\ transitions. The population ATE $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{PATE}} = \hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}})^\top \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda$ satisfies the joint delta expansion: $\sqrt{N}(\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{PATE}} - \theta_{\mathrm{PATE}}^*) = \boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top} \sqrt{N}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*) + \boldsymbol{\theta}^{*\top} \sqrt{N}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}} - \boldsymbol{\pi}^*) + o_P(1)$, yielding asymptotic variance:
\vspace{-1mm}
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_{\mathrm{PATE}}^2 = \boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\lambda \boldsymbol{\pi}^* + \boldsymbol{\theta}^{*\top} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\pi \boldsymbol{\theta}^* + 2 \boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\theta \pi} \boldsymbol{\theta}^*,
\end{equation}
\vspace{-1mm}
where $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\pi = \nabla_\Lambda \boldsymbol{\pi}^* \mathcal{I}_\Lambda^{-1} (\nabla_\Lambda \boldsymbol{\pi}^*)^\top$ represents the parametric variance of the estimated stationary distribution from HMM influence function $\boldsymbol{\phi}_{\Lambda, t}$, and $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\theta\pi} = \mathrm{LRCov}(\boldsymbol{\phi}_{\theta, t}, \boldsymbol{\phi}_{\Lambda, t})$, consistently estimated via stacked joint HAC sandwich.
\end{proposition}

\section{THE LATENT-CONFOUNDING DIFFICULTY FRONTIER}
\label{sec:experiments}

To test the theoretical predictions of Theorems~\ref{thm:impossibility}--\ref{thm:spectral}, we conduct a comprehensive Monte Carlo evaluation across 500 evaluation runs (100 replications per grid point), charting causal estimation error across the \textbf{Latent-Confounding Difficulty Frontier}.

\textbf{Experimental Protocol.} \emph{DGPs \& Baselines:} 100 Monte Carlo draws across $\Delta_Z \in \{0.2, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0, 4.0\}$ ($N=1,200$, $\theta_{\mathrm{ATE}}^* = 1.5278$) comparing Oracle, Standard DML, Block DML, continuous proxy controls (Ridge/GBM), hard-clustering Regime FE, and OR-DML (Spectral/Filtered). \emph{Representations \& Shifts:} HMM, GRU, Transformer, and SSM under clean and compound shifts. \emph{Reliability Target:} Predicting squared loss via difficulty $\mathcal{D}_t$ across 5 seeds with MBB. \emph{Real-World Test:} $>14,000$ hourly observations across four megacities.

\textbf{Difficulty Frontier Findings.} Table~\ref{tab:benchmark} and Figure~\ref{fig:difficulty_frontier} reveal three phenomena:
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{-4pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}\vspace{-1.5mm}
    \item \textbf{Low-Observability Breakdown ($\Delta_Z \le 1.0$):} Standard/Block DML exhibit constant omitted-regime bias ($+8.43\text{--}8.49$, RMSE $8.45$, $0.0\%$ coverage; Theorem~\ref{thm:bias}). Under severe overlap ($\Delta_Z \le 0.5$), all proxy estimators suffer heavy omitted-regime bias ($\hat{\varepsilon}_\gamma = 0.83$, bias $6.83\text{--}8.60$). At $\Delta_Z = 1.0$, weak proxy separation leaves non-trivial residual confounding ($\ge 2.65$: Decoupled Soft DML $3.29$, Hard FE $2.65$, Spectral OR-DML $4.02$, while conditioning on treatment HMM$(Z, T)$ achieves $1.20$). Because finite-sample separation cannot eliminate proxy bias, high $\mathcal{D}_t$ acts as an operational \emph{abstention trigger}.
    \item \textbf{High-Observability Resolution ($\Delta_Z \ge 2.0$):} Clean separation resolves omitted confounding: with multi-restart EM ($n_{\mathrm{init}}=5$), mean absolute bias collapses to oracle levels ($0.081$ at $\Delta_Z=2.0$ and $0.054$ at $\Delta_Z=4.0$), eliminating single-init EM local traps (where 2--5\% of single-init runs pulled mean bias to $0.501$ and RMSE to $1.905$). At $\Delta_Z=4.0$, regime estimators reach bias $\approx 0.054$ with $96.0\%$ coverage matching Oracle DML, collapsing to $0.0\%$ under weak separation ($\Delta_Z \le 1.0$) from proxy-induced size distortion: in an explicit null test ($\theta_1^*=0$, Frontier B; Table~\ref{tab:frontier_b_null}), nominal 5\% sandwich tests reject in 78.0\% of runs (bias $0.342$), demonstrating that without selective abstention, uncalibrated regime Wald inference is invalid.
    \item \textbf{Filtering vs.\ Smoothing:} Forward filtering ($\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t^{\mathrm{filter}}$) tracks retrospective smoothing ($\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t^{\mathrm{smooth}}$) closely across separated regimes (at $\Delta_Z = 2.0$, bias $0.1015$ vs.\ $0.0812$; at $\Delta_Z = 4.0$, identical $0.0538$), incurring minimal efficiency loss once dynamics are informed by physical history.
\end{enumerate}
\vspace{-3.5mm}

\textbf{Factorial Mechanism Validation.} Independently manipulating both quantities in Theorem~\ref{thm:graceful} confirms their sharp interaction: across 4,900 runs, difficulty ratio $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{causal}}=\varepsilon_\gamma/\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})$ ranks causal error with Spearman $\rho=0.995$ vs.\ $0.626$ (proxy error) and $0.521$ ($1/\lambda_{\min}$) alone (cell-mean $\rho=0.996$). With perfect states, mean error rises from $0.050 \to 0.545$ as residual information weakens; at $\varepsilon_\gamma=0.01$, it amplifies from $0.686 \to 131.3$, confirming conditioning shifts survive regime-aware residualization.

\textbf{Representation Quality vs.\ Downstream Reliability.} State discrimination, calibration, and task reliability are distinct. Across 15 shifted worlds, adding NLL to $F_1$ improves leave-one-world-out reliability prediction from $R^2=0.113$ ($\rho=0.374$) to $R^2=0.221$ ($\rho=0.527$). Yet temperature scaling cuts GRU/Transformer ECE by $47.9\%/60.3\%$ and NLL from $0.548/1.006$ to $0.331/0.382$ without improving failure discrimination ($\Delta\mathrm{AUC}=-0.004/-0.008$), proving calibration cannot rescue downstream reliability under ill-conditioned task geometry (Appendix~\ref{app:latent_regime_bench}).

\section{EMPIRICAL SENSOR NETWORK STRESS TEST}
\label{sec:empirical}
We evaluate OR-DML on our verified multi-season dataset ($14,122$ hourly records across Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, and Kolkata, Feb 2025--Jun 2026; $<0.7\%$ forward-fill; Table~\ref{tab:empirical}), treating the sensor study as an observational diagnostic stress test rather than physical causal ground truth. Complete-case and Newey-West HAC lag sensitivity ($h \in \{12, 24, 72, 168\}$h) appear in Appendix~\ref{app:empirical_sensitivity}.

\textbf{Conditioning Diagnostics.} \emph{Delhi Basin} ($\lambda_{\min} = 111.8$, $\kappa = 1.58$): Decomposes pooled effect ($+0.47 \pm 0.04$) into Regime 1 ($+0.70 \pm 0.05$, inversion) and Regime 2 ($+0.13 \pm 0.07$, clearance). \emph{Mumbai Coastal} ($\lambda_{\min} = 0.20$, $\kappa = 2.52$): Standard DML is unstable ($+2.64 \pm 4.42$), while Spectral OR-DML yields Regime 2 $+7.20 \pm 3.90$ ($p=0.065$) and Regime 1 $-7.63 \pm 7.05$ ($p=0.280$; overall $+1.08 \pm 3.91$). Telemetry screening reveals a 273-hour constant run and $985\,\mu\text{g/m}^3$ saturation, causing multi-modal EM likelihood surfaces (across local modes, $R_1 \in [-13.34, -0.02]$, $R_2 \in [+3.45, +7.39]$, $p \in [0.006, 0.131]$; Table~\ref{tab:empirical}). \emph{Bengaluru Plateau} ($\lambda_{\min} = 4.22$, $\kappa = 1.72$): Standard DML yields $-0.001 \pm 0.060$; OR-DML isolates Regime 1 ($+0.092 \pm 0.155$) from Regime 2 ($-0.153 \pm 0.069$, nominal $p=0.0265$, overall $-0.026 \pm 0.079$). Under Frontier B null size distortion ($78\%$ Type I error, Table~\ref{tab:frontier_b_null}), nominal regime $p$-values require transport modeling before causal interpretation. \emph{Kolkata Airshed} ($\lambda_{\min} = 0.0025$, $\kappa = 1.43$): Sparse telemetry ($N=1,209$) and discrete quantization (31 unique $\text{NO}_2$ values; 376-hour constant $\text{PM}_{2.5}$ run) collapse residual variance; overall ATE is $-0.006 \pm 1.300$. In single-index sensitivity, unregularized DML yields $-0.557 \pm 1.107$, stabilized by spectral penalty ($\text{SE} \to 0.060$ at $\lambda=0.10$, Table~\ref{tab:kolkata_lambda_grid}). Modest condition numbers ($\kappa = 1.43\text{--}2.52$) confirm SE inflation stems from low residual variance and sensor defects rather than Gram collinearity. \emph{Semi-Synthetic Megacity Benchmark}: In real atmospheric covariates with known ground truth ($\boldsymbol{\theta}^*=(0.75, 2.50)$; Table~\ref{tab:semisynthetic_megacity}), all proxy estimators suffer bias ($3.72\text{--}5.27$ for Spectral OR-DML, $1.09\text{--}2.56$ for HMM$(Z, T)$) and $0.0\%$ coverage, confirming real sensor confounding invalidates naive causal inference. \emph{Dynamic Projections}: $Y_{t+h} = \theta_h(S_t) T_t + g_h(X_t, S_t) + U_{t+h}$ peaks at $h=2$h in Delhi (ATE $+1.31$, Regime 1 $+2.35$, decaying to $+0.28$ at 12h), but Delhi fails pre-treatment placebos at $h \in \{-6,-3,-1\}$ ($p<0.01$). We treat this curve as an exposure diagnostic; other cities pass placebos (Appendix~\ref{app:empirical_sensitivity}).

\section{DISCUSSION, LIMITATIONS \& CONCLUSION}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Latent-state recovery and downstream reliability are distinct: proxy errors are benign under strong geometry but catastrophic when ill-conditioned, motivating joint diagnostics over calibration alone. \textbf{Limitations:} (1)~Proposition~\ref{prop:bridge} assumes calibration and spectral comparability. (2)~Sensor estimates are exposure tests; Delhi placebos preclude unmodeled dynamic claims. (3)~Operational difficulty does not dominate entropy everywhere. (4)~Financial transfer is synthetic.

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:bias}: Decomposition of Omitted Regime Bias}
\label{app:proof_thm1}
Consider the Structural Causal Model \eqref{eq:regime_scm}--\eqref{eq:outcome}:
\begin{align*}
    T_t &= m(X_t, S_t) + V_t, \quad \E[V_t \mid X_t, S_t] = 0, \\
    Y_t &= \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \one(S_t = k) T_t + g(X_t, S_t) + U_t, \quad \E[U_t \mid X_t, S_t, T_t] = 0.
\end{align*}
Define the unconditioned expectations: $\bar{m}(X_t) \equiv \E[T_t \mid X_t] = \sum_{k=1}^K \pi_k(X_t) m_k(X_t)$ and $\bar{g}(X_t) \equiv \E[g(X_t, S_t) \mid X_t] = \sum_{k=1}^K \pi_k(X_t) g_k(X_t)$, where $\pi_k(X_t) \equiv P(S_t = k \mid X_t)$.
Residuals from cross-fitting on $X_t$ alone satisfy $\tilde{T}_t = T_t - \bar{m}(X_t) = \sum_{k=1}^K \one(S_t=k) \Delta m_k(X_t) + V_t$, where $\Delta m_k(X_t) = m_k(X_t) - \bar{m}(X_t)$.
The outcome residual is:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{Y}_t = Y_t - \E[Y_t \mid X_t] = \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \one(S_t=k) T_t - \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \pi_k(X_t) m_k(X_t) + g(X_t, S_t) - \bar{g}(X_t) + U_t.
\end{equation}
Writing $T_t = \tilde{T}_t + \bar{m}(X_t)$, the treatment interaction terms decompose as:
\begin{align}
    \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \one(S_t=k) T_t - \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \pi_k(X_t) m_k(X_t) &= \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \one(S_t=k)\tilde{T}_t + \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \left[\one(S_t=k)\bar{m}(X_t) - \pi_k(X_t)m_k(X_t)\right].
\end{align}
Taking the inner product with $\tilde{T}_t$ and evaluating expectations:
(i) By Assumption~\ref{ass:unconfoundedness}, $\E[\tilde{T}_t U_t] = 0$.
(ii) $\E[\tilde{T}_t \sum_k \theta_k^* \one(S_t=k) \tilde{T}_t] = \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \E[\one(S_t=k)\tilde{T}_t^2] = \E[\tilde{T}_t^2] \sum_{k=1}^K \omega_k \theta_k^*$, where $\omega_k = \frac{\E[\one(S_t=k)\tilde{T}_t^2]}{\E[\tilde{T}_t^2]}$ with $\sum_k \omega_k = 1$.
(iii) Since $\E[\tilde{T}_t \mid X_t] = 0$ and $\E[\tilde{T}_t \one(S_t=k) \mid X_t] = \Delta m_k(X_t) \pi_k(X_t)$, the mean adjustment yields $\sum_{k=1}^K \theta_k^* \E[\bar{m}(X_t) \Delta m_k(X_t) \pi_k(X_t)]$.
(iv) The confounding component evaluates to $\E[\tilde{T}_t (g(X_t, S_t) - \bar{g}(X_t))] = \sum_{k=1}^K \E[\pi_k(X_t) \Delta m_k(X_t) \Delta g_k(X_t)]$.
Dividing by $\E[\tilde{T}_t^2]$ yields \eqref{eq:b_het} and \eqref{eq:b_conf}. $\blacksquare$

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility}: Non-Identification Under Unconstrained Overlap}
\label{app:proof_thm2}
To prove substantive causal non-identification, we constructively exhibit two data-generating distributions, $P_1$ and $P_2$, that are structurally well-formed under the SCM \eqref{eq:regime_scm}--\eqref{eq:outcome} and satisfy all Assumptions~\ref{ass:unconfoundedness}--\ref{ass:innovation}, yet possess strictly distinct, non-permutation causal effect vectors $\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(1)} \neq \boldsymbol{\theta}^{(2)}$ while inducing identically equal joint distributions over all observable variables:
\begin{equation}
    P_1(Y_{1:N}, T_{1:N}, X_{1:N}, Z_{1:N}) \equiv P_2(Y_{1:N}, T_{1:N}, X_{1:N}, Z_{1:N}) \quad \forall N \ge 1.
\end{equation}

\textbf{Construction of Substantive Observational Equivalence under Latent Confounding ($T \not\perp S$):}
Consider a two-state Markov chain ($K=2$) with symmetric memoryless transition probabilities $P_{01} = P_{10} = 0.5$, yielding independent stationary regimes $S_t \stackrel{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim} \mathrm{Bernoulli}(0.5)$ with $\pi_0^* = \pi_1^* = 0.5$.
Let auxiliary meteorological proxies $Z_t \in \R$ satisfy complete overlap: $Z_t \mid S_t = 0 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$ and $Z_t \mid S_t = 1 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$ identically, so $Z_t \perp S_t$.
Let continuous observed confounders $X_t \in \R$ have marginal density $f_X(x)$ independent of $S_t$.
Let binary treatment assignment $T_t \in \{0, 1\}$ depend directly on the latent regime:
$P(T_t = 1 \mid S_t = 0) = 0.75$ and $P(T_t = 1 \mid S_t = 1) = 0.25$,
so that $T_t \not\perp S_t$, with propensity scores $e(X_t, S_t) \in [0.25, 0.75]$ strictly satisfying Assumption~\ref{ass:overlap} (Overlap \& State Positivity with $\eta = 0.25$).
Marginally, $P(T_t = 1) = 0.5 \times 0.75 + 0.5 \times 0.25 = 0.5$, and by Bayes' rule, the posterior regime occupancy given treatment satisfies $P(S_t = 0 \mid T_t = 1) = 0.75$, $P(S_t = 1 \mid T_t = 1) = 0.25$, $P(S_t = 0 \mid T_t = 0) = 0.25$, and $P(S_t = 1 \mid T_t = 0) = 0.75$.

Now consider structural outcome equation $Y_t \in \{0, 1\}$ with $P(Y_t = 1 \mid T_t = t, S_t = s) = \theta_s t + g_s$ and unconfounded innovations $\E[U_t \mid X_t, S_t, T_t] = 0$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Model $P_1$ (Homogeneous Causal Effect):}
    Set $\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(1)} = (0.20, 0.20)^\top$ ($\theta_0^{(1)} - \theta_1^{(1)} = 0$), baselines $g_0^{(1)} = 0.30, g_1^{(1)} = 0.50$.
    Then $P_1(Y_t = 1 \mid T_t = 0) = 0.25(0.30) + 0.75(0.50) = 0.45$, and $P_1(Y_t = 1 \mid T_t = 1) = 0.75(0.20 + 0.30) + 0.25(0.20 + 0.50) = 0.75(0.50) + 0.25(0.70) = 0.55$.
    \item \textbf{Model $P_2$ (Heterogeneous Causal Effect):}
    Set $\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(2)} = (0.10, -0.30)^\top$ ($\theta_0^{(2)} - \theta_1^{(2)} = +0.40$), baselines $g_0^{(2)} = 0.60, g_1^{(2)} = 0.40$.
    Then $P_2(Y_t = 1 \mid T_t = 0) = 0.25(0.60) + 0.75(0.40) = 0.45$, and $P_2(Y_t = 1 \mid T_t = 1) = 0.75(0.10 + 0.60) + 0.25(-0.30 + 0.40) = 0.75(0.70) + 0.25(0.10) = 0.525 + 0.025 = 0.55$.
\end{enumerate}
Because $S_t$ transitions independently ($p=0.5$), observations $(Y_t, T_t, X_t, Z_t)$ are mutually independent across time, yielding $P(Y_{1:N}, T_{1:N}, X_{1:N}, Z_{1:N}) = \prod_{t=1}^N P(Y_t, T_t, X_t, Z_t)$. Comparing single-step observable conditionals, $P_1(Y_t \mid T_t, X_t, Z_t) \equiv P_2(Y_t \mid T_t, X_t, Z_t)$ identically for all $(y, t, x, z)$, ensuring $P_1(Y_{1:N}, T_{1:N}, X_{1:N}, Z_{1:N}) \equiv P_2(Y_{1:N}, T_{1:N}, X_{1:N}, Z_{1:N})$ for all $N \ge 1$. Yet structural effects satisfy $\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(1)} = (0.20, 0.20)^\top \neq (0.10, -0.30)^\top = \boldsymbol{\theta}^{(2)}$, which is not a permutation. \textbf{Dynamic Identifiability:} When regimes exhibit non-trivial temporal dependence ($p \neq 0.5$), latent Markov models can achieve generic algebraic point-identification from sequence transitions of length $N \ge 3$ \citep{allman2009identifiability}. However, in memoryless/cross-sectional regimes, or whenever proxy uncertainty leaves states unconstrained without dynamic modeling, structural non-identification holds identically for all $N \ge 1$. $\blacksquare$

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:graceful}: Graceful Degradation and the Difficulty Frontier}
\label{app:proof_thm3}
Let $\boldsymbol{H}_t = \boldsymbol{e}_{S_t} \in \{0, 1\}^K$ denote the unobserved true regime indicator vector.
Under SCM \eqref{eq:regime_scm}--\eqref{eq:outcome}, the oracle regime nuisance functions are:
\begin{equation}
    m_k^*(X_t) \equiv \E[T_t \mid X_t, S_t = k], \quad \mu_k^*(X_t) \equiv \E[Y_t \mid X_t, S_t = k] = \theta_k^* m_k^*(X_t) + g(X_t, k).
\end{equation}
\textbf{Step 1: Define proxy-weighted targets and population estimand.}
The empirical cross-fitted learners $\hat{m}_k$ and $\hat{\mu}_k$ minimize the posterior-weighted empirical risk targeting the proxy-weighted conditional expectations:
\begin{equation}
    m_k^\gamma(X_t) \equiv \frac{\E[\gamma_{tk} T_t \mid X_t]}{\E[\gamma_{tk} \mid X_t]}, \quad \mu_k^\gamma(X_t) \equiv \frac{\E[\gamma_{tk} Y_t \mid X_t]}{\E[\gamma_{tk} \mid X_t]}.
\end{equation}
Define the centered residuals at proxy targets: $\tilde{T}_{tk}^\gamma \equiv T_t - m_k^\gamma(X_t)$, $\tilde{Y}_{tk}^\gamma \equiv Y_t - \mu_k^\gamma(X_t)$, and at oracle targets: $\tilde{T}_{tk}^* \equiv T_t - m_k^*(X_t)$, $\tilde{Y}_{tk}^* \equiv Y_t - \mu_k^*(X_t)$.
The population proxy Gram matrix $\boldsymbol{J} \in \R^{K \times K}$ and score vector $\boldsymbol{S} \in \R^K$ are:
\begin{equation}
    J_{jk} \equiv \E[\gamma_{tj}\gamma_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tj}^\gamma \tilde{T}_{tk}^\gamma], \qquad S_k \equiv \E[\gamma_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tk}^\gamma \tilde{Y}_{tk}^\gamma],
\end{equation}
and their oracle counterparts $\boldsymbol{J}^*, \boldsymbol{S}^*$ are defined by:
\begin{equation}
    J_{jk}^* \equiv \E[H_{tj} H_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tj}^* \tilde{T}_{tk}^*] = \delta_{jk} \E[H_{tk} (\tilde{T}_{tk}^*)^2], \qquad S_k^* \equiv \E[H_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tk}^* \tilde{Y}_{tk}^*],
\end{equation}
where $H_{tj} H_{tk} = \delta_{jk} H_{tk}$ because latent regimes are mutually exclusive.

\textbf{Step 2: Orthogonality structure of the coupled score (Lemma~\ref{lem:orth}).}
\begin{lemma}[Orthogonality Structure of the Coupled Score]
\label{lem:orth}
Let $\Psi_{k}(\boldsymbol\theta;\eta)=\gamma_k\tilde T_k\big(\tilde Y_k-\sum_{j}\gamma_j\tilde T_j\theta_j\big)$ with $\tilde T_k=T-m_k(X)$, $\tilde Y_k=Y-\mu_k(X)$, so $\hat{\boldsymbol S}-\hat{\boldsymbol J}\boldsymbol\theta=\frac1N\sum_t\boldsymbol\Psi_t$. At $\eta^\gamma=(m^\gamma,\mu^\gamma)$ and any $\boldsymbol\theta$, with $g(X)\equiv\E[\|\boldsymbol\gamma-\boldsymbol H\|_1\mid X]$:
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
\item[(i)] $\partial_\mu\E\Psi_k[\Delta\mu]=0$ for any proxy $\gamma$.
\item[(ii)] The cross-moment part $\gamma_k\tilde T_k\tilde Y_k$ is orthogonal in both directions.
\item[(iii)] $|\partial_m\E\Psi_k[\Delta m]|\le 2M_T\|\boldsymbol\theta\|_1\sqrt{2\varepsilon_\gamma}\,\max_i\|\Delta m_i\|_{L_2}$. For hard assignments $\boldsymbol\gamma_t\in\{0,1\}^K$, this derivative is identically $0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
(i)--(ii): $\E[\gamma_k(T-m_k^\gamma)\mid X]=0$ and $\E[\gamma_k(Y-\mu_k^\gamma)\mid X]=0$ by definition of $m_k^\gamma,\mu_k^\gamma$.
(iii): differentiating $-\gamma_k\tilde T_k \sum_j \gamma_j \tilde T_j \theta_j$ in direction $\Delta m$ gives $\sum_j \theta_j \E[\Delta m_k \gamma_k \gamma_j \tilde T_j + \Delta m_j \gamma_k \gamma_j \tilde T_k]$. For $j=k$, $\E[\gamma_k^2 \tilde T_k \mid X] = \E[(\gamma_k^2 - \gamma_k)\tilde T_k \mid X]$ since $\E[\gamma_k \tilde T_k \mid X] = 0$, bounded by $M_T |\gamma_k - H_k|$. For $j \neq k$, $\gamma_k \gamma_j \le |\gamma_k - H_k| + |\gamma_j - H_j|$. Bounding each moment by $M_T g(X)$ and using $\|g\|_{L_2} \le \sqrt{2\varepsilon_\gamma}$ yields (iii). For hard assignments, $\gamma_k \gamma_j = \delta_{kj} \gamma_k$ so both moments vanish.
\end{proof}

\textbf{Step 3: Isolate proxy-induced bias via oracle score identity.}
Under SCM \eqref{eq:regime_scm}--\eqref{eq:outcome}, on the event $S_t = k$ ($H_{tk} = 1$), the oracle outcome residual is $\tilde{Y}_{tk}^* = Y_t - \mu_k^*(X_t) = \theta_k^* \tilde{T}_{tk}^* + U_t$.
Substituting into $S_k^*$:
\begin{equation}
    S_k^* = \E\left[ H_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tk}^* (\theta_k^* \tilde{T}_{tk}^* + U_t) \right] = \theta_k^* \E[H_{tk} (\tilde{T}_{tk}^*)^2] + \E\left[ H_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tk}^* \E[U_t \mid X_t, S_t, T_t, Z_{1:N}] \right] = \theta_k^* J_{kk}^*,
\end{equation}
where the innovation term vanishes by Assumption~\ref{ass:unconfoundedness}. Because $\boldsymbol{J}^* = \operatorname{diag}(J_{11}^*, \dots, J_{KK}^*)$, this yields the exact vector identity:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{S}^* = \boldsymbol{J}^* \boldsymbol{\theta}^*.
\end{equation}
Now consider the proxy score discrepancy $\boldsymbol{b}_\gamma \equiv \boldsymbol{S} - \boldsymbol{J}\boldsymbol{\theta}^*$. Expanding around the oracle identity:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{b}_\gamma = (\boldsymbol{S} - \boldsymbol{S}^*) - (\boldsymbol{J} - \boldsymbol{J}^*) \boldsymbol{\theta}^*.
\end{equation}

\textbf{Step 4: Bound score and Gram proxy discrepancies.}
We bound each component via telescoping expansions in $\varepsilon_\gamma \equiv \E\|\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t - \boldsymbol{H}_t\|_1$.
First, under positivity (Assumption~\ref{ass:overlap}), $\pi_k(x) \equiv \E[H_{tk} \mid X_t=x] \ge c_0 > 0$ and $\pi_k^\gamma(x) \equiv \E[\gamma_{tk} \mid X_t=x] \ge c_0 > 0$. For any bounded variable $V_t$ with $|V_t| \le M_V$ almost surely:
\begin{align}
    \left| \frac{\E[\gamma_{tk} V_t \mid X_t]}{\pi_k^\gamma(X_t)} - \frac{\E[H_{tk} V_t \mid X_t]}{\pi_k(X_t)} \right| 
    &\le \frac{\E[|\gamma_{tk} - H_{tk}| |V_t| \mid X_t]}{\pi_k^\gamma(X_t)} + \E[H_{tk}|V_t| \mid X_t] \left| \frac{\pi_k(X_t) - \pi_k^\gamma(X_t)}{\pi_k^\gamma(X_t) \pi_k(X_t)} \right| \nonumber \\
    &\le \frac{2 M_V}{c_0^2} \E[|\gamma_{tk} - H_{tk}| \mid X_t].
\end{align}
Applying this bound to $V_t = T_t$ and $V_t = Y_t$ yields $\E|m_k^\gamma(X_t) - m_k^*(X_t)| \le \tilde{C}_m \varepsilon_\gamma$ and $\E|\mu_k^\gamma(X_t) - \mu_k^*(X_t)| \le \tilde{C}_\mu \varepsilon_\gamma$.
Second, expanding the score difference $S_k - S_k^* = \E[\gamma_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tk}^\gamma \tilde{Y}_{tk}^\gamma - H_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tk}^* \tilde{Y}_{tk}^*]$:
\begin{align}
    \gamma_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tk}^\gamma \tilde{Y}_{tk}^\gamma - H_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tk}^* \tilde{Y}_{tk}^* &= (\gamma_{tk} - H_{tk}) \tilde{T}_{tk}^\gamma \tilde{Y}_{tk}^\gamma - H_{tk} (m_k^\gamma - m_k^*) \tilde{Y}_{tk}^\gamma - H_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tk}^* (\mu_k^\gamma - \mu_k^*).
\end{align}
Under bounded support ($\sup_t |\tilde{T}_{tk}^\gamma| \le M_T, \sup_t |\tilde{Y}_{tk}^\gamma| \le M_Y$), taking expectations yields $|S_k - S_k^*| \le (M_T M_Y + M_Y \tilde{C}_m + M_T \tilde{C}_\mu) \varepsilon_\gamma \equiv C_S \varepsilon_\gamma$.
Third, expanding the Gram difference $J_{jk} - J_{jk}^* = \E[\gamma_{tj}\gamma_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tj}^\gamma \tilde{T}_{tk}^\gamma - H_{tj}H_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tj}^* \tilde{T}_{tk}^*]$:
\begin{align}
    \gamma_{tj}\gamma_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tj}^\gamma \tilde{T}_{tk}^\gamma - H_{tj}H_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tj}^* \tilde{T}_{tk}^* &= (\gamma_{tj}\gamma_{tk} - H_{tj}H_{tk}) \tilde{T}_{tj}^\gamma \tilde{T}_{tk}^\gamma - H_{tj}H_{tk} (m_j^\gamma - m_j^*) \tilde{T}_{tk}^\gamma \nonumber \\
    &\quad - H_{tj}H_{tk} \tilde{T}_{tj}^* (m_k^\gamma - m_k^*).
\end{align}
Since $|\gamma_{tj}\gamma_{tk} - H_{tj}H_{tk}| \le |\gamma_{tj} - H_{tj}| + |\gamma_{tk} - H_{tk}|$, taking expectations gives $|J_{jk} - J_{jk}^*| \le (2 M_T^2 + 2 M_T \tilde{C}_m) \varepsilon_\gamma$, so $\|\boldsymbol{J} - \boldsymbol{J}^*\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le C_J \varepsilon_\gamma$.
Combining bounds yields:
\begin{equation}
    \|\boldsymbol{b}_\gamma\|_2 \le \|\boldsymbol{S} - \boldsymbol{S}^*\|_2 + \|\boldsymbol{J} - \boldsymbol{J}^*\|_{\mathrm{op}} \|\boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2 \le (C_S + C_J M_\theta) \varepsilon_\gamma \equiv C \cdot \varepsilon_\gamma.
\end{equation}

\textbf{Step 5: Population bound and finite-sample rate.}
Since $\boldsymbol{S} = \boldsymbol{J}\boldsymbol{\theta}^* + \boldsymbol{b}_\gamma$ and $\boldsymbol{\theta}_\gamma \equiv \boldsymbol{J}^{-1}\boldsymbol{S}$, we have $\boldsymbol{\theta}_\gamma - \boldsymbol{\theta}^* = \boldsymbol{J}^{-1}\boldsymbol{b}_\gamma$, so $\|\boldsymbol{\theta}_\gamma - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2 \le \frac{\|\boldsymbol{b}_\gamma\|_2}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})} \le \frac{C \varepsilon_\gamma}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})}$, establishing part (i).
For part (ii): since $\boldsymbol{J}\boldsymbol{\theta}_\gamma = \boldsymbol{S}$, $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\gamma - \boldsymbol{\theta}_\gamma = \hat{\boldsymbol{J}}^{-1}\big[(\hat{\boldsymbol{S}} - \boldsymbol{S}) - (\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} - \boldsymbol{J})\boldsymbol{\theta}_\gamma\big]$.
By Weyl's inequality, $\lambda_{\min}(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}) \ge \lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) - \|\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} - \boldsymbol{J}\|_{\rm op} \ge \lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})/2$ on event $\mathcal{E}_N$, so $\|\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}^{-1}\|_{\rm op} \le 2/\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:orth} gives $\|\hat{\boldsymbol{S}} - \boldsymbol{S}\|_2 = \mathcal{O}_P(N^{-1/2} + r_N^2)$, while non-orthogonality of the coupled Gram matrix yields $\|\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} - \boldsymbol{J}\|_{\rm op} = \mathcal{O}_P(N^{-1/2} + r_N)$. Combining with $\|\boldsymbol{\theta}_\gamma\|_2 \le M_\theta + C\varepsilon_\gamma/\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})$ establishes the finite-sample bound in (ii). $\blacksquare$

\begin{proposition}[Exact Proxy Bias in the Symmetric Flip Model]
\label{prop:flip}
Let $K=2$, $\boldsymbol\pi^*=(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$, $T_t=V_t$ with $V_t\perp(S_t,X_t,U_t)$, $\E V_t=0$, $\Var V_t=\sigma^2$, $Y_t=\theta_{S_t}T_t+g_{S_t}+U_t$, $\E[U_t\mid T_t,S_t]=0$, and $\boldsymbol\gamma_t=(1-\tfrac\varepsilon2)\boldsymbol H_t+\tfrac\varepsilon2(\boldsymbol 1-\boldsymbol H_t)$ with $\varepsilon\in[0,1)$ ($\varepsilon_\gamma=\varepsilon$). Then $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol J)=\sigma^2(1-\varepsilon)^2/2$ and
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol\theta_\gamma-\boldsymbol\theta^*=\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\cdot\frac{\theta_1-\theta_2}{2}\,(1,-1)^\top,\qquad
\|\boldsymbol\theta_\gamma-\boldsymbol\theta^*\|_2=\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\frac{|\theta_1-\theta_2|}{\sqrt2}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$m^\gamma_k=0$ since $\E V=0, V\perp S$, so $\tilde T_k=V$ and constants in $\tilde Y_k$ drop out of $\boldsymbol S$. With $a=1-\varepsilon/2$, $b=\varepsilon/2$, $\boldsymbol M=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\b&a\end{smallmatrix}\right)$: $\boldsymbol J=\frac{\sigma^2}{2}\boldsymbol M^2$ and $\boldsymbol S=\frac{\sigma^2}{2}\boldsymbol M\boldsymbol\theta^*$. Since $\boldsymbol M$ has eigenvalues $1$ (eigenvector $(1,1)^\top$) and $1-\varepsilon$ (eigenvector $(1,-1)^\top$), $\boldsymbol\theta_\gamma=\boldsymbol J^{-1}\boldsymbol S=\boldsymbol M^{-1}\boldsymbol\theta^*$, $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol J)=\sigma^2(1-\varepsilon)^2/2$, and $(\boldsymbol M^{-1}-\boldsymbol I)\boldsymbol\theta^*$ evaluates to the displayed vector.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Matching Minimax Lower Bound under Latent Confounding]
\label{thm:lower_bound}
Let $\mathcal{P}(\varepsilon_\gamma, \lambda_0)$ denote the family of data-generating processes satisfying Assumptions~\ref{ass:unconfoundedness}--\ref{ass:rates} and Assumption~\ref{ass:envelopes} with binary regimes ($K=2$), state proxy error $\E\|\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t - \boldsymbol{H}_t\|_1 \le \varepsilon_\gamma \in (0, 1/2]$, and minimal Gram eigenvalue $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) \ge \lambda_0 > 0$. Then there exists a universal numerical constant $c > 0$ such that for any sample size $N \ge 1$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:minimax_lower_bound}
\inf_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}} \sup_{P \in \mathcal{P}(\varepsilon_\gamma, \lambda_0)} \E_P\left[ \|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}} - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*(P)\|_2 \right] \ge c \frac{\varepsilon_\gamma}{\lambda_0}.
\end{equation}
Consequently, the degradation rate $\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon_\gamma / \lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}))$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:graceful} is minimax optimal and cannot be improved by any estimator.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We employ Le Cam's two-point method \citep{tsybakov2009introduction}. Consider a static binary regime setup ($K=2$) with balanced priors $\pi_1 = \pi_2 = 1/2$, $X_t \in \{0\}$, and scalar treatment innovation $V_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$ independent of $(S_t, U_t)$, where $\sigma^2 = 2\lambda_0 / (1-\varepsilon_\gamma)^2$.
Let proxy $Z_t \in \{1, 2\}$ satisfy $P(Z_t = S_t \mid S_t) = 1 - \varepsilon_\gamma$ and $P(Z_t = 3 - S_t \mid S_t) = \varepsilon_\gamma$, so $\E\|\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t - \boldsymbol{H}_t\|_1 = \varepsilon_\gamma$. By Proposition~\ref{prop:flip}, $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) = \sigma^2(1-\varepsilon_\gamma)^2/2 = \lambda_0$.
Construct two candidate data-generating mechanisms $P_0$ and $P_1$: under $P_0$, $\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(0)} = (\delta/2, -\delta/2)^\top$ with baselines $g_1(0) = g_2(0) = 0$; under $P_1$, $\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(1)} = (-\delta/2, \delta/2)^\top$ with baseline shifts $g_1^{(1)} = \Delta g$ and $g_2^{(1)} = -\Delta g$, where $\Delta g = \frac{1 - 2\varepsilon_\gamma}{2\varepsilon_\gamma} \delta \E[|T_t|]$.
Parameter separation is $\|\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(0)} - \boldsymbol{\theta}^{(1)}\|_2 = \sqrt{2} \delta$.
Choosing $\delta = c_1 \frac{\varepsilon_\gamma}{\lambda_0}$ for sufficiently small $c_1 > 0$ ensures the Kullback-Leibler divergence between joint observable distributions satisfies $\mathrm{KL}(P_0^N, P_1^N) \le \alpha < \infty$.
By Le Cam's inequality \citep{tsybakov2009introduction, bickel1993efficient}, the minimax risk over $\{P_0, P_1\} \subset \mathcal{P}(\varepsilon_\gamma, \lambda_0)$ satisfies:
\[
\inf_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}} \sup_{P \in \{P_0, P_1\}} \E_P\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}} - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*(P)\|_2 \ge \frac{\|\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(0)} - \boldsymbol{\theta}^{(1)}\|_2}{4} \exp(-\mathrm{KL}(P_0^N, P_1^N)) \ge \frac{\sqrt{2}\delta}{4} e^{-\alpha} \ge c \frac{\varepsilon_\gamma}{\lambda_0},
\]
which proves the matching lower bound \eqref{eq:minimax_lower_bound}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Latent-Confounding Difficulty Frontier $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{causal}}$}
\label{app:difficulty_frontier}
Theorem~\ref{thm:graceful} demonstrates that the upper bound on structural causal estimation error decomposes into:
\begin{equation}
    \|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\gamma - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2 \le C \cdot \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{causal}} + \mathcal{O}_P(N^{-1/2}), \quad \text{where} \quad \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{causal}} \equiv \frac{\varepsilon_\gamma}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})}.
\end{equation}
We highlight several structural properties of the difficulty index $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{causal}}$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Scale-Dependent Difficulty Ratio:} Both population proxy error $\varepsilon_\gamma$ and Gram conditioning $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})$ interact multiplicatively in governing downstream estimation risk (dimensionless under standardized treatment units). When either proxy recovery is poor ($\varepsilon_\gamma \to 1$) or overlap is weak ($\lambda_{\min} \to 0$), estimation risk escalates sharply.
    \item \textbf{Asymmetric Sensitivity:} In our factorial simulations (Table~\ref{tab:bench_results}), reducing $\lambda_{\min}$ by an order of magnitude produces an equivalent variance and bias inflation to a tenfold increase in state proxy error $\varepsilon_\gamma$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:bridge}: Surrogate Entropy-Difficulty Bridge}
\label{app:proof_bridge}
Let $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t \equiv P(S_t \in \cdot \mid \mathcal{F}_t) \in \Delta^K$ be the Bayesian posterior state distribution with respect to filtration $\mathcal{F}_t \equiv \sigma(Z_{1:t})$. Under proper Bayesian calibration, conditional on $\mathcal{F}_t$, the true latent indicator $\boldsymbol{H}_t \equiv \boldsymbol{e}_{S_t}$ is distributed as a categorical random variable taking value $\boldsymbol{e}_k$ with probability $\gamma_{tk}$.

\textbf{Step 1: Expected $L_1$ Error as Gini Impurity.}
Evaluating the conditional expectation of the $L_1$ error:
\begin{align}
    \E\left[ \|\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t - \boldsymbol{H}_t\|_1 \;\middle|\; \mathcal{F}_t \right] &= \sum_{k=1}^K \gamma_{tk} \|\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t - \boldsymbol{e}_k\|_1 = \sum_{k=1}^K \gamma_{tk} \left( (1 - \gamma_{tk}) + \sum_{j \neq k} \gamma_{tj} \right) \nonumber \\
    &= \sum_{k=1}^K \gamma_{tk} \cdot 2(1 - \gamma_{tk}) = 2\left( 1 - \sum_{k=1}^K \gamma_{tk}^2 \right) = 2 \operatorname{Gini}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t).
\end{align}

\textbf{Step 2: Entropy Bound on Gini Impurity.}
By the standard concave inequality $\ln(x) \le x - 1$, for any $x > 0$:
\begin{equation}
    H(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t) = -\sum_{k=1}^K \gamma_{tk} \ln \gamma_{tk} = \sum_{k=1}^K \gamma_{tk} \ln\left(\frac{1}{\gamma_{tk}}\right) \ge \sum_{k=1}^K \gamma_{tk} (1 - \gamma_{tk}) = \operatorname{Gini}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t).
\end{equation}
Furthermore, for any binary regime partition ($K=2$) with $\gamma_{t1} = p$ and $\gamma_{t2} = 1-p$, $2 \operatorname{Gini}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t) = 4p(1-p)$. Comparing to binary Shannon entropy $H(p) = -p\ln p - (1-p)\ln(1-p)$, the ratio $\frac{4p(1-p)}{H(p)}$ achieves its maximum at $p = 1/2$, where $\frac{4(1/4)}{\ln 2} = \frac{1}{\ln 2} \approx 1.4427$. Hence for all $p \in [0, 1]$:
\begin{equation}
    2 \operatorname{Gini}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t) \le C_K H(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t), \quad \text{where } C_K \le \frac{1}{\ln 2} \approx 1.4427.
\end{equation}

\textbf{Step 3: Population and Observable Bridge.}
Taking the population expectation of Step 1 and applying Step 2:
\begin{equation}
    \varepsilon_\gamma \equiv \E\left[ \E\left[ \|\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t - \boldsymbol{H}_t\|_1 \;\middle|\; \mathcal{F}_t \right] \right] = 2 \E[\operatorname{Gini}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t)] \le C_K \E[H(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t)].
\end{equation}
Dividing by the global minimal singular value $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{causal}} \equiv \frac{\varepsilon_\gamma}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})} \le C_K \E\left[ \frac{H(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t)}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})} \right] = C_K \E[\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{global}}^H(t)].
\end{equation}
Furthermore, under Assumption~\ref{ass:spectral_comparability} (Two-Sided Spectral Comparability), the rolling Gram matrix satisfies $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}_t) \le \overline{c}_J \lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})$ almost surely for all evaluation windows $t \ge w$, which implies $\frac{1}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})} \le \frac{\overline{c}_J}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}_t)}$. Pointwise substitution yields:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{causal}} \le C_K \overline{c}_J \E\left[ \frac{H(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t)}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}_t)} \right] = C_K \overline{c}_J \E[\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{operational}}(t)],
\end{equation}
and $\E[\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{operational}}(t)] \le \underline{c}_J^{-1} \E[\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{global}}^H(t)]$. In simulation benchmarks where true states are known and posteriors are evaluated at population parameters $\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*$, the ergodic theorem and central limit theorem for stationary $\alpha$-mixing chains ensure that $\hat{\varepsilon}_{\gamma, N} \equiv \frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N \|\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t - \boldsymbol{H}_t\|_1 = \varepsilon_\gamma + \mathcal{O}_P(N^{-1/2})$ (preserved under $\sqrt{N}$-consistent estimation of $\boldsymbol{\Lambda}$), providing an evaluation-only benchmark surrogate $\hat{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathrm{causal}, N} \equiv \hat{\varepsilon}_{\gamma, N}/\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) = \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{causal}} + \mathcal{O}_P(N^{-1/2})$. We emphasize that these bounds establish $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{operational}}(t)$ as an observable surrogate upper bound for $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{causal}}$, not a two-sided equivalence. Across our multi-season ground-sensor streams, the empirical ratio $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}_t)/\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})$ has 95th percentile in $[1.06, 1.72]$ and maximum $\le 7.35$, providing empirical support for the spectral-comparability assumption with finite $\overline{c}_J$. $\blacksquare$

\subsection{A Priori Proxy Diagnostics and Separation Screening}
\label{app:proxy_diagnostics}
Before fitting OR-DML or downstream prediction heads, practitioners should assess proxy informativeness on auxiliary measurements $Z_t \in \R^q$. Let $\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{Z,k} \equiv \frac{1}{N_k}\sum_{t: \hat{S}_t=k} Z_t$ and $\hat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{Z,k}$ denote empirical cluster moments. We define the standardized separation index:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_Z \equiv \min_{j \neq k} \frac{\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{Z,j} - \hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{Z,k}\|_2}{\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}(\mathrm{Tr}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{Z,j}) + \mathrm{Tr}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{Z,k}))/q}}.
\end{equation}
When $\Delta_Z < 0.5$ (or silhouette score $< 0.15$), the overlap between regime emission densities $P(Z_t \mid S_t=j)$ and $P(Z_t \mid S_t=k)$ is severe, driving posterior entropy $\bar{H}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}) \to \ln K$. By Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility}, point identification fails identically in the limit $\Delta_Z \to 0$. Deployed pipelines should enforce an automated screening gate: if $\Delta_Z < 0.5$ or mean entropy exceeds $0.80 \ln K$, the system must flag non-identifiability and refuse to emit point causal effects without external structural constraints.

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral}: Leading-Order Risk Bound and Spectral Regularization}
\label{app:proof_thm4}
Let $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda = (\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \hat{\boldsymbol{S}}$, and define the population regularized estimand $\boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda \equiv (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \boldsymbol{S}$.
We decompose the estimation error around the regularized population estimand $\boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda$ via the exact algebraic identity:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:exact_decomp}
    \E\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2^2 = \|\boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2^2 + \E\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda\|_2^2 + 2 \langle \E[\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda], \; \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^* \rangle,
\end{equation}
which accounts explicitly for the cross term between stochastic estimation error and deterministic regularization bias.
For the deterministic bias, substituting $\boldsymbol{S} = \boldsymbol{J}\boldsymbol{\theta}^* + \boldsymbol{b}_\gamma$:
\begin{align}
    \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^* &= (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \left( \boldsymbol{S} - (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})\boldsymbol{\theta}^* \right) \nonumber \\
    &= (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \left( \boldsymbol{S} - \boldsymbol{J}\boldsymbol{\theta}^* - \lambda \boldsymbol{\theta}^* \right) \nonumber \\
    &= (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \left( \boldsymbol{b}_\gamma - \lambda \boldsymbol{\theta}^* \right), \label{eq:reg_identity}
\end{align}
which establishes the exact deterministic identity \eqref{eq:reg_identity}.
Since $\boldsymbol{J} \succeq 0$ with minimal eigenvalue $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) \ge 0$, the spectral operator norm is $\|(\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}} = \frac{1}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) + \lambda}$.
Applying the triangle inequality:
\begin{equation}
    \|\boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2 \le \|(\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}} \left( \|\boldsymbol{b}_\gamma\|_2 + \lambda \|\boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2 \right) \le \frac{C \varepsilon_\gamma + \lambda M_\theta}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) + \lambda}.
\end{equation}
For the stochastic variance, expanding $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda$:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda = (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \left( \hat{\boldsymbol{S}} - \boldsymbol{S} - (\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} - \boldsymbol{J})\boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda \right) + o_p(N^{-1/2}).
\end{equation}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:asymptotics}(a), $\sqrt{N}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda) \Rightarrow \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\lambda^{\mathrm{tot}})$. Under uniform integrability of $N\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda\|_2^2$, stochastic variance satisfies $V_N \equiv \E\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda\|_2^2 = \frac{1}{N}\mathrm{Tr}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\lambda^{\mathrm{tot}}) + o(N^{-1}) \le \frac{\mathrm{Tr}(\boldsymbol{\Omega}_\lambda^{\mathrm{tot}})}{N (\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) + \lambda)^2} + o(N^{-1})$, using $\mathrm{Tr}(\boldsymbol{A}^{-1}\boldsymbol{\Omega}\boldsymbol{A}^{-1}) \le \|\boldsymbol{A}^{-2}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\mathrm{Tr}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})$ for $\boldsymbol{A} = \boldsymbol{J}+\lambda\boldsymbol{I} \succ 0$.
Applying Minkowski's inequality in $L_2(P; \R^K)$ to the decomposition $(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*) = (\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda) + (\boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*)$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:minkowski_risk}
    \left(\E\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2^2\right)^{1/2} \le \|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda\|_{L_2} + \|\boldsymbol{\theta}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2 \le \sqrt{V_N} + B_\lambda,
\end{equation}
which establishes the finite-sample risk upper bound:
\begin{equation}
    \E\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2^2 \le (B_\lambda + \sqrt{V_N})^2 \le 2 B_\lambda^2 + 2 V_N \le 2\left(\frac{C \varepsilon_\gamma + \lambda M_\theta}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) + \lambda}\right)^2 + \frac{2\mathrm{Tr}(\boldsymbol{\Omega}_\lambda^{\mathrm{tot}})}{N (\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) + \lambda)^2} + o(N^{-1}),
\end{equation}
completely avoiding any requirement to bound the stochastic bias cross-term.

\textbf{Risk Bound Minimizer vs. Empirical Grid Optimum:}
Differentiating the leading-order risk upper bound $R_{\mathrm{bound}}(\lambda) \equiv \left(\frac{C \varepsilon_\gamma + \lambda M_\theta}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) + \lambda}\right)^2 + \frac{\mathrm{Tr}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}{N (\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) + \lambda)^2}$ with respect to $\lambda \ge 0$, the bound-optimal shrinkage parameter $\lambda^*_{\mathrm{bound}} \equiv \arg\min_{\lambda \ge 0} R_{\mathrm{bound}}(\lambda)$ satisfies:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\partial}{\partial \lambda} \left[ \left(\frac{C \varepsilon_\gamma + \lambda M_\theta}{\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) + \lambda}\right)^2 + \frac{\mathrm{Tr}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}{N (\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) + \lambda)^2} \right] = 0.
\end{equation}
When the overlap Gram matrix is ill-conditioned ($\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) \approx 0$), the variance term $\frac{\mathrm{Tr}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}{N (\lambda_{\min} + \lambda)^2}$ dominates for small $\lambda$, while the deterministic shrinkage bias $(\frac{\lambda M_\theta}{\lambda_{\min} + \lambda})^2$ dominates for large $\lambda$. The interior minimizer $\lambda^*_{\mathrm{bound}} > 0$ balances variance reduction against shrinkage bias in the upper bound. We distinguish $\lambda^*_{\mathrm{bound}}$ and the empirical surrogate minimizer $\hat{\lambda}^*_{\mathrm{emp}} = \arg\min_\lambda \hat{R}(\lambda)$ from the true infeasible risk minimizer $\lambda^*_{\mathrm{true}} \equiv \arg\min_\lambda \E\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2^2$.
In our empirical simulation benchmark across 100 Monte Carlo replications (\texttt{reports/or\_dml\_regularization\_frontier.csv}), the discrete grid minimum occurs at $\hat{\lambda}^*_{\mathrm{empirical}} \approx 0.20$ ($\text{MSE} = 0.0321$, representing a $25.9\times$ empirical risk reduction in this benchmark DGP relative to the unregularized estimator $\lambda = 0.0$ where $\text{MSE} = 0.8313$), while $\lambda = 0.10$ achieves $\text{MSE} = 0.0362$ ($23.0\times$ empirical reduction) with peak coverage of $85.0\%$.
We emphasize that Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral} provides the functional upper-bound characterization of the bias-variance trade-off; the exact numerical reduction factors (23.0$\times$ and 25.9$\times$) are empirical simulation properties of the benchmark DGP rather than universal theoretical constants. $\blacksquare$

\subsection{Derivation of Adaptive Spectral Regularization Parameter and Oracle Bound}
\label{app:adaptive_lambda_derivation}
We consider the data-driven selection of the spectral shrinkage parameter $\lambda \ge 0$. While classical Tikhonov regularization selects shrinkage via prediction cross-validation, in OR-DML the target is the structural causal parameter vector $\boldsymbol{\theta}^*$, where prediction cross-validation conflates causal identification with prognostic predictive fit.
We distinguish two distinct regularization regimes:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Inferential Regularization ($\lambda = o(N^{-1/2})$):} Required by Theorem~\ref{thm:asymptotics} to guarantee asymptotic unbiasedness $\sqrt{N}\mathrm{Bias}(\lambda) \to \mathbf{0}$, ensuring centered Gaussian limit laws and valid nominal confidence interval coverage.
    \item \textbf{Finite-Sample Risk Minimization ($\lambda_{\mathrm{risk}} = \mathcal{O}(1)$):} Minimizes the finite-sample expected $\ell_2$ estimation risk $\E\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_2^2$ under severe causal ill-conditioning ($\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) \approx 0$).
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Empirical Risk Surrogate and Convexity:}
Define the empirical plug-in risk surrogate $\hat{R}(\lambda)$ by substituting empirical estimators $(\hat{\varepsilon}_\gamma, \lambda_{\min}(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}), \hat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}, \|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_0\|_2)$ into the risk upper bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral}:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{R}(\lambda) = \frac{(C \hat{\varepsilon}_\gamma + \lambda \|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_0\|_2)^2 + N^{-1}\mathrm{Tr}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}})}{(\lambda_{\min}(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}) + \lambda)^2}.
\end{equation}
Let $u = \lambda_{\min}(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}) + \lambda \ge \lambda_{\min}(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}) > 0$. Rewriting $\lambda = u - \lambda_{\min}$, the numerator becomes:
\begin{equation}
    \left( C \hat{\varepsilon}_\gamma - \lambda_{\min}(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}) \|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_0\|_2 + u \|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_0\|_2 \right)^2 + N^{-1}\mathrm{Tr}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}) = (A + u B)^2 + V,
\end{equation}
where $A = C \hat{\varepsilon}_\gamma - \lambda_{\min}(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}) \|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_0\|_2$, $B = \|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_0\|_2$, and $V = N^{-1}\mathrm{Tr}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}})$.
Dividing by $u^2$:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{R}(u - \lambda_{\min}) = B^2 + \frac{2AB}{u} + \frac{A^2 + V}{u^2}.
\end{equation}
Differentiating with respect to $u$:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d \hat{R}}{du} = -\frac{2AB}{u^2} - \frac{2(A^2 + V)}{u^3} = -\frac{2}{u^3} \left[ AB u + (A^2 + V) \right].
\end{equation}
Setting the derivative to zero yields the unique critical point $u^* = \frac{A^2 + V}{|A| B}$ when $A < 0$. When $A \ge 0$ (i.e., $C \hat{\varepsilon}_\gamma \ge \lambda_{\min}(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}) \|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_0\|_2$), $\frac{d\hat{R}}{du} < 0$ on $(0, \infty)$, so that the objective is strictly decreasing toward $B^2$ as $\lambda \to \infty$. Constraining the search to a compact admissible set $\lambda \in [0, \bar{\lambda}]$ with a fixed upper bound $\bar{\lambda} < \infty$ (independent of $N$), the bounded empirical minimizer is:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\lambda}^* = \begin{cases} 
    \mathrm{clip}\left( \frac{(C\hat{\varepsilon}_\gamma - \lambda_{\min}\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_0\|_2)^2 + N^{-1}\mathrm{Tr}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}})}{(\lambda_{\min}\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_0\|_2 - C\hat{\varepsilon}_\gamma)\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_0\|_2} - \lambda_{\min}(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}), \; 0, \; \bar{\lambda} \right), & \text{if } A < 0, \\
    \bar{\lambda}, & \text{if } A \ge 0.
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Because $R'(u) = -\frac{2}{u^3}[AB u + (A^2+V)]$ changes sign strictly from negative to positive at $u^*$ when $A < 0$, $u^*$ is the unique global interior minimizer on $(0, \infty)$.

\begin{lemma}[Oracle Inequality for the Surrogate]
\label{lem:surrogate_oracle}
Let $R_{\rm surr}$ be the population surrogate, $\hat R$ its plug-in, and $\Delta_N=\sup_{\lambda\in[0,\bar\lambda]}|\hat R(\lambda)-R_{\rm surr}(\lambda)|=\mathcal{O}_P(N^{-1/2})$.
Then $R_{\rm surr}(\hat\lambda^*)\le\inf_{\lambda\in[0,\bar\lambda]}R_{\rm surr}(\lambda)+2\Delta_N$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For any $\lambda^\circ\in[0,\bar\lambda]$: $R_{\rm surr}(\hat\lambda^*)\le\hat R(\hat\lambda^*)+\Delta_N\le\hat R(\lambda^\circ)+\Delta_N\le R_{\rm surr}(\lambda^\circ)+2\Delta_N$.
\end{proof}
This guarantee governs the surrogate, not the true risk; calibrated plug-in $\hat\varepsilon_\gamma = \frac{2}{N}\sum_t \operatorname{Gini}(\boldsymbol\gamma_t)$ preserves ranking when $C\hat\varepsilon_\gamma$ dominates. $\blacksquare$

\subsection{State Space Scalability and Matrix Concentration for $K > 2$}
\label{app:state_space_scalability}
While our primary experiments study binary regimes ($K=2$), the OR-DML coupled estimator generalizes directly to arbitrary finite state dimensions $K \ge 2$. Here we formalize the computational and statistical scalability of the coupled Gram matrix $\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} \in \R^{K \times K}$.

\textbf{Computational Complexity and Matrix Concentration under $\alpha$-Mixing:}
Constructing $\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}$ and score $\hat{\boldsymbol{S}}$ requires evaluating $K$ nuisance pairs across $B$ folds ($\mathcal{O}(B K \cdot \mathrm{Cost}(\text{Nuisance}))$). Forming $\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}$ takes $\mathcal{O}(N K^2)$ operations, and solving $(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1}\hat{\boldsymbol{S}}$ via Cholesky factorization takes $\mathcal{O}(K^3)$ operations, taking $<1$ ms for $K \le 20$.
Statistically, the empirical Gram matrix $\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N \boldsymbol{\Gamma}_t \tilde{\boldsymbol{T}}_t \tilde{\boldsymbol{T}}_t^\top \boldsymbol{\Gamma}_t$ concentrates around $\boldsymbol{J}$ under geometric $\alpha$-mixing with spectral gap $\rho_{\mathrm{gap}} \equiv 1 - |\lambda_2(\boldsymbol{\Pi})| > 0$ via the Matrix Bernstein inequality \citep{banna2016bernstein}:
\begin{equation}
    \|\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} - \boldsymbol{J}\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le C_J \sqrt{\frac{K \log(K/\delta)}{N \cdot \rho_{\mathrm{gap}}}} + \frac{C_J' K \log(K/\delta)}{N \cdot \rho_{\mathrm{gap}}}.
\end{equation}
By Weyl's theorem, $|\lambda_{\min}(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}) - \lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J})| \le \|\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} - \boldsymbol{J}\|_{\mathrm{op}}$, ensuring that $\lambda_{\min}(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}})$ concentrates at the standard $\mathcal{O}_P(N^{-1/2})$ rate whenever $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}) > 0$.

\textbf{Non-Identifiability vs.\ Shrinkage: When Systems Must Abstain:}
Spectral shrinkage $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda = (\hat{\boldsymbol{J}} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1}\hat{\boldsymbol{S}}$ stabilizes variance under ill-conditioned overlap ($\lambda_{\min} \approx 0$). However, when data lack identifying variation entirely—diagnosed by condition number $\kappa(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}) > 10^4$ or a moving-block bootstrap 95\% confidence interval for $\lambda_{\min}(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}})$ that covers zero—the structural parameter $\boldsymbol{\theta}^*$ is non-identified (Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility}). In this regime, heavy shrinkage $\lambda \to \infty$ artificially forces $\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda\| \to 0$ without recovering structural effects. Deployed decision pipelines should treat $\kappa(\hat{\boldsymbol{J}}) > 10^4$ as a structural abstention trigger rather than masking non-identifiability with excessive shrinkage.

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:asymptotics}: Asymptotic Normality Under Dependent Purged Cross-Fitting}
\label{app:proof_thm5}

\begin{assumption}[HMM Regularity, Gram-Nuisance Rate, Moments]
\label{ass:hmm_gram}
(H) The emission model is identifiable up to label permutation; $\hat{\boldsymbol\Lambda}$ is the MLE fitted on a calibration window of length $N_{\rm cal}$ separated by the embargo, with $N/N_{\rm cal}\to\kappa\in[0,\infty)$ ($\kappa=0$ for oracle filter), and $\gamma_{tk}(\boldsymbol\Lambda)$ is continuously differentiable with bounded derivative. Then $\sqrt{N_{\rm cal}}(\hat{\boldsymbol\Lambda}-\boldsymbol\Lambda^*)\Rightarrow\mathcal N(\mathbf{0},\mathcal I_\Lambda^{-1})$ \citep{leroux1992maximum,bickel1998asymptotic,douc2004asymptotic}.
(G) $\sqrt{\varepsilon_\gamma}\max_k\|\hat m_k-m_k^\gamma\|_{L_2}=o_P(N^{-1/2})$; true for hard assignments and under Assumption~\ref{ass:rates} when $\varepsilon_\gamma=\mathcal{O}(N^{-1/2})$.
(M) $\E\|\boldsymbol\Psi_t\|^{2+\delta}<\infty$ for some $\delta>0$, and $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol J)+\lambda\ge c_\lambda>0$ is fixed.
\end{assumption}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:asymptotics}]
\emph{Step 1 (exact algebra, centering at $\boldsymbol\theta_\lambda$).} Because $(\hat{\boldsymbol J}+\lambda\boldsymbol I)\hat{\boldsymbol\theta}_\lambda=\hat{\boldsymbol S}$ and $(\boldsymbol J+\lambda\boldsymbol I)\boldsymbol\theta_\lambda=\boldsymbol S$:
\begin{equation}
\hat{\boldsymbol\theta}_\lambda-\boldsymbol\theta_\lambda=(\hat{\boldsymbol J}+\lambda\boldsymbol I)^{-1}\Big[(\hat{\boldsymbol S}-\hat{\boldsymbol J}\boldsymbol\theta_\lambda)-(\boldsymbol S-\boldsymbol J\boldsymbol\theta_\lambda)\Big]
=(\hat{\boldsymbol J}+\lambda\boldsymbol I)^{-1}\frac1N\sum_{t=1}^N \tilde{\boldsymbol\Psi}_t,
\end{equation}
with $\tilde{\boldsymbol\Psi}_t=\boldsymbol\Psi_t(\boldsymbol\theta_\lambda)-\E\boldsymbol\Psi_t(\boldsymbol\theta_\lambda)$ strictly mean zero. (At $\boldsymbol\theta^*$ the summands have non-vanishing mean $\boldsymbol b_\gamma$, so the CLT cannot be centered there without rate conditions on $\varepsilon_\gamma$.)

\emph{Step 2 (nuisance errors).} Expanding in $\hat\eta-\eta^\gamma$: by Lemma~\ref{lem:orth}(i)--(ii) the $\mu$-direction and cross-moment have zero first-order term; the Gram part contributes $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\varepsilon_\gamma}\,\|\hat m-m^\gamma\|)=o_P(N^{-1/2})$ by (G); second-order remainder is $\mathcal{O}_P(r_N^2)=o_P(N^{-1/2})$ by Assumption~\ref{ass:rates}.

\emph{Step 3 (cross-fitting under dependence).} By Bradley's coupling lemma \citep{bradley2005basic}, nuisance fits on blocks separated by $\tau^*$ from test blocks match an independent copy except on an event of probability $\le N\alpha(\tau^*)\le MN^{1-c_\alpha C}\to0$ when $C>1/c_\alpha$.

\emph{Step 4 (CLT).} $\tilde{\boldsymbol\Psi}_t$ is strictly stationary, mean zero, $\alpha$-mixing with geometric rate and $(2{+}\delta)$ moments, so $N^{-1/2}\sum_t\tilde{\boldsymbol\Psi}_t\Rightarrow\mathcal N(\mathbf{0},\boldsymbol\Omega_\lambda)$ \citep{ibragimov1962some}.

\emph{Step 5 (HMM parameters).} With estimated $\hat{\boldsymbol\Lambda}$, $N^{-1/2}\sum_t\tilde{\boldsymbol\Psi}_t(\hat{\boldsymbol\Lambda})=N^{-1/2}\sum_t\tilde{\boldsymbol\Psi}_t(\boldsymbol\Lambda^*)+\boldsymbol A_\Lambda\sqrt N(\hat{\boldsymbol\Lambda}-\boldsymbol\Lambda^*)+o_P(1)$. By (H), $\sqrt N(\hat{\boldsymbol\Lambda}-\boldsymbol\Lambda^*)\Rightarrow\mathcal N(\mathbf{0},\kappa\,\mathcal I_\Lambda^{-1})$, asymptotically independent of the evaluation sum across the embargo buffer, so the asymptotic covariances add: $\boldsymbol\Omega_\lambda^{\mathrm{tot}} = \boldsymbol\Omega_\lambda + \kappa \boldsymbol A_\Lambda \mathcal I_\Lambda^{-1} \boldsymbol A_\Lambda^\top$.

\emph{Step 6 (Slutsky).} $\hat{\boldsymbol J}\to_p\boldsymbol J$ and $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol J)+\lambda\ge c_\lambda$ yield $(\hat{\boldsymbol J}+\lambda\boldsymbol I)^{-1}\to_p(\boldsymbol J+\lambda\boldsymbol I)^{-1}$. (b) is the identity $\boldsymbol\theta_\lambda-\boldsymbol\theta^*=\mathrm{Bias}$; (c) follows by Slutsky because $\sqrt N\|\mathrm{Bias}\|\le\sqrt N(C\varepsilon_\gamma+\lambda M_\theta)/(\lambda_{\min}+\lambda)$. $\blacksquare$
\end{proof}

\textit{Operational Modes of Forward Filtering.} In offline retrospective smoothing, $\gamma_{tk}^{\mathrm{smooth}} = P(S_t = k \mid Z_{1:N})$. In real-time decision making, three distinct forward modes exist: (1) \emph{Oracle Filter}: $P(S_t = k \mid Z_{1:t}, \boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*)$ using the true HMM parameters; (2) \emph{Training-Fitted Forward Filter}: $P(S_t = k \mid Z_{1:t}, \hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}})$ where parameters $\hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}$ are fitted on a historical calibration window strictly preceding the evaluation window ($\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{cal}} < \mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{eval}}$) and state inference updates strictly forward on past-to-present observations $Z_{1:t}$; and (3) \emph{Expanding-Window Online Filter}: where both parameters $\hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}_t$ and state updates proceed sequentially. Our implementations evaluate Mode 1 and Mode 2, where Mode 2 enforces causal forward state filtering without future observation leakage (distinguishing forward state updating from online sequential parameter re-estimation).

\subsection{Observed-Data Orthogonal Score under Estimated Latent Dynamics}
\label{app:observed_data_score}
In semiparametric causal inference \citep{chernozhukov2018double, bickel1993efficient}, when nuisance parameters consist of both non-parametric outcome/propensity regressions $\boldsymbol{\eta} = (m, \mu)$ and finite-dimensional parameters $\boldsymbol{\Lambda}$ governing latent state transitions and emissions, one must characterize the sensitivity of the causal estimator to the first-order estimation error in $\hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}$.

Let the state-weighted regime score vector be:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\psi}_{\mathrm{regime}}(W_t; \boldsymbol{\theta}, \boldsymbol{\eta}, \boldsymbol{\Lambda}) \equiv \sum_{k=1}^K \gamma_{tk}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}) (T_t - m_k(X_t)) \left( Y_t - \mu_k(X_t) - \theta_k (T_t - m_k(X_t)) \right).
\end{equation}
In a hidden Markov model satisfying standard regularity and mixing conditions \citep{bickel1993efficient}, the latent parameters $\boldsymbol{\Lambda}$ are estimated via sequence-level maximum likelihood with sequence score $\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}} \log p(Z_{1:N}; \boldsymbol{\Lambda}) = \sum_{t=1}^N \boldsymbol{\phi}_{\Lambda, t} + o_P(\sqrt{N})$, where $\boldsymbol{\phi}_{\Lambda, t} \equiv \nabla_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}} \log p(Z_t \mid Z_{1:t-1}; \boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*)$ denotes the sequence-level prediction-error score increment satisfying $\E[\boldsymbol{\phi}_{\Lambda, t} \mid Z_{1:t-1}] = \mathbf{0}$ and $\frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N \E[\boldsymbol{\phi}_{\Lambda, t}\boldsymbol{\phi}_{\Lambda, t}^\top] \to \mathcal{I}_\Lambda$. Evaluating the Gateaux derivative with respect to $\boldsymbol{\Lambda}$ at the oracle targets $(\boldsymbol{\theta}^*, \boldsymbol{\eta}^*, \boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*)$:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{A}_\Lambda \equiv \lim_{N \to \infty} \frac{1}{N} \sum_{t=1}^N \sum_{k=1}^K \E\left[ \frac{\partial \gamma_{tk}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*)}{\partial \boldsymbol{\Lambda}^\top} (T_t - m_k^*(X_t)) \left( Y_t - \mu_k^*(X_t) - \theta_k^* (T_t - m_k^*(X_t)) \right) \right].
\end{equation}
Importantly, $\boldsymbol{A}_\Lambda$ does not vanish pointwise across regimes. While Assumption~\ref{ass:innovation} guarantees that the structural innovation has zero mean conditional on the true state, $\E[Y_t - \mu_k^*(X_t) - \theta_k^*(T_t - m_k^*(X_t)) \mid X_t, T_t, S_t = k] = 0$, conditioning on observed history $Z_{1:N}$ alone yields:
\begin{equation}
    \E\left[ Y_t - \mu_k^*(X_t) - \theta_k^* (T_t - m_k^*(X_t)) \;\middle|\; X_t, T_t, Z_{1:N} \right] = \sum_{j \neq k} \gamma_{tj}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*) \left[ (\mu_j^*(X_t) - \mu_k^*(X_t)) + (\theta_j^* - \theta_k^*)(T_t - m_j^*(X_t)) \right].
\end{equation}
This residual is non-zero whenever regimes differ in baseline potential outcomes ($\mu_j^* \neq \mu_k^*$) or treatment responsiveness ($\theta_j^* \neq \theta_k^*$).

Under purged block cross-fitting (Algorithm 1), this first-order variation is controlled via two complementary mechanisms:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Decoupling via Purged Embargo Buffer:} The parameters $\hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}$ are estimated on historical calibration blocks or out-of-fold data separated by an embargo buffer $\tau^* \ge C \log N$. By the $\alpha$-mixing decay of the underlying process (Assumption~\ref{ass:mixing}), the covariance between evaluation fold innovations and the parameter estimation error $\hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}} - \boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*$ decays as $O(N^{-C c_\alpha})$.
    \item \textbf{Stacked Joint HAC Variance Representation:} When $\hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}$ is estimated at parametric rate $\sqrt{N}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}} - \boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*) = \mathcal{I}_\Lambda^{-1} \frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{t=1}^N \boldsymbol{\phi}_{\Lambda, t} + o_P(1)$, first-order parameter variation is absorbed into the sequence-level influence representation $\boldsymbol{\phi}_t = \boldsymbol{\phi}_{\theta, t} + \boldsymbol{A}_\Lambda \mathcal{I}_\Lambda^{-1} \boldsymbol{\phi}_{\Lambda, t}$, yielding the stacked M-estimation limiting distribution:
    \begin{equation}
        \sqrt{N} \begin{pmatrix} \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^* - \mathrm{Bias}(\lambda) \\ \hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}} - \boldsymbol{\Lambda}^* \end{pmatrix} \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}\left(\mathbf{0}, \begin{bmatrix} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\lambda & \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\theta \Lambda} \\ \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\theta \Lambda}^\top & \mathcal{I}_\Lambda^{-1} \end{bmatrix}\right),
    \end{equation}
    where $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\theta \Lambda} \equiv \operatorname{LRCov}(\boldsymbol{\phi}_{\theta, t}, \boldsymbol{\phi}_{\Lambda, t})$, and the long-run covariance $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\lambda$ and cross-term $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\theta \Lambda}$ are consistently captured by the joint Newey-West HAC sandwich estimator. In well-separated regimes ($\Delta_Z \to \infty$), posterior uncertainty vanishes ($\gamma_{tk} \to \mathbb{I}(S_t=k)$) and $\partial \gamma_{tk} / \partial \boldsymbol{\Lambda} \to \mathbf{0}$ exponentially fast, rendering $\|\boldsymbol{A}_\Lambda\| \le C e^{-c \Delta_Z^2}$ and recovering asymptotic orthogonality.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:variance}: Joint Delta-Method Inference for Population ATE}
\label{app:proof_prop6}
We distinguish three distinct regime weighting vectors:
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{-3pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
    \item \textbf{True Stationary Distribution $\boldsymbol{\pi}^*$:} The invariant measure satisfying $\boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top} \boldsymbol{\Pi}^* = \boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top}$.
    \item \textbf{Estimated HMM Stationary Distribution $\hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}})$:} The stationary vector derived from estimated HMM matrix $\hat{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}$.
    \item \textbf{Sample Posterior Occupation Distribution $\bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_N$:} The empirical average $\bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_N \equiv \frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t$.
\end{enumerate}

The sample Average Treatment Effect estimator is $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SATE}} = \sum_{k=1}^K \bar{\gamma}_{N, k} \hat{\theta}_{\lambda, k} = \bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_N^\top \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda$, with conditional variance $\sigma_{\mathrm{SATE}}^2 = \bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_N^\top \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\lambda \bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_N$.
For the population estimand $\theta_{\mathrm{PATE}}^* = \boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top} \boldsymbol{\theta}^*$, the plug-in estimator is $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{PATE}} = \hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}}^\top \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda$. Expanding $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{PATE}}$ around $(\boldsymbol{\pi}^*, \boldsymbol{\theta}^*)$ via the multivariate delta method:
$\sqrt{N}\left( \hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{PATE}} - \theta_{\mathrm{PATE}}^* \right) = \boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top} \sqrt{N}\left( \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda - \boldsymbol{\theta}^* \right) + \boldsymbol{\theta}^{*\top} \sqrt{N}\left( \hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}} - \boldsymbol{\pi}^* \right) + o_P(1)$, yielding asymptotic variance:
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_{\mathrm{PATE}}^2 = \lim_{N \to \infty} \Var\left( \sqrt{N}(\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{PATE}} - \theta_{\mathrm{PATE}}^*) \right) = \boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\lambda \boldsymbol{\pi}^* + \boldsymbol{\theta}^{*\top} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\pi \boldsymbol{\theta}^* + 2 \boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\theta\pi} \boldsymbol{\theta}^*,
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\lambda \equiv \lim_{N \to \infty} N \Var(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda)$, $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\pi \equiv \lim_{N \to \infty} N \Var(\hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}})$, and $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\theta\pi} \equiv \lim_{N \to \infty} N \Cov(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda, \hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}})$.

\textbf{Cross-Covariance Analysis and HMM Influence Orthogonality:}
When $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_\lambda$ and $\hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}}$ share estimated parameters $\hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}$, let $\boldsymbol{\phi}_\theta(W_t) \equiv (\boldsymbol{J} + \lambda \boldsymbol{I})^{-1} \boldsymbol{\Psi}_t$ and $\boldsymbol{\phi}_{\Lambda, t} \equiv \nabla_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}} \log p(Z_t \mid Z_{1:t-1}; \boldsymbol{\Lambda}^*)$. With $\sqrt{N}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}} - \boldsymbol{\pi}^*) = \nabla_\Lambda \boldsymbol{\pi}^* \mathcal{I}_\Lambda^{-1} \frac{1}{\sqrt{N}} \sum_{t=1}^N \boldsymbol{\phi}_{\Lambda, t} + o_P(1)$, the cross-covariance evaluates to:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\theta\pi} = \lim_{N \to \infty} \frac{1}{N} \sum_{t=1}^N \sum_{s=1}^N \E\left[ \boldsymbol{\phi}_\theta(W_t) \boldsymbol{\phi}_{\Lambda, s}^\top \right] \mathcal{I}_\Lambda^{-1} (\nabla_\Lambda \boldsymbol{\pi}^*)^\top = \operatorname{LRCov}(\boldsymbol{\phi}_\theta, \boldsymbol{\phi}_\Lambda) \mathcal{I}_\Lambda^{-1} (\nabla_\Lambda \boldsymbol{\pi}^*)^\top.
\end{equation}
A sufficient condition for $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\theta\pi} = \mathbf{0}$ is long-run cross-orthogonality $\operatorname{LRCov}(\boldsymbol{\phi}_\theta, \boldsymbol{\phi}_\Lambda) = \mathbf{0}$. In practice, $\sigma_{\mathrm{PATE}}^2$ is consistently estimated via a joint Newey-West HAC sandwich over the stacked $(2K)$-dimensional influence sequence $(\boldsymbol{\phi}_\theta(W_t)^\top, \boldsymbol{\phi}_\pi(Z_t)^\top)^\top$, where $\boldsymbol{\phi}_\pi(Z_t) \equiv \nabla_\Lambda \boldsymbol{\pi}^* \mathcal{I}_\Lambda^{-1} \boldsymbol{\phi}_{\Lambda, t}$.

\textbf{Stationary Distribution Variance vs.\ Sample Regime Occupancy:}
The parametric variance of $\hat{\boldsymbol{\pi}}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}})$ is $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\pi = \nabla_\Lambda \boldsymbol{\pi}^* \mathcal{I}_\Lambda^{-1} (\nabla_\Lambda \boldsymbol{\pi}^*)^\top$. For the sample occupancy process $\bar{\boldsymbol{S}}_N \equiv \frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N \boldsymbol{H}_t$, asymptotic covariance is governed by fundamental matrix $\mathbf{Z} \equiv (\mathbf{I} - \boldsymbol{\Pi}^* + \mathbf{1}\boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top})^{-1}$ \citep{kemeny1976finite}:
$\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{occ}} = \mathbf{D}_{\pi} \mathbf{Z} + \mathbf{Z}^\top \mathbf{D}_{\pi} - \mathbf{D}_{\pi} - \boldsymbol{\pi}^* \boldsymbol{\pi}^{*\top}$, with $\mathbf{D}_{\pi} = \mathrm{diag}(\boldsymbol{\pi}^*)$.
For a two-state Markov chain ($K=2$) with persistence $\rho = \lambda_2(\boldsymbol{\Pi}^*)$:
\begin{equation}
    \Var(\bar{S}_N) = \frac{\pi_0^* \pi_1^*}{N} \left( \frac{1 + \rho}{1 - \rho} \right) + o(N^{-1}).
\end{equation}
When $\Delta \theta^* \neq 0$, Markov persistence inflates the sampling variance of $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SATE}}$ by $\frac{1+\rho}{1-\rho} \approx 15.7\times$ under persistent stagnation ($\rho \approx 0.88$) relative to independent transitions. $\blacksquare$

\subsection{Independent Factorial Validation of the Graceful-Degradation Mechanism}
\label{app:factorial_reliability}
The earlier treatment-mean-separation diagnostic is intentionally not used because correctly specified regime-aware nuisance residualization removes regime mean shifts. Instead, we independently manipulate (i) controlled posterior proxy error $\varepsilon_\gamma$ and (ii) state-specific treatment innovation variance, which changes residual information and hence $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol J)$ \emph{after} oracle regime-aware cross-fitting. The $7\times7$ design uses 100 independent Markov sequences per cell ($N=1,200$; 4,900 runs). Across runs, causal $L_2$ error has Spearman correlation $0.9955$ with $\varepsilon_\gamma/\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol J)$, versus $0.6260$ with $\varepsilon_\gamma$ alone, $0.5209$ with $1/\lambda_{\min}$ alone, and $0.5355$ with an additive combination $\varepsilon_\gamma + 1/\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol J)$; cell-mean correlations are $0.9963$, $0.6249$, $0.5294$, and $0.5483$, respectively (\texttt{reports/factorial\_reliability\_correlations.csv}). Selective retention of the lowest-difficulty 40\% of runs reduces mean causal error from $48.81$ to $2.61$ and the 95th percentile from $228.10$ to $8.39$ (\texttt{reports/factorial\_risk\_coverage.csv}). These results validate an interaction, not dominance of either constituent.

\textbf{Representation Accuracy vs.\ Downstream Task Reliability.}
To evaluate whether latent representations translate to reliable prediction under shift, we fit a Ridge predictor $f_m: (X_t, T_t, \boldsymbol{\gamma}_t^{(m)}) \mapsto Y_t$ on training data ($N=1,200$) and freeze it. Residualized treatments $\tilde{T}_{tk} = T_t - \hat{m}_k(X_t)$ yield rolling coupled Gram matrix $\boldsymbol{J}_t = \frac{1}{w}\sum_{s=t-w+1}^t (\boldsymbol{\gamma}_s \odot \tilde{\boldsymbol{T}}_s)(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_s \odot \tilde{\boldsymbol{T}}_s)^\top$ ($w=48$) and minimum eigenvalue $\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}_t)$. On held-out Shift C ($N=1,200$, 5 seeds), a calibration split ($N_{\mathrm{cal}}=400$) fixes failure loss threshold $q_{0.90}$ out-of-sample. We evaluate future lead loss $L_{t+1} = (Y_{t+1} - \hat{Y}_{t+1})^2$ against: (i) State Entropy $H_t = H(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_t)$, (ii) Conditioning $1/\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}_t)$, and (iii) Task Difficulty $\mathcal{D}_t = H_t / \lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}_t)$. Moving-Block Bootstrap ($B=1,000$, $L=24$h) quantifies uncertainty over dependent series; sensitivity across $L \in \{12, 24, 48, 72\}$ confirms CI widths are stable ($0.142\text{--}0.147$; \texttt{reports/representation\_zoo\_mbb\_sensitivity.csv}).

\textbf{Offline Representation Diagnostics vs.\ Prospective Failure Signals.}
We formalize a three-level \textbf{Reliability Triangle}: (1)~\emph{State Identification} ($F_1$), (2)~\emph{Probabilistic Trustworthiness} ($\text{ECE}, \text{NLL}$), and (3)~\emph{Downstream Reliability} ($\text{ROC-AUC}, \mathcal{D}_t$). While retrospective diagnostics ($F_1, \text{ECE}$) require ground truth, online monitoring relies on prospective signals ($H_t, \lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{J}_t), \mathcal{D}_t$). In Table~\ref{tab:bench_models} (Panel~B), neural sequence models (GRU, Transformer, SSM) achieve competitive recovery ($F_1 \approx 0.84\text{--}0.86$) but near-chance failure detection ($\text{ROC-AUC} \approx 0.515\text{--}0.549$). In contrast, Gaussian HMM causal filtering achieves well-calibrated states ($\text{ECE} = 0.0397$) and informative failure anticipation ($\text{ROC-AUC} = \mathbf{0.6153 \pm 0.0246}$, MBB 95\% CI $[0.540, 0.683]$, $\text{PR-AUC} = 0.1300$). In fixed-effects regressions across 15 benchmark worlds ($N=60$; \texttt{reports/representation\_zoo\_hierarchical\_regression.csv}), classification $F_1$ explains substantial reliability ($\beta_1 = +0.9817 \pm 0.2011, p = 0.0002, R^2_{\mathrm{within}} = 0.4313$), while posterior log-loss provides incremental explanatory power ($\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{within}} = +0.0924, \beta_{\mathrm{NLL}} = -0.0653 \pm 0.0295, p = 0.0442$, reaching $R^2_{\mathrm{within}} = 0.5237$). Calibration ECE exhibits negative association ($\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{within}} = 0.0647$, reaching $R^2_{\mathrm{within}} = 0.4960$). Numerical eigenvalue floor sensitivity across $\epsilon \in [10^{-6}, 10^{-3}]$ confirms invariance (AUC $0.6153$).

\section{Synthetic Financial Macro Stress Test with Real Covariate Context}
\label{app:financial_transfer}
To verify that the latent regime confounding and difficulty framework reflects a general mathematical reality of sequential decision systems under hidden persistent context rather than an idiosyncratic atmospheric feature, we evaluate the Regime Intelligence engine on a synthetic macro financial stress test with real macroeconomic covariate context (daily FRED macroeconomic interest rate, spread, and liquidity indicators; $N=1,500$ daily trading periods; Figure~\ref{fig:financial_transfer_fig}). Regimes reflect low-volatility normal market dynamics versus severe liquidity stress / crisis regimes.
